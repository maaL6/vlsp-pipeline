                                                        LAW

                                                LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  The Law on Legal Aid No. 11/2017/QH14 dated June 20, 2017 of the National Assembly, which takes effect from January 1, 2018, is amended and supplemented by:

  1. The Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking No. 53/2024/QH15 dated November 28, 2024 of the National Assembly comes into force from July 01, 2025;

  2. The Law on Juvenile Justice No. 59/2024/QH15 dated November 30, 2024 of the National Assembly comes into force from January 01, 2026;

  3. The Law No. 05/2026/QH16 dated April 23, 2026 of the National Assembly amending and supplementing a number of articles of the Law on Legal Aid comes into force from January 01, 2027.

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;

  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Legal Aid [1].

  Chapter I

                                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for legally-aided persons; legal aid-providing organizations; legal aid-providing persons; legal aid services; responsibilities of agencies, organizations and individuals for legal aid services.

  Article 2. Legal aid

  Legal aid means the provision of free-of-charge legal services to legally-aided persons in legal aid-related cases according to this Law, contributing to ensuring human rights and civil rights in access to justice and equality before law.

  Article 3. Principles of legal aid services

  1. Comply with laws and professional rules of legal aid.

  2. Be timely, independent, honest and respect objective truth.

  3. Best protect lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons.

  4. Not to collect money, material benefits or other benefits from legal aid beneficiaries.

  Article 4. State policies on legal aid

  1. Legal aid is the responsibility of the State.

  2. The State shall adopt policies to ensure the right to receive legal aid in conformity with socio-economic conditions.

  3. The State shall adopt policies to improve the quality of legal aid and attract resources for providing legal aid.

  4. The State shall support, encourage, recognize and honor agencies, organizations and individuals participating in and contributing to legal aid activities.

  Article 5. Financial sources for legal aid services

  1. Financial sources for legal aid services include state budget funds; contributions and donations from domestic and foreign organizations and individuals and other lawful sources.

  2. Funding for legal aid services shall be allocated in annual state budget estimates of agencies performing state management of legal aid services in accordance with laws on state budget, public investment, science, technology and innovation, digital transformation, digital technology industry and relevant specialized laws.

  For local governments unable to balance their budgets, priority shall be given to allocating budget from annual budget balance to support the handling of complex and typical legal aid cases.

  3. Funding for legal aid services provided by voluntary organizations shall be covered by these organizations.

  Article 6. Prohibited acts in legal aid services

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations and persons are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon honor, dignity, legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons; discriminating against legally-aided persons;

  b) Receiving or demanding any amount of money, material benefits or other benefits from legally-aided persons; harassing legally-aided persons;

  c) Disclosing information about legal aid cases, legally-aided persons, unless they have given written consent or otherwise agreed by law;

  d) Refusing or discontinuing legal aid services, except for cases prescribed in this Law and the law on proceedings;

  dd) Abusing legal aid services to seek personal benefits, infringing upon national defense and security, causing social disorder and insecurity, and adversely affecting social ethics;

  e) Inciting or encouraging legally-aided persons to provide false information and documents, lodge complaints or denunciations, or initiate lawsuits in contravention of law.

  2. Legal aid beneficiaries, agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid services are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon the health, life, honor and dignity of legal aid-providing persons and the reputation of legal aid-providing organizations;

  b) Deliberately providing false information and documents about legal aid-related cases;

  c) Illegally threatening, obstructing or intervening in legal aid services; causing disturbances, disrupting order, seriously breaching internal regulations of places where legal aid is provided.

  Chapter II

   LEGAL AID RECEIVING PERSONS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID RECEIVING PERSONS

  Article 7. Legal aid beneficiaries

  1. People with meritorious services to the revolution; biological parents, spouses and children of martyrs and people with meritorious services to the care of martyrs.

  2. People in poor households, people in near-poor households.

  3. People infected with dioxin.

  4. People with disabilities.

  5. Children.

  6. Ethnic minority people living in areas with extremely disadvantaged socio-economic conditions; ethnic minority people in small ethnic minority groups.

  7. Persons receiving monthly social allowances are the elderly, persons subjected to violence in family-related violence cases, and HIV-infected persons.

  8. Low-income workers are accused persons, victims.

  9. Persons with unexpected difficulties due to natural disasters or fires shall receive legal aid in cases directly related to the overcoming of consequences arising from such events.

  10. Persons aged between full 16 and under 18 years who are denounced, recommended for prosecution, held in emergency custody, accused, victims, witnesses, persons serving diversion measures, and inmates according to the Law on Juvenile Justice.

  11. Victims, persons undergoing victim identification for human trafficking acts, and accompanying persons under 18 years of age according to the Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking.

  12. Individuals suffering damage without hiring protection of legitimate rights and interests according to the Law on State Compensation Liability.

  13. Foreigners under international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory who are entitled to legal aid.

  14. Other legally-aided persons as prescribed by law.

  Article 8. Rights of legally-aided persons

  1. Receive legal aid services free of charge.

  2. Request legal aid services by themselves or through their relatives, competent procedural authorities and persons, procedural authorities and persons or other agencies, organizations and individuals.

  3. Be informed about their rights to receive legal aid services, procedures for receiving legal aid services when they come to a legal aid-providing organization and relevant state agencies.

  4. Request confidentiality of contents of legal aid-related cases.

  5. Choose a local legal aid-providing organization and legal aid-providing person from the published list; request change of legal aid-providing person when he/she falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 25 of this Law.

  6. Change or withdraw his/her request for legal aid services.

  7. Receive compensation for damage as prescribed by law.

  8. Make complaints and denunciations about legal aid services in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 9. Obligations of legally-aided persons

  1. Provide documents proving their eligibility for legal aid.

  2. Cooperate and provide timely and adequate information, documents and evidence related to legal aid cases and take responsibility for the accuracy of such information, documents and evidence.

  3. Respect legal aid-providing organizations, persons and other agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid cases.

  4. Do not request other legal aid-providing organizations to provide legal aid for the same case which is being handled by another legal aid-providing organization.

  5. Comply with the law on legal aid and internal rules of places where they receive legal aid.

  Chapter III

   LEGAL AID PROVIDING ORGANIZATIONS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID PROVIDING ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 10. Legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations include state legal aid centers and legal aid-participating organizations.

  2. Departments of Justice shall publish lists of local legal aid-providing organizations on their websites and send them to the Ministry of Justice for consolidation and posting on the National Law Portal.

  Article 11. State legal aid centers

  1. The state legal aid center is a public non-business unit under the Department of Justice, established by the provincial People’s Committee, has legal status, seal, head office and separate account.

  2. The state legal aid center may have its branches.

  Branch is a dependent unit of the state legal aid center. The state legal aid center is responsible for all activities of its branches. Based on socio-economic conditions and legal aid needs in the province, the President of the provincial People’s Committee shall decide to establish branches of the state legal aid center.

  3. [7] The Government shall provide regulations on organization and operation of state legal aid centers, branches of state legal aid centers; procedures for establishment, dissolution and merger of branches of state legal aid centers.

  Article 12. Legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations include organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid and organizations registering to participate in legal aid services.

  2. Organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid with Departments of Justice according to this Law.

  3. Organizations registering to participate in legal aid services include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations registering to participate in legal aid services according to this Law.

  Article 13. Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid services;

  b) Request relevant agencies and organizations to cooperate in providing information and documents on legal aid-related cases;

  c) Pay compensation for damage caused by their employees during the provision of legal aid services;

  d) Comply with regulations on statistics, reporting, and communication regarding legal aid services;

  dd) Resolve complaints as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 45 of this Law;

  e) Make recommendations to competent state agencies on matters related to the resolution of legal aid-related cases.

  2. State legal aid centers have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Perform other tasks authorized or requested by competent state management agencies in charge of legal aid services.

  3. Organizations signing legal aid contracts have the following rights and obligations:

  a/ Rights and obligations specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b/ Receiving remuneration and expenses for the settlement of legal aid cases under the Government’s regulations;

  c/ Other rights and obligations under legal aid contracts.

  4. Organizations registering to participate in legal aid services have the following rights and obligations:

  a/ Rights and obligations specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b/ Providing legal aid services according to registered contents.

  Article 14. Legal aid contracts

  1. Legal aid service contracts shall be signed between Departments of Justice and law-practicing organizations or legal counseling organizations and between state legal aid centers and lawyers or legal aid collaborators on providing legal aid services in accordance with civil laws.

  2. Based on legal aid needs and local conditions, Departments of Justice shall select and sign legal aid service contracts with organizations wishing to provide legal aid services and satisfying the following requirements:

  a) The registered operation field is suitable for legal aid services as prescribed by this Law;

  b) It is a law-practicing organization or legal counseling organization having at least 01 legal counselor with at least 02 years of experience in legal counseling or 01 lawyer working regularly at the organization;

  c) It has facilities suitable for legal aid services;

  d) It is not being served an administrative sanction decision on law practice or legal counseling.

  3. State legal aid centers shall select and sign legal aid service contracts with lawyers satisfying the following requirements based on legal aid needs and resources available for providing legal aid services in their localities:

  a) They are not being served an administrative sanction decision on law practice;

  b) Not banned from practicing or restricted from professional activities under decisions of competent authorities;

  c) Not being examined for penal liability;

  d) Having written consent of the law-practicing organization where the lawyer works or the agency/organization with which the lawyer has concluded labor contract.

  4. State legal aid centers shall select and conclude legal aid contracts with legal aid collaborators according to Article 24 of this Law.

  5. Organizations and individuals that have committed prohibited acts specified in Clause 1, Article 6 of this Law shall not be allowed to select and conclude legal aid contracts within at least 2 years after the date of obtaining violation conclusions.

  6. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 15. Registration for participation in providing legal aid

  1. Law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations that voluntarily provide legal aid by using their own resources may register for participation in providing legal aid as follows:

  a) The law-practicing organization fully satisfies the conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law;

  b) The legal consultancy organization fully satisfies the conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law and has at least one legal consultant with two years or more of experience in providing legal consultancy or one full-time lawyer working for the organization.

  2. The law-practicing organization or legal consultancy organization providing legal aid shall register with the provincial-level Justice Department with which it has obtained its operation registration certificate the scope, form, sector and objects of legal aid provided.

  3. The Minister of Justice shall prescribe the order and procedures for registering participation in the provision of legal aid.

  Article 16. Termination of provision of legal aid by legal aid-providing organizations

  1. An organization signing a contract to provide legal aid shall terminate the provision of legal aid when falling into one of the following cases:

  a/ It no longer satisfies either of the conditions for signing a contract to provide legal aid specified in Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law;

  b/ It terminates under a contract on provision of legal aid.

  c) Providing legal aid with serious consequences;

  d) Terminating operation as prescribed by law.

  2. Organizations registering for participation in legal aid shall terminate their provision of legal aid when falling into one of the following cases:

  a) Failing to meet one of the conditions for registration as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 15 of this Law;

  b) Terminating their participation in legal aid after having a written notice sent to the Department of Justice where they register for participation in legal aid;

  c) Failing to provide legal aid for 02 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  d) Providing legal aid with serious consequences;

  dd) Terminating operation as prescribed by law.

  3. When terminating legal aid services, legal aid-participating organizations shall notify in writing the Department of Justice of such termination and transfer the unfinished cases to the legal aid-providing organization assigned by the Department of Justice for further provision.

  Chapter IV

      LEGAL AID-PROVIDING PERSONS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID-PROVIDING PERSONS

  Article 17. Legal aid-providing persons

  1. Legal aid-providing persons include:

  a/ Legal aid professionals;

  b/ Lawyers providing legal aid under contracts with state legal aid centers; lawyers providing legal aid under assignments of legal aid-participating organizations;

  c/ Legal counseling officers who have at least two years of experience in legal counseling and work at legal aid-participating organizations;

  d) Legal aid collaborators.

  2. Departments of Justice shall announce lists of legal aid-providing persons in their localities, post them on their websites and send them to the Ministry of Justice for compilation and posting on the National Law Portal [8].

  Article 18. Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing persons

  1. Legal aid-providing persons have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid;

  b) Be assured to provide legal aid independently, not threatened, obstructed, harassed or illegally interfered;

  c) Refuse or discontinue providing legal aid in cases specified in Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 25, Clause 1 Article 37 of this Law and according to regulations of law on procedures;

  d) Receive training in professional skills and practices of legal aid;

  dd) Ensure the quality of legal aid cases;

  e) Comply with the principles of legal aid services;

  g) Strictly comply with internal regulations of places where legal aid is provided;

  h) Provide compensation or refund to legal aid-providing organizations for amounts paid by them to persons suffering damage caused by their faults when providing legal aid in accordance with law.

  2. Legal aid professionals have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Participate in training courses on improvement of knowledge and skills about professional legal aid;

  c) Perform other tasks as assigned;

  d) Benefits and incentives as prescribed.

  3. Legal aid lawyers or legal aid collaborators who sign contracts for providing legal aid services with state legal aid centers shall be entitled to remuneration and expenses for providing legal aid services as prescribed.

  4. The Government shall elaborate Point d Clause 2 and Clause 3 of this Article.

  Article 19. Eligibility criteria for legal aid professionals

  A Vietnamese citizen who is an official working at a state legal aid center and meets the following criteria may become a legal aid professional:

  1. Possesses good moral qualities;

  2. Has a bachelor of laws degree or higher;

  3. Has undergone lawyer training or has been exempted from lawyer training;

  4. Having completed the period of law practice probation or legal aid practice probation, except for those who have worked as legal aid professionals;

  5. Being physically fit to perform legal aid.

  Article 20. Legal aid practice probation

  1. Officials of State legal aid centers who have certificates of graduation from lawyer training courses or are exempted from lawyer training courses under the Lawyer Law may undergo legal aid practice probation at State legal aid centers.

  The duration of legal aid practice probation is 12 months. State legal aid centers shall assign legal aid professionals to guide probationers and certify their probation. Legal aid professionals guiding probationers must have at least 3 years of experience in providing legal aid services. At any given time, a legal aid professional may not guide more than 2 probationers.

  2. Probationers may be assisted by legal aid professionals in their professional activities but may not represent, plead or protect the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons in court sessions; and may not sign legal counseling documents.

  Probationers may meet with legally-aided persons and other involved parties in legal aid cases with the consent of such persons together with legal aid professionals; assist legal aid professionals in studying case files, collecting documents, items and circumstances related to the cases and other professional activities. Legal aid professionals guiding probationers shall supervise and take responsibility for the activities of probationers specified in this Clause.

  3. Persons who are exempted from or reduced the period of law practice probation under the Lawyer Law shall also be exempted from or reduced the period of legal aid practice probation.

  4. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate on legal aid apprenticeship and examination results and the form of certificate of legal aid apprenticeship examination results.

  Article 21. Appointing, issuing and reissuing legal aid assistant’s cards

  1. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider appointing and issuing legal aid assistant’s cards to persons who meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law but do not fall into one of the following cases:

  a) They are subject to disciplinary actions or administrative handling measures and sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers or compulsory educational establishments;

  b) They are subject to criminal prosecution; have been convicted of intentional serious crimes, intentional very serious crimes, intentional extremely serious crimes, including cases where they have had their criminal records expunged; have been convicted of unintentional very serious crimes, unintentional extremely serious crimes, or unintentional less serious crimes.

  2. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider appointing and issuing legal aid assistant’s cards to persons who have been dismissed or have their legal aid assistant’s cards revoked in the cases specified in Points a, dd, or g Clause 1 Article 22 of this Law when they meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law and the reasons for dismissal or revocation of cards no longer exist.

  3. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall reissue legal aid assistant’s cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  4. The Government shall elaborate on this Article; procedures for appointing, issuing and reissuing legal aid assistant’s cards.

  Article 22. Removal and revocation of legal aid professional’s card

  1. A legal aid professional shall be removed and revoked his/her card in one of the following cases:

  a) He/she no longer satisfies the criteria for legal aid professionals prescribed in Article 19 of this Law;

  b) He/she fails to participate in a case or matter for 12 consecutive months, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  c) He/she is disciplined in the form of dismissal;

  d) He/she is disciplined in the form of warning or more severe disciplinary actions due to committing acts prescribed in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law;

  dd) He/she is banned from providing legal aid services under a competent authority's decision;

  e) He/she has been convicted and the judgment has taken legal effect;

  g) Administrative sanction in the form of placement in a compulsory rehabilitation center or compulsory educational institution.

  2. A legal aid assistant shall be automatically dismissed and his/her card shall be invalidated in the following cases:

  a) He/she is reassigned to another job; resigns or is let go from his/her job;

  b) He/she retires.

  3. The President of the People’s Committee of province shall decide on dismissal and revocation of legal aid assistant cards.

  4. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for dismissal and revocation of legal aid assistant cards.

  Article 23.[13] (annulled)

  Article 24. Legal aid collaborators [14]

  1. The following persons may become legal aid collaborators:

   a/ Persons who have retired or resigned voluntarily or resigned due to objective reasons, have full civil act capacity, good moral qualities, good health and wish to provide legal aid, including those who used to be legal aid workers, judges, court inspectors, procurators, procuracy inspectors, investigators, enforcers, civil judgment enforcement inspectors, holders of senior official specialist ranks and equivalent or higher in the legal sector; holders of specialist ranks and equivalent who have worked in the legal sector for 5 years or more at agencies of the Communist Party of Vietnam, the State, the Vietnam Fatherland Front and its member organizations; and those who used to be legal aid collaborators;

  b/ Officials holding rank III or equivalent who have worked in the legal sector for 5 years or more, or officials holding rank I, II or equivalent who have worked in the legal sector and have obtained written consent from their working agencies.

  2. Directors of provincial-level Justice Departments shall consider and grant legal aid collaborator cards to qualified persons defined in Clause 1 of this Article who do not fall into one of the following cases:

  a/ They are being examined for penal liability; having been convicted of an intentional serious crime, a very serious intentional crime or a particularly serious intentional crime, even if they have had their criminal records expunged; having been convicted but not yet had their criminal records expunged for an unintentional serious crime, a particularly serious unintentional crime or an unintentional less serious crime;

  b/ They are being subject to an administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or compulsory education institution; or are being disciplined.

  3. Directors of state legal aid centers shall sign contracts with persons granted legal aid collaborator cards to provide legal counseling and extrajudicial representation services in localities.

  4. Legal aid collaborators shall have their collaborator cards revoked in one of the following cases:

  a) Failing to meet the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Failing to provide legal aid services for 2 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  c) Committing violations prescribed in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law or having been administratively sanctioned for violations against regulations on legal aid but not yet subject to revocation of legal aid collaborator’s card and continuing to commit violations;

  d) The contract for provision of legal aid services is terminated or the legal aid collaborator fails to enter into a contract with the State legal aid center within 30 days from the date of receipt of the request for contract signing without a legitimate reason;

  dd) The legal aid collaborator is an official who has been disciplined by dismissal;

  e) Being subject to the administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or a compulsory education institution;

  g) Having been convicted and the judgment has taken legal effect.

  5. Directors of Departments of Justice shall decide to revoke legal aid collaborator’s cards; re-grant legal aid collaborator’s cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  6. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for issuance, reissuance and revocation of legal aid collaborator cards.

  Article 25. Cases where legal aid is not allowed to continue or refused

  1. Legal aid-providing persons are not allowed to continue providing legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Committing prohibited acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law, unless they have completely served the penalties for violations and are allowed to provide legal aid as prescribed by this Law;

  b) Having their legal aid assistant cards, legal aid collaborator cards, lawyer practicing certificates or legal counselor cards revoked;

  c) Not being allowed to participate in proceedings according to regulations of law on proceedings.

  2. Legal aid-providing persons must refuse to provide legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Providing legal aid for legally-aided persons who are parties with opposing interests in the same case, unless otherwise agreed upon by the parties in the case of legal counseling or extrajudicial representation in civil matters;

  b) There are grounds to believe that the legal aid-providing person may not be objective in providing legal aid services;

  c) There are reasons to believe that the legal aid-related case cannot be provided with effective assistance and affects the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons.

  3. The organization providing legal aid services must notify in writing and clearly state the reason to the legally-aided persons and appoint another person to provide legal aid services in the cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article.

  Chapter V

                SCOPE, FIELD, FORMS AND ACTIVITIES OF LEGAL AID SERVICES

  Article 26. Scope of legal aid services

  1. State legal aid centers shall provide legal aid services in one of the following cases:

  a) The legally-aided persons are residing in their localities;

  b) Legal aid cases occurring within local areas;

  c) Legal aid cases requested by competent central authorities in charge of legal aid services;

  d) [15] Cases transferred under Clause 3 Article 35 hereof.

  2. Organizations signing contracts for provision of legal aid shall provide legal aid within the scope of such contracts.

  3. Organizations registering to participate in legal aid shall provide legal aid within the scope of their registration.

  Article 27. Fields and forms of legal aid

  1. Legal aid shall be provided in fields of law, except for business and trade.

  2. Forms of legal aid include:

  a) Engage in legal proceedings;

  b) Provide legal counseling;

  c) Represent outside of legal proceedings.

  Article 28. Venues for receiving legally-aided persons

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations shall arrange venues for receiving legally-aided persons at their head offices or other locations outside of head offices to ensure that presentation of requests is easy and convenient.

  2. At head offices of legal aid-providing organizations, schedule and internal regulations on reception of legally-aided persons must be posted up.

  Article 29. [16] (annulled)

  Article 30. Requesting and accepting legal aid-related cases

  1. When requesting legal aid, the requester shall submit a dossier to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Legal aid requests shall be accepted only when there is a specific case directly related to the lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons and in compliance with this Law.

  3. The legal aid-providing organization may refuse to accept and clearly state the reason in writing to the requester in one of the following cases:

  a/ The legal aid request fails to meet the requirements specified in Clause 2 of this Article;

  b/ The legal aid request contains unlawful contents;

  c/ The legally-aided person has died;

  d/ The case is being accepted and settled by another legal aid-providing organization;

  e/ The legal aid request has been settled and no new matter arises.

  4. Where the requesting person is unable to provide a complete application but requires immediate legal aid due to the fact that the case is about to expire, or is approaching the trial date, or the case has been transferred by the competent procedural authority or procedural agency to the legal aid-providing organization for legal aid provision, or to avoid causing damage to the legitimate rights and interests of the legally-aided person, the receiving person shall report it to the head of the legal aid-providing organization and accept it immediately, and at the same time guide the legal aid-requesting person to supplement necessary papers and documents.

  5. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; and prescribe the procedures for accepting, immediately accepting, and rejecting acceptance of legal aid-related cases.

  Article 31. Participation in legal proceedings

  1. Legal aid assistants and lawyers providing legal aid shall participate in legal proceedings in the capacity as defense counsels or protectors of the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons in accordance with this Law and the law on legal proceedings.

  2. When the legally-aided person requests the assignment of a legal aid-providing person to participate in legal proceedings, within 03 working days from the date of acceptance of the case, the legal aid-providing organization shall assign a legal aid-providing person.

  In case the legally-aided person is an arrestee or a person held in temporary custody requests the assignment of a legal aid-providing person, within 12 hours from the time of acceptance, the legal aid-providing organization shall assign a legal aid-providing person.

  3. Within 12 hours from the time of receipt of the legal aid request from the arrestee or the person held in temporary custody, or within 24 hours from the time of receipt of the legal aid request from the defendant, the victim who is a legally-aided person in accordance with the law on legal proceedings, the competent procedural agency or person shall notify the local state legal aid center. Immediately after receiving the notice from the competent procedural agency or person, the state legal aid center shall accept the case in accordance with Clause 4, Article 30 of this Law and assign a legal aid-providing person to participate in legal proceedings.

  4. The assignment of persons to participate in legal proceedings must be made in writing and sent to the legally-aided person and the relevant procedural-conducting agency.

  Article 32. Legal counseling

  1. Legal aid-providing persons shall provide legal counseling to legally-aided persons by guiding, giving opinions and assisting in drafting documents related to disputes, complaints or legal difficulties; guiding parties to reach agreement on settlement direction of cases.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; procedures for providing legal counseling.

  Article 33. Extrajudicial representation

  1. Legal aid assistants, lawyers providing legal aid services and legal aid collaborators shall act as extrajudicial representatives before competent state agencies for legally-aided persons.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; procedures for providing extrajudicial representation.

  Article 34. Cooperation in verifying legal aid-related cases

  1. In case it is necessary to verify circumstances and events related to a legal aid-related case in another province, the legal aid-providing organization that has accepted the case shall request the legal aid-providing organization in the province where verification is required to cooperate in the verification. A written request for verification must clearly state contents to be verified and time limit for response.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for cooperation in verifying legal aid-related cases.

  Article 35. Transfer of requests and legal aid-related cases

  1. In case a request for legal aid does not meet the conditions specified in Points a and b Clause 1 Article 26 of this Law, the state legal aid center shall transfer the request to the competent state legal aid center and notify it to the requester.

  2. In case resources are insufficient to provide legal aid, the legal aid-providing organization shall transfer the request to the local state legal aid center and notify it to the requester.

  3. The state legal aid center that is handling a legal aid-related case may transfer such case to another state legal aid center when the case has been transferred to the competent procedural authority or local procedural authority and the legal aid recipient is informed.

  Article 36. Recommendations in legal aid activities

  1. During the provision of legal aid, the legal aid-providing organization shall make written recommendations to the competent authority on matters related to the settlement of legal aid-related cases. The receiving agency shall, within its jurisdiction, respond in writing as prescribed by law.

  2. If the receiving agency fails to respond as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article, the legal aid-providing organization may recommend that the superior agency of the receiving agency consider and resolve the matter.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for petition in legal aid.

  Article 37. Termination of legal aid

  1. Legal aid shall be terminated in one of the following cases:

  a) Refusal as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 30 hereof;

  b) Legal aid beneficiaries commit prohibited acts as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 6 hereof;

  c) Legal aid beneficiaries withdraw their requests for legal aid.

  2. In case of termination of legal aid, legal aid-providing organizations or persons must send written notice and state reasons to legal aid beneficiaries.

  3. In case where legal aid is being provided but legal aid beneficiaries no longer meet requirements under Article 7 hereof, legal aid shall continue until completion.

  Article 38. Legal aid case files [24]

  When providing legal aid, legal aid-providing organizations and persons shall prepare legal aid case files in accordance with regulations of the Minister of Justice.

  Article 39. Storage of legal aid case files

  1. Within 30 days from the date on which a case is closed, the legal aid-providing person must hand over the legal aid case file to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Documents in the legal aid case file must be listed, numbered, arranged by date and stored as per the law.

  3. Electronic files of each legal aid case shall be digitized, updated to the system for management of legal aid cases and stored in the database on legal aid.

  Chapter VI

          RESPONSIBILITIES OF AGENCIES AND ORGANIZATIONS FOR LEGAL AID PROVISION

  Article 40. Responsibilities for state management of legal aid

  1. The Government shall perform the unified state management of legal aid.

  2. The Ministry of Justice shall act as the focal point assisting the Government in performing the state management of legal aid and have the following tasks and powers:

  a) Take charge of developing, promulgating or requesting competent authorities to promulgate legislative documents on legal aid;

  b) Develop, promulgate or request competent authorities to promulgate strategies and plans for development of legal aid services and organize implementation thereof;

  c) Promulgate regulations on professional guidelines, assessment of quality and effectiveness of legal aid cases; forms of documents used in legal aid services; criteria for determining complex, typical, high-quality and successful legal aid cases; implement reporting and statistical regulations on legal aid;

  d) Provide guidance, organize and monitor implementation of legislative documents on legal aid;

  dd) Organize training, advanced training and professional skills training for legal aid-providing persons;

  e) Organize communication about legal aid services;

  g) Inspect the provision of legal aid services; commend, discipline, and handle violations against regulations on legal aid services;

  h) Receive support and contributions from organizations and individuals for legal aid services;

  i) Conduct international cooperation in legal aid services;

  k) Apply information technology and digital technology and implement digital transformation in legal aid services; develop, manage, operate, and regularly upgrade the legal aid information system and database; connect, integrate, and share legal aid data on the National Law Portal.

  3. Ministries and ministerial agencies shall, within their tasks and powers, cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in state management and organization of legal aid services; create, integrate, share, extract, and use data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  4. Provincial People’s Committees shall, within their tasks and powers, perform state management of legal aid services in their provinces, ensure working conditions of state legal aid centers; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in creating, integrating, sharing, extracting, and using data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  5. Commune-level People’s Committees shall cooperate with state legal aid centers in providing legal aid services in their communes.

  6.[32] The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 41. Responsibilities of agencies involved in legal aid activities in legal proceedings

  1. Within their tasks and powers, competent procedural authorities and procedural authorities shall cooperate and coordinate in implementing interdisciplinary mechanisms for providing legal aid at People’s Courts and in criminal investigation; enable legally-aided persons to enjoy the right to legal aid, enable legal aid-providing persons to participate in legal proceedings as prescribed by law.

  2. The Minister of Justice, Chief Justice of the Supreme People’s Court, Procurator General of the Supreme People’s Procuracy, Minister of National Defense, Minister of Public Security, and Minister of Finance shall cooperate in developing interdisciplinary councils on legal aid in legal proceedings at central and provincial levels.

  3. Agencies involved in legal aid activities in legal proceedings shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in establishing, integrating, sharing, extracting, and using data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  Article 42. Responsibilities of the Vietnam Fatherland Front, its member organizations, and relevant state agencies

  1. The Vietnam Fatherland Front and its member organizations shall, within their functions and tasks, supervise the implementation of policies and laws on legal aid; cooperate with relevant state agencies and organizations in legal aid work as prescribed by law.

  2. During the handling and resolution of cases related to citizens who are legally-aided persons, state agencies shall explain the right to legal aid and introduce them to legal aid-providing organizations.

  Article 43. Responsibilities of socio-professional organizations of lawyers

  1. The Vietnam Bar Federation shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers in accordance with this Law.

  2. Bar associations of provinces and centrally-affiliated cities shall cooperate with provincial-level Justice Departments in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers in accordance with this Law.

  Article 44. Responsibilities of managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations Managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations shall cooperate with competent state management agencies in legal aid in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by legal consultancy organizations and legal consultants in accordance with this Law.

  Chapter VII

                            SETTLEMENT OF COMPLAINTS, DENUNCIATIONS AND DISPUTES

  Article 45. Settlement of complaints and denunciations

  1. Legal aid beneficiaries may lodge complaints about the following acts of legal aid-providing organizations or persons when having grounds to believe that such acts are illegal and infringe upon their legitimate rights and interests:

  a) Refuse to accept legal aid-related cases;

  b) Fail to provide legal aid;

  c) Provide legal aid in a manner that violates the law;

  d) Change legal aid-providing persons in a manner that violates the law.

  2. [35] The head of the legal aid-providing organization shall settle complaints against the acts specified in Clause 1 of this Article. If the complainant disagrees with the complaint settlement decision of the head of the legal aid-providing organization or the complaint is not settled within the prescribed time limit, he/she may further lodge a complaint with the director of the Department of Justice.

  If the complainant disagrees with the complaint settlement decision of the director of the Department of Justice or the complaint is not settled within the prescribed time limit, he/she may file a lawsuit at court.

  The Government shall elaborate on this clause and procedures for settling complaints in legal aid.

  3. [36] Complaints and lawsuits in legal aid are not covered by the provisions of Clause 1 of this Article shall comply with the provisions of the law on complaints and other relevant laws.

  4. Individuals have the right to lodge complaints with competent authorities about violations against this Law. Complaints and settlement thereof shall comply with regulations of law on complaints.

  Article 46. Dispute resolution

  1. In case of a dispute between legally-aided persons and legal aid professionals, legal counseling consultants, legal aid collaborators, state legal aid centers or legal counseling organizations related to the provision of legal aid, such dispute shall be resolved in accordance with civil laws.

  2. In case of a dispute between legally-aided persons and lawyers or law-practicing organizations related to the provision of legal aid, such dispute shall be resolved in accordance with the law on lawyers and other relevant laws.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION CLAUSES [37]

  Article 47. Entry into force

  1. This Law comes into force as of January 01, 2018.

  2. The Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 ceases to be effective as from the effective date of this Law.

  Article 48. Transitional provisions

  1. As from the effective date of this Law, persons who have been appointed as legal aid professionals under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 may continue their activities under this Law; after 5 years from the effective date of this Law, if the persons who have been appointed as legal aid professionals do not possess certificates of lawyer training, their legal aid cards shall be revoked.

  2. Within 1 year after the effective date of this Law, legal aid-providing organizations and persons under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 must meet the requirements set forth in this Law; if they fail to meet these requirements, they shall terminate their participation in legal aid services and transfer the dossiers of cases in which they are engaged in legal aid services to the State legal aid centers where they have registered for participation in legal aid services for further implementation.

  3. Legal aid cases which are being implemented under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 but have not yet been completed by the effective date of this Law shall continue to be implemented under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 until the completion of such cases.

  4. Within 1 year after the effective date of this Law, provincial-level People’s Committees shall review the branches of State legal aid centers which have been established in their localities and base themselves on the demand for legal aid, resources for providing legal aid and operation effectiveness to report to the Ministry of Justice for reaching agreement on the maintenance, merger or dissolution of such branches.
