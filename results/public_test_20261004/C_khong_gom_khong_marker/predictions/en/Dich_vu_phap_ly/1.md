                                                        LAW

                                                LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  The Law on Legal Aid No. 11/2017/QH14 dated June 20, 2017 of the National Assembly, which takes effect from January 1, 2018, is amended and supplemented by:

  1. The Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking No. 53/2024/QH15 dated November 28, 2024 of the National Assembly, which comes into force from July 01, 2025;

  2. The Law on Juvenile Justice No. 59/2024/QH15 dated November 30, 2024 of the National Assembly, which takes effect from January 1, 2026;

  3. Law No. 05/2026/QH16 dated April 23, 2026 of the National Assembly on amendments to the Law on Legal Aid, which comes into force from January 1, 2027.

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;

  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Legal Aid [1].

  Chapter I

                                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for legally-aided persons; legal aid-providing organizations; legal aid-providing persons; legal aid services; and responsibilities of agencies, organizations and individuals for legal aid services.

  Article 2. Legal aid

  Legal aid means the provision of free-of-charge legal services to legally-aided persons in legal aid-related cases under this Law, contributing to ensuring human rights and civil rights in access to justice and equality before law.

  Article 3. Legal aid principles

  1. Comply with laws and professional rules of legal aid services.

  2. To be prompt, independent, honest and respectful to objective truth.

  3. To protect the legitimate rights and interests of legal aid beneficiaries.

  4. Not to collect money, material benefits or other benefits from legal aid beneficiaries.

  Article 4. The State’s policies on legal aid

  1. Legal aid is the responsibility of the State.

  2. The State shall adopt policies to ensure the right to legal aid in conformity with socio-economic conditions.

  3. The State shall adopt policies to improve legal aid quality and attract resources for providing legal aid.

  4. The State shall support, encourage, recognize and honor agencies, organizations and individuals participating in and contributing to legal aid activities.

  Article 5. Financial sources for legal aid services

  1. Financial sources for legal aid work include state budget funds; contributions and donations from domestic and foreign organizations and individuals, and other lawful sources.

  2. [2] Funding for state budget shall be allocated in annual state budget estimates of agencies performing state management of legal aid in accordance with laws on state budget, public investment, science, technology and innovation, digital transformation, digital technology industry and relevant specialized laws.

  For localities that have not yet managed to balance their budgets, priority shall be given to allocating budget from the annual budget balance to support the handling of complex and typical legal aid cases.

  3. Funding for legal aid activities of voluntary organizations shall be self-arranged by such organizations.

  Article 6. Prohibited acts in legal aid services

  1. Organizations providing legal aid services and persons providing legal aid services are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon honor, dignity, legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons; discriminating against legally-aided persons;

  b/ To receive or demand any money, material benefits or other benefits from legal aid beneficiaries; to harass legal aid beneficiaries;

  c) Disclosing information about legal aid-related cases and persons receiving legal aid, unless otherwise agreed in writing by the persons receiving legal aid or provided for by law;

  d) Refuse or discontinue legal aid services, except for cases prescribed in this Law and the law on proceedings;

  dd) Taking advantage of legal aid services to commit acts of abuse for personal gain, infringing upon national defense and security, causing social disorder or insecurity, adversely affecting social ethics;

  e) Encourage or incite legal aid beneficiaries to provide false information and documents, lodge complaints, denunciations or initiate lawsuits in contravention of law.

  2. Legal aid beneficiaries and agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid activities are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon the health, life, honor and dignity of legal aid-providing persons and the reputation of legal aid-providing organizations;

  b) Deliberately provide false information or documents about legal aid-related cases;

  c/ Illegally threatening, obstructing or intervening in legal aid services; causing disturbances, disorder or seriously breaching rules of places where legal aid is provided.

  Chapter II

   PERSONS RECEIVING LEGAL ASSISTANCE, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF PERSONS RECEIVING LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  Article 7. Legal aid beneficiaries

  1. Those who have made meritorious services to the revolution; natural parents, spouses and children of martyrs and those who have taken in the martyrs.

  2. People in poor households or near-poor households.

  3. Persons infected with dioxin.

  4. Persons with disabilities.

  5. Children.

  6. Ethnic minority people living in areas with extremely disadvantaged socio-economic conditions; ethnic minority people in small communities.

  7. Persons receiving monthly social allowances are the elderly, persons subjected to violence in family-related violence cases and persons infected with HIV.

  8. Low-income workers are those who are accused or victims.

  9. Persons with unexpected difficulties due to natural disasters or fires shall be provided with legal assistance in cases directly related to the overcoming of consequences arising for them due to such events.

  10. Persons aged between full 16 and under 18 who are accused or facing requisitions for charges, held in emergency custody, charged, crime victims, witness testifiers, persons serving diversion measures, and inmates according to the Law on Juvenile Justice.

  11. Victims, persons undergoing victim identification for human trafficking acts, and accompanying individuals under 18 years of age in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking.

  12. Individuals suffering damage without hiring protection of rights and legitimate interests in accordance with the Law on State Compensation Liability.

  13. Foreigners who are covered by international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory and have legal aid provisions.

  14. Other legal aid beneficiaries as prescribed by law.

  Article 8. Rights of legally-aided persons

  1. To receive legal aid services free of charge.

  2. Request legal aid by themselves or through their relatives, competent procedural authorities and persons, procedural authorities and persons [4], or other agencies, organizations and individuals.

  3. To be informed of their right to legal aid, and the order and procedures for legal aid upon coming to a legal aid-providing organization or a relevant state agency.

  4. Request to keep confidential contents of legal aid cases.

  5. Select a legal aid organization and legal aid professional in their province or city from the list of eligible organizations and professionals; request change of legal aid professional when he/she falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 25 of this Law.

  6. Change or withdraw legal aid requests.

  7. Receive compensation as prescribed by law.

  8. Complaints and denunciations about legal aid services in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 9. Obligations of legal aid beneficiaries

  1. Provide documents proving eligibility for legal aid.

  2. To cooperate in and promptly provide sufficient information, documents and evidence related to legal aid-related cases and take responsibility for the accuracy of such information, documents and evidence.

  3. Respect for legal aid-providing organizations, persons and other agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid-related cases.

  4. Do not request another legal aid organization to provide legal aid for the same case that is being handled by another legal aid organization.

  5. Comply with laws on legal aid and internal regulations of places where legal aid is provided.

  Chapter III

   ORGANIZATION OF LEGAL ASSISTANCE, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL ASSISTANCE ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 10. Organization of legal aid services

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations include state legal aid centers and legal aid-participating organizations.

  2. The Department of Justice shall announce the list of legal aid-providing organizations in their provinces and cities on its website and send it to the Ministry of Justice for compilation and posting on the National Law Portal.

  Article 11. State legal aid centers

  1. State legal aid centers are public service providers under provincial Departments of Justice, established by provincial People’s Committees, have their own legal status, seals, head offices and accounts.

  2. [6] State legal aid centers may have their branches.

  A branch is a dependent unit of a State legal aid center. The State legal aid center shall be responsible for all activities of its branches. Based on socio-economic conditions and legal aid needs in localities, chairpersons of provincial-level People’s Committees shall decide to establish branches of State legal aid centers.

  3. [7] The Government shall regulate the organization and operation of state legal aid centers and their branches; procedures for establishment, dissolution and merger of branches of state legal aid centers.

  Article 12. Legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-participating organizations include organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid and organizations registering to participate in legal aid.

  2. Organizations signing legal aid contracts include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations that sign legal aid contracts with Departments of Justice in accordance with this Law.

  3. Organizations registering for participation in legal aid services include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations which register for participation in legal aid services under this Law.

  Article 13.- Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid;

  b) Request relevant agencies and organizations to cooperate in providing information and documents on legal aid-related cases;

  c) To pay compensation for damage caused by faults of their members while providing legal aid;

  d) Implement regulations on statistics, reporting and communication on legal aid;

  dd) Settle complaints in accordance with Clause 2 Article 45 hereof;

  e) To propose competent state agencies on matters related to the settlement of legal aid cases.

  2. State legal aid centers have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Perform other tasks as authorized or requested by competent state management agencies in charge of legal aid.

  3. Organizations signing legal aid contracts have the rights and obligations as follows:

  a) Rights and obligations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Receive remuneration and expenses for legal aid services in accordance with regulations of the Government;

  c/ Other rights and obligations under legal aid contracts.

  4. Organizations registering for participation in legal aid services have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Provide legal aid according to registered contents.

  Article 14. Contracts for provision of legal aid services

  1. Legal aid service contracts shall be signed between Departments of Justice and law-practicing organizations or legal counseling organizations and between state legal aid centers and lawyers or legal aid collaborators on providing legal aid services in accordance with civil laws.

  2. Based on legal aid needs and local conditions, the Department of Justice shall select and sign a contract for providing legal aid with an organization that wishes to provide legal aid and meets the following conditions:

  a) The field of operation is consistent with legal aid field as prescribed in this Law;

  b) Being a law-practicing organization or legal consultancy organization with at least one legal consultant having at least two years of experience in legal consultancy or one lawyer working regularly for such organization;

  c) Have adequate facilities suitable for legal aid services;

  d) Not being in the period of execution of decisions on sanctioning administrative violations in law practice or legal consultancy.

  3. The State legal aid center shall, based on legal aid needs and resources for providing legal aid in the locality, select and sign a contract to provide legal aid with a qualified lawyer who meets the following conditions:

  a) Not being in the period of execution of decisions on administrative sanctions against violations in law practice activities;

  b) Not banned from practicing or restricted from carrying out professional activities under decisions of competent authorities;

  c) Not being examined for penal liability;

  d) Obtain written consent from the law firm where the lawyer is working or the agency/organization with which the lawyer has concluded an employment contract.

  4. State legal aid centers shall select and sign legal aid contracts with legal aid collaborators in accordance with Article 24 of this Law.

  5. Organizations and individuals that have committed prohibited acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law shall not be allowed to select or sign legal aid service contracts for at least 2 years from the date on which the violation conclusions are issued.

  6. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 15. Registration for participation in legal aid services

  1. Law-practicing organizations and law consultancy organizations that voluntarily provide legal aid with their own resources may register to participate in providing legal aid as follows:

  a) The law-practicing organization meets the conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law;

  b) The legal consultancy organization meets all conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law and has at least one legal consultant with two years or more of experience in legal consultancy or one full-time lawyer working for the organization.

  2. Law-practicing organizations and legal consultancy organizations providing legal aid services shall register with the provincial-level Justice Department of the locality where they have been granted operation registration certificates their scope of, forms and domains of, and beneficiaries of, legal aid services.

  3. The Minister of Justice shall provide for procedures for registration of participation in legal aid services.

  Article 16. Termination of legal aid services provided by legal aid-providing organizations

  1. The organization signing the legal aid contract shall terminate the provision of legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) No longer satisfies one of the conditions for signing a legal aid contract as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 14 of this Law;

  b) Termination under legal aid contracts;

  c) Committing serious violations of legal aid services;

  d) Shall terminate its operation in accordance with law.

  2. The organization registering for participation in legal aid services shall terminate the provision of legal aid services when falling into one of the following cases:

  a) No longer satisfy one of the conditions for registration to participate in legal aid services specified in Clause 1, Article 15 of this Law;

  b/ To terminate their participation in legal aid services after having made written notification thereon to the provincial-level Justice Department with which they have registered for participation in legal aid services;

  c) Failing to provide legal aid for 2 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  d) Committing serious violations of legal aid services;

  dd) Shall terminate its operation in accordance with law.

  3. When terminating legal aid services, legal aid-providing organizations shall notify in writing the Department of Justice of such termination and transfer the unfinished cases to the assigned legal aid-providing organization for further provision.

  Chapter IV

      LEGAL ASSISTANTS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL ASSISTANTS

  Article 17.- Persons providing legal aid

  1. Legal aid-providing persons include:

  a) Legal aid professionals;

  b) Lawyers providing legal aid under contracts with state legal aid centers; lawyers providing legal aid under assignments of legal aid-participating organizations;

  c) The legal counselor has at least 2 years of experience in providing legal counseling and has worked for a legal aid-providing organization;

  d) Legal aid collaborators.

  2. The Department of Justice shall announce the list of legal aid-providing persons in their province or city on its website and send it to the Ministry of Justice for compilation and posting on the National Law Portal.

  Article 18.- Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing persons

  1. Legal aid-providing persons have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid;

  b) Have their right to independent legal aid guaranteed and not threatened, obstructed, harassed or illegally interfered upon;

  c) Refuse or discontinue legal aid services in cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 25, Clause 1 Article 37 of this Law and in accordance with procedural laws;

  d) To be provided with training in legal aid professional skills and operations;

  dd) Assuring the quality of legal aid cases;

  e) Comply with the principles of legal aid services;

  g) Strictly comply with the rules of the place where legal aid is provided;

  h) To pay compensation or refund a sum of money to the legal aid-providing organization which has paid for damage caused by its fault to the aggrieved person when providing legal aid in accordance with law.

  2. Legal aid professionals have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations specified in clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Attend training courses on improvement of professional knowledge and skills in legal aid;

  c) Perform other tasks as assigned;

  d) Enjoy benefits and policies as prescribed.

  3. Lawyers and legal aid collaborators who sign contracts for providing legal aid services with state legal aid centers shall be entitled to remuneration and expenses for providing legal aid services as prescribed.

  4. The Government shall elaborate Point d Clause 2 and Clause 3 of this Article.

  Article 19. Legal aid professional standards [10]

  Vietnamese citizens who are officials of the State Legal Aid Center and meet the following criteria may become legal aid professionals:

  1. Possessing good moral qualities;

  2. Possess a bachelor of laws degree or higher;

  3. Having received lawyer training or being exempted from lawyer training;

  4. Have completed their law practice probation or legal aid practice probation, except for cases where they have been legal aid professionals;

  5. Have good health to ensure legal aid provision.

  Article 20. Legal aid apprenticeship

  1. Officials of a State legal aid center who have obtained certificates of graduation from lawyer training courses or are exempted from lawyer training courses under the Lawyer Law may undergo legal aid probation at such center.

  The legal aid probation period is 12 months. The State legal aid center shall assign a legal aid assistant to guide the probationer and certify the legal aid probation. A legal aid assistant who guides a probationer must have at least 3 years of experience as a legal aid assistant. At the same time, a legal aid assistant may not guide more than 2 probationers.

  2. A legal aid apprentice may be assisted by a legal aid professional in his/her professional activities but may not represent, defend or protect the lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons at court sessions; and may not sign legal counseling documents.

  Legal aid probationers may, together with legal aid professionals, guide and meet legal aid beneficiaries and other involved parties in legal aid cases when so agreed by such persons; assist legal aid professionals in studying case files, collecting documents, items and circumstances related to the cases and performing other professional activities. Legal aid probation officers shall supervise and be responsible for the activities of legal aid probationers specified in this Clause.

  3. Persons who are exempted or reduced from the duration of legal practice probation under the Law on Lawyers shall be exempted or reduced from the duration of legal aid practice probation.

  4. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate on legal aid apprenticeship and examination results and the form of certificate of legal aid apprenticeship examination results.

  Article 21. Assignment, issuance and reissuance of legal aid assistant’s cards

  1. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider appointing and issuing legal aid assistant cards to persons who fully meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law, except for the following cases:

  a) Being subject to disciplinary actions; being subject to administrative actions and sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers or compulsory educational establishments;

  b) Being criminally prosecuted; having been convicted of a serious intentional crime, a very serious intentional crime, or a particularly serious intentional crime, including in cases where the conviction has been expunged; having been convicted but not yet expunged of a conviction for a very serious unintentional crime, a particularly serious unintentional crime, or a less serious intentional crime.

  2. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider and appoint and issue legal aid assistant cards to persons who have been dismissed or have their legal aid assistant cards revoked in cases specified at Points a, dd or g, Clause 1, Article 22 of this Law when they fully meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law and the reasons for dismissal or revocation of cards are no longer available.

  3. The Chairperson of the provincial People’s Committee shall reissue legal aid assistant cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  4. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for appointment, issuance and reissuance of legal aid assistant’s cards.

  Article 22. Removal and revocation of legal aid assistant’s card

  1. Legal aid professionals shall be dismissed and have their cards revoked in one of the following cases:

  a) No longer satisfying eligibility requirements as legal aid professionals prescribed in Article 19 of this Law;

  b) Failing to participate in legal proceedings for 12 consecutive months, except for cases of objective reasons;

  c) To be disciplined in the form of dismissal;

  d) Being disciplined in the form of warning or more severe forms due to committing acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 hereof;

  dd) Being banned from providing legal aid services under a competent authority's decision;

  e) Being convicted but the judgment has taken legal effect;

  g) To be subject to the administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or a compulsory education institution.

  2. Legal aid professionals shall be automatically dismissed and their cards shall become invalid in the following cases:

  a) Transfer to another job; dismissal or discharge;

  b) Retirement.

  3. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall decide to dismiss or revoke legal aid assistant’s cards.

  4. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for dismissal and revocation of legal aid assistant’s card.

  Article 23.[13] (annulled)

  Article 24. Legal aid collaborators [14]

  1. The following persons may become legal aid collaborators:

   a) Persons who have retired or resigned voluntarily or resigned due to objective reasons, have full civil act capacity, good moral qualities, good health and wish to receive legal aid, including those who used to be: legal aid workers, Judges, Court inspectors, Procurators, Procurator Inspectors, Investigators, Enforcers, Civil Judgment Enforcement Inspectors, holders of senior specialist ranks and equivalent or higher in the legal sector; persons who held senior specialist ranks and equivalent or higher for 5 years or more in the agencies of the Communist Party of Vietnam, the State, the Vietnamese Fatherland Front and its member organizations; and persons who used to be legal aid collaborators;

  b) Officials holding third-level or equivalent positions engaged in legal affairs for 5 years or more, or officials holding first-level, second-level or equivalent positions engaged in legal affairs and obtaining written consent from their working agencies.

  2. The Director of the Department of Justice shall consider and grant legal aid collaborator cards to those who fully meet the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article and do not fall into one of the following cases:

  a) Being criminally prosecuted; having been convicted of a serious intentional crime, a very serious intentional crime, or a particularly serious intentional crime, including in cases where the conviction has been expunged; having been convicted but not yet expunged of a conviction for a very serious unintentional crime, a particularly serious unintentional crime, or a less serious intentional crime;

  b) Being subject to an administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory rehabilitation center or a compulsory educational institution; being subject to disciplinary action.

  3. Directors of State legal aid centers shall sign contracts for providing legal aid services to persons granted legal aid collaborator cards to provide legal counseling and extrajudicial representation in their localities.

  4. Legal aid collaborators whose cards are revoked in one of the following cases:

  a) No longer satisfying the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Failing to provide legal aid services for 2 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  c) Committing acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law or committing administrative violations against regulations on legal aid but not yet subject to revocation of legal aid collaborator’s card and continuing to commit violations;

  d) The legal aid contract is terminated or the legal aid collaborator fails to enter into a legal aid contract with the State legal aid center within 30 days from the date of receipt of the request for contract signing, without a legitimate reason;

  dd) Legal aid collaborators who are public employees subject to disciplinary actions in the form of dismissal;

  e) To be subject to the administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or a compulsory education institution;

  g) Being convicted but the judgment has already taken legal effect.

  5. The Director of the Department of Justice shall decide to revoke legal aid collaborator’s cards and re-grant legal aid collaborator’s cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  6. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for issuance, reissuance and revocation of legal aid collaborator cards.

  Article 25.- Cases where legal aid is not allowed to continue or must be refused

  1. A legal aid provider shall not continue providing legal aid services in one of the following cases:

  a) Committing prohibited acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law, except for cases where they have completely served the penalties and are eligible for legal aid services as prescribed by this Law;

  b) Have their legal aid assistant cards, legal aid collaborator cards, lawyer practicing certificates or legal counselor cards revoked;

  c) Cases in which participation in legal proceedings is not permitted as prescribed by procedural laws.

  2. The legal aid-providing person must refuse to provide legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Have provided legal aid for legal aid beneficiaries who have opposing interests in the same case, unless otherwise agreed by the parties regarding legal counseling or extrajudicial representation in civil matters;

  b) There are grounds to believe that the legal aid-providing person may not be objective in providing legal aid;

  c) There are reasons to believe that legal aid services cannot be provided effectively and affect the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons.

  3. The legal aid-providing organization shall notify in writing and clearly state the reason to the legal aid recipient and appoint another person to provide legal aid in the cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article.

  Chapter V

                JURISDICTION, SECTORS, FORMS AND ACTIVITIES OF LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  Article 26. Scope of legal aid services

  1. State legal aid centers provide legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Legal aid beneficiaries who are residing in their localities;

  b) Legal aid cases occurring in their localities;

  c/ Legal aid cases requested by competent legal aid agencies at the central level;

  d) The case is transferred in accordance with Clause 3 Article 35 of this Law.

  2. The organization signing the legal aid contract shall provide legal aid within the scope of the contract.

  3. Organizations registering for participation in legal aid shall provide legal aid within their registered scope.

  Article 27.- Legal aid domains and forms

  1. Legal aid shall be provided in legal fields, except for business and trade fields.

  2. Legal aid forms include:

  a) Engage in legal proceedings;

  b) Legal counseling;

  c) Extrajudicial representatives.

  Article 28. Places of reception of legal aid beneficiaries

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations shall arrange places for receiving legal aid beneficiaries at their head offices or other locations outside the head offices of such organizations to ensure easy and convenient presentation of requests.

  2. At the head office of the legal aid-providing organization, the schedule and rules for receiving legal aid beneficiaries must be posted up.

  Article 29.[16] (annulled)

  Article 30. Legal aid request and acceptance

  1. When requesting legal aid, the requester shall submit a dossier to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Legal aid requests shall be accepted only when there are specific cases directly related to the legitimate rights and interests of legal aid beneficiaries and in compliance with this Law.

  3. Legal aid-providing organizations must refuse to accept and clearly notify the requesters in writing of the reasons in one of the following cases:

  a) The request for legal aid does not meet the requirements prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article;

  b) Legally inappropriate legal aid requests;

  c/ The legal aid beneficiary has died;

  d) The case is being accepted and settled by another legal aid organization;

  dd) Legal aid requests have been resolved and no new matters arise.

  4. Where the requesting person is unable to provide a complete application but requires immediate legal aid due to the fact that the case is about to expire, or is approaching the trial date, or the case has been transferred by the competent procedural authority or procedural agency to the legal aid-providing organization for legal aid provision, or to avoid causing damage to the legitimate rights and interests of the legally-aided person, the receiving person shall report it to the head of the legal aid-providing organization and accept it immediately, and at the same time guide the requesting person to supplement necessary papers and documents.

  5. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for immediate acceptance or rejection of legal aid-related cases.

  Article 31. Participation in legal proceedings

  1. Legal aid professionals and lawyers providing legal aid participate in proceedings in the capacity as defense counsels or protectors of rights and legitimate interests of legally-aided persons according to regulations of this Law and procedural laws.

  2. When a legal aid recipient requests the assignment of a legal aid participant to participate in legal proceedings, within 3 working days from the date of case acceptance, the legal aid-providing organization shall assign a legal aid participant.

  In case legal aid recipients are arrestees or temporary detainees who request the assignment of legal aid-providing persons, within 12 hours from the time of acceptance, legal aid-providing organizations shall assign legal aid-providing persons.

  3. Within 12 hours from the moment in which legal aid request of arrestees or temporary detainees is received or within 24 hours from the moment in which legal aid request of suspects, defendants and victims who receive legal aid is received as per procedural laws, competent procedural authorities and persons are responsible for notifying local state legal aid centers. Immediately after receiving notice from competent procedural authorities and persons, state legal aid centers are responsible for accepting the requests as per Clause 4 Article 30 hereof and assigning legal aid-providing individuals to participate in legal proceedings.

  4. The appointment of participants in legal proceedings must be made in writing and sent to legal aid beneficiaries and relevant procedural-conducting agencies.

  Article 32. Legal counseling [18]

  1. Legal aid-providing persons shall provide legal counseling to legally-aided persons by guiding, giving opinions and assisting in drafting documents related to disputes, complaints or legal problems; guiding parties to reconcile, negotiate and agree on the direction of case settlement.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for legal counseling cases.

  Article 33. Extrajudicial representation

  1. Legal aid professionals, lawyers providing legal aid services, and legal aid collaborators acting as representatives of persons receiving legal aid before competent state agencies.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for handling extrajudicial representation cases.

  Article 34. Cooperation in verifying legal aid cases

  1. In case of necessity to verify details and events related to legal aid cases in other localities, legal aid-providing organizations that have accepted the cases shall request legal aid-providing organizations in the localities where verification is needed to cooperate in the verification. The verification requests must be made in writing, clearly stating contents to be verified and time limit for reply.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for cooperation in verifying legal aid cases.

  Article 35. Transfer of legal aid requests and cases [21]

  1. In case of a request for legal aid that fails to meet the conditions specified at Points a and b, Clause 1, Article 26 of this Law, the state legal aid center shall transfer such request to a competent state legal aid center and notify it to the requester.

  2. In case of insufficient resources for providing legal aid, the legal aid-providing organization shall transfer the request to a local state legal aid center and notify it to the requesting person.

  3. [22] The State legal aid center that is handling a legal aid case may transfer it to another local state legal aid center when the case has been transferred to a competent procedural authority or procedural authority in the same area for settlement and notify the legal aid beneficiary thereof.

  Article 36. Recommendations in legal aid services

  1. During the process of providing legal aid, legal aid-providing organizations shall make written recommendations to competent state agencies on matters related to the settlement of legal aid-related cases. Agencies receiving such recommendations shall, within the scope of their tasks and powers, respond in writing according to law.

  2. In case the receiving agency does not respond as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article, the legal aid-providing organization may request the immediate superior agency to consider and resolve it.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article and prescribe procedures for proposing in legal aid activities.

  Article 37. Termination of legal aid-related cases

  1. Legal aid services shall not be provided in the following cases:

  a) In case of refusal as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 30 hereof;

  b) The legal aid beneficiary commits one of the prohibited acts specified in Clause 2, Article 6 of this Law;

  c/ The legal aid beneficiary withdraws his/her request for legal aid.

  2. In case of discontinuation of legal aid services, legal aid-providing organizations or persons must send written notices clearly stating reasons to legally-aided persons.

  3. In case a legal aid-related case is being carried out but the legal aid recipient no longer meets the conditions specified in Article 7 of this Law, the case shall continue to be carried out until completion.

  Article 38. Legal aid case files

  When providing legal aid, legal aid-providing organizations and persons shall prepare dossiers on legal aid-related cases in accordance with regulations of the Minister of Justice.

  Article 39. Archive of legal aid dossiers

  1. Within 30 days from the date of completion of a case, the legal aid provider must hand over the legal aid-related case file to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Documents in legal aid dossiers must be listed, numbered and arranged by date, month and year and stored as prescribed by law.

  3. Each electronic application for legal aid shall be digitized and updated in the system for management of legal aid cases and stored in the database on legal aid.

  Chapter VI

          RESPONSIBILITIES OF AGENCIES AND ORGANIZATIONS FOR LEGAL ASSISTANCE ACTIVITIES

  Article 40. Responsibilities for state management of legal aid

  1. The Government shall perform the unified state management of legal aid.

  2. The Ministry of Justice shall act as the focal agency assisting the Government in performing the state management of legal aid and have the following tasks and powers:

  a) Take charge in developing and promulgating or requesting competent state agencies to promulgate legal normative documents on legal aid;

  b) Develop, promulgate or submit to competent authorities for promulgation strategies and plans for development of legal aid services and organize implementation thereof;

  c) [25] Promulgate regulations on professional guidelines, quality and effectiveness assessment of legal aid cases; forms used in legal aid services; criteria for determining complex, typical, high-quality and successful legal aid cases; implement reporting and statistical regulations on legal aid services;

  d) Guide, organize and monitor the implementation of legal documents on legal aid;

  dd) Organize training, advanced training and professional skills training for persons providing legal aid services;

  e) Organize communication about legal aid[26];

  g) [27] Inspect the provision of legal aid services; commend, discipline and handle violations in the provision of legal aid services;

  h) Receive support and contributions from organizations and individuals for legal aid work;

  i) Implement international cooperation in legal aid;

  k) [28] Apply information technology and digital technology and implement digital transformation in legal aid services; develop, manage, operate and regularly upgrade legal aid information systems and databases; connect, integrate and share legal aid data on the National Law Portal.

  3. [29] Ministries and ministerial agencies shall, within their tasks and powers, cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in state management and organization of legal aid services; create, integrate, share, exploit and use data through connection and interconnection with legal aid databases on the National Law Portal.

  4. [30] The provincial People’s Committees shall, within their tasks and powers, perform state management of legal aid services in their provinces and ensure working conditions for the State Legal Aid Centers; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in creating, integrating, sharing, extracting and using data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  5. [31] The commune-level People’s Committee shall cooperate with the State legal aid center in providing legal aid services in their area.

  6. [32] The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 41. Responsibilities of agencies involved in legal aid activities in legal proceedings

  1. Within their tasks and powers, competent procedural authorities and procedural authorities shall cooperate and organize cooperation in implementing interdisciplinary mechanisms and providing legal aid at People’s Courts and in criminal investigation; enable legally-aided persons to enjoy the right to legal aid, enable legal aid-providing persons to participate in proceedings as prescribed by law.

  2. The Minister of Justice, the Chief Justice of the Supreme People’s Court, the Procurator General of the Supreme People’s Procuracy, the Minister of National Defense, the Minister of Public Security and the Minister of Finance shall cooperate in developing inter-sectoral cooperation councils on legal aid in central and provincial-level procedural activities.

  3. Agencies involved in legal aid activities in legal proceedings shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in creating, integrating, sharing, extracting and using data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  Article 42. Responsibilities of the Vietnam Fatherland Front, its member organizations and relevant state agencies

  1. The Vietnam Fatherland Front and its member organizations shall, within their functions and tasks, supervise the implementation of policies and laws on legal aid; and coordinate with state agencies and relevant organizations in legal aid work in accordance with law.

  2. During the process of handling and settling cases related to citizens, if the citizen is a legal aid beneficiary, the state agency shall explain the right to receive legal aid and introduce him/her to a legal aid-providing organization.

  Article 43. Responsibilities of lawyers’ socio-professional organizations

  1. The Vietnam Bar Federation shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers in accordance with this Law.

  2. The bar associations of provinces and centrally-affiliated cities shall cooperate with the provincial-level Justice Departments in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers under this Law.

  Article 44. Responsibilities of managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations Managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations shall coordinate with competent state management agencies in charge of legal assistance in managing and supervising the provision of legal assistance by legal consultancy organizations and legal consultants under this Law.

  Chapter VII

                            RESOLUTION OF COMPLAINTS, DENUNCIATIONS AND DISPUTES

  Article 45. Settlement of complaints and denunciations

  1. Legal aid beneficiaries have the right to lodge complaints about the following acts of legal aid-providing organizations or persons when having grounds to believe that such acts are illegal and infringe upon their legitimate rights and interests:

  a) Refuse to accept legal aid cases;

  b) Failing to provide legal aid;

  c) Providing legal aid in a manner that violates laws;

  d) Changing legal aid-providing persons in contravention of law.

  2. [35] The head of legal aid organization shall settle complaints about acts specified in Clause 1 of this Article. If the complainant disagrees with the complaint settlement decision of the head of legal aid organization or the complaint is not settled within the prescribed time limit, he/she may further lodge a complaint to the director of the Department of Justice.

  In case the complainant disagrees with the complaint settlement decision of the Director of the Department of Justice or the complaint is not settled within the prescribed time limit, he/she may initiate a lawsuit at court.

  The Government shall elaborate this clause; and procedures for settlement of complaints in legal aid.

  3. [36] Complaints and lawsuits in legal aid not specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall comply with regulations of law on complaints and other relevant laws.

  4. Individuals have the right to lodge complaints with competent authorities about violations of this Law. Complaints and settlement thereof shall comply with regulations of law on complaints.

  Article 46. Dispute settlement

  1. In case of a dispute between legal aid beneficiaries and legal aid professionals, legal counselors, legal aid collaborators, state legal aid centers or legal counseling organizations related to the provision of legal aid services, such disputes shall be resolved in accordance with civil laws.

  2. In case of a dispute between legal aid beneficiaries and lawyers or law practice organizations related to the provision of legal aid, the dispute shall be resolved in accordance with the law on lawyers and other relevant laws.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION CLAUSES [37]

  Article 47. Effect

  1. This Law comes into force as of January 01, 2018.

  2. The Legal Aid Law No. 69/2006/QH11 ceases to be effective as from the date this Law takes effect.

  Article 48. Transitional provisions

  1. From the effective date of this Law, persons who have been appointed as legal aid professionals under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 may continue to operate under this Law; after 5 years from the effective date of this Law, if the persons who are appointed as legal aid professionals do not possess certificates of lawyer training, their legal aid professional cards shall be revoked.

  2. Within one year after this Law takes effect, legal aid-providing organizations and persons under the Legal Aid Law No. 69/2006/QH11 must meet the requirements of this Law; if they fail to meet these requirements, they shall terminate their participation in legal aid services and transfer the dossiers of cases under their management to the State legal aid centers where they have registered for participation so that they may continue providing legal aid services.

  3. Legal aid cases which are being handled under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 but have not yet been completed by the effective date of this Law shall continue to be handled under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 until they are completed.

  4. Within one year from the effective date of this Law, provincial People’s Committees shall review their local branches of the State legal aid center and, based on legal aid demand, legal aid resources and operation effectiveness, report to the Ministry of Justice for agreement on the maintenance, merger or dissolution of such branches.
