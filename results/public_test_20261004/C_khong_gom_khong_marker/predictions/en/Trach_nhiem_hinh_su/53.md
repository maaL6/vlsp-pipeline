                                                       LAW

                                           NARCOTIC SUBSTANCE ADDICTION PREVENTION

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;
  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Drug Prevention and Control.

  Chapter I

                                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for prevention and control of narcotic substances; management of illicit drug users; narcotic substance rehabilitation; responsibilities of individuals, families, agencies, and organizations in prevention and control of narcotic substances; state management and international cooperation on prevention and control of narcotic substances.

  Article 2. Interpretation of terms

  In this Law, the terms below are construed as follows:

  1. Narcotic substances are addictive and psychotropic substances specified in the list of narcotic substances issued by the Government.

  2. Addictive substance means a stimulant or a depressant of nerves which easily leads to addiction for users.

  3. Psychotropic substances are stimulants or depressants of nerves or cause hallucinations, which may lead to addiction for users if used repeatedly.

  4. Precursor is an essential chemical in the process of preparing and producing narcotic substances specified in the list of precursors issued by the Government.

  5. Veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances or precursors are veterinary drugs containing substances prescribed in clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article.

  6. Plants containing narcotic substances are opium poppy, coca, cannabis and other plants containing narcotic substances shall be prescribed by the Government.

  7. Drug prevention and control means preventing, combating and fighting drug-related crimes and vices; controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances.

  8. Drug abuse refers to illegal use of narcotic substances, addiction to narcotic substances and violations against regulations on narcotic substances which are not liable to criminal prosecution or are subject to criminal prosecution but not liable to criminal prosecution.

  9. Control of lawful drug-related activities means permission for, monitoring, inspection and supervision of, and prevention and suppression of abuse of, lawful drug-related activities for other purposes.

  10. Illicit drug users are those who use narcotic substances without permission of competent authorities or professionals and whose drug test results are positive.

  11. Drug test is the application of specialized techniques to determine narcotic substances in the human body through urine samples, blood samples or other samples of the human body.

  12. Drug addicts are those who use narcotics, addictive drugs and psychotropic medicines and become dependent on these substances.

  13. Drug addiction rehabilitation means the process of providing medical, psychological and social support to help drug addicts stop using narcotic substances, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, physically and mentally recover, raise awareness and change behavior in order to stop illegally using these substances.

  14. Drug rehabilitation facilities are established to implement rehabilitation process in accordance with this Law, including public drug rehabilitation facilities and voluntary drug rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 3. State policies on drug prevention and control

  1. To implement measures to prevent and combat narcotic substances in combination with HIV/AIDS prevention and combat and other social evils.

  2. Intensify communication and education on drug prevention and control; encourage organizations and individuals to participate in communication and education on drug prevention and control.

  3. Prioritize drug prevention and control resources for ethnic minority and mountainous areas, remote areas, islands, border areas and areas with complex drug-related issues.

  4. Officers and soldiers of drug-related crime preventing authorities and rehabilitation workers in public rehabilitation facilities shall be entitled to incentives appropriate to their duties and areas of operation as prescribed by the Government.

  5. Protect and support individuals, families, agencies, and organizations participating in drug prevention and control.

  6. Strictly manage illicit drug users and addicts; encourage addicts to voluntarily undergo rehabilitation and treatment for narcotic substances with substitution drugs; encourage individuals, families, agencies, and organizations to participate in and support rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  7. Ensure funding for compulsory rehabilitation; support funding for voluntary rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  8. Domestic and foreign organizations and individuals investing in rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management support, and prevention and control of relapse shall be exempted from or reduced land rent and corporate income tax in accordance with the law.

  9. To encourage scientific research, technological development and application of high technology in drug prevention and combat.

  10. To commend individuals, organizations and agencies with achievements in drug prevention and combat.

  Article 4. Financial resources for drug prevention and control

  1. State budget.

  2. Sources of financial aid, assistance, investment and donation from domestic and foreign organizations and individuals.

  3. Spending of families and drug addicts.

  4. Other lawful financial sources.

  Article 5. Prohibited acts

  1. Planting narcotic-containing plants and providing guidance on planting narcotic-containing plants.

  2. Research, expertise, inspection, testing, production, storage, transportation, preservation, inventory, trading, distribution, processing, exchange, import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, illegal transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors.

  3. Appropriating narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors.

  4. Deliver, receive, manage, control, store, issue, and preserve narcotic substances and precursors in violation of the law; permit users of narcotic substances, addictive drugs, or psychotropic medicines to use them in violation of the law.

  5. Illegally using or organizing the use of narcotic substances; forcing or inducing other persons to illegally use narcotic substances; harboring or supporting the illegal use of narcotic substances.

  6. Producing, storing, transporting, or trading in instruments and equipment used for illegally producing or using narcotic substances.

  7. Combat or obstruct drug test, determination of narcotic substance dependence, management of illicit drug users, rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  8. Taking revenge or obstructing drug prevention and control officers and participants in drug prevention and control.

  9. Abusing positions, powers and occupations to commit violations against regulations on drug prevention and control.

  10. Illegally guiding production and use of narcotic substances; advertising and marketing narcotic substances.

  11. To impose penalties on illicit drug users, rehabilitating individuals, and post-rehabilitation individuals.

  12. Other prohibited acts related to narcotic substances as prescribed by law.

  Chapter II

                                  RESPONSIBILITY TO PREVENT AND COMBAT NARCOTICS

  Article 6. Responsibilities of individuals and families

  1. Disseminate and educate family members and relatives about the harms of narcotic substances and comply with regulations of law on prevention and control of narcotic substances; manage and prevent family members from violating regulations of law on prevention and control of narcotic substances.

  2. Comply with the prescription of competent persons regarding the use of addictive drugs, psychotropic drugs, pre-substances, veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and pre-substances.

  3. Cooperate with relevant agencies in combating drug-related crimes and addiction; participate in supporting community-based and family-based voluntary rehabilitation, rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities, and substitution therapy for narcotic substance addiction; monitor and assist individuals post-rehabilitation in reintegrating into the community; prevent and combat relapse.

  4. Promptly provide information on drug-related crimes, drug abuse and narcotic crop cultivation to police authorities or competent state agencies; participate in eradication of narcotic crop cultivation organized by local authorities.

  Article 7. Responsibilities of regulatory agencies

  1. Drug prevention and control organizations in agencies and units; preventing and controlling officials, public employees, and employees under their management and officers and soldiers of the people’s armed forces committing violations against regulations on drug prevention and control; disseminating and mobilizing the public to detect, denounce, and combat drug-related crimes and evils.

  2. Implement state policies on planning and socio-economic development to replace planting of narcotic-containing plants in areas where such plants are removed.

  Article 8. Responsibilities of educational institutions

  1. Organize implementation of education programs on drug prevention and control; disseminate and educate students about the law on drug prevention and control; strictly manage and prevent students from violating the law on drug prevention and control.

  2. Cooperate with families, agencies, organizations and local authorities in managing and educating students on drug prevention and control.

  3. Cooperate with competent agencies, organizations and individuals in organizing drug test when necessary to detect students who illegally use narcotic substances.

  Article 9.- Responsibilities of the press bodies

  Press agencies shall coordinate with relevant agencies and organizations in propagating policies and laws on drug prevention and combat.

  Article 10. Responsibilities of the Vietnam Fatherland Front, its member organizations and other organizations

  1. To organize and coordinate with competent agencies in propagating and popularizing knowledge about and laws on drug prevention and combat among the people; to mobilize the people to participate in drug prevention and combat and carry out campaigns on drug prevention and combat.

  2. To prevent and stop their own members and all citizens from committing violations of the law on drug prevention and combat.

  3. Cooperate with local governments at all levels and competent authorities in mobilizing drug addicts to implement rehabilitation measures and treatment of narcotic substances with substitution drugs; participate in rehabilitation, education, vocational training, job hunting, and assistance for post-rehabilitation individuals to reintegrate into the community; prevent and combat relapse.

  Article 11. Drug-related crime preventing authorities

  1. Drug-related crime preventing authorities include:

  a) Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to the People’s Public Security;

  b) Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to Vietnam Border Guard, Vietnam Coast Guard and Customs authorities.

  2. Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to the People’s Public Security shall, within their tasks and powers, take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies and organizations in preventing, combating drug-related crimes.

  3. Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to Vietnam Border Guard, Vietnam Coast Guard, and Customs, within their tasks and powers, shall take charge and cooperate with public security authorities and other relevant agencies in preventing, combating drug-related crimes in areas or regions under their management and control.

  4. In the same area, if detecting a violation related to duties and powers of multiple agencies, the agency that detects it first shall handle it within its jurisdiction as prescribed by law; in case where the violation does not fall under its jurisdiction, it shall transfer the case file, the violator and exhibits to the competent authority for settlement.

  5. The Government shall stipulate the cooperation between drug-related crime preventing authorities.

  Chapter III

                  CONTROL OF LEGAL ACTS RELATED TO NARCOTICS

  Article 12. Legal activities related to narcotic substances

  1. Legal drug-related activities are those permitted by competent state authorities, including:

  a) Research, expertise, testing, inspection, and production of narcotic substances (excluding narcotic substance-containing plants), precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for medicine production, and veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors;

  b) Transport, preservation, storage, trading, distribution, use, processing, and exchange of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, and veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors;

  c) Import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines and veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors.

  2. Legal drug-related activities specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall be strictly controlled in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 13. Control of research, appraisal and production of narcotic substances and precursors

  1. Research, appraisal, and production of narcotic substances (excluding cultivation of plants containing narcotic substances) and precursors shall be monitored, inspected, and supervised by competent authorities.

  2. The Government shall stipulate competent authorities’ licensing, procedures for licensing, monitoring, inspection and supervision specified in Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 14. Control of narcotic substances and precursors transportation

  1. Agencies, organizations and individuals that have been permitted by competent authorities may transport narcotic substances and precursors; when transporting, they must package and seal them in accordance with regulations of competent authorities; take responsibility for quantity, quality, take measures to ensure safety, prevent loss during transportation and be subject to monitoring, inspection and supervision of competent authorities.

  2. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 15. Control of activities related to addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, and precursors used for production of medicines

  The control of activities related to addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, and precursors used for production of medicines shall comply with regulations of law on pharmaceuticals, except for the activities specified in Point b Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 17 hereof.

  Article 16. Control of veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors

  The control of activities related to veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances or precursors shall comply with regulations of the Government.

  Article 17. Control of import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of drugs

  1. The following activities must be permitted by competent authorities:

  a) Import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import of narcotic substances and precursors;

  b) Temporarily import, re-export, temporarily export, re-import addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances and medicinal ingredients which are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or pre-substances used for production of medicines.

  2. The transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs and medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or precursors used for production of medicines shall be carried out according to the route specified in the transit permit. The agency or organization that performs the transit shall carry out procedures and submit to the control of customs agencies and competent authorities of Vietnam.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 18. Preparation of documents on legal activities related to narcotic substances

  Agencies, organizations and individuals shall, when conducting activities specified in Clause 1 Article 12 of this Law, prepare dossiers and submit reports as prescribed by the Government.

  Article 19. Control of lawful activities related to narcotic substances for national defense and security

  Research, appraisal, production, transportation, storage, preservation, use, import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances, precursors for national defense and security shall comply with regulations of the Government.

  Article 20. Control of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for purposes of first aid and emergency treatment during international journeys, travel and self-treatment of persons entering, exiting or transiting through Vietnam

  1. The carrying of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for first aid and emergency treatment on board a ship, aircraft, train, automobile or other means of transport during international journeys or tours shall not be regarded as carrying exports, imports or transit goods in the territory of Vietnam.

  The commander or driver of a transport vehicle shall declare to the Vietnamese customs agency, explain the quantity of drugs used, apply appropriate safety measures to prevent the use for wrong purposes or illegal transportation of such drugs and be subject to the control of the competent authority of Vietnam.

  2. The bringing of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for self-treatment of persons entering, exiting or transit through the territory of Vietnam shall comply with regulations of the Government and under control of competent authorities of Vietnam.

  Article 21. Handling of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors seized in cases of law violations Narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors seized in criminal cases and administrative violations shall be handled according to regulations of law on criminal procedure and law on handling of administrative violations.

  Chapter IV

                          MANAGEMENT OF DRUG-USER

  Article 22. Drug test

  1. Drug test shall be performed on the following individuals:

  a) The person is found to have illegally used narcotic substances;

  b) The person whose illegal use of narcotic substances is suspected by competent authorities or persons;

  c) The illegal drug user is under management;

  d) Individuals subject to educational administrative measures at communes, wards, or townships due to illegal use of narcotic substances; individuals undergoing narcotic substance rehabilitation; individuals receiving substitution therapy for opium-based narcotic substances; individuals under post-rehabilitation management.

  2. Figureheads of rehabilitation facilities and treatment centers for narcotic substance addiction using substitution drugs; individuals authorized to impose penalties for administrative violations involving illegal use of narcotic substances shall conduct narcotic substance testing within their jurisdiction or request specialized agencies and individuals to test narcotic substances in the body in cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article.

  In case of positive test results, send the results immediately to the President of the commune-level People’s Committee where the person with positive test results resides, except for cases where such person is undergoing compulsory narcotic rehabilitation.

  3. The State shall ensure funding for drug test in case specified under Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 23. Management of illicit drug users

  1. Managing illicit drug users is a preventive measure to help illicit drug users not continue using illicit drugs and prevent their illegal acts.

  Managing illicit drug users is not an administrative measure.

  2. The management period of illicit drug users shall be 1 year from the date on which the President of the commune-level People’s Committee issues the decision on management.

  3. Management of illicit drug users includes:

  a) Provide counseling, encouragement, education, and assistance to illicit drug users to prevent them from continuing to use narcotic substances illegally;

  b) Drug test;

  c) Prevent illicit drug users from committing acts of social disorder and insecurity.

  4. Within 3 working days from the date on which positive test results of local residents are received, Chairpersons of commune-level People’s Committees shall issue decision and take responsibility for management of illicit drug users residing in their communes.

  5. Within the management period, the President of the commune-level People’s Committee shall issue a decision to stop managing illicit drug users in the following cases:

  a) The illegal drug user is determined to be a drug addict;

  b) The illicit drug user shall be subject to administrative handling measures of education at the commune, ward or township for using illicit narcotic substances;

  c) The illegal drug user shall be subject to administrative measures such as being sent to a compulsory educational institution or reformatory;

  d) The illegal drug user must serve their prison sentence;

  dd) The illegal drug user on the list of drug users dies or is declared missing by the court.

  6. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 24. Responsibility of illegal drug users

  1. Fully and accurately provide information on their illegal use of narcotic substances to the commune-level police authority where they reside.

  2. To submit to management by the commune-level People’s Committees.

  Article 25. Responsibilities of families, agencies, organizations and community in managing illicit drug users

  1. Families of illicit drug users shall take responsibility to:

  a) Manage and educate illicit drug users; prevent illicit drug use;

  b) Provide information on illicit drug use of illicit drug users to commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where such persons reside;

  c) Prevent illicit drug users from committing acts of social disorder and insecurity;

  d) Cooperate with competent authorities in sending illicit drug users for drug test.

  2. Agencies and organizations where illicit drug users work or communities where illicit drug users live shall take responsibility to:

  a) Encourage, assist and educate illicit drug users; prevent illicit drug use;

  b) Cooperate with families and competent authorities in sending illicit drug users for narcotic substance testing.

  Article 26. Making list of illicit drug users

  1. Commune-level police authorities shall assist the People’s Committees at the same level in compiling lists of illicit drug users residing in their localities.

  2. When illicit drug users change their residence, commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move to shall notify commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move from within 5 working days from the date on which they leave the administrative divisions to put them in the list and continue to manage them.

  3. Commune-level police authorities shall assist the People’s Committees at the same level in removing entities from the list of illicit drug users in the following cases:

  a) The illicit drug user does not commit acts of illicit drug use during the management period specified in Clause 2 Article 23 of this Law;

  b) The illicit drug user falls under the case of suspension of management as prescribed in Clause 5 Article 23 of this Law;

  c) The illicit drug user moves to reside in another locality.

  Chapter V

                                              CRYSTALINE NARCOTIC ADDICTION

  Article 27. Determination of narcotic substance dependence

  1. Determination of narcotic substance dependence shall be carried out for the following persons:

  a) The illegal drug user is found to be using narcotic substances during the management period;

  b) The illicit drug user does not have a stable residence;

  c) Persons who are subject to educational administrative measures at communes, wards or townships due to illegal use of narcotic substances or within 01 year from the date of completely serving such educational administrative measures, if they are detected using narcotic substances illegally;

  d) Individuals under post-rehabilitation management are found to use narcotic substances illegally;

  dd) Voluntarily determine narcotic substance dependence.

  2. The commune-level police authority where the person is found to fall under the cases specified in Points a, b, c, and d Clause 1 of this Article shall prepare a medical record requesting the competent health facility to determine the status of narcotic substance addiction.

  3. Where the police of district or provincial level directly detects or during the process of investigation and handling of violations of law detects cases specified in Points a, b, c and d Clause 1 of this Article, the police agency handling the case shall prepare a request for competent health facility to determine the drug addiction.

  4. Upon obtaining results of narcotic substance dependence test, medical facilities shall promptly send such results to requesting agencies and persons whose narcotic substance dependence is determined.

  5. Individuals proposed for determination of narcotic substance dependence have the following rights and responsibilities:

  a) Have their honor and dignity protected; receive travel, accommodation, and rehabilitation support during the period in which narcotic substance dependence is determined;

  b) Comply with internal rules and regulations of facilities determining narcotic substance dependence; truthfully declare to medical personnel narcotic substance use history and manifestations of narcotic substance use;

  c) Individuals aged 18 years or older, or parents, guardians, or legal representatives of individuals aged between 12 years and under 18 years as prescribed in Points a, b, c, and dd Clause 1 of this Article, upon receiving results confirming narcotic substance dependence, shall register for voluntary rehabilitation in accordance with Article 28 of this Law or register for treatment of opium-based substances using substitution therapies in cases of opium-based substance dependence with the People’s Committee of the commune where they reside.

  6. The Government shall provide for medical facilities eligible to determine narcotic substance dependence; application, procedures for determining narcotic substance dependence.

  7. The Minister of Health shall provide for diagnostic criteria and professional procedures for determining narcotic substance dependence.

  8. The State shall ensure funding for determination of narcotic substance dependence in cases specified in points a, b, c and d clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 28. Rehabilitation measures

  1. Rehabilitation measures include:

  a) Voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) Compulsory narcotic rehabilitation.

  2. Voluntary rehabilitation shall be implemented at home, community or rehabilitation facilities; compulsory rehabilitation shall be implemented at public rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 29. Rehabilitation process

  1. The rehabilitation process consists of the following stages:

  a) Receive and classify;

  b/ Treatment of convulsions, detoxification, treatment of mental disorders and other medical conditions;

  c) Educate, counsel, rehabilitate behavior and personality;

  d) Occupational therapy and vocational training;

  dd) Preparation for community reintegration.

  2. Compulsory rehabilitation shall ensure completion of all stages under Clause 1 of this Article; voluntary rehabilitation shall ensure completion of 3 stages under Points a, b, and c Clause 1 of this Article.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 30. Voluntary rehabilitation at home or community

  1. Community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation means a drug addict voluntarily receiving rehabilitation at home or community with professional assistance from organizations and individuals providing rehabilitation services, cooperation and assistance from families and community, and under management of commune-level People’s Committees.

  2. Duration of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation is from 6 months to 12 months.

  3. Voluntary rehabilitation participants in community or family settings shall receive funding support when completing at least 3 stages under Points a, b, and c Clause 1 Article 29 hereof.

  4. Voluntary rehabilitation participants have the responsibility to:

  a) Comply with and fully implement regulations on voluntary rehabilitation and comply with guidance of specialized agencies;

  b) Pay expenses related to rehabilitation as prescribed.

  5. The President of the commune-level People’s Committee shall take responsibility to:

  a) Receive registration for community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) Guide and manage community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation counselors;

  c) Issue certificates of completion of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation.

  6. The chairpersons of the district-level People’s Committees shall take responsibility to:

  a) Assign public service providers within their jurisdiction to provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services;

  b) Receive applications and announce list of organizations and individuals eligible to provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service;

  c) Notify the commune-level People’s Committee of the list of organizations and individuals providing community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services;

  d) Allocate funding for community-based and family-based voluntary rehabilitation;

  dd) Direct, guide and inspect community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation.

  7. Rehabilitation facilities and organizations or individuals eligible to provide one or more rehabilitation activities according to rehabilitation procedures under Clause 1 Article 29 hereof may provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services and have the following responsibilities:

  a) Receive and organize implementation of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service provision;

  b) Comply with professional procedures as prescribed by competent authorities;

  c) Within 5 working days from the date on which rehabilitating individuals use or discontinue service without permission or complete service, they must inform People’s Committees of communes where they register for community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation.

  8. Organizations and individuals eligible shall be permitted to register for provision of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service with Chairpersons of district-level People’s Committees.

  9. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 31. Voluntary rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities

  1. The period of voluntary rehabilitation at a rehabilitation facility shall be between 6 months and 12 months.

  2. Individuals completing at least 3 stages under Points a, b, and c Clause 1 Article 29 hereof shall be eligible for funding support.

  3. Voluntary rehabilitating individuals at rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to:

  a) Comply with and fully implement regulations on voluntary rehabilitation and comply with guidance of specialized agencies;

  b) Pay expenses related to rehabilitation as prescribed.

  In case of rehabilitation facility-based voluntary rehabilitating individuals meeting with financial difficulties, they may be considered for reduction or exemption from fees payable.

  4. Rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to issue written confirmation of completion of voluntary rehabilitation to rehabilitating individuals.

  5. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 32. Entities subject to administrative handling measures of being sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers

  Drug addicts aged 18 years or older who are subject to administrative handling shall be sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers in accordance with the Law on Handling of Administrative Violations in one of the following cases:

  1. Failing to register, failing to implement or arbitrarily terminating voluntary rehabilitation;

  2. During voluntary rehabilitation, if illicit drug use is detected;

  3. Drug addicts who use opium preparations without registration, fail to receive treatment or terminate the treatment of opium preparations without permission with substitution drugs or have their treatment terminated due to violations against regulations on narcotic substance addiction treatment;

  4. Relapse during post-rehabilitation management period.

  Article 33. Rehabilitation of drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age

  1. Drug addicts aged between full 12 and under 18 years shall be sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers in one of the following cases:

  a) Failing to register, failing to implement or arbitrarily terminating voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) During voluntary rehabilitation, if they are found to have illegally used narcotic substances;

  c) Drug addicts who use opium preparations without registration, without treatment or without permission to stop using opium preparations with substitution drugs or have their treatment with opium preparations with substitution drugs stopped due to violations of regulations on narcotic substance treatment.

  2. Drug addicts aged between full 12 and under 18 years who are sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers shall take responsibility to:

  a) Comply with regulations on compulsory narcotic rehabilitation, internal rules and regulations, and submit to management, education, and treatment by compulsory narcotic rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Participate in medical treatment, education, counseling, cultural education, vocational education, occupational therapy and behavior and personality rehabilitation activities.

  3. The period of compulsory rehabilitation for drug addicts aged between full 12 years and under 18 years shall be between 6 months and 12 months.

  4. The placement of drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age in compulsory rehabilitation centers shall be decided by district-level People’s Courts and are not administrative handling measures.

  5. The Standing Committee of the National Assembly shall provide for the procedures for the People’s Court to consider and decide on the placement of drug addicts aged from full 12 years to under 18 years in compulsory rehabilitation centers.

  Article 34. Preparation of application for admission to compulsory rehabilitation centers for drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age

  1. The preparation of a dossier requesting admission of a drug addict from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age to a compulsory rehabilitation center shall be carried out as follows:

  a) The President of the People’s Committee of the commune where the person resides or commits violations in cases where his/her place of residence cannot be identified shall prepare a dossier proposing the placement of such person in a compulsory rehabilitation center;

  b) In case a drug addict is detected by the police of district or province or during the process of investigation and handling of violation cases and is under 18 years old and must be sent to compulsory rehabilitation center, the police authority handling the case shall verify, collect documents and prepare a request for sending him/her to compulsory rehabilitation center;

  c) The application includes a record of violation, a résumé, documents proving the current state of narcotic substance addiction of the applicant, a report from the drug addict or his/her legal representative, opinions of parents, guardians or legal representatives;

  d) The commune-level police authority shall assist the President of the People’s Committee at the same level in collecting documents and preparing the application as prescribed in Point a Clause 1 of this Article.

  2. The agency or person preparing the application specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall be responsible for the legality of the application. After completing the preparation of the application, the agency or person must notify in writing the person proposed to be placed in a compulsory rehabilitation center, and their parents, guardian, or legal representative of the preparation of the application. These individuals have the right to read the application and provide comments on the preparation of the application within 3 working days from the date of receipt of the notification.

  3. Within 1 working day from the expiry date of the application, the agency or person preparing the application shall submit it to the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division where the person concerned resides or commits violations in cases where his/her place of residence cannot be identified. Within 2 working days from the receipt of the application, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall decide to transfer the application to the district-level People’s Court for decision on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation center. In case the application is incomplete, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall return it to the agency or person preparing the application for supplementation within 2 working days from the receipt of the application. Within 2 working days from the receipt of the supplemented application, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall decide to transfer the application to the district-level People’s Court for decision on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation center.

  4. The application for the district-level People’s Court to consider and decide on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation center shall include:

  a) Application for admission to a compulsory rehabilitation facility as prescribed in Point c Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Written request of the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division for consideration of admission to a compulsory rehabilitation center.

  The document of the Director of the Department of Labor, Invalids and Social Affairs must include opinions on protection of children’s rights.

  5. The application for admission of drug addicts aged between full 12 and under 18 to compulsory rehabilitation centers must be numbered and archived in accordance with law on archives.

  6. The Government shall elaborate preparation of application for admission of drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age to compulsory rehabilitation facilities; rehabilitation regimes applicable to drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age.

  Article 35. Public rehabilitation facilities

  1. Public rehabilitation facilities shall be established under decisions of Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees.

  2. Public rehabilitation facilities shall arrange the following areas:

  a) Temporary accommodation for individuals proposed for determination of narcotic substance dependence and those during the period in which dossiers on application for administrative measures are being prepared shall be placed in compulsory rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Compulsory rehabilitation areas;

  c) Voluntary rehabilitation areas;

  d) Rehabilitation areas for individuals from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age;

  dd) Rehabilitation centers for persons with infectious diseases of Group A and Group B in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases;

  e) Rehabilitation area for individuals who commit acts of disturbing order or violating internal rules and regulations of rehabilitation facilities.

  3. In the areas specified in Clause 2 of this Article, separate areas shall be arranged for men and women. Persons having differences between their identity and gender recorded in their dossiers or personal records shall be managed at separate rooms in the area according to gender represented on their learner’s identity.

  4. Public rehabilitation facilities shall operate in:

  a) Receive and organize rehabilitation for rehabilitating individuals placed in compulsory rehabilitation facilities and voluntary rehabilitating individuals;

  b) Determine narcotic substance dependence;

  c/ Receiving, managing, educating, counseling and treating withdrawal syndromes, mental disorders and other diseases for persons who are in the process of making dossiers for being sent to compulsory detoxification centers.

  5. Public rehabilitation facilities have the following rights:

  a) Receive rehabilitating individuals in voluntary rehabilitation according to reception capacity of rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Figureheads of public rehabilitation facilities shall decide on appropriate measures for managing, educating, and treating rehabilitating individuals.

  6. Public rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to:

  a) Comply with regulations on determination of narcotic substance dependence, rehabilitation process, and management of individuals during preparation for submission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Respect for life, health, honor, dignity and property of rehabilitating individuals, individuals proposed to be determined with narcotic substance dependence and individuals during period in which dossiers on admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities are being prepared;

  c) Ensure rights of drug addicts during rehabilitation period and issue written confirmation of completion of compulsory rehabilitation for individuals from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age;

  d) Figureheads of public rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to ensure operational requirements of rehabilitation facilities; publicly post costs of rehabilitation at facilities as per the law;

  dd) Prevent and combat drug smuggling into facilities;

  e) Within 5 working days from the date on which rehabilitating individuals voluntarily receive or discontinue service use without permission or complete rehabilitation process, they must notify the People’s Committees of communes where they register for voluntary rehabilitation.

  7. The Government shall regulate conditions regarding facilities, equipment, and personnel of public rehabilitation facilities and management regulations at public rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 36. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities

  1. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities shall be established by individuals or organizations providing rehabilitation services. The Government shall stipulate conditions and authority, procedures for issuance and revocation of operating licenses of voluntary rehabilitation facilities.

  2. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities have the following rights:

  a) Receive rehabilitating individuals in voluntary rehabilitation according to reception capacity of rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Collect expenses related to rehabilitation;

  c) Enjoy preferential treatment as prescribed by law.

  3. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to:

  a) Comply with rehabilitation process;

  b) Respect for life, health, honor, dignity and property of rehabilitating individuals;

  c) Ensure the rights of drug addicts aged from full 12 to under 18 during rehabilitation period;

  d) Figureheads of rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to ensure operational conditions of rehabilitation facilities; publicly post costs related to rehabilitation at facilities as per the law;

  dd) Prevent and combat drug smuggling into facilities;

  e) Arrange separate areas or rooms when receiving subjects who are persons aged between full 12 and under 18 years, persons suffering from infectious diseases of group A and group B as prescribed by the Law on Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases, females; persons having differences between their body and gender specified in their dossiers and personal records and to be managed in separate rooms in the area according to gender indicated on their learner’s identity cards;

  g) Within 5 working days from the date on which rehabilitating individuals receive or discontinue service without permission or complete rehabilitation process, they must notify People’s Committees of communes where they register for voluntary rehabilitation.

  Article 37. Application of narcotic rehabilitation measures to Vietnamese people who are expelled by foreign countries for using narcotic substances illegally or being addicted to narcotic substances; foreigners addicted to narcotic substances living in Vietnam

  1. Vietnamese citizens who are expelled by foreign countries and return to Vietnam for committing acts of illegally using narcotic substances or being addicted to narcotic substances must undergo determination of their narcotic substance addiction status. In case they are determined to be addicted to narcotic substances, they shall implement narcotic substance rehabilitation measures in accordance with this Law.

  2. Foreign drug addicts living in Vietnam shall register for voluntary rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities according to this Law and pay all costs related to rehabilitation. If they fail to receive voluntary rehabilitation, they shall be handled as prescribed by law.

  Article 38. Rehabilitation of persons held in temporary detention, inmates, inmates of compulsory educational establishments and students of reformatories

  1. The State shall apply appropriate rehabilitation measures to drug addicts who are persons held in temporary detention, inmates, inmates of compulsory education establishments, and students at reformatories.

  2. Superintendents of prisons, detention centers, custody centers, directors of compulsory education facilities, and principals of reform schools shall closely cooperate with local medical facilities in implementing the provisions of Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 39. Exemption from serving the remaining duration in the decision to send to compulsory rehabilitation centers for persons aged between full 14 and under 18 years who are serving compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation

  Individuals aged between 14 and under 18 who are undergoing compulsory rehabilitation and are found to have committed criminal acts before or during the period of serving the decision on compulsory rehabilitation and sentenced to imprisonment by the Court but are not entitled to suspended sentences shall be exempt from serving the remaining duration in the decision to send them to compulsory rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 40. Post-rehabilitation management at residence

  1. Individuals who have completed voluntary rehabilitation, individuals who have completed opioid substitution therapy, and individuals aged 12 years to under 18 years who have completely served the decision to be placed in a compulsory rehabilitation facility shall be subject to post-rehabilitation management for 1 year from the date of completion of rehabilitation or serving of the decision.

  2. Individuals who have completely served decisions on administrative measures and are placed in compulsory rehabilitation facilities shall be subject to post-rehabilitation management for 2 years from the date of completion of serving such decisions.

  3. Post-rehabilitation management includes:

  a) Make a list of individuals placed under post-rehabilitation management;

  b) Provide counseling, assistance, and prevention of relapse;

  c) Monitor, detect and prevent illegal use of narcotic substances.

  4. Social support during post-rehabilitation management includes:

  a) Provide cultural education support for individuals aged from 12 years to under 18 years;

  b) Provide vocational training, loans, job hunting and participation in social activities to facilitate community reintegration.

  5. The President of the commune-level People’s Committee shall issue decisions and organize social management and support after narcotic substance rehabilitation.

  6. The Government shall provide regulations on application, procedures, management and support policies for individuals placed under post-rehabilitation management.

  Article 41. Intervention measures for reducing harms to illicit drug users and addicts

  1. Intervention measures to reduce harms for illicit drug users and drug addicts are measures to reduce harms related to illicit drug use causing harm to themselves, their families, and the community.

  2. Intervention measures to reduce harms for illicit drug users and drug addicts include:

  a/ Treating opium addiction with substitution drugs;

  b) Other measures as prescribed by the Government.

  Article 42. Responsibilities of families of drug addicts and community

  1. Drug addicts’ families shall take responsibility to:

  a) Support drug addicts during rehabilitation, after rehabilitation and community reintegration;

  b) Cooperate with competent authorities and individuals in preparing documents for compulsory rehabilitation; cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations, and units in supporting drug addicts during rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management, and community reintegration.

  2. The community where drug addicts reside shall have the following responsibilities:

  a) Encourage and assist drug addicts;

  b) Cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations and units in supporting drug addicts during rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management and community reintegration.

  Article 43. List of rehabilitating addicts and individuals under rehabilitation management

  1. Commune-level police authorities shall assist the People’s Committees at the same level in compiling lists of drug addicts and persons under post-rehabilitation management residing in their localities.

  2. When rehabilitating individuals or persons under post-rehabilitation management change their residence, commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move to shall notify commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move from within 5 working days from the date on which they leave the administrative divisions to include them in the list and cooperate in management.

  3. Commune-level police authorities shall assist the People’s Committees at the same level in removing individuals from the list of drug addicts and people placed under post-rehabilitation management in the following cases:

  a) During post-rehabilitation management period under Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 40 hereof, if they are not found to illegally use narcotic substances;

  b) Drug addicts and persons under post-rehabilitation management shall serve prison sentences;

  c) Drug addicts or persons under post-rehabilitation management move to reside in other areas;

  d) Drug addicts or persons under post-rehabilitation management die or are declared missing by the court.

  Chapter VI

                            STATE MANAGEMENT OF NARCOTIC SUBSTANCE PREVENTION AND COMBAT

  Article 44. Contents of state management of drug prevention and combat

  1. Formulate and implement strategies, policies, and plans for drug prevention and control.

  2. Promulgate and organize implementation of legal documents on drug prevention and control.

  3. Organize apparatus and training for persons engaged in drug prevention and combat.

  4. Control legal activities related to narcotic substances.

  5. Provide rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  6. Drug prevention and combat organizations.

  7. Conduct state statistics on drug prevention and control.

  8. To organize research and application of scientific and technological advances in drug prevention and combat.

  9. Organize communication and education on drug prevention and control.

  10. International cooperation in drug prevention and combat.

  11. To commend, inspect, examine, settle complaints and denunciations, and handle violations of the law on drug prevention and control.

  Article 45. Responsibilities for state management of drug prevention and control

  1. The Government shall perform the unified state management of drug prevention and combat.

  2. The Ministry of Public Security shall act as the focal agency assisting the Government in performing state management of drug prevention and combat.

  3. Ministries, ministerial-level agencies and Government-attached agencies shall, within the scope of their tasks and powers, implement and coordinate with relevant agencies and organizations in drug prevention and control.

  4. People’s Committees at all levels shall, within their tasks and powers, perform state management of drug prevention and control in their localities.

  Article 46. Responsibilities of Ministry of Public Security

  1. Promulgate or request competent regulatory agencies to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies and organizations in drug prevention and control; receive and process information on drug-related crimes; control legal activities related to narcotic substances within their competence.

  3. Organize assessment of narcotic substances and precursors.

  4. Training and fostering persons engaged in drug-related crime investigation and prevention.

  5. Provide guidance on the preparation of lists of illicit drug users, drug addicts and individuals placed under post-rehabilitation management.

  6. Take charge of state statistics on drug prevention and control; management of information on drug-related crimes, illicit drug users, drug addicts, people under post-rehabilitation management, and results of control of legal activities related to narcotic substances.

  7. Implement international cooperation in drug prevention and combat.

  Article 47. Responsibilities of Ministry of Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs

  1. Promulgate or request competent regulatory agencies to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations and local governments in guiding community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation, rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities and post-rehabilitation management.

  3. Provide guidance on establishment, dissolution, and organization of public rehabilitation facilities.

  4. Training and fostering persons engaged in rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  5. Guide and direct the organization of propagation and education on drug prevention and control in vocational education and training institutions.

  6. Implement international cooperation in narcotic substance rehabilitation.

  7. Statistics on rehabilitating drug addicts in community or at rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 48. Responsibilities of Ministry of National Defense

  1. Promulgate or request competent regulatory agencies to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Organize implementation of plan for prevention and control of narcotic substances in border areas on land, at sea, contiguous zone, exclusive economic zone and continental shelf.

  3. Direct Vietnam Border Guard and Coast Guard forces to take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations, and competent authorities in receiving and handling information on drug-related crimes, detecting, preventing, and combating violations of the law on narcotic substances, and controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances within the areas and localities specified in Clause 2 of this Article in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  4. Training and fostering personnel in charge of drug-related crime prevention and control of Vietnam Border Guard and Coast Guard.

  5. Statistics on drug addicts in prisons, detention centers and custody centers under management of the Ministry of National Defense.

  Article 49. Responsibilities of the Ministry of Health

  1. Promulgate or request competent regulatory agencies to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Research narcotic substances and rehabilitation methods; issue and revoke licenses for narcotic substance circulation and rehabilitation methods; provide personnel and medical technical expertise for narcotic substance rehabilitation.

  3. Statistics on persons undergoing treatment for addiction to opium substitutes.

  Article 50. Responsibilities of Ministry of Finance

  Direct customs authorities to implement drug prevention and control activities in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Chapter VII

                             INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION ON NARCOTIC PREVENTION AND COMBAT

  Article 51. Principles in international cooperation on drug prevention and combat

  1. The State of Vietnam shall implement international treaties on drug prevention and combat and other relevant international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory on the principles of respecting independence, sovereignty, territorial integrity and mutual benefit; cooperate with foreign countries, international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals in drug prevention and combat activities.

  2. On the basis of international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory, this Law and other relevant laws, signed international agreements, competent authorities of Vietnam shall implement cooperation programs on drug prevention and combat with relevant agencies of foreign countries, international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals.

  Article 52. International cooperation policies on drug prevention and combat

  The State of Vietnam shall implement multilateral and bilateral international cooperation policies in the field of drug prevention and combat on the principle of respecting independence and national sovereignty with other countries; encourage international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals to cooperate with Vietnamese agencies and organizations in supporting each other in terms of material facilities, enhancing legal, information and training technology and medical capabilities for drug prevention and combat activities.

  Article 53. Controlled goods transfer

  The State of Vietnam shall comply with requests for controlled goods transfer on the basis of international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory in order to detect and criminally prosecute persons committing drug-related crimes. The decision to apply and implement this measure shall be made under agreements between competent authorities of Vietnam and relevant countries.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS

  Article 54. Effect

  1. This Law comes into force as of January 01, 2022.

  2. The Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10, as amended by Law No. 16/2008/QH12, ceases to be effective as from the effective date of this Law.

  Article 55. Transitional provisions

  1. From the effective date of this Law:

  a) Individuals who are currently undergoing community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation shall continue their voluntary rehabilitation until the end of the registration period in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10, as amended by Law No. 16/2008/QH12, and shall be subject to post-rehabilitation management under this Law;

  b) Persons under post-rehabilitation management shall continue to comply with decisions on post-rehabilitation management until the end of the post-rehabilitation management period in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10, as amended by Law No. 16/2008/QH12. Where a person who is under post-rehabilitation management and is full 18 years old from the effective date of this Law relapses into drug addiction, he/she shall be subject to administrative handling measures and sent to a compulsory rehabilitation center;

  c) Persons who are serving community-based compulsory rehabilitation under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10, as amended by Law No. 16/2008/QH12, may register for voluntary rehabilitation under this Law. The period served under the community-based compulsory rehabilitation decision shall be included in the period of voluntary rehabilitation;

  d) Individuals subject to mandatory rehabilitation centers under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by Law No. 16/2008/QH12 while preparing documents for admission to mandatory rehabilitation centers shall undergo rehabilitation in accordance with this Law;

  dd) The license related to lawful drug-related activities that has been issued shall continue to be used until the expiry date stated on the license.

  2. Within 2 years from the effective date of this Law, compulsory rehabilitation facilities established under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 must meet all conditions of public rehabilitation facilities under this Law.

  Other rehabilitation establishments under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10, amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12, which have been granted operation licenses before the effective date of this Law, shall continue operating until the expiry of their licenses.
