                                                        LAW

                                                LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  The Law on Legal Aid No. 11/2017/QH14 dated June 20, 2017 of the National Assembly, which takes effect from January 1, 2018, is amended and supplemented by:

  1. The Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking No. 53/2024/QH15 dated November 28, 2024 of the National Assembly, which comes into force from July 01, 2025;

  2. The Law on Juvenile Justice No. 59/2024/QH15 dated November 30, 2024 of the National Assembly, which comes into force from January 01, 2026;

  3. Law No. 05/2026/QH16 dated April 23, 2026 of the National Assembly amending and supplementing a number of articles of the Law on Legal Aid, which comes into force from January 01, 2027.

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;

  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Legal Aid [1].

  Chapter I

                                           GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for legally-aided persons; legal aid-providing organizations; legal aid-providing persons; legal aid services; responsibilities of agencies, organizations and individuals for legal aid services.

  Article 2. Legal aid

  Legal aid means the provision of free-of-charge legal services to legally-aided persons in legal aid-related cases according to this Law, contributing to ensuring human rights and civil rights in access to justice and equality before law.

  Article 3. Principles of legal aid services

  1. Complying with laws and professional rules of legal aid.

  2. Being timely, independent, honest and respecting objective truth.

  3. Protecting the lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons.

  4. Not collecting money, material benefits or other benefits from legal aid beneficiaries.

  Article 4. State policies on legal aid

  1. Legal aid is the responsibility of the State.

  2. The State shall adopt policies to ensure the right to receive legal aid in conformity with socio-economic conditions.

  3. The State shall adopt policies to improve the quality of legal aid and attract resources for providing legal aid.

  4. The State shall support, encourage, recognize and honor agencies, organizations and individuals participating in and contributing to legal aid activities.

  Article 5. Financial sources for legal aid

  1. Financial sources for legal aid include state budget; contributions and donations from domestic and foreign organizations and individuals and other lawful sources.

  2. Funding for legal aid services shall be allocated in annual state budget estimates of agencies performing state management of legal aid services in accordance with laws on state budget, public investment, science, technology and innovation, digital transformation, digital technology industry and relevant specialized laws.

  For local governments unable to balance their budgets, priority shall be given to allocating budget from annual budget balance to support the handling of complex and typical legal aid cases.

  3. Funding for legal aid services provided by voluntary organizations shall be covered by these organizations.

  Article 6. Prohibited acts in legal aid services

  1. Legal aid service providers and legal aid-providing persons are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon honor, dignity, legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons; discriminating against legally-aided persons;

  b) Receiving or demanding any amount of money, material benefits or other benefits from legally-aided persons; harassing legally-aided persons;

  c) Disclosing information about legal aid cases, legally-aided persons, unless they have given written consent or otherwise provided for by law;

  d) Refusing or discontinuing legal aid services, except for cases prescribed in this Law and procedural laws;

  dd) Abusing legal aid services to seek personal benefits, infringing upon national defense and security, causing social disorder and insecurity, and adversely affecting social ethics;

  e) Inciting or encouraging legally-aided persons to provide false information and documents, lodge complaints or denunciations, or initiate lawsuits in contravention of law.

  2. Legal aid beneficiaries, agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid services are prohibited from committing the following acts:

  a) Infringing upon health, life, honor and dignity of persons receiving legal aid services and reputation of legal aid-providing organizations;

  b) Deliberately providing false information and documents about legal aid-related cases;

  c) Illegally threatening, obstructing or intervening in legal aid services; causing disturbances, disorder and seriously breaching internal regulations of places where legal aid is provided.

  Chapter II

   LEGAL AID RECEIVERS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID RECEIVERS

  Article 7. Legal aid beneficiaries [3]

  1. People with meritorious services to the revolution; biological parents, spouses, children of martyrs and people with meritorious services to the care of martyrs.

  2. People in poor households, people in near-poor households.

  3. People infected with dioxin.

  4. People with disabilities.

  5. Children.

  6. Ethnic minority people living in areas with extremely disadvantaged socio-economic conditions; ethnic minority people with very small population.

  7. Monthly social allowance beneficiaries are the elderly, persons subjected to violence in family-related violence cases, and HIV-infected persons.

  8. Low-income workers are accused persons, crime victims.

  9. Persons with unexpected difficulties due to natural disasters or fires shall receive legal aid in cases directly related to the overcoming of consequences arising from such events.

  10. Persons aged between full 16 and under 18 years who are denounced, recommended for prosecution, held in emergency custody, accused, crime victims, witnesses, persons serving diversion measures, and inmates as prescribed by the Law on Juvenile Justice.

  11. Victims, persons undergoing victim identification for human trafficking acts, and accompanying persons under 18 years of age as prescribed by the Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking.

  12. Individuals suffering damage without hiring protection of lawful rights and interests as prescribed by the Law on State Compensation Liability.

  13. Foreigners under international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory who are entitled to legal aid.

  14. Other legally-aided persons as prescribed by law.

  Article 8. Rights of legally-aided persons

  1. To receive legal aid services free of charge.

  2. To request legal aid services by themselves or through their relatives, competent procedural authorities and persons, procedural authorities and persons [4] or other agencies, organizations and individuals.

  3. To be informed about their right to receive legal aid services, procedures for receiving legal aid services when they come to a legal aid-providing organization and relevant state agencies.

  4. To request confidentiality of contents of legal aid-related cases.

  5. To select a local legal aid-providing organization and legal aid-providing person from the published list; to request change of legal aid-providing person when such person falls into one of the cases specified in clause 1 and clause 2 Article 25 of this Law.

  6. To change or withdraw their requests for legal aid services.

  7. To receive compensation for damage as prescribed by law.

  8. Make complaints and denunciations about legal aid services in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 9. Obligations of legally-aided persons

  1. Provide documents proving their eligibility for legal aid.

  2. Cooperate and provide timely and adequate information, documents and evidence related to legal aid-related cases and take responsibility for the accuracy of such information, documents and evidence.

  3. Respect legal aid-providing organizations, persons and other agencies, organizations and individuals involved in legal aid-related cases.

  4. Do not request other legal aid-providing organizations to provide legal aid for the same case being handled by another legal aid-providing organization.

  5. Comply with the law on legal aid and internal rules of places where legal aid is provided.

  Chapter III

   LEGAL AID PROVIDING ORGANIZATIONS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID PROVIDING ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 10. Legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations include state legal aid centers and legal aid-participating organizations.

  2. Departments of Justice shall publish lists of local legal aid-providing organizations on their websites and send them to the Ministry of Justice for consolidation and posting on the National Law Portal[5].

  Article 11. State legal aid centers

  1. The state legal aid center is a public non-business unit under the Department of Justice, established by the provincial People’s Committee, has legal status, seal, head office and separate account.

  2. The state legal aid center may have branches.

  Branches are dependent units of the state legal aid center. The state legal aid center is responsible for all activities of its branches. Based on socio-economic conditions and legal aid needs in the province, the President of the provincial People’s Committee shall decide to establish branches of the state legal aid center.

  3. The Government shall provide regulations on organization and operation of state legal aid centers and their branches; procedures for establishment, dissolution and merger of state legal aid center’s branches.

  Article 12. Legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations include organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid and organizations registering to participate in legal aid.

  2. Organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations signing contracts for providing legal aid with Departments of Justice according to this Law.

  3. Organizations registering to participate in legal aid include law-practicing organizations and legal counseling organizations registering to participate in legal aid according to this Law.

  Article 13. Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing organizations

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid;

  b) Request relevant agencies and organizations to cooperate in providing information and documents on legal aid-related cases;

  c) Pay compensation for damage caused by their members during the provision of legal aid services;

  d) Comply with regulations on statistics, reporting, and communication regarding legal aid services;

  dd) Resolve complaints as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 45 of this Law;

  e) Propose to competent state authorities matters related to the resolution of legal aid-related cases.

  2. State legal aid centers have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Perform other tasks as authorized or requested by competent state management agencies in charge of legal aid services.

  3. Legal aid-providing organizations have the following rights and obligations:

  a/ Rights and obligations specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b/ Receiving remuneration and expenses for providing legal aid as prescribed by the Government;

  c/ Other rights and obligations under contracts on provision of legal aid.

  4. Organizations registering for participation in legal aid services have the following rights and obligations:

  a/ Rights and obligations specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b/ Providing legal aid according to registered contents.

  Article 14. Contracts on provision of legal aid

  1. Legal aid service contracts shall be signed between Departments of Justice and law-practicing organizations or legal counseling organizations and between state legal aid centers and lawyers or legal aid collaborators on providing legal aid services in accordance with civil laws.

  2. Based on legal aid needs and local conditions, Departments of Justice shall select and sign legal aid service contracts with organizations wishing to provide legal aid services and satisfying the following requirements:

  a) Having operation registration fields suitable for legal aid services as prescribed by this Law;

  b) Being law-practicing organizations or legal counseling organizations having at least 1 legal counselor with at least 2 years of experience in legal counseling or 1 lawyer working regularly at the organization;

  c) Having facilities suitable for legal aid services;

  d) Not being subject to decisions on penalties for administrative violations in law practice or legal counseling.

  3. State legal aid centers shall select and sign legal aid service contracts with lawyers satisfying the following requirements based on legal aid needs and resources for providing legal aid services in their localities:

  a) Not being subject to decisions on penalties for administrative violations in law practice;

  b) Not banned from practicing or restricted from professional activities under decisions of competent authorities;

  c) Not facing criminal prosecution;

  d) Having written consent of the law firm where the lawyer works or the agency/organization with which the lawyer enters into an employment contract.

  4. State legal aid centers shall select and enter into legal aid cooperation contracts with legal aid collaborators in accordance with Article 24 hereof.

  5. Organizations and individuals that commit prohibited acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 hereof are not allowed to select and enter into legal aid cooperation contracts for at least 2 years from the date on which violation conclusions are issued.

  6. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 15. Registration for participation in provision of legal aid

  1. Law firms and legal consultancy organizations that voluntarily provide legal aid by using their own resources may register for participation in provision of legal aid as follows:

  a) Law-practicing organizations that fully satisfy the conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law;

  b) Legal consultancy organizations that fully satisfy the conditions specified at Points a, c and d, Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law and have at least one legal consultant with two years or more of experience in providing legal consultancy or one full-time lawyer working for such organization.

  2. Law-practicing organizations and legal consultancy organizations providing legal aid shall register with the provincial-level Justice Department with which they are granted operation registration certificates their scope, forms, domains and beneficiaries of legal aid services.

  3. The Minister of Justice shall prescribe the order and procedures for registering participation in the provision of legal aid.

  Article 16. Termination of provision of legal aid by legal aid-participating organizations

  1. Organizations signing contracts to provide legal aid shall terminate the provision of legal aid when falling into one of the following cases:

  a/ Failing to meet one of the conditions for signing contracts to provide legal aid as prescribed in Clause 2, Article 14 of this Law;

  b/ Terminating under contracts on provision of legal aid;

  c) Committing serious violations of legal aid services;

  d) Shall terminate its operation in accordance with law.

  2. The organization registering for participation in legal aid services shall terminate the provision of legal aid services when falling into one of the following cases:

  a) No longer satisfy one of the conditions for registration to participate in legal aid services specified in Clause 1, Article 15 of this Law;

  b/ To terminate their participation in legal aid services after having made written notification thereon to the provincial-level Justice Department with which they have registered for participation in legal aid services;

  c) Failing to provide legal aid for 2 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  d) Committing serious violations of legal aid services;

  dd) Shall terminate its operation in accordance with law.

  3. When terminating legal aid services, legal aid-participating organizations shall notify in writing the Department of Justice of such termination and transfer the unfinished cases to the legal aid-providing organization assigned by the Department of Justice for further provision.

  Chapter IV

      LEGAL AID-PROVIDING PERSONS, RIGHTS AND OBLIGATIONS OF LEGAL AID-PROVIDING PERSONS

  Article 17. Legal aid-providing persons

  1. Legal aid-providing persons include:

  a/ Legal aid professionals;

  b/ Lawyers providing legal aid under contracts with state legal aid centers or as assigned by legal aid-participating organizations;

  c/ Legal counseling officers who have at least two years of experience in legal counseling and work at legal aid-participating organizations;

  d) Legal aid collaborators.

  2. The Department of Justice shall announce the list of legal aid-providing persons in their province or city on its website and send it to the Ministry of Justice for compilation and posting on the National Law Portal.

  Article 18.- Rights and obligations of legal aid-providing persons

  1. Legal aid-providing persons have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Provide legal aid;

  b) Have their right to independent legal aid guaranteed and not threatened, obstructed, harassed or illegally interfered upon;

  c) Refuse or discontinue legal aid services in cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 25, Clause 1 Article 37 of this Law and in accordance with procedural laws;

  d) To be provided with training in legal aid professional skills and operations;

  d) Ensure quality of legal aid cases;

  e) Comply with principles of legal aid services;

  g) Strictly comply with internal regulations of places where legal aid is provided;

  h) Provide compensation or refund to legal aid-providing organizations for amounts paid by them to the aggrieved persons due to their faults when providing legal aid as prescribed by law.

  2. Legal aid professionals have the following rights and obligations:

  a) Rights and obligations specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Participate in training courses on improvement of knowledge and skills about legal aid;

  c) Perform other tasks assigned;

  d) Enjoy benefits and policies as prescribed.

  3. Lawyers and legal aid collaborators who sign contracts for providing legal aid services with state legal aid centers shall be entitled to remuneration and expenses for providing legal aid services as prescribed.

  4. The Government shall elaborate Point d Clause 2 and Clause 3 of this Article.

  Article 19. Legal aid professional standards [10]

  Vietnamese citizens who are officials of the State Legal Aid Center and meet the following criteria may become legal aid professionals:

  1. Possessing good moral qualities;

  2. Possess a bachelor of laws degree or higher;

  3. Having received lawyer training or being exempted from lawyer training;

  4. Having completed the period of law practice probation or legal aid practice probation, except for those who have worked as legal aid professionals;

  5. Being physically fit to perform legal aid.

  Article 20. Legal aid practice probation

  1. Officials of State legal aid centers who have obtained certificates of graduation from lawyer training courses or are exempted from lawyer training courses under the Lawyer Law may undergo legal aid practice probation at State legal aid centers.

  The period of legal aid practice probation is 12 months. State legal aid centers shall assign legal aid professionals to guide probationers and certify their probation. Legal aid professionals guiding probationers must have at least 03 years of experience in providing legal aid services. At any given time, a legal aid professional may not guide more than 02 probationers.

  2. Probationers may be assisted by legal aid professionals in their professional activities but may not represent, plead or protect the lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons in court sessions; and may not sign legal counseling documents.

  Probationers may meet with legally-aided persons and other involved parties in legal aid cases with the consent of such persons together with legal aid professionals; assist legal aid professionals in studying case files, collecting documents, items and circumstances related to the cases and other professional activities. Legal aid professionals guiding probationers shall supervise and take responsibility for the activities of probationers specified in this Clause.

  3. Persons who are exempted from or have their probation period of law practice reduced under the Lawyer Law shall also be exempted from or have their probation period of legal aid practice reduced.

  4. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate on legal aid apprenticeship and examination results and the form of certificate of legal aid apprenticeship examination results.

  Article 21. Appointment, issuance and reissuance of legal aid assistant’s cards [11]

  1. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider appointing and issuing legal aid assistant’s cards to persons who meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law but do not fall into one of the following cases:

  a) They are under disciplinary actions; they are subject to administrative actions and sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers or compulsory education institutions;

  b) They are facing criminal prosecution; they have been convicted of intentional serious crimes, intentional very serious crimes, intentional extremely serious crimes, including cases where their criminal records have been expunged; they have been convicted of unintentional very serious crimes, unintentional extremely serious crimes, or unintentional less serious crimes.

  2. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall consider appointing and issuing legal aid assistant’s cards to persons who have been dismissed or have their legal aid assistant’s cards revoked in the cases specified in Points a, dd, or g Clause 1 Article 22 of this Law when they meet the criteria for legal aid assistants prescribed in Article 19 of this Law and the reasons for dismissal or revocation of cards no longer exist.

  3. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall reissue legal aid assistant’s cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  4. The Government shall elaborate on this Article; procedures for appointment, issuance and reissuance of legal aid assistant’s cards.

  Article 22. Removal and revocation of legal aid professional’s card [12]

  1. A legal aid professional shall be removed and revoked his/her card in one of the following cases:

  a) He/she no longer satisfies the criteria for legal aid professionals prescribed in Article 19 of this Law;

  b) He/she fails to participate in legal proceedings for 12 consecutive months, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  c) He/she is disciplined in the form of dismissal;

  d) He/she is disciplined in the form of warning or more severe forms due to committing acts prescribed in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law;

  dd) He/she is banned from providing legal aid services under decisions of competent authorities;

  e) He/she has been convicted and the judgment has taken legal effect;

  g) To be subject to the administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or a compulsory education institution.

  2. Legal aid professionals shall be automatically dismissed and their cards shall become invalid in the following cases:

  a) Transfer to another job; dismissal or discharge;

  b) Retirement.

  3. Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees shall decide to dismiss or revoke legal aid assistant’s cards.

  4. The Government shall elaborate this Article and procedures for dismissal and revocation of legal aid assistant’s card.

  Article 23.[13] (annulled)

  Article 24. Legal aid collaborators [14]

  1. The following persons may become legal aid collaborators:

   a) Persons who have retired or resigned voluntarily or resigned due to objective reasons, have full civil act capacity, good moral qualities, good health and wish to provide legal aid, including those who used to be legal aid workers, judges, court inspectors, procurators, procuracy inspectors, investigators, enforcers, civil judgment enforcement inspectors, holders of senior official specialist ranks and equivalent or higher in the legal sector; persons who hold senior official specialist ranks and equivalent and have worked in the legal sector for 5 years or more at agencies of the Communist Party of Vietnam, the State, the Vietnamese Fatherland Front and its member organizations; and persons who used to be legal aid collaborators;

  b) Officials holding rank III and equivalent who have worked in the legal sector for 5 years or more, or officials holding rank I, II and equivalent who have worked in the legal sector and have obtained written consent from their working agencies.

  2. Directors of provincial-level Justice Departments shall consider and grant legal aid collaborator cards to persons who fully meet the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article and do not fall into one of the following cases:

  a) They are being examined for penal liability; they have been convicted of an intentional serious crime, an intentional very serious crime or an intentionally very serious crime, even if they have had their criminal records expunged; they have been convicted but have not yet had their criminal records expunged for an unintentional very serious crime, an intentionally very serious crime or an unintentional serious crime;

  b) They are being subject to an administrative handling measure of being sent to a compulsory detoxification center or a compulsory education institution; or they are being subject to disciplinary handling.

  3. Directors of State Legal Aid Centers shall enter into contracts on provision of legal aid with persons granted legal aid collaborator cards to provide legal counseling and extrajudicial representation in localities.

  4. Legal aid collaborators shall have their collaborator cards revoked when falling into one of the following cases:

  a) Failing to meet the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) Failing to provide legal aid services for 2 consecutive years, except for cases due to objective reasons;

  c) Committing violations prescribed in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law or having been administratively sanctioned for violations against regulations on legal aid but not yet subject to revocation of legal aid collaborator’s card and continuing to commit violations;

  d) The contract for provision of legal aid services is terminated or the legal aid collaborator fails to enter into a contract with the State legal aid center within 30 days from the date of receipt of the request for contract signing without a legitimate reason;

  dd) The legal aid collaborator is an official who has been disciplined by dismissal;

  e) Subject to administrative handling measures such as being sent to compulsory detoxification centers or compulsory education establishments;

  g) Having been convicted and the judgment has taken legal effect.

  5. Directors of Departments of Justice shall decide to revoke legal aid collaborator’s cards; re-grant legal aid collaborator’s cards in case they are lost or damaged.

  6. The Government shall elaborate this Article; procedures for issuance, reissuance and revocation of legal aid collaborator’s cards.

  Article 25. Cases where legal aid is not allowed to continue or refused

  1. Legal aid-providing persons are not allowed to continue providing legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Committing prohibited acts specified in Clause 1 Article 6 of this Law, unless they have completely served the penalties for violations and are allowed to provide legal aid as prescribed by this Law;

  b) Being revoked legal aid worker’s card, legal aid collaborator’s card, lawyer practicing certificate, legal counselor’s card;

  c) Cases where they are not allowed to participate in proceedings according to regulations of law on proceedings.

  2. Legal aid-providing persons must refuse to provide legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Providing legal aid for legally-aided persons who are parties with opposing interests in the same case, unless otherwise agreed upon by the parties in the case of legal counseling or extrajudicial representation in civil matters;

  b) There are grounds to believe that the legal aid-providing person may not be objective in providing legal aid;

  c) There are reasons to believe that legal aid services cannot be provided effectively and affect the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons.

  3. The legal aid-providing organization shall notify in writing and clearly state the reason to the legal aid recipient and appoint another person to provide legal aid in the cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article.

  Chapter V

                JURISDICTION, SECTORS, FORMS AND ACTIVITIES OF LEGAL ASSISTANCE

  Article 26. Scope of legal aid services

  1. State legal aid centers provide legal aid in one of the following cases:

  a) Legal aid beneficiaries who are residing in their localities;

  b) Legal aid cases occurring in local areas;

  c) Legal aid cases requested by competent central authorities in charge of legal aid;

  d) [15] Cases transferred as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 35 hereof.

  2. Organizations signing contracts for provision of legal aid shall provide legal aid within the scope of the contract.

  3. Organizations registering to participate in legal aid shall provide legal aid within the scope of their registration.

  Article 27. Fields and forms of legal aid

  1. Legal aid shall be provided in fields of law, except for business and trade.

  2. Forms of legal aid include:

  a) Engage in legal proceedings;

  b) Provide legal counseling;

  c) Represent outside of legal proceedings.

  Article 28. Venues for receiving legally-aided persons

  1. Legal aid-providing organizations shall arrange venues for receiving legally-aided persons at their head offices or other locations outside of head offices to ensure that presentation of requests is easy and convenient.

  2. At head offices of legal aid-providing organizations, schedule and internal regulations on reception of legally-aided persons must be posted up.

  Article 29.[16] (annulled)

  Article 30. Request and acceptance of legal aid-related cases

  1. When requesting legal aid, the requester shall submit an application to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Legal aid requests shall be accepted only when there is a specific case directly related to the lawful rights and interests of legally-aided persons and consistent with this Law.

  3. The legal aid-providing organization must refuse to accept the request and clearly state the reason in writing to the requester in one of the following cases:

  a) The legal aid request fails to meet the requirements prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article;

  b) The legal aid request contains unlawful contents;

  c) The legally-aided person has died;

  d) The case is being accepted or handled by another legal aid-providing organization;

  dd) The legal aid request has been resolved and no new content arises.

  4. Where the requesting person is unable to provide a complete application but requires immediate legal aid due to the fact that the case is about to expire, or is approaching the trial date, or the case has been transferred by the competent procedural authority or procedural-conducting agency to the legal aid-providing organization, or to avoid causing damage to the legitimate rights and interests of the legally-aided person, the receiving person shall report to the head of the legal aid-providing organization for immediate acceptance and guidance the requesting person to supplement necessary documents.

  5. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; and prescribe the procedures for accepting, immediately accepting, or rejecting acceptance of legal aid-related cases.

  Article 31. Participation in legal proceedings

  1. Legal aid assistants and lawyers providing legal aid shall participate in legal proceedings in the capacity as defense counsels or protectors of the legitimate rights and interests of legally-aided persons in accordance with this Law and the law on legal proceedings.

  2. When the legally-aided person requests the assignment of a legal aid-providing person to participate in legal proceedings, within 03 working days from the date of acceptance of the case, the legal aid-providing organization shall assign a legal aid-providing person.

  In case the legally-aided person is an arrestee or a person held in temporary custody who requests the assignment of a legal aid-providing person, within 12 hours from the time of acceptance, the legal aid-providing organization shall assign a legal aid-providing person.

  3. Within 12 hours from the time of receipt of the legal aid request from the arrestee or the person held in temporary custody, or within 24 hours from the time of receipt of the legal aid request from the defendant, the victim being the legally-aided person in accordance with the law on legal proceedings, the competent procedural-conducting agency or person shall notify the local state legal aid center. Immediately after receiving the notice from the competent procedural-conducting agency or person, the state legal aid center shall accept the case in accordance with Clause 4, Article 30 of this Law and assign a legal aid-providing person to participate in legal proceedings.

  4. The appointment of participants in legal proceedings must be made in writing and sent to legal aid beneficiaries and relevant procedural-conducting authorities.

  Article 32. Legal counseling [18]

  1. Legal aid-providing persons shall provide legal counseling for legal aid beneficiaries by guiding, giving opinions, assisting in drafting documents related to disputes, complaints or legal difficulties; guiding parties to reach agreement on settlement directions.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; procedures for legal counseling.

  Article 33. Extrajudicial representation [19]

  1. Legal aid assistants, lawyers providing legal aid, legal aid collaborators shall act as extrajudicial representatives before competent state authorities for legal aid beneficiaries.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article; procedures for extrajudicial representation.

  Article 34. Cooperation in verifying legal aid cases [20]

  1. Where details and events related to legal aid cases in other provinces are required to be verified, the legal aid-providing organization that has accepted the case shall request the legal aid-providing organization in the province where verification is required to cooperate in conducting the verification. The verification request must be made in writing, clearly stating the contents to be verified and the time limit for response.

  2. The Minister of Justice shall elaborate this Article and procedures for cooperation in verifying legal aid cases.

  Article 35. Transfer of requests and legal aid cases [21]

  1. Where a legal aid request does not meet the conditions specified in Points a and b Clause 1 Article 26 of this Law, the State legal aid center shall transfer the request to the competent state legal aid center and notify the requester.

  2. Where resources for providing legal aid are insufficient, the legal aid-providing organization shall transfer the request to the local state legal aid center and notify the requester.

  3. [22] The state legal aid center that is handling a legal aid case may transfer it to another state legal aid center in another province when the legal aid case has been transferred to the competent procedural authority or local procedural authority for settlement and notify the legal aid recipient.

  Article 36. Recommendations in legal aid activities [23]

  1. During the provision of legal aid, the legal aid-providing organization shall make written recommendations to the competent authority on matters related to the resolution of legal aid cases. The receiving authority shall, within its jurisdiction, respond in writing as prescribed by law.

  2. In case the receiving authority does not respond as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article, the legal aid-providing organization may request the immediate superior authority of such authority to consider and resolve it.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article; and prescribe the procedures for requesting in legal aid activities.

  Article 37. Termination of legal aid-related cases

  1. A legal aid-related case shall be terminated when:

  a) It is necessary to refuse as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 30 of this Law;

  b) The legal aid recipient commits one of the prohibited acts prescribed in Clause 2 Article 6 of this Law;

  c) The legal aid recipient withdraws his/her request for legal aid.

  2. In case of termination of legal aid-related cases, the legal aid-providing organization or the legal aid-providing person must send a written notice clearly stating the reasons to the legal aid recipient.

  3. Where legal aid is being provided but the legal aid recipient no longer meets the criteria specified in Article 7 of this Law, the case shall continue to be processed until completion.

  Article 38. Legal aid case files [24]

  When providing legal aid, legal aid-providing organizations and persons shall prepare legal aid case files in accordance with regulations of the Minister of Justice.

  Article 39. Storage of legal aid case files

  1. Within 30 days from the date of completion of a case, the legal aid-providing person must transfer the legal aid case file to the legal aid-providing organization.

  2. Documents in the legal aid case file must be listed, numbered, arranged by date, and stored in accordance with the law.

  3. The electronic file of each legal aid case is digitized and updated in the system for managing legal aid cases and stored in the database on legal aid.

  Chapter VI

          RESPONSIBILITIES OF AGENCIES AND ORGANIZATIONS FOR LEGAL AID PROVISION

  Article 40. Responsibilities for state management of legal aid provision

  1. The Government shall perform the unified state management of legal aid provision.

  2. The Ministry of Justice shall act as the focal point assisting the Government in performing the state management of legal aid provision and have the following tasks and powers:

  a) Take charge of developing, promulgating or requesting competent authorities to promulgate legislative documents on legal aid;

  b) Develop, promulgate or request competent authorities to promulgate strategies and plans for development of legal aid and organize implementation thereof;

  c) [25] Promulgate regulations on professional guidelines, assessment of quality and effectiveness of legal aid cases; forms of documents used in legal aid activities; criteria for determining complex, typical, high-quality and successful legal aid cases; implement reporting and statistical regulations on legal aid;

  d) Guide, organize and monitor the implementation of legislative documents on legal aid;

  dd) Organize training, advanced training, and professional skills training for persons providing legal aid services;

  e) Organize communication on legal aid[26];

  g) [27] Inspect the provision of legal aid services; commend, discipline, and handle violations in legal aid services;

  h) Receive support and contributions from organizations and individuals for legal aid services;

  i) Conduct international cooperation on legal aid;

  k)[28] Apply information technology and digital technology and implement digital transformation in legal aid services; develop, manage, operate, and regularly upgrade the legal aid information system and database; connect, integrate, and share legal aid data on the National Law Portal.

  3.[29] Ministries and ministerial agencies shall, within their tasks and powers, cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in state management and organization of legal aid services; create, integrate, share, extract, and use data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  4.[30] Provincial People’s Committees shall, within their tasks and powers, perform state management of legal aid services in their provinces, ensuring working conditions of state legal aid centers; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in creating, integrating, sharing, extracting, and using data through connection and interconnection with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  5.[31] The People’s Committee of commune shall cooperate with the State Legal Aid Center in providing legal aid services in their area.

  6.[32] The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 41. Responsibilities of agencies involved in legal aid services in legal proceedings [33]

  1. Within their tasks and powers, competent procedural authorities and procedural authorities shall cooperate and organize inter-sectoral cooperation in legal aid services at People's Courts and criminal investigation; enable legally-aided persons to enjoy the right to legal aid, enable legal aid-providing persons to participate in legal proceedings as prescribed by law.

  2. The Minister of Justice, Chief Justice of the Supreme People’s Court, Chief Procurator of the Supreme People’s Procuracy, Minister of National Defense, Minister of Public Security, and Minister of Finance shall cooperate in developing inter-sectoral cooperation councils on legal aid services in legal proceedings at central and provincial levels.

  3. Agencies involved in legal aid services in legal proceedings shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in establishing, integrating, sharing, extracting, and using data through connection and interoperability with the legal aid database on the National Law Portal.

  Article 42. Responsibilities of the Vietnam Fatherland Front, its member organizations, and relevant state agencies [34]

  1. The Vietnam Fatherland Front and its member organizations shall, within their functions and tasks, supervise the implementation of policies and laws on legal aid; cooperate with state agencies and relevant organizations in legal aid services as prescribed by law.

  2. During the process of handling and resolving cases related to citizens, if the citizen is a legal aid beneficiary, the state agency shall explain the right to receive legal aid and introduce him/her to a legal aid-providing organization.

  Article 43. Responsibilities of socio-professional organizations of lawyers

  1. The Vietnam Bar Federation shall cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers in accordance with this Law.

  2. Bar associations of provinces and centrally-affiliated cities shall cooperate with provincial-level Justice Departments in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by law-practicing organizations and lawyers in accordance with this Law.

  Article 44. Responsibilities of managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations Managing organizations of legal consultancy organizations shall cooperate with competent state management agencies in charge of legal aid in managing and supervising the provision of legal aid by legal consultancy organizations and legal consultants in accordance with this Law.

  Chapter VII

                            RESOLUTION OF COMPLAINTS, DENUNCIATIONS AND DISPUTES

  Article 45. Resolution of complaints and denunciations

  1. Legal aid beneficiaries have the right to lodge complaints against the following acts of legal aid-providing organizations or legal aid-providing persons when having grounds to believe that such acts are illegal and infringe upon their legitimate rights and interests:

  a) Refusing to accept legal aid-related cases;

  b) Failing to provide legal aid;

  c) Providing legal aid in contravention of law;

  d) Changing legal aid-providing persons in contravention of law.

  2. [35] Heads of legal aid-providing organizations shall resolve complaints against the acts specified in Clause 1 of this Article. If the complainant disagrees with the complaint resolution decision of the head of the legal aid-providing organization or the complaint is not resolved within the prescribed time limit, he/she may further lodge a complaint with the director of the Department of Justice.

  If the complainant disagrees with the complaint resolution decision of the director of the Department of Justice or the complaint is not resolved within the prescribed time limit, he/she may initiate a lawsuit at court.

  The Government shall elaborate this clause; and procedures for resolving complaints in legal aid.

  3. [36] Complaints and lawsuits in legal aid not specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall comply with regulations of law on complaints and other relevant laws.

  4. Individuals have the right to lodge complaints with competent authorities about violations of this Law. Complaints and settlement thereof shall comply with regulations of law on complaints.

  Article 46. Dispute settlement

  1. In case of a dispute between legal aid beneficiaries and legal aid professionals, legal counselors, legal aid collaborators, state legal aid centers or legal counseling organizations related to the provision of legal aid services, such disputes shall be resolved in accordance with civil laws.

  2. In case of a dispute between legal aid beneficiaries and lawyers or law practice organizations related to the provision of legal aid, the dispute shall be resolved in accordance with the law on lawyers and other relevant laws.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION CLAUSES [37]

  Article 47. Effect

  1. This Law comes into force as of January 01, 2018.

  2. The Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 ceases to be effective from the effective date of this Law.

  Article 48. Transitional provisions

  1. From the effective date of this Law, persons who have been appointed as legal aid professionals under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 may continue their operation in accordance with this Law; after 05 years from the effective date of this Law, if persons who have been appointed as legal aid professionals do not possess certificates of lawyer training, their legal aid professional’s cards shall be revoked.

  2. Within 01 year from the effective date of this Law, organizations and individuals providing legal aid services under the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 must meet requirements set forth in this Law; if they fail to meet these requirements, they shall terminate their provision of legal aid services and transfer their ongoing cases to state legal aid centers where they are registered for participation in order to continue providing legal aid services.

  3. Legal aid cases which are being provided in accordance with the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 but have not yet been completed by the effective date of this Law shall continue to be provided in accordance with the Law on Legal Aid No. 69/2006/QH11 until completion of such cases.

  4. Within 01 year from the effective date of this Law, provincial People’s Committees shall review their branches of state legal aid centers established in their provinces and, based on legal aid needs, legal aid resources and efficiency of operations, report to the Ministry of Justice for agreement on the maintenance, merger or dissolution of branches of state legal aid centers.
