                                            CIRCULAR
 GUIDING THE IMPLEMENTATION OF THE GOVERNMENT’S DECREE NO. 103/2006/ND-CP OF SEPTEMBER 22, 2006, DETAILING AND GUIDING A NUMBER OF ARTICLES OF THE LAW ON INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY
Pursuant to the Intellectual Property Law dated November 29, 2005;
Pursuant to the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP dated September 22, 2006 detailing and guiding a number of articles of the Intellectual Property Law on industrial property;
Pursuant to the Government’s Decree No. 54/2003/ND-CP dated May 19, 2003 defining the functions, tasks, powers and organizational structure of the Ministry of Science and Technology and the Government’s Decree No. 28/2004/ND-CP dated January 16, 2004 amending and supplementing a number of articles of Decree No. 54/2003/ND-CP;
The Ministry of Science and Technology shall guide the implementation of the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP dated September 22, 2006 as follows.
Chapter I
                 PROCEDURES FOR ESTABLISHMENT OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
Section 1. GENERAL REGULATIONS ON PROCEDURES FOR ESTABLISHMENT OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
1. Basis for establishment of industrial property rights
1.1 Industrial property rights arising or established on the basis of the grounds specified in Clause 3, Article 6 of the Intellectual Property Law dated November 29, 2005 (hereinafter referred to as the "Intellectual Property Law"), Clauses 1, 2, 3 and 4, Article 6 of Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP dated September 22, 2006 of the Government detailing and guiding a number of articles of the Intellectual Property Law on industrial property (hereinafter referred to as the "Decree on Industrial Property") and according to specific provisions at this Point.
1.2 Industrial property rights to inventions, layout designs of semi-conducting closed circuits (hereinafter referred to as layout designs), industrial designs and marks shall be established on the basis of decisions of the National Office of Intellectual Property on granting protection titles to registrants of such objects. Persons who are granted protection titles by the National Office of Intellectual Property are owners and enjoy rights to industrial property objects within the scope of protection stated in the protection titles and during the validity duration of the protection titles. When disputes arise, owners of industrial property objects may use protection titles as bases for proving their rights without any other evidence.
1.3 Industrial property rights to geographical indications shall be established on the basis of decisions of the National Office of Intellectual Property on granting certificates of registration of geographical indications to organizations managing geographical indications.

1.4 Industrial property rights to internationally registered marks under the Madrid Agreement and Protocol (hereinafter referred to as internationally registered marks) shall be established on the basis of decisions on acceptance of protection or certificates of protection of internationally registered marks in Vietnam issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property at the request of mark owners. The above-mentioned decisions and certificates have the same value as protection titles granted to mark registrants in Vietnam.
1.5 Industrial property rights to well-known marks established on the basis of extensive practical use which make such marks well known shall not require registration procedures at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. When exercising and settling disputes over rights to well-known marks, owners of such marks must prove their rights with evidence in accordance with Article 75 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
1.6 Industrial property rights to trade names established on the basis of lawful use of such trade names without requiring registration procedures at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. When exercising and settling disputes over rights to trade names, entities having such trade names must prove their rights with evidence showing time, territory and field in which such trade names have been used.
1.7 Industrial property rights to business secrets established on the basis of financial or intellectual investment activities or results of other lawful activities to discover, create or obtain information forming a business secret and to keep such information confidential without requiring registration procedures at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. When exercising and settling disputes over rights to business secrets, entities having such business secrets must prove their rights with evidence showing activities in which information forming the business secrets is created, discovered, obtained and measures to keep such information confidential.
1.8 Right to oppose unfair competition established on the basis of practical competition activities without requiring registration procedures at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. When exercising the right to oppose unfair competition, entities must prove their rights with evidence showing subjects, fields, territories and time of business related to competition activities.
2. Industrial property registration applicants
2.1 Applicant for industrial property registration (hereinafter referred to as applicant) means an organization or individual filing an application for registration of an invention, layout design, industrial design, mark or geographical indication. When a title of protection of an invention, layout design, industrial design, mark or geographical indication is granted, the applicant shall be recognized as the owner of such title. When a title of protection of a geographical indication is granted, the applicant shall be recognized as the registrant of such geographical indication.
2.2 The applicant must satisfy the conditions on the right to register industrial property specified in Articles 86, 87 and 88 of the Law on Intellectual Property and Articles 7, 8 and 9 of the Decree on Industrial Property. If such conditions are not satisfied, the industrial property registration shall be considered invalid.
3. Representatives of applicants
3.1 The applicant may, by himself/herself or through a lawful representative in Vietnam, carry out procedures for registration of industrial property at the Department
Intellectual property as prescribed in this Point and Point 4 of this Circular.
3.2 The following organizations and individuals may represent the applicant:
a) For organizations and individuals specified in Clause 1 Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property:
(i) In case the applicant is an individual: the legal representative or authorized representative of the applicant, or the industrial property representation service provider authorized by the applicant;
(ii) In case the applicant is an organization: the legal representative of the applicant or a person authorized by the legal representative of the applicant; an industrial property representation service organization (authorized by the applicant); the head of a representative office or a branch in Vietnam (if the applicant is a foreign organization).
b) For organizations and individuals specified in Clause 2 Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property: industrial property representation service organizations (authorized by the applicants).
3.3 When carrying out procedures for industrial property registration, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may only conduct transactions with the applicant or the lawful representative of the applicant. Organizations and individuals not falling into the cases specified in Point 3.2 of this Circular that act as representatives of the applicant shall be considered as unlawful representatives.
4. Authorization for representation to carry out procedures for industrial property registration 4.1 The authorization for representation and the performance of authorization for representation to carry out procedures for industrial property registration (hereinafter referred to as "authorization") must comply with the provisions of law on authorization in Part Three of the Civil Code and the provisions of this Circular.
4.2 The authorization must be expressed in writing (power of attorney) and must contain the following main contents:
a) Name (full name), full address of the authorizing party and the authorized party;
b) Name (full name), full address of the authorized party or the re-authorized party (if any);
c) Scope of authorization, authorized workload;
d) The authorization period (an indefinite letter of authorization shall cease to be effective when the authorizing party declares termination of authorization);
e) Date of signing of the letter of authorization;
g) Signature (specifying full name, position and seal, if any) of the lawful representative of the authorizing party (and of the party receiving substituted authorization or re-authorization, if any).
4.3 The time at which a letter of authorization becomes legally valid in transactions with the National Office of Intellectual Property is determined as follows:
a) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a valid letter of authorization;
b) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property accepts valid substituted authorization or re-authorization;

c) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a notice of change to the scope of authorization, premature termination of authorization, or change to the address of the authorized party.
4.4 In case of substituted authorization or re-authorization, the letter of authorization shall only be considered valid if the party receiving substituted authorization or re-authorization commits to take responsibility for all issues arising from the authorizing party's previous transaction with the National Office of Intellectual Property.

4.5 If the letter of authorization covers multiple procedures independent of each other and the original copy of the letter of authorization has been submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property, when carrying out subsequent procedures, the authorized party must submit copies of the letter of authorization and provide accurate instructions on the number of the application containing the original copy of such letter of authorization.
5. Responsibilities of the applicant and representative of the applicant
5.1 The applicant and representative of the applicant shall ensure the truthfulness of information and documents provided to the National Office of Industrial Property during the process of industrial property registration in accordance with the following regulations:
a/ All transaction documents must be certified by the applicant or his/her representative or organization’s certification seal (if any). In case it is necessary to have notarization or certification by another competent agency, such certification must comply with relevant regulations;
b/ All Vietnamese translations of foreign-language documents must be certified by the applicant or his/her representative that they are made verbatim from the original documents.
5.2 The applicant shall take responsibility for all consequences and obligations arising from transactions with the National Office of Industrial Property conducted by his/her representative.
5.3 The representative of the applicant shall take responsibility before the applicant for all consequences caused by untruthful declaration and provision of information in transactions with the National Office of Industrial Property. If causing damage, he/she shall pay compensation.
5.4 Below, except for special provisions, the applicant and representative of the applicant shall be collectively referred to as “applicant”.
6. Handling opinions of third parties prior to issuance of decisions on grant of protection titles

6.1 From the date an industrial property registration application is published in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property up until before the date of issuance of a decision on grant of a protection title, any organization or individual may express opinions in writing to the National Office of Industrial Property on the registration right, priority right, protection conditions and other matters related to the industrial property registration application according to Article 112 of the Law on Intellectual Property. Written opinions of third parties shall be regarded as a source of information for the processing of industrial property registration applications.
6.2 Within 1 month from the date of receipt of a written opinion from a third party, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify such opinion to the applicant and impose a maximum time limit of 1 month from the notification date for the applicant to provide a written response. After receiving the feedback from the applicant, if necessary, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify such feedback to the third party and impose a maximum time limit of 1 month from the notification date for the third party to provide a written response. The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall process the feedback from the applicant and the third party based on evidence, arguments provided by the parties and documents included in the application.
6.3 Where the opinion of the third party is deemed groundless, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam is not required to notify the applicant of such opinion but must notify the third party of the refusal to consider the opinion, clearly stating the reasons.
6.4 Where the opinion of the third party relates to registration rights, if it is deemed impossible to determine whether the opinion of the third party is well-grounded, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall request the third party to file a lawsuit for resolution. If within 1 month from the date of notification, the third party fails to notify the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam of the filing of a lawsuit, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall consider the opinion of the third party as withdrawn. If the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam receives such notification within the aforementioned time limit, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall suspend the processing of the application pending the dispute resolution result of the Court. After receiving the dispute resolution result of the Court, the application processing shall proceed accordingly.
6.5 The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall organize direct dialogue between the third party and the applicant to clarify issues subject to the objection if necessary and at the request of both parties.
6.6 The time limit for the applicant to respond to the objection of the third party shall not be included in the time limit for the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to carry out relevant procedures as prescribed.
7. General requirements for applications for registration of industrial property
7.1 Minimum documents

The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall accept an application for registration of industrial property (hereinafter referred to as "application") if it contains at least the documents specified in Points a, b and e Clause 1 Article 100, Clause 1 Article 108 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following specific regulations:
a) For applications for registration of inventions, layout designs, industrial designs, marks and geographical indications, the following mandatory documents are required for acceptance of the application:
(i) Declaration of registration;
(ii) Documents, samples and information showing the subject matter of industrial property to be registered; Specifically: for an invention registration application, the invention description; for an industrial design registration application, a set of photos, a set of drawings and a description of the industrial design; for a mark registration application, the mark samples and the list of goods and services bearing the mark; for a geographical indication registration application, a description of the particular characteristics and quality of products bearing the geographical indication and a map of the geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication;
(iii) Receipt of payment of fees and charges.
If any of the above documents is missing, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may refuse to accept the application.
b) In addition to the documents specified in Point 7.1.a above, an application for registration of collective marks or certification marks must also contain the following documents:
(i) Regulations on use of the collective mark/certification mark;
(ii) Presentation of the characteristics and specific quality of products bearing the mark (if the mark is registered as a collective mark used for products with specific characteristics or as a certification mark of product quality or as a certification mark of geographical origin);

(iii) Map of territory (if the mark is registered as a certification mark of geographical origin of products).
7.2 Requirements on applications
a) Applications shall satisfy the general requirements prescribed in Articles 100 and 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the special requirements applicable to each type of industrial property subject matter prescribed in Articles 102, 103, 104, 105 and 106 of the Law on Intellectual Property, which are guided in detail in Points 23, 28, 33, 37 and 43 of this Circular.
b) To ensure technical requirements during processing, applications must also satisfy the following formal requirements:
(i) Each application may request the grant of only one protection title and the type of protection title requested must be appropriate to the industrial property subject matter stated in the application;
(ii) All documents of the application must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents that may be made in other languages according to the provisions of Points 7.3 and 7.4 of this Circular;
(iii) All documents of the application shall be presented horizontally (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented vertically) on one sheet of paper of A4 size (210mm x 297mm), with margins of 20mm on each side, except for supplementary documents whose origin is not intended to be included in the application;
(iv) For documents required to be made according to a set form, such forms must be used and all information as requested shall be filled in at appropriate places;
(v) If a document contains multiple pages, each page must bear the ordinal number in Arabic numerals;
(vi) Documents must be typed or printed with an ink type that is difficult to fade, clear and clean, and cannot be erased or modified; if minor errors involving spelling mistakes are found in documents submitted to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, the applicant may correct these errors, but the correction must be certified by the applicant’s signature (and stamped, if applicable);
(vii) Terms used in the application must be common terms (not local languages, rare words or self-created terms). Signs, measurement units, electronic fonts and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese standards;
(viii) The application may be enclosed with supplementary documents being objects carrying electronic data of part or all of the contents of the application.
c) The application must fully satisfy requirements regarding the quantity of documents, samples, drawings, photos and specific requirements applicable to each type of application prescribed in this Circular.

d) The declaration and other documents of the application must ensure that they contain all mandatory and consistent information; the Vietnamese translation of documents of the application must be consistent with the original; the power of attorney must cover the tasks within the scope of authorization.
e) The subjects stated in the application must be accurately grouped and classified as prescribed.
g/ For documents requiring certification by competent agencies (the first receiving agency, notary public, People's Committee, etc.), there must be their seals for certification. 7.3 The following documents may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese:
a) Power of attorney;
b) Documents certifying registration rights if the applicant inherits registration rights from another person (certificate of inheritance; certificate or agreement on transfer of the right to file an application, including transfer of an already filed application; assignment contract or labor contract, etc.);
c/ Documents evidencing the grounds for enjoying priority right (certification of the receiving agency for copies of the first application(s); list of goods or services included in the first mark registration application; certificate of assignment of priority right if such right is acquired from another person).
7.4 The following documents may be made in other languages besides Vietnamese, but if so requested by the National Office of Intellectual Property, they must be translated into Vietnamese:
a) The first copy of the application to prove the grounds for priority right;
b/ Other documents to support the application.
8. Fees and charges for industrial property registration
8.1 The applicant shall pay fees and charges according to regulations of the Ministry of Finance.
8.2 Collection of fees and charges
a) When receiving an application or requesting to carry out any other procedures, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to pay fees and charges according to regulations and check the fee and charge receipts attached to the application documents.
b) If the fees and charges have not been fully paid as prescribed, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall make a collection notice and notify it to the applicant.
In case the applicant fully pays the fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue 02 copies of the receipt of fees and charges, clearly stating the amounts and levels of fees and charges already paid, of which 01 copy is sent together with the application as a fee and charge payment document.
8.3 Refund of fees and charges
a) The paid fees and charges shall be partially or fully refunded at the request of the applicant in the following cases:
(i) The paid fees and charges exceed the prescribed limits;
(ii) The paid fees and charges are not used for corresponding tasks because no such situation occurs.
b) In case of accepting the request for refund of fees and charges, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of refund of fees and charges, clearly stating the amount and method of refund, and send it to the applicant.
c) In case of rejecting the request for refund of fees and charges, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant, clearly stating the reason.
9. Time limit
9.1 The time limits specified in the Law on Intellectual Property, the Decree on Industrial Property and this Circular shall be calculated according to the provisions of Chapter VIII, Part One of the Civil Code.
9.2 The time limit for the applicant and related parties to submit, amend or supplement documents or give opinions may be extended once for the exact duration already set, provided that the applicant submits an application for extension before the deadline and pays the fee as prescribed.
9.3 The applicant may request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to carry out procedures ahead of time when there is a written request and the fee is paid as prescribed. In case the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam rejects such request, it must notify the applicant, clearly stating the reason.
10. Forms of documents and titles of protection

10.1 Forms of applications are provided in the annexes to this Circular. Applicants shall use the above forms to make documents of their applications when carrying out procedures for industrial property registration.
10.2 Forms of protection titles are prescribed in the Appendices to this Circular. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall keep the issued forms of protection titles for examination of the legality of used protection titles. The change of forms of protection titles shall be made only on the basis of decisions of the Minister of Science and Technology.
11. General procedures
All types of industrial property registration applications shall be processed by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam in the following order: receipt of the application; formal examination of the application; publication of the valid application; substantive examination of the application (except for layout design registration applications, which are not subject to substantive examination); grant or refusal to grant a protection title; registration and publication of the decision on grant of a protection title.
12. Submit and receive applications
12.1 An application may be filed at the National Office of Industrial Property or other places designated by the National Office of Industrial Property. Applications may also be sent by post to the places designated by the National Office of Industrial Property.

12.2 Upon receipt of an application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall check the documents enclosed with the application and compare them with the list of documents stated in the application form in order to conclude whether the application is received or not.
a) In case the application contains all the minimum documents specified in Point 7.1 of this Circular, the applicant shall receive the application, affix a seal to certify the submission date and number of the application on the declarations;
b) In case the application lacks one of the minimum documents specified in Point 7.1 of this Circular, the officer receiving the application shall refuse to accept it or send a notice of refusal to accept the application from the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to the applicant (if the application is submitted by post). For rejected applications, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam is not required to return the application documents to the applicant but must refund the fees and charges already paid according to the refund procedures for fees and charges specified in Point 8 of this Circular;
c) In case the application is accepted, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue an application form to the applicant, which bears the seal of acceptance of the application, the number of the application, and the results of the examination of the list of documents specified in the application, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application. The issued application form has the same value as the receipt of the application.
13. Format appraisal of applications
13.1 Purposes and contents of format appraisal of applications
Format appraisal of an application means the inspection of compliance with regulations on the format of the application, thereby making a conclusion on whether the application is considered valid or invalid.
A valid application will be further considered. An invalid application will be rejected (not further considered).

13.2 Valid applications
An application is considered valid if it meets the requirements specified in Point 7 of this Circular and does not fall into one of the following cases:
a) The application is made in a language other than Vietnamese, except for the cases specified in Points 7.3 and 7.4 of this Circular;
b) The declaration does not contain sufficient information on the author (regarding an invention registration application, industrial design registration application or layout design registration application), on the applicant, on the representative, or there is no signature and/or seal of the applicant or the representative; the mark registration application lacks a list of goods and services; the geographical indication registration application fails to list products bearing the geographical indication;
c) There are grounds to confirm that the applicant does not have the registration right;
d) The application is filed in contravention of Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
e) The application has errors prescribed in Point 13.3 of this Circular which affect the validity of the application and although it has been requested by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to be corrected, the applicant still fails to correct them or the correction is unsatisfactory;
g) There are grounds to confirm that the subject matter stated in the application is clearly not protected by the State according to Articles 59, 64, 69, 73 and 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property. For an application with multiple subjects, if it falls under the cases prescribed in Point 13.2.a and Points 13.3.b and c of this Circular and the errors are related to one or several subjects in the application, the application shall be considered partially invalid (corresponding to the erroneous subjects), while for the remaining subjects, the application shall still be considered valid.
13.3 Handling of errors in the application during the stage of formal examination

If the application has the following errors, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify such to the applicant and within 01 month from the date of notification, the applicant must correct such errors:
a/ The application fails to meet the formal requirements prescribed at Point 7.2 of this Circular (not in the required number of copies of one of the mandatory documents; not uniform; not satisfying the formal presentation requirements; the registration application for a mark does not clearly state the type of mark registered, lacks description of the mark, the results of classification of goods or services are inaccurate, lacks translation of documents on priority right enjoyment (if necessary); information about the applicant is inconsistent with each other or erased or unconfirmed as prescribed, etc.);
b/ The fee for filing and the fee for publication of the application have not been fully paid;
c/ There is no power of attorney or an invalid power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative).

13.4 Determination of the filing date
The filing date shall be determined as follows:
a/ The filing date is the date the National Office of Industrial Property receives the application, which is indicated by the seal of receipt on the declaration according to Point 12.2.a of this Circular;
b/ For an international application designated or selected to be filed in Vietnam, the filing date is the date of filing of the international application.

13.5 Determination of the priority date
a/ If the application does not request the priority right or although it requests the priority right, the National Office of Industrial Property does not approve it, the application shall be considered having no priority date.
b/ If the application requests the priority right, the priority date (or priority dates) is the date stated in the above-mentioned request and approved by the National Office of Industrial Property.
c) The priority date determined according to the request for priority rights on the first application submitted in Vietnam shall comply with the principles prescribed in Article 91 of the Law on Intellectual Property and corresponding provisions at Points b, c, and dd Clause 1 Article 10 of the Decree on Industrial Property.
13.6 Notification of the results of the formal examination, notification of acceptance of the valid application
a) If the application falls under one of the cases specified in Point 13.2 of this Circular or contains deficiencies specified in Point 13.3 of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send a notice to the applicant stating the intended refusal to accept the application as valid. The notice must clearly state the name and address of the applicant; the name of the industrial property representation service organization (if the application is submitted through such organization); the subject matter stated in the application; the submission date and application number; reasons and deficiencies that may lead to the application being rejected, and impose a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide their opinion or correct errors.
b) In case the application is valid, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send a notice to the applicant stating the acceptance of the valid application, including the name and address of the applicant, the name of the authorized representative (if any), and information about the subject matter stated in the application, the submission date, and the priority date (in case the request for priority rights is not accepted, the reasons must be clearly stated).
13.7 Refusal to accept the application
In case the applicant has been notified by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam of the intended refusal to accept the application as valid as prescribed in Point 13.6.a of this Circular but fails to correct errors or provides inadequate corrections or does not have any objection or provides an unjustifiable objection within the imposed time limit, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant of the refusal to accept the application and refund the fees and charges already paid related to the post-formal examination work at the request of the applicant.
13.8 Time limit for formal examination of applications
a) The time limit for formal examination is 01 month from the date of submission.
b) If during the process of formal examination of an application, the applicant takes initiative or at the request of the National Office of Industrial Property to amend or supplement documents, the time limit for formal examination shall be extended by the duration for amendment or supplementation of documents.
c) Before the end of the time limit specified in Point 13.8.a above, the National Office of Industrial Property shall complete the examination of the form of the application and send a notice of results to the applicant according to the provisions of Point 13.6 of this Circular.
14. Publication of valid applications
14.1 All applications accepted as valid shall be published by the National Office of Industrial Property in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property. Applicants shall pay an application publication fee.
14.2 Time limit for publication of applications

a) Publication of invention registration applications:
(i) An invention registration application shall be published in the 19th month from the priority date or the submission date, if the application does not have a priority date, or within 02 months from the date of acceptance of the valid application, whichever is later;
(ii) An invention registration application under the Patent Cooperation Treaty (hereinafter referred to as "PCT application") shall be published within 02 months from the date of acceptance of the valid application after it has entered the national stage;

(iii) An invention registration application with early publication request shall be published within 02 months from the date the National Office of Industrial Property receives the early publication request or from the date of acceptance of the valid application, whichever is later.
b) Disclosure of other applications: layout design registration applications, industrial design registration applications, mark registration applications and geographical indication registration applications shall be disclosed within 2 months from the date of acceptance of valid applications.
14.3 Contents of disclosure of applications
Information related to valid applications, including split applications, shall be disclosed in the Industrial Property Gazette, including information related to valid applications in terms of form specified in the notice of acceptance of valid applications, information related to valid applications (transfer of applications, application splitting, original number of split applications, etc.); invention summaries enclosed with drawings (if any); sets of photos or drawings of industrial designs; samples of marks and lists of goods and services enclosed therewith; summary of specific characteristics of products bearing geographical indications and names of products bearing geographical indications.
14.4 Access to information about valid applications disclosed
Everyone may access detailed information about the nature of subject matters stated in disclosed applications in the Industrial Property Gazette or request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to provide such information and pay fees for information provision as prescribed.
15. Content appraisal of applications
15.1 Purposes and scope of application
a) The purpose of content appraisal of an application is to assess the protection capacity of the subject matter stated in the application according to the protection conditions, and determine the corresponding scope (volume) of protection.
b) Procedures for substantive examination shall not apply to layout design registration applications.
15.2 Use of information lookup results
a) During the process of substantive examination of an invention or industrial design registration application with priority right, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may use information lookup results and substantive examination results of corresponding applications filed overseas.
b) The applicant may (proactively or at the request of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam) provide the following documents to serve the substantive examination of the application:
(i) For an invention or industrial design registration application: information lookup results or substantive examination results of applications filed overseas for subject matters stated in the application; copies of protection titles issued based on the identical application filed overseas; documents related to the technical status of subject matters stated in the invention registration application provided by the applicant to a competent authority of a foreign country and other documents;
(ii) For a mark or geographical indication registration application: documents proving that the mark or geographical indication has been protected overseas, including documents describing the process of using the mark or geographical indication and other documents.
15.3 Correction of errors, explanation of application contents, provision of information
a) The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application and provide information within the scope of the nature of subject matters stated in the application if it is necessary to fully disclose the nature of the subject matters for substantive examination of the application.
b) Any amendment or addition to documents of the application containing errors shall be made by the applicant. The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may only make such amendments or additions if so requested in writing by the applicant. The applicant must pay an amendment fee as prescribed. Documents requesting amendments or additions to the application shall be attached to relevant documents of the application and shall be considered official documents of the application.
15.4 Early termination of content appraisal
a) In the following cases, the content appraisal of the application shall be terminated early:

(i) The application does not clearly indicate the nature of the subject matter: documents related to the nature of the subject matter, such as the description, the list of goods or services, etc., lack information to the extent that the nature of the subject matter cannot be determined, or the information about the nature of the subject matter of the invention registration application is unclear or too brief or too general to the extent that the subject matter of protection request cannot be determined;
(ii) The subject matter is not suitable for the type of protection title requested to be granted or the subject matter is not protected by the State as prescribed;
(iii) There are reasons to confirm that the subject matter fails to satisfy one or more certain protection conditions, therefore it is unnecessary to evaluate other conditions but it can still be concluded that the subject matter fails to satisfy the protection conditions;
(iv) The applicant fails to comply with requests for correction of errors, explanation of the application contents, or provision of necessary information at the request of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam as prescribed in Point 15.3 of this Circular;
(v) The applicant requests termination of the content appraisal of the application or declares withdrawal or abandonment of the application.
b) Except for cases specified in Point 15.4.a (v) above, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant of the early termination of content appraisal, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 2 months from the notification date for the applicant to provide their opinions.
15.5 Restoring content appraisal of applications
a) In case the applicant submits a written objection against the notification of early termination of content appraisal within the time limit specified in Point 15.4.b of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall consider the applicant's objection.
b) If the objection is justifiable, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall restore the content appraisal and the period reserved for the applicant to provide opinions shall not be included in the time limit for content appraisal. If the objection is unjustifiable, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall officially terminate the content appraisal and issue a notice of refusal to grant the protection title. The applicant may file a complaint about this notice according to the procedures specified in Point 22 of this Circular.
15.6 Content of appraisal
a) Content appraisal of an application includes the following contents:
(i) Assessment of the conformity between the subject matter stated in the application and the type of protection title requested; (ii) Assessment of the subject matter according to each protection condition;
(iii) Examination of the first-to-file principle.
b) The assessment according to protection conditions shall be carried out separately for each subject matter (if the application includes multiple subjects while ensuring consistency). For each subject matter, the assessment shall be carried out separately for each protection condition:
(i) For an invention registration application, the assessment shall be carried out separately for each point mentioned in the scope of (requested) protection;
(ii) For an industrial design registration application, the assessment shall be carried out separately for each product design (if the application mentions a set of products); in case it mentions multiple solutions, each solution shall be assessed separately, starting from the basic solution (the first solution mentioned in the application);
(iii) For a mark registration application, the assessment shall be carried out separately for each component of the mark for each goods or service mentioned in the list of goods and services.
c) The content appraisal for each subject matter mentioned in Points 15.6.b(i), (ii), (iii) above shall be completed when such subject matter has been assessed with all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that such subject matter fails to satisfy or satisfies the protection conditions, specifically:
(i) There is a reason to conclude that the subject matter fails to satisfy one or more than one or all protection conditions; or (ii) There is no reason to conclude that the subject matter fails to satisfy at least one protection condition.
d) Before issuing the notice specified in Point 15.7.a of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall review the content appraisal results of applications on the basis of examining applications with earlier priority dates received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam after the date of commencement of content appraisal. 15.7. Tasks ending the content appraisal
a) Notification of content appraisal results of applications

At least by the end date of the time limit for content appraisal prescribed in Point 15.8 of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send one of the following notices to the applicant:
(i) If the subject matter stated in the application does not satisfy the protection conditions, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intended refusal to grant the protection title, clearly stating the reasons for refusal, and may provide guidance on amendments to the scope (volume) of protection and impose a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide their opinion and meet the requirements. The applicant may request an extension of such time limit according to the provisions of Point 9.2 of this Circular;
(ii) If the subject matter stated in the application satisfies the protection conditions but the application contains deficiencies, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intended refusal to grant the protection title, clearly stating the deficiencies of the application, and impose a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide their explanation or rectify the deficiencies. The applicant may request an extension of such time limit according to the provisions of Point 9.2 of this Circular;
(iii) If the subject matter stated in the application satisfies the protection conditions or the applicant has rectified deficiencies to the required extent or provided a legitimate explanation within the time limit prescribed in Points 15.7.a(i) and (ii) above, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intended grant of the protection title and impose a 1-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for the grant of the protection title, the fee for the announcement of the decision on the grant of the protection title, the registration fee and the fee for maintenance of validity for the first year of an invention. The applicant may request an extension of such time limit according to the provisions of Point 9.2 of this Circular.
b) If, upon the expiration of the time limit prescribed in Points 15.7.a(i) and (ii) above, the applicant fails to rectify deficiencies or provides inadequate rectification, no objection or provides an unjustifiable objection, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intended refusal to grant the protection title within 15 days from the date of expiration of such time limit. The applicant may file a complaint about such notice according to the provisions of Point 22 of this Circular.
c) If the applicant fails to pay the fee for issuance of protection title, the fee for publication of decision on issuance of protection title or the registration fee within the time limit prescribed in Point 15.7.a (iii) above, then within 15 days from the end date of the corresponding time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to issue the protection title. Particularly for invention registration applications, if within the time limit prescribed in Point 15.7.a (iii) above, the applicants have paid the fees for issuance of protection titles, the fees for publication of decisions on issuance of protection titles and the registration fees but fail to pay the fees for maintenance of validity of first year of invention patents or utility solution patents, such titles shall still be issued but immediately terminated after their issuance.
15.8 Time limit
a) The time limit for content appraisal of an application is determined as follows:
(i) 12 months for invention registration applications from the date of receipt of the request for content appraisal (if such request is submitted after the date of application publication) or from the date of application publication (if such request is submitted before the date of application publication);
(ii) 6 months from the date of application publication for industrial design registration applications, mark registration applications and geographical indication registration applications.
b) If during the process of content appraisal of an application, the applicant proactively or at the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property carries out the amendment of the application, addition of documents or explanation, the time limit for content appraisal shall be extended accordingly with the time limit reserved for the applicant to perform such tasks.
16. Re-appraisal of application
16.1 Re-appraisal of application due to objection after notification of intended issuance/intended refusal to issue protection title
a) The re-examination of an application under Clause 4 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be carried out in the following cases:
(i) There is a written opinion from the applicant submitted to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam from the date of issuance of the notice of intended grant/intended refusal to issue a protection title until before the date of issuance of the decision on issuance/notification of official refusal to issue a protection title related to the application; or there is a written opinion from a third party with plausible reasons for the lack of reasonable conditions or opportunities to express their opinions; (ii) The opinion mentioned in Point 16.1.a(i) above is well-grounded, accompanied by evidence or indications leading to reliable information sources;
(iii) The reasoning and evidence proving the opinion mentioned in Point 16.1.a(i) above are different from the reasoning and evidence (if any) presented in the previous stage, or although such reasoning and evidence are not different, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam has not yet responded according to the provisions of Point 6.2 of this Circular.

b) The time limit for re-examination of an application is two-thirds of the time limit for initial examination prescribed in Point 15.8 of this Circular; for complex cases with many details requiring verification or expert opinions, it may be extended but must not exceed the time limit for initial examination.
c) The contents and procedures for re-examination shall comply with the corresponding provisions of Points 15.6 and 15.7 of this Circular.
d) The re-examination of an application may only be carried out once.
16.2 Re-examination of applications due to requests for narrowing the scope of protection of protection title holders In case a protection title holder requests narrowing the scope of protection of industrial property rights as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall carry out the re-examination of the corresponding application according to the contents and procedures prescribed in Points 15.6 and 15.7 of this Circular, provided that the requester pays fees and charges as prescribed.
17. Amendment/split-up/transfer of applications
17.1 Amendments to applications
a) Before the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam issues a notice of refusal to accept the valid application or a notice of refusal to grant a protection title or a decision on grant of a protection title, the applicant may, at their discretion or upon request of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, amend and supplement documents included in the application.
b) In case of amendments to the following documents, applicants shall submit amended documents enclosed with detailed explanations of amendments made to the original documents submitted:
(i) Description and summary of the invention for an invention registration application;
(ii) Drawings, photos and descriptions of industrial design registration applications;
(iii) Mark sample, list of goods and services bearing the mark for a mark registration application;

(iv) Description of particular characteristics, map of geographical area corresponding to a GI for a GI registration application.
c/ The amendment or supplementation of an application must not expand the scope (volume) of protection beyond the contents already disclosed in the description for applications for registration of inventions or industrial designs, or in the list of goods or services for applications for registration of marks, and must not change the nature of the subject matter stated in the application. If the amendment expands the scope (volume) of protection or changes the nature of the subject matter, the applicant shall have to file a new application and carry out all procedures again from the beginning.
d) The applicant may request correction of errors in the name and address of the applicant or author.
e) All requests for amendments shall be made in writing according to Form No. 01-SDD specified in Appendix B of this Circular. Requests may be made for amendments to the same content related to many applications, provided that applicants pay fees corresponding to the number of applications.
g) In case the applicant voluntarily amends or supplements the application after the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam has issued a notice of acceptance of the valid application, such amendment or supplementation shall comply with Points 13.2, 13.3 and 13.6 of this Circular. The contents of the amended or supplemented application shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette according to Point 14 of this Circular and the applicant shall pay an application publication fee as prescribed.
h) The amendment or supplementation of an application, including changes in the authorized person, shall be made by the applicant at their discretion or upon request by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and must be documented in writing, clearly stating the amended or supplemented contents, enclosed with the receipt for payment of fees for amendment or supplementation of the application. Documents used for amendment or supplementation of applications must comply with corresponding provisions specified in Points 7, 10 and 13 of this Circular.
17.2 Splitting of applications
a) The applicant may, on their own initiative or at the request of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, separate an application (separate one or more technical solutions in an invention registration application, one or more industrial designs in an industrial design registration application, one or more components of a mark or part of a goods or service list in a trademark registration application into one or more new applications, called split applications).
b) The separate application shall bear a new serial number and shall be submitted on the same day as the initial application or on the priority date of the initial application (if any); it shall be published in accordance with Point 14 of this Circular after the notice of acceptance of the valid application is issued.
c) For each application for division, the applicant shall pay an application fee and all charges and fees for procedures carried out independently from the initial application but is not required to pay the charge for requesting priority right. The division application shall be appraised in terms of form and continue to be processed according to the unfinished procedures for the initial application. The division application must be re-published and the applicant shall pay a publication fee if the division is made after the National Office of Intellectual Property has issued a notice of acceptance of the initial application as valid.
d) The initial application (after being divided) shall continue to be processed according to common procedures and the applicant shall pay an amendment and supplementation fee.
17.3 Application conversion
a) Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice of refusal to grant a protection title or a decision on grant of a protection title, the applicant may convert the request for the invention patent into a request for the utility solution patent or vice versa as prescribed at Point dd, Clause 1, Article 115 of the Law on Intellectual Property, provided that the applicant pays a fee for application conversion as prescribed.
b) After receiving a valid application conversion request, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall continue to carry out the conversion procedures as prescribed but does not carry out again the procedures already carried out for the application before the conversion request.
c) Requests for application conversion filed after the time limit prescribed at Point 17.3.a above shall not be considered. The applicant may file a new application but may take the filing date (priority date, if any) of the initial application.
17.4 Application transfer
Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues one of the notices prescribed at Point 17.1.a of this Circular, the applicant may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to record the application transfer to another person. The request for recording the application transfer shall be made according to Form No. 02-CGD prescribed in Annex B of this Circular and the corresponding provisions at Point 17.1 of this Circular. The request for recording the application transfer must contain documents proving that the transferee satisfies the registration right requirement.
18. Refusal to grant, grant and re-grant of copies of protection titles 18.1 Refusal to grant protection titles
Applications shall be refused for grant of protection titles in one of the cases specified in clauses 1 and 2 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property. In these cases, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out procedures for refusal of grant of protection titles according to the provisions of clauses 3 and 4 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
18.2 Grant of protection titles
a/ Within 10 days after an applicant fully and punctually pays fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out procedures for granting a protection title according to the provisions of Article 118 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
b/ After being granted a protection title, if the protection title holder finds any error, he/she may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to correct it according to the provisions of point 20.2 of this Circular.
c/ From the date the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant a protection title, the transfer of applications shall not be considered.
18.3 Grant of copies of protection titles and re-grant of protection titles
a/ In case industrial property rights are under joint ownership, a protection title shall be granted only to the first person in the list of joint applicants. Other co-owners may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to grant copies of protection titles, provided that they pay the fee for such copies.
b) In the following cases, the industrial property owner who has been granted a protection title/replica of a protection title may request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to re-grant a protection title/replica of a protection title, provided that the corresponding fee is paid:
(i) The protection title/replica of a protection title is lost;
(ii) The protection title/replica of a protection title is damaged, torn, dirty or faded beyond the point of being unusable.
c) Request for issuance of a replica/protection title re-grant
Except for requests for issuance of replicas stated in the declaration of registration of industrial property objects, requests for issuance of replicas/protection title re-grant must be made in writing and include the following documents:
(i) Declaration requesting the issuance of a replica/protection title re-grant, made according to Form No. 03-PBVB specified in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) 01 sample of a mark; 01 set of photos of drawings of an industrial design identical with the sample of the mark, and a set of photos of drawings of the industrial design in the original protection title;

(iii) A power of attorney (if the request is submitted by a representative);
(iv) Documents on fees for issuance of protection titles or fees for issuance of copies of protection titles.
d) Processing requests for issuance of copies, reissuance of protection titles
(i) Within 01 month from the date of receipt of an application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall consider the request for issuance of a copy/reissue of a protection title. If the request for issuance of a copy/reissue of a protection title meets the above requirements, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to issue a copy/reissue of a protection title and record it in the register section of the corresponding protection title in the National Register of Industrial Property;
(ii) The contents of the copy of a protection title fully reflect the information of the corresponding protection title. The contents of the reissue of a protection title/copy of a protection title fully reflect the information of the first-time issued protection title and must be accompanied by the instructions "Reissue", "Copy" or "Reissue of Copy";
(iii) In case the request for reissuance/copy issuance of a protection title fails to meet the requirements specified in Point 18.3.c above, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of refusal to issue a copy/reissue of a protection title, clearly stating the reason.

(iii) All information on amendments to protection titles, validity status of protection titles (validity maintenance, extension, termination, cancellation); transfer of ownership rights, use rights of industrial property objects; number, date of issuance and persons issued with copies or reissued with protection titles.

e) National registration books made and kept by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam in paper or electronic form or other means. Any person may search the electronic registration book or request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to issue a copy or extract from the registration book, provided that the fee for issuance of copies is paid.
a/ All decisions on grant of protection titles and decisions on acceptance of international registration shall be published by the National Office of Industrial Property in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property within 2 months after their issuance after the applicants have paid the publication fee according to regulations.
b) Information disclosed under Point 19.2.a above includes information specified in the corresponding decision: summary of the invention; set of photos or drawings of the industrial design; sample of the mark and list of goods and services bearing the mark; geographical indication and products bearing the geographical indication.
20. To amend or change the title holders, narrow the scope of protection, maintain and extend the validity of the titles of protection
20.1 Recording changes in names and addresses of protection title holders, narrowing the scope of protection and amending descriptions of properties/quality/reputation of products bearing GIs or maps of geographical areas corresponding to GIs, regulations on use of collective marks or regulations on use of certification marks
a/ To request amendment or replacement of the title holder of the protection title
The owner of a protection title may request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to record changes in the protection title in the following cases:
(i) Changes to the name and address of the owner of the protection title;
(ii) Changes to the owner of the protection title (transfer of ownership due to inheritance, acquisition, merger, separation, joint venture, association, establishment of a new legal entity under the same ownership, business format conversion or decision of a competent regulatory authority).
The requester for recording changes to the name and address and changes to the owner of the protection title must pay a fee for modification of the protection title and a fee for publication of the decision on recording of modifications to the protection title.
b) Request for narrowing the scope of protection and modification of the description of characteristics/quality/reputation of products bearing GIs, maps of geographical areas corresponding to GIs, regulations on use of collective marks, regulations on use of certification marks
Requests for narrowing the scope of protection as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property may include one of the following contents:
(i) Request for modification of certain details without making significant changes to the mark pattern stated in the mark registration certificate;
(ii) Request for reduction of one or more goods, services or groups of goods, services from the list of goods, services stated in the mark registration certificate without changing the mark pattern;
(iii) Request for reduction of one or several independent or dependent points within the scope of protection (request) stated in the invention patent, utility solution patent;
(iv) Request for removal of one or several industrial designs, one or several products in the product set stated in the industrial design patent; request for removal of one or several basic design features of the industrial design.
c) Written request for modification of the protection title
Depending on the content to be modified as prescribed in Point 20.1.a and Point 20.1.b above and Point b Clause 1 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the written request shall include the following documents: (i) Declaration requesting modification of the protection title, clearly stating the request for recording changes to the name and address of the protection title holder, changes to the protection title holder, requests for narrowing the protection scope or modifying the description of properties/quality/reputation of products bearing geographical indications, maps of geographical areas corresponding to geographical indications, regulations on use of collective marks, regulations on use of certification marks, made according to Form No. 01-SDB prescribed in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Original copy of the protection title;

(iii) Documents certifying the change of name and address (original or notarized copies of decisions on change of name and address; business registration permits recording the change of name and address; other legal documents proving the change of name and address) - if the content of the modification request is name and address; (iv) Documents proving the transfer of ownership as prescribed in Point 20.1.a (ii) above, if the request is for change of the protection title holder (documents proving inheritance, acquisition, merger, separation, joint venture, association, establishment of a new legal entity under the same owner, business format conversion or decision of the competent regulatory authority);
(v) Documentary explanation of the details of the modification;
(vi) 05 samples of modified marks (if a detailed modification of a mark is requested); 05 sets of photos or drawings of industrial designs (if an industrial design is requested to be modified); 02 descriptions of properties/quality/reputation of products bearing a geographical indication, maps of geographical areas corresponding to the modified geographical indication (if a geographical indication is requested to be modified); 02 regulations on use of collective marks, regulations on use of modified certification marks (if a collective mark or certification mark is requested to be modified);
(vii) Authorization letter (if the application is filed by a representative);
(viii) Documents proving payment of fees for modification of protection titles, fees for content appraisal for requests for narrowing of the protection scope, and fees for publication of decisions on modification and registration according to regulations.
An application for modification of a protection title may relate to more than one protection title if they have the same modification content, provided that the applicant pays fees and charges for each protection title.
d) Processing applications for modification of protection titles
Within 01 month from the date of receipt of an application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall consider the request for modification of the protection title. If the request is considered valid, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to modify the protection title and register it and publish such decision in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property. In the opposite case, the National Office of Industrial Property shall send a notice of intended refusal to accept the modification to the applicant, clearly stating the reasons therefor and setting a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to correct errors or provide its objection. If within the set time limit the applicant fails to correct errors or fails to meet requirements, does not provide an objection or provides unjustifiable objection, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of refusal to accept the request for modification of the protection title.
20.2 Correction of errors in protection titles
a) In case of detecting any error in the protection title, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall, on its own initiative or at the request of the person who detects the error, revoke the protection title with errors and grant a new one.
b) The owner of the protection title must pay the fee for modification of the protection title as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property if such error is caused by the fault of the owner of the protection title. If such error is caused by the fault of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, the owner of the protection title is not required to pay the fee for correction.
20.3 Maintenance of validity of invention protection titles
In order to maintain the validity of an invention protection title, the owner of the protection title must pay the validity maintenance fee within 6 months before the expiration date of the term of validity. The validity maintenance fee may be paid later than the above-mentioned time limit but must not exceed 6 months from the expiration date of the previous term of validity and the owner of the protection title must pay an additional fee equal to 10% of the fee for each month of late payment.

20.4 Extension of validity of protection titles
a) An invention patent, utility solution patent or certificate of registered layout design shall not be extended. An industrial design patent may be extended up to 2 times for 5 years each time. A certificate of registered mark may be extended multiple times for 10 years each time.
b) In order to have the validity of an industrial design patent or a certificate of registered mark extended, within 6 months before the industrial design patent or the certificate of registered mark expires, the owner of the industrial design patent or the owner of the certificate of registered mark must file an application for extension with the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam.
The application for extension may be filed later than the above-mentioned time limit but must not exceed 6 months from the expiration date of the protection title and the owner of the protection title must pay the extension fee plus 10% of the extension fee for each month of late payment.
c) Application for extension
An application for extension of validity of a protection title shall include the following documents:

(i) Declaration requesting extension of validity of the protection title, made according to Form No. 02-GHVB in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Original copy of the protection title (in case of request for recording of extension in the protection title);

(iii) Power of attorney (if the application is filed by a representative);
(iv) Receipt of payment of fee for extension, announcement of decision on extension and registration of decision on extension of validity of the protection title as prescribed.
d) Processing of applications for extension
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider applications for extension within 01 month from the date of receipt of such applications. In case the applications are flawless, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on extension, record it in the protection title, register it and publish it in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property.
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intended refusal of extension, clearly stating the reasons therefor and setting a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to correct errors or provide its opinion opposing the refusal, if the application falls into one of the following cases:
(i) The application for extension is invalid or is filed improperly;
(ii) The applicant for extension is not the owner of the corresponding protection title.
If within the prescribed time limit, the applicant fails to correct errors or his/her correction of errors is unsatisfactory, or does not have any objection or his/her objection is unjustifiable, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of refusal of extension.
21. Termination and invalidation of protection titles
21.1 Request for termination and invalidation of protection titles
Requests for termination and invalidation of protection titles shall be handled in accordance with Articles 95 and 96 of the Law on Intellectual Property and this Point.
21.2 Written request for termination and invalidation of protection titles
a) In one document, one or more than one protection title may be requested to be terminated or invalidated if they are based on the same reason, provided that the requesting party pays fees as prescribed for each protection title.
b) A written request for termination and invalidation of a protection title shall include the following documents: (i) Declaration requesting termination and invalidation of a protection title, made according to Form No. 04-CDHB specified in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Evidence (if any);
(iii) Power of attorney (in case of submission of written request for authorization);
(iv) Explanation of the reason for the request (specifying the number of title, reason, legal grounds, contents proposed to terminate, cancel partially or entirely the validity of the protection title) and relevant documents as prescribed in corresponding points 7.2, 22.2 and 22.3 of this Circular;
(v) Receipts of fees and charges as prescribed.
21.3 Handling requests for termination or cancellation of validity of protection titles
a) In case of a request for termination or cancellation of validity of a protection title made by a third party, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify in writing the opinion of the third party to the owner of the protection title, setting a time limit of 02 months from the date of notification for the owner of the protection title to give their opinion. The National Office of Intellectual Property may organize direct exchange of opinions between the third party and the owner of the protection title.
b) Based on consideration of the parties' opinions, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on partial/entire termination/cancellation of validity of the protection title or issue a notice of refusal to terminate/cancel the validity of the protection title according to regulations in clause 4 Article 95 and clause 4 Article 96 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
c) If disagreeing with the content of the decision on handling the request for termination or cancellation of validity of the protection title issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property, the requester or related party may file a complaint about the decision or related notice according to the procedures specified in point 22 of this Circular.
d) Decision to terminate or invalidate protection titles published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette and recorded in the National Register of Industrial Property within 02 months from the date of signing of the decision.
21.4 Termination or invalidation of international mark registrations

a) For applications for termination or invalidation of third parties’ international mark registrations under the Madrid Agreement or Madrid Protocol, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the contents of such applications to mark owners via International Office within 03 months from the date of notification for the mark owners’ opinions.
b) Decisions to terminate or invalidate international mark registrations shall be sent to International Office for relevant procedures as prescribed in corresponding provisions of the Madrid Agreement or Madrid Protocol and published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette.
22. Complaints and settlement of complaints related to procedures for establishment of industrial property rights 22.1 Persons with right to complain, objects and statute of limitations for complaining Persons with right to complain specified in Clause 1 Article 14, within the statute of limitations specified in Clause 4 Article 14 of the Decree on Industrial Property, may carry out procedures for complaining official notices and decisions of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam related to procedures for establishment of industrial property rights.

22.2 Complaint applications
a) Each complaint application mentions one decision or notice complained. One complaint application may also mention multiple decisions or notices if they have the same content and reason for complaint, provided that the complainant must pay complaint fees according to regulations applicable to each complained decision or notice.
b) A complaint application must include the following documents:
(i) The written complaint made using Form No. 05-KN as prescribed in Appendix C of this Circular; (ii) Written explanation of the complaint and evidence proving the grounds for the complaint (specifying reasons, legal grounds, content of the complaint, list of accompanying evidence, if any);
(iii) Copy of the decision or notice subject to the complaint issued by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam;
(iv) Copy of the decision on the first resolution of the complaint (for the second complaint); (v) Power of attorney (in case of submission through a representative);
(vi) Receipts of fees and charges related to the complaint as prescribed.
c) Evidence means documents (evidence) or exhibits (material evidence) used to prove and clarify the grounds for the complaint. Evidence must meet the following requirements:
(i) Evidence may be documents in foreign languages, provided that they are accompanied by Vietnamese translations in cases where the person competent to resolve the complaint requests it;
(ii) In cases where evidence is documents bearing the name of an individual or organization without a lawful seal or a foreign individual or organization, the signature must be certified by a notary public or a competent authority;
(iii) In cases where evidence is objects carrying information ( publications, video tapes, etc.), depending on each case, it is required to specify the origin, distribution time, or publication date of the above-mentioned documents, or specify the origin and publication date of the information shown on such objects.
(iv) The exhibits must be accompanied by documents clearly describing characteristics directly related to the content of the complaint.

22.3 Responsibility of complainants
Complainants shall ensure the truthfulness in providing evidence and shall take responsibility for the consequences of providing untruthful evidence.
22.4 Withdrawal of complaints
a) At any time, complainants may send written notices of withdrawal of their complaints. If the withdrawal of complaints is made by industrial property representation service organizations, the authorization for withdrawal of complaints must be clearly stated in the power of attorney.
b) Withdrawn complaints shall be considered not submitted. Complainants shall not be refunded their complaints and paid complaint fees and charges.
22.5 Acceptance of complaints
a) Within 10 days after receiving a complaint, the person competent to settle complaints shall examine the complaint according to the form requirements and issue a written notice to the complainant whether or not the complaint has been accepted, which shall record the date of acceptance or clearly state the reason for non-acceptance.
b) Complaints shall not be accepted if they fall into one of the following cases:
(i) The complainant has no right to lodge a complaint;

(ii) The complaint is filed beyond the prescribed statute of limitations;
(iii) The complaint form does not meet the requirements specified in Points 22.1 and 22.2 of this Circular.
a) For complaints already accepted, the person competent to resolve complaints shall notify in writing the content of the complaint to the person with direct related rights and interests (“related party”) and impose a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for such person to provide opinions.
b/ The concerned party may provide information and evidence to support its arguments within the time limit prescribed at Point 22.6.a above; the person competent to settle the complaint shall have to consider such information and evidence when settling the complaint.
c/ If the above-said time limit expires but the concerned party has no opinion, the complaint shall be settled on the basis of the complainant's opinions.
22.7 Decision on complaint settlement
a) Based on arguments and evidence of the complainant and relevant parties, the person competent to resolve complaints shall issue a decision to resolve the complaint within the time limit for resolving complaints as prescribed by law on complaints.
b) Before issuing a decision to resolve the complaint, the person competent to resolve the complaint shall notify the complainant and the related party of the arguments and evidence used by the other party to resolve the complaint as well as the conclusion on the resolution of the complaint.
c) The decision to resolve the complaint must contain the contents prescribed by law on complaints. 22.8 The decision to resolve the complaint shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 02 months from the date of its signing.
22.9 Effect of the decision to resolve the complaint
Any procedures for establishing industrial property rights that depend on the results of the resolution of the complaint shall only be carried out on the basis of:
a) The first decision to resolve the complaint, if after the expiration of the statute of limitations for the second complaint, the complainant does not file a second complaint, and after the expiration of the statute of limitations for administrative lawsuit, the complainant does not initiate an administrative lawsuit; or b) The second decision to resolve the complaint and the complainant does not initiate an administrative lawsuit within the prescribed time limit or according to the effective decision of the court if the complainant initiates an administrative lawsuit.
Section 2. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF INVENTIONS
23. Requirements for invention registration applications
23.1 An invention registration application (application) must meet the general requirements specified at Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements specified at this Point.
23.2 The application must specify that the subject matter to be protected is a product or process in accordance with Clause 12 Article 4 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
23.3 The application must ensure consistency as prescribed in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property and under the following guidelines.
An application shall be considered consistent if:
a) It requests protection for a single subject matter; or b) It requests protection for a group of subjects with technical connections, expressing a unique common creative intent, falling into the following cases:
(i) One subject is used to create (manufacture, develop, or prepare) the other;
(ii) One subject is used to perform the other;
(iii) One subject is used to utilize the other;
(iv) Subjects are of the same type and have the same function to ensure the same result. 23.4 In case there are grounds (information or evidence) to suspect the authenticity of information in the application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may request the applicant to submit documents verifying such information within 01 month from the date of issuance of the notification requesting such verification, especially documents confirming lawful registration rights if the applicant inherits submission rights from another person (certificate of inheritance rights, certificate or agreement on transfer of application submission rights; assignment contract or labor contract, etc.); documents showing drug test results on humans, animals, or plants mentioned in the description (when the subject matters of the protection request are pharmaceuticals for humans, animals, or plants).
23.5 Requirements for declarations
The applicant shall submit 02 declarations, made according to Form 01-SC in Appendix A of this Circular. In the section "International classification of inventions" in the declaration, the applicant shall specify the classification code of the technical solution to be protected according to the latest International Classification of Inventions (according to the Strasbourg Agreement) published by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam in the Industrial Property Gazette. If the applicant fails to classify or classify incorrectly, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall classify and the applicant shall pay a classification fee as prescribed.
23.6 Requirements on invention descriptions
The applicant shall submit 2 invention descriptions. An invention description must include an invention description and a protection scope.
a) The description section of an invention description must fully disclose the nature of the technical solution registered. The description must contain sufficient information to enable any person with average knowledge in the relevant technical field to implement such a solution; it must clarify the novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability of the technical solution (if the protection title requires the issuance of an invention patent); and clarify the novelty and industrial applicability of the technical solution (if the protection title requires the issuance of a utility solution patent).
Persons with average knowledge in a corresponding technical field means persons who have practical skills in common techniques and know general common knowledge in the corresponding technical field.
b) The description must include the following contents:
(i) Name of invention: briefly present subject matter or subjects registered (hereinafter referred to as "subject matters"); name of invention must be concise and not promotional or advertising nature;

(ii) Field of use of invention: field in which subject matters are used or related;

(iii) Technical status of field of use of invention: technical status in the above-mentioned field at the time of filing (similar known subject matters, if any);
(iv) Technical nature of invention: nature of subject matters, in which must clearly state signs (characteristics) creating subject matters and must indicate new signs (characteristics) compared with similar known technical solutions;
(v) Brief description of accompanying drawings (if any);
(vi) Detailed description of schemes for implementing invention;
(vii) Example of implementation of invention;
(viii) Benefits (efficiency) that may be achieved.
c) Scope of protection of invention (hereinafter referred to as "scope of protection" or "protection request") Scope (request) of protection is used to determine scope of industrial property rights to inventions. Scope (request) of protection must be presented briefly and clearly, consistent with description and drawings, in which must clarify new signs of subject matters requested for protection (hereinafter referred to as "subject matters") and must be consistent with the following regulations:
d) Scope (request) of protection must be fully illustrated by description, including basic technical signs needed and sufficient to identify subject matters, achieve set objectives and distinguish subject matters from known subject matters.
e) Technical signs within the scope of protection must be clear, accurate and accepted in the corresponding technical field.
g) Scope of protection (requirements) must not be cited to description and drawings, except for cases where it is cited to parts that cannot be accurately described in words, such as the order of nucleotides and the order of amino acids, interference diagrams, state diagrams...
h/ If the application contains drawings illustrating the protection request, the signs mentioned in the scope of (requested) protection may be accompanied by indication numbers which must be enclosed in parentheses. Such indication numbers shall not be regarded as limiting the scope of (requested) protection.
i) The scope of protection (requested) shall be divided into two parts: "limited part" and "differential part". The "limited part" includes the name of the subject matter and signs of the subject matter which are identical to those of the nearest known subject matters, and is connected with the "differential part" by the phrase "differs in" or "is characterized by" or equivalent words; the "differential part" includes distinctive signs of the subject matter compared to the nearest known subject matters, and these signs combined with the signs of the "limited part" constitute the subject matter of the protection request.
k) The scope of protection may include one or more points. Where the scope of protection covers multiple points, it may be used to represent a single protected subject matter, with the first point (referred to as the independent point) and subsequent points (referred to as dependent points) used to specify the independent point; or to represent a group of protected subject matters, with several independent points, each representing a protected subject matter within the group, each of which may have dependent points.
l) Points of the protected scope (requirements) must be numbered in Arabic numerals followed by a period.
m) Scope of protection (requirements) for multiple points used to represent a group of subject matters must satisfy the requirements: independent points, representing separate subject matters, not being cited to other points of the scope of protection (requirements), except that such citation allows avoiding repetition of the entire content of other points; dependent points must be presented immediately after the independent points from which they depend.
23.7 Requirements for invention summaries
The applicant shall submit 02 invention summaries. The invention summary shall be used to briefly describe (not exceeding 150 words) the nature of the invention. The summary shall present the main contents of the technical solution for information purposes. The summary may contain drawings and characteristic formulas.
23.8 Additional provisions for invention registration applications related to biotechnology
a) In addition to the general requirements for invention description specified in Point 23.6 of this Circular, for invention registration applications concerning the gene order or a part of the gene order, the description must include a list of gene orders presented according to the WIPO ST.25 standard section 2 (ii) (the standard for presenting the list of nucleotide sequences and amino acid sequences in invention registration applications).
b) The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may request the applicant to submit electronic objects (e.g., floppy disks, optical disks, etc.) readable by common electronic devices containing nucleotide sequences and amino acid sequences identical with the list of sequences stated in the description.
c) Specifically, inventions relating to biological materials that cannot be described or fully described to the extent that persons with average knowledge of biotechnology can implement them shall only be considered fully disclosed if the following conditions are satisfied: (i) Biological material samples have been submitted to the storage agency under Point 23.9 of this Circular no later than the application submission date;
(ii) The description clearly states necessary information about the properties of biological materials that the applicant may obtain;
(iii) The declaration must specify the biological material storage agency, the storage number of the biological material sample already submitted for storage issued by the storage agency, and documents confirming these information submitted to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam within 16 months from the priority date or no later than the submission date of the early application (if any), whichever is earlier, except for the case specified in Point 23.9.d of this Circular.
d) In cases where the applicant is not the biological material depositor, the declaration must clearly state the name and address of the depositor and documents confirming the lawful use of biological materials, which must be submitted to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam within 16 months from the priority date or no later than the submission date of the early application (if any), whichever is earlier, except for the case specified in Point 23.9.d of this Circular.
23.9 Deposition of biological material samples
a) The purpose of depositing biological material samples is to serve the content appraisal process of invention registration applications related to biological materials.
b) Biological material samples must be deposited with the biological material storage agency no later than the submission date of the invention registration application related to such biological materials.
c) The biological material storage agency is an agency in Vietnam or abroad designated or recognized by the Ministry of Science and Technology for the function of storing biological materials.
d) The deposition of biological material samples and confirmation documents for international applications for inventions shall comply with the provisions of the Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT).

e) For biological material samples deposited at a foreign storage agency, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may request the applicant to deposit additional biological material samples at a competent storage agency in Vietnam if it deems necessary to clarify the nature of the subject matter requested for protection or to meet the requirements of a third party regarding access to such subject matter.
23.10 Additional regulations on applications for registration of inventions related to pharmaceuticals In addition to the general requirements for invention descriptions prescribed in Clause 23.6 of this Circular, applications for registration of inventions related to pharmaceuticals must describe the results of clinical tests and pharmacological effects of pharmaceutical products, including at least the following information:
a) The substance/mixture used;
b) The method (system) of test used;
c) Test results;
d) The relationship between the pharmacological effects obtained from the test and the practical application of the pharmaceutical product in the field of diagnosis and treatment of diseases.
23.11 Additional regulations on applications for registration of inventions related to genetic resources or traditional knowledge
In addition to the general requirements for invention registration applications prescribed from Clause 23.1 to Clause 23.7 of this Circular, applications for registration of inventions related to genetic resources or traditional knowledge must also include explanatory documents on the origin of such genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge that the author of the invention or applicant has accessed, if the invention is directly based on such genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge. If the author of the invention or applicant cannot identify the origin of the genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge, such must be clearly stated and they shall take responsibility for the truthfulness of such identification.
24. Format appraisal and publication of invention registration applications
The procedures for formal examination and publication of invention registration applications shall comply with the general provisions prescribed in clauses 13 and 14 of this Circular.
25. Content appraisal of an invention registration application
25.1 Request for substantive examination of an invention registration application
a) The applicant or any third party has the right to request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to conduct a content appraisal of an invention registration application in accordance with Article 113 of the Law on Intellectual Property, and is specified as follows:
(i) The request for content appraisal of an invention registration application must be made in writing according to Form No. 03-YCTD specified in Appendix B of this Circular or stated in the invention registration application declaration (if the applicant is the applicant and such request is made immediately upon filing the application);

(ii) The request for content appraisal of an invention registration application must be filed within 42 months from the priority date if it requests the issuance of an invention patent, or within 36 months from the priority date if it requests the issuance of a utility solution patent; The time limit for filing a request for content appraisal may be extended but shall not exceed 6 months if there are legitimate reasons;
(iii) The applicant for content appraisal of an invention registration application shall pay a fee for searching and a content appraisal fee as prescribed; if the content appraisal request is submitted beyond the prescribed time limit, the applicant must pay an extension fee as prescribed in Point 20.4.b of this Circular; if the content appraisal fee is not paid, the content appraisal request shall be considered invalid and the National Office of Intellectual Property will not conduct content appraisal.
b/ Requesting content appraisal of an invention registration application filed after the date of its publication shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette in the second month after receipt of such request; if such request is made by a third party, it shall be notified to the applicant.
An application for substantive examination of an invention registration application filed before the date of disclosure shall be disclosed together with the corresponding application.
c) In case no request for content appraisal is filed within the time limit prescribed in Point 25.1.a above, the application shall be considered as withdrawn at the end of such time limit.
25.2 Order of carrying out procedures for content appraisal
The content appraisal of an invention registration application shall be carried out according to the general order prescribed in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific provisions of this Point.
25.3 Assessment of compatibility between subject matters stated in the application and the type of invention protection title

a) Subject matters stated in an invention registration application shall be considered incompatible with the type of invention protection title requested by the applicant (invention patent/utility solution patent) if such subject matters are not technical solutions, specifically products or processes. The identification of technical solutions is prescribed in Point 25.3.b below.
b) Technical solutions - subject matters protected as inventions - are necessary and sufficient information sets on technical methods and/or technical means to solve a defined task (problem).
Technical solutions may fall into one of the following forms:
(i) Products in physical form (instruments, machinery, equipment, components, electrical circuits, etc.) expressed by a set of information defining a man-made product characterized by signs (features) of structure, which product has the function (utility) as a means to meet a certain need of humans; or products in solid form (materials, substances, foods, pharmaceuticals, etc.) expressed by a set of information defining a man-made product characterized by signs (features) of presence, proportion and state of elements, which product has the function (utility) as a means to meet a certain need of humans; or products in biological materials (genes, genetically modified plants/animals, etc.) expressed by a set of information defining a product containing genetic information altered under human influence, capable of self-reproduction;
(ii) Process (technological process; method of diagnosis, prediction, inspection, handling, etc.) expressed as a set of information defining the manner in which a specific process or work is carried out, characterized by signs (features) regarding the order, conditions, participants, measures and means for performing operations to achieve a certain purpose.
c) Subject matters stated in the application shall not be considered technical solutions in the following cases: (i) Subject matters stated in the application are merely ideas or intentions, or issues only stated but not solutions, or answers to questions such as “by what means” or “by what means?”; (ii) Issues (tasks) stated for resolution are not technical issues and cannot be resolved by technical means;
(iii) Natural products, not created by humans.
25.4 Assessment of industrial applicability according to Article 62 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) Technical solutions stated in the application shall be considered “feasible” if:
(i) Information regarding the nature of the solution, together with instructions on necessary technical conditions, is presented clearly and sufficiently so that persons with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field may create, manufacture, use, exploit or implement such solution;
The concept of “person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field” is understood in accordance with Point 23.6.a of this Circular;
(ii) The creation, manufacture, use, exploitation or implementation of the above-mentioned solution may be repeated with identical results and similar to the results stated in the invention description.
b) Technical solutions are considered incapable of industrial application in the following cases: (i) The nature of the subject matter or instructions for implementation of the subject matter is contrary to fundamental principles of science (for example, not complying with the principle of energy conservation, etc.);
(ii) The subject matter includes elements and components that have no technical connection between them or cannot be connected (joined, bound, dependent, etc.) to each other;
(iii) The subject matter contains internal contradictions;
(iv) Instructions on the subject matter can only be implemented within a certain number of times (cannot be repeated);
(v) To implement the solution, the implementer must possess special skills that cannot be transmitted or taught to others;
(vi) Results obtained from the implementation are not consistent;
(vii) Results obtained are different from those stated in the application;
(viii) There are no or insufficient most important instructions for implementing the solution; (ix) Other cases with legitimate reasons.
25.5 Assessment of novelty under Article 60 of the Law on Intellectual Property

a) Minimum mandatory information sources
In order to assess the novelty of the technical solutions stated in the application, it is necessary to conduct searches of information in the following mandatory sources (but not limited to the minimum sources): (i) All invention registration applications received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application currently undergoing appraisal - up to the third sub-classification index and with an earlier publication date than the submission date or priority date of the application currently undergoing appraisal;
(ii) Invention registration applications or invention protection titles published by other organizations or countries within 25 years before the submission date or priority date of the application currently undergoing appraisal (if the application is eligible for priority rights) stored in the invention database at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and other information sources prescribed by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, with the search scope specified in Point 25.5.a(i) above.
Where necessary and possible, the search may be extended to scientific reports, reports on results of research programs and projects, and other documents in the same technical field published and stored at the National Center for Science and Technology Information.
b) Search purposes
The purpose of searching information is to find technical solutions with similar or identical nature to the technical solutions stated in the application.
In this point:
(i) Two technical solutions shall be regarded as identical when they have all basic signs (characteristics) identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
(ii) Two technical solutions shall be considered similar when they have most of their basic signs (characteristics) identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
(iii) “Controlled technical solution” means a technical solution identical or most similar to the one stated in the application;
(iv) “Comparison documents” are documents describing the technical solution of comparison or evidence proving that the technical solution of comparison has been publicly disclosed.
c) Reference report
Search results must be presented in the search report, specifying the searched field, scope and results within such scope (statistics of found control technical solutions, specifying identical signs, names of control documents, page numbers, line numbers, origin of documents and date of publication of corresponding documents) and full name of the person making the report (searcher).
d) Methods for assessing the novelty of technical solutions
In order to evaluate the novelty of a technical solution stated in an application, it is required to compare fundamental signs (characteristics) of such technical solution with signs of control technical solutions found during the information lookup process; in which:
(i) Fundamental signs of a technical solution may be features related to its function, utility, structure, composition, connection, components, etc., together with other fundamental signs forming a necessary and sufficient set for determining the nature (content) of the subject matter;
(ii) Fundamental signs of a technical solution stated in an application or protection title shall be expressed within the scope (request) of protection of the invention;
(iii) Fundamental signs of a technical solution stated in other documents shall be expressed and detected according to the description document or the actual form of such technical solution.
e) Conclusion on the novelty of the technical solution
Corresponding to a point of the scope (request) of protection, the technical solution stated in the application shall be considered new compared to the world's technical level if:
(i) No control technical solution is found during the information search; or (ii) A control technical solution is found but the technical solution stated in the application has at least one fundamental sign not present in the control technical solution (and such sign is called a different fundamental sign).
25.6 Assessment of creativity level under Article 61 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) Minimum mandatory information sources

When assessing the creativity of technical solutions, it is required to at least search information in compulsory sources (but not limited to such a minimum source as prescribed in Point 25.5.a of this Circular).
b) Assess creativity level
The assessment of the level of creativity of technical solutions stated in the application shall be carried out by assessing fundamental distinctive signs (signs) stated within the scope (requirements) of protection in order to make a conclusion that: (i) Fundamental distinctive signs (signs) have been considered as having been disclosed in the mandatory minimum information source or not, and;
(ii) Collecting other basic distinctive signs which are considered obvious to persons with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field or not.
In response to a point within the scope of (requested) protection, a technical solution shall be considered creative if the addition of a distinctive basic sign to the collection of basic signs of the technical solution is the result of creative activity and not the obvious result of common knowledge in the relevant technical field.
c/ In the following cases (but not only in these cases), for a point within the protection scope, a technical solution shall be considered not being creative:
(i) A collection of other obviously distinctive fundamental signs (anyone with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field would know that in order to perform a given function or achieve a given purpose, it is necessary to use such a collection of signs and vice versa when using such a collection of signs, it is necessary to achieve the corresponding purpose or perform the corresponding function); (ii) A collection of distinct fundamental signs which have been presented in the form of uniformity or equivalence in one or several known technical solutions in the mandatory minimum information source; (iii) Technical solutions are simple combinations of known technical solutions with functions, purposes and effectiveness, which are also simple combinations of functions, purposes and effectiveness of each known technical solution.
d) In this point:
(i) Two signs shall be considered identical if they have the same nature;
(ii) Two signs shall be considered similar if they have similar nature and the same purpose and method of achieving the basic purpose.
25.7 Examination of the first-to-file principle as prescribed in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property Before issuing a decision to grant a protection title, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the first-to-file principle for invention registration applications which have been substantively appraised and concluded to satisfy the protection conditions according to the following regulations:
a) Minimum compulsory information sources
In order to examine the first-to-file principle, it is required to search information in the following compulsory sources (but not limited to such minimum sources):
All invention registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property (by the time of examination) with the same classification index with the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application currently undergoing appraisal - up to the third sub-classification index and with an earlier filing date or priority date than the filing date or priority date of the application currently undergoing appraisal (if the application enjoys priority rights) that has not yet been published or with a later publication date than the filing date or priority date of the application currently undergoing appraisal (if the application enjoys priority rights);
b) The purpose of the search is to find (applications) for registration of the same invention and determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date;
c) In case there are many applications for registration of the same invention, the protection title may only be granted to the valid application with the earliest priority date or filing date among the applications satisfying the conditions for the grant of a protection title;
d) In case there are many applications for registration of the same invention which meet all conditions for grant of a protection title and have the same priority date or the same earliest filing date, the protection title may only be granted to one of such applications as agreed upon by all applicants; otherwise, all applications shall be rejected from the grant of a protection title;
e) In case an applicant for registration of an invention requests the priority right on the basis of the first application for registration of the same invention filed in Vietnam, the protection title may only be granted to the application for registration of the invention requesting the priority right accepted and the first application filed in Vietnam shall be considered withdrawn.

25.8 Notification of content appraisal results
The notification of content appraisal results of an invention registration application shall be carried out according to the general procedures specified at Point 15.7.a of this Circular.

Grant, registration and publication of invention patents and utility solution patents The procedures for issuing decisions on grant, registration and publication of invention patents and utility solution patents shall comply with the general procedures specified at Points 18 and 19 of this Circular.
27. Processing of international applications for inventions
27.1 Receipt of applications
The agency competent to receive international applications for inventions in Vietnam is the National Office of Intellectual Property. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall take responsibility:
a) Receiving international applications originating in Vietnam;
b) Collecting fees for sending international applications and notifying fees as prescribed to enable applicants to transfer them to International Office and International Searching Agency under provisions of the Agreement on Cooperation in Inventions - PCT (hereinafter referred to as "the Agreement");
c) Checking whether fees are paid on time or not;
d) Examining and processing international applications originating in Vietnam according to the provisions of the Agreement;
e) Determining subjects of protection requests: if subjects of protection requests of the applications are classified as national secrets, further work shall not be carried out and fees shall be refunded to applicants, except for fees for sending and fees for copying international applications;
g) Sending one copy (application dossier) of the international application originating in Vietnam to the International Office and one copy (reference copy) to the International Searching Agency;
h) Sending and receiving letters from applicants and from international agencies.
27.2 Language
International applications originating in Vietnam filed with the National Office of Intellectual Property must be made in English. Each application shall be made in 03 copies.
In case the number of copies is not enough, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall make additional copies to meet the required number and the applicant shall pay an international application copying fee.
27.3 International search agencies and preliminary examination agencies The competent international search agencies and preliminary examination agencies for international applications originating in Vietnam are the Inventions Agency, the Industrial Property Agency or the Intellectual Property Agency of Austria, Switzerland, Germany, Italy, Japan, South Korea and the European Inventions Agency.

27.4 International applications with designation of Vietnam
a/ If an international application contains a designation of Vietnam, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall be designated as the agency. In this case, in order to enter the national stage, within 31 months after the priority date, the applicant shall submit to the National Office of Intellectual Property:
(i) An invention registration declaration, made according to form 01-SC in Annex A of this Circular; (ii) A copy of the international application (if the applicant requests entry into the national stage before the international publication);
(iii) The Vietnamese translation of the international application: the description, including the description, protection request, notes on drawings and summary (publication or original submission if the application has not yet been published and the amended version and explanation of the amended part if the international application is amended under Article 19 of the Treaty); (iv) National fees and charges.
b/ An international application filed with the National Office of Intellectual Property within 6 months after the expiration of the time limit prescribed at Point 27.4.a above may be accepted provided that the applicant pays the fees and charges as prescribed.
27.5 International applications with selection of Vietnam
a/ If Vietnam is selected in an international application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall be the selected agency. In this case, if Vietnam is selected within 19 months from the priority date so as to enter the national stage, within 31 months from the priority date, the applicant shall submit to the National Office of Industrial Property the following documents:
(i) The invention registration declaration, made according to Form No. 01-SC in Annex A of this Circular;

(ii) The Vietnamese translation of the international application: the description, including the description, protection request, notes on drawings and summary (the initial published or original copy, if the application has not yet been published, and the amended version and explanation of the amended part, if the international application is amended under Article 19 and/or Article 34(2)(b) of the Treaty);
(iii) The Vietnamese translation of the appendixes of the preliminary examination report (if there is a request for examination of the contents of the application);
(iv) National fees and charges.
b/ An international application filed with the National Office of Industrial Property within 6 months after the expiration of the time limit prescribed at Point 27.5.a above may be accepted, provided that the applicant pays the fee under regulations.
27.6 Priority right claim
In order to enjoy the priority right, the applicant must re-state it in the declaration, pay the fee for claiming the priority right and, at the request of the National Office of Industrial Property, submit the Vietnamese translation of the documents already submitted to the International Office of the necessary documents according to Rule 17.1(a) of the Regulations on Implementation of the Treaty.
For PCT applications, the handling of requests for priority right in accordance with the PCT Convention and the Regulations on implementation of the Convention.
27.7 Handling of international applications in the national phase
a) Amendment to documents in the national phase
In accordance with Rule 51bis of the Regulations on implementation of the Convention, the applicant shall submit a power of attorney or transfer of the right to file an application in the international phase (if any) within 34 months from the priority date.
In accordance with Article 28 and Article 41 of the Convention and Rules 52.1(b) and 78.1(b) of the Regulations on implementation of the Convention, the applicant may amend or supplement documents of the application in the national phase. At the time of entering the national phase, the applicant may also amend or supplement the description. The above amendment or supplementation must comply with Point 17 of this Circular.
Additional documents and amendments submitted by the applicant to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam must be made in Vietnamese.
b) Starting time of the national phase
The starting time of the handling of international applications with designated Vietnam or selected Vietnam in the national phase shall be the first day of the 32nd month from the priority date, if the applicant does not have a written request for early entry into the national phase earlier than the above time limit.
c) Examination of international applications
After entering the national stage, an international application shall be examined both formally and substantively according to the procedures applicable to ordinary invention registration applications. If the applicant submits a written request for early examination of the application and pays the fee as prescribed, the international application shall be examined ahead of schedule in accordance with Point 27.7.b above and Article 23(2) of the Agreement.
d) International applications considered to be withdrawn
Apart from cases considered to be withdrawn under the provisions of the Agreement and the Implementing Regulations of the Agreement, in cases where the national fees are not paid to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam or there is no Vietnamese translation after the prescribed time limit, international applications designated or selected to be processed in Vietnam shall be considered to be withdrawn.
27.8 International registration fees and charges
a) Applicants who submit international applications at the national stage shall pay fees and charges as prescribed for invention registration applications submitted directly in Vietnam.
b) Applicants submitting international applications of Vietnamese origin shall pay fees and charges as prescribed by the Implementing Regulations of the Agreement and the Circular on guidelines for collection, payment, management and use of industrial property fees and charges issued by the Ministry of Finance.

28.5 Requirements for declarations
The applicant shall submit 02 declarations made according to Form No. 02-TKBT prescribed in Annex A of this Circular.

28.6 Requirements for sets of photos or sets of layout design drawings
a) General requirements: The set of photos and drawings of layout design must fully present the spatial structure of components and their interconnections in semi-conducting integrated circuits so that based on such set of photos/drawings, only one unique layout design can be identified. For the above-mentioned purposes, the set of photos/drawings of layout design must satisfy the conditions specified in Points 28.6.b, c and d below.
b) Document type:
The set of photos/drawings of layout design must include at least one of the following three types of documents: (i) Drawings made by a layout-designing machine for each layer of the integrated circuit;
(ii) Drawings or photos of the mask engraved for production of each layer of the integrated circuit;
(iii) Photos of each layer of the layout design shown in the integrated circuit;
c) Document form: The set of photos/drawings of layout design must be submitted in paper form and may be accompanied by supplementary documents being objects carrying electronic data of part or all of such set of photos/drawings.
d) Form of documents
(i) The set of photos/drawings of layout design must include separate photos/drawings for each layer of the layout design, with symbols of each layer, size of the integrated circuit and magnification; (ii) All photos/drawings of layout design must be under the same scale. For paper documents: each photo/drawing of layout design must be a magnified copy of the smallest size of the integrated circuit so that the basic circuit design can be seen by the naked eye;
(iii) Each photo or drawing of layout design may be presented on an A4 paper sheet or a larger paper sheet provided that it is folded into an A4 size;
(iv) The photo or drawing of the layout design must be clear and sharp.
28.7 Requirements for samples of integrated circuits produced under layout designs
a) The submitted samples must be the whole or part of the integrated circuit corresponding to the layout design stated in the application. If the integrated circuit produced under a layout design is an integral part of another product, the product enclosed with the application must contain documents indicating the integrated circuit produced under such a layout design.
b) If the layout design has been commercially exploited anywhere in the world before the filing date, the applicant must submit the sample of the first commercially exploited one.
28.8 Requirements for descriptions of semiconductor integrated circuits produced under layout designs The description must include the following detailed information about the semiconductor integrated circuit produced under a layout design requested for protection:
a) Name/symbol: a collection of letters and/or numbers used to distinguish this integrated circuit from other integrated circuits when put on the market;

b) Description of basic functions of the integrated circuit (e.g., memory function or logic function or other functions);
c) Description of fundamental structure of integrated circuit (e.g., bipolar or MOS, BiMOS, optoelectronic or other structures);
d) Description of technology for manufacturing integrated circuits (e.g., TTL, DTL, ECL, ILT, CMOS, NMOS, PMOS or other technologies);
e) Description of principal characteristics distinguishing from other semi-conducting integrated circuits on the market at the time of filing the application or at the time of first commercial exploitation in the world, whichever is earlier.
29. Confidentiality of information in layout design registration applications
Applicants may request confidentiality of information filed in their layout design registration applications as follows:
29.1 Maximum level of confidentiality is allowed:
a) For layout designs not yet commercially exploited: 50% of surface of each layer;
b) For layout designs already commercially exploited: 2 layers in each group of 5 layers from top to bottom.

29.2 To have information kept confidential, applicants must file requests for confidentiality of information under guidance of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and must indicate documents and materials containing confidential information.

29.3 Documents containing confidential information must be separated into confidential document packages and may be submitted in the following forms:
a) Microfilm or similar form of documents showing size of design on computer drawings;
b) Electronic data;
c) Drawings or photos with invisible parts, provided that characteristics of layout design are basically visible.
29.4 The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall be responsible for keeping confidential information at the request of applicants in accordance with Point 29.1 of this Circular.
30. Format appraisal of layout design registration applications
30.1 Procedures for format appraisal of layout design registration applications shall comply with general provisions in Points 13.1, 13.2, 13.3, 13.4 and 13.8 of this Circular and specific procedures applicable to layout design registration applications prescribed in this Point.
30.2 Notification of format appraisal results
a) If the application falls under one of the cases prescribed in Point 13.2 of this Circular or contains deficiencies prescribed in Point 13.3 of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall carry out the procedures prescribed in Point 13.6.a of this Circular.
b) If the application is valid, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send a notice of acceptance to the applicant, specifying the name and address of the applicant, the name of the representative (if any), and information about the subject matter stated in the application, the submission date, and clearly stating whether the subject matter stated in the application may be granted a layout design registration certificate if, after 3 months from the date on which the layout design is published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette, there is no legitimate objection from a third party against the registration of such layout design.
30.3 Refusal to accept applications
In cases where the applicant has been notified by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam of the results of the formal examination, including deficiencies and the intended refusal to accept the application as prescribed in Point 13.6.a of this Circular, but the applicant fails to correct deficiencies or provides inadequate corrections, or does not have any objection or provides an unjustifiable objection within the prescribed time limit, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant of the refusal to accept the layout design registration application and refund fees and charges already paid related to the post-formal examination work at the request of the applicant.
31. Publication of layout design registration applications
31.1 Layout design registration applications that have been accepted as valid shall be published according to the general procedures specified in Point 14 of this Circular and the provisions of this Point.
31.2 Access to detailed information about valid layout design registration applications

a) From the date on which the application is published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette, everyone may access detailed information about the nature of the layout design stated in the published application, except for information that is kept confidential under Point 29 of this Circular.
b) Only agencies competent to carry out procedures for invalidation of protection titles or procedures for handling acts of infringement upon rights to new layout designs are permitted to access confidential information about layout designs.
32. Issuance, registration and publication of decisions on issuance of certificates of registered layout design

32.1 Notification of intended issuance of a certificate of registered layout design If, within 03 months from the date on which the layout design is published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette, there is no third party’s objection to the registration of the layout design or, despite having an objection, the handling results prove that the objection is unjustified, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the intended issuance of a certificate of registered layout design for the subject matter stated in the application; the time limit for the applicant to pay the fee for publication of the protection title, the registration fee and the protection title issuance fee.

The above time limit is 01 month from the date of notification.
32.2 Issuance, registration and publication of decisions on issuance of certificates of registered layout design The procedures for issuance, registration and publication of certificates of registered layout design shall be carried out according to general procedures prescribed in Points 18.2 and 19 of this Circular.
32.3 Refusal to issue certificates of registered layout design
If, within 03 months from the date on which the layout design is published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette, there is a third party’s objection to the issuance of a certificate of registered layout design and such objection is proved to be justifiable, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall carry out the procedure for refusal to issue a certificate of registered layout design according to general procedures prescribed in Point 18.1 of this Circular.
Section 4. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF INDUSTRIAL DESIGNS
33. Requirements for applications for registration of industrial designs
33.1 An application for registration of an industrial design must satisfy the general requirements prescribed in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements prescribed in this Point.
33.2 An application for registration of an industrial design must ensure uniformity as prescribed in Clauses 1 and 3 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following regulations.
An industrial design registration application shall be considered as ensuring uniformity if:
a/ It requests protection for an industrial design of a product; or b/ It requests protection for an industrial design of many products in a set of products, each of which has an industrial design; or c/ It requests protection for an industrial design of a product accompanied by one or more variants of such industrial design.
33.3 Information provision request
a/ In case there are grounds (information and evidence) to suspect the authenticity of information in an industrial design registration application, the National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant within 1 month to submit documents verifying such information, especially documents certifying the lawful registration right if the applicant inherits the submission right from another person (certificate of inheritance right, certificate of transfer of the right to file an application or written agreement on assignment of the right to file an application; assignment contract or labor contract, etc.);
b/ The National Office of Intellectual Property may also request the applicant within 1 month to submit documents certifying the ownership or lawful use right of commercial indications (marks, geographical indications, trade names), protected industrial designs of others if there are grounds to suspect that the industrial designs stated in the application contain such objects.
33.4 Request for declarations
The applicant shall submit 2 declarations made according to Form No. 03-KDCN in Annex A to this Circular. In addition to other declaration items, the declaration must indicate the international classification code of the industrial design requested for protection in accordance with the International Classification of Industrial Designs (according to the Locarno Agreement). If the applicant fails to classify or improperly classify, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall classify it and the applicant shall pay a classification fee under regulations.
33.5 Request for descriptions of industrial designs
The applicant shall submit one description of the industrial design, including the following contents:

a) Name of the industrial design: the name of the product bearing the industrial design, expressed in common words, not for advertising purposes, without commercial signs, annotations or indications;
b) Field of use of the industrial design: the specific field of use of the product bearing the industrial design, clearly stating the intended use of such product;
c) Closest similar industrial design: clearly stating the industrial design that is least different from the industrial design of the same type of product stated in the application and widely known before the filing date or the priority date (if the application requests priority rights), indicating the source of information disclosing the closest similar industrial design;
d) List of photos or drawings: a list of perspective photos, three-dimensional drawings, projections, cross-sections, etc., of the industrial design, consistent with the serial numbers of the photos and drawings;
e) The description of the industrial design must comply with the following regulations:
(i) Fully disclose the nature of the industrial design requested for protection, clearly stating all design characteristics expressing the nature of the industrial design, and at the same time, indicating new and distinctive design characteristics compared to the closest similar industrial design as prescribed in Point 33.5.c above, consistent with the design characteristics shown in the set of photos and drawings;
(ii) Design characteristics of the industrial design requested for protection must be presented in the following order: geometric features, lines, correlation between geometric features and/or lines, color characteristics (if any);
(iii) For products with different usage states (e.g., products with covers or foldable covers), the industrial design of the product in different states must be described; (iv) If the industrial design consists of multiple schemes, it is required to specify the distinctive features of the remaining schemes compared to the basic scheme (the first one mentioned in the application); (v) If the industrial design is the design of a set of products, the design of each product in the set must be described.
g) Scope of protection of the industrial design (or "protection request"): The list of necessary and sufficient design features must be provided to determine the nature of the industrial design requested for protection and the scope of industrial property rights to such industrial design, which is shown on photos and drawings mentioned in the application, including new and distinctive features compared to similar known industrial designs.

33.6 Requirements for sets of photos and drawings of industrial designs The applicant must submit 05 sets of photos or 05 sets of drawings of the industrial design. The sets of photos and drawings must fully present the design features of the industrial design requested for protection, based on which any person with average knowledge in the relevant field can identify such industrial design according to the following guidelines:
a) Photos and drawings must be clear and sharp; drawings must be drawn with continuous lines; the background of photos and drawings must have a uniform color and contrast with the industrial design; on photos and drawings, only products bearing the industrial design requested for protection shall be shown (without other products).
b) Photos and drawings must show the industrial design at the same scale. The size of the industrial design in photos and drawings must not be smaller than 90mm x 120mm and not larger than 190mm x 277mm.
c) Photos and drawings must show the industrial design in the same orientation and in the following order: perspective views (three-dimensional) of the industrial design, projections of the industrial design from the front, back, right, left, top and bottom; the projections must be presented in the frontal view.
d) For a symmetrical industrial design, photos or drawings may not need to further show the symmetry, provided that such fact is clearly stated in the list of photos and drawings included in the description.
e) For an industrial design of a product which can be developed (e.g. a container, packaging material, etc.), the views of the industrial design may be replaced by photos or drawings of the industrial design in its developed state.
g) Depending on the complexity of the industrial design, it may be necessary to have additional perspective photos or drawings from different angles, cross-sections, enlarged parts, separate details of the product, etc., sufficient to clearly show the new and distinctive features of the industrial design subject to protection.
h) For a product with different use states (e.g. a product with a lid or a foldable cover), there must be photos and drawings of the industrial design of the product in different states.

i) For the industrial design of a complete product part, there must be additional photos and drawings illustrating the installation and use of the part on the complete product.
k) Each scheme of an industrial design must have a set of photos and drawings fully showing each scheme according to the provisions of this Point.
l) For a set of products, there must be perspective drawings of the entire set of products and a set of photos and drawings of each product in the set according to the provisions of this Point.
33.7 Design features of industrial designs
a) Design features of an industrial design means elements expressed in the form of lines, shapes, colors, position correlation or size correlation so that when combined with other characteristics (signs), they form a necessary and sufficient set to create such industrial design.
b) The following elements shall not be considered as design features of an industrial design: (i) Shapes and lines determined by the technical function of the product (e.g., the flat shape of a data recording disk determined by the relative movement between the disk and the reader...);
(ii) Elements whose presence in the set of signs is insufficient to cause aesthetic impression (the appearance of the product remains unchanged whether or not such elements are present; e.g., changes to a familiar shape or line but such changes are insufficient to be recognized, therefore the changed shape or line is still only recognized as the old one); (iii) Materials used for manufacturing the product;
(iv) Signs affixed or stuck on the product are only for the purpose of providing information or instructions about the origin, characteristics, composition, uses, etc. of the product; e.g., words on the label;
(v) Size of the product, except for cases where the decorative size of fabric samples and similar materials is changed.
c) Fundamental design features and non-fundamental design features Fundamental design features are easily recognizable/rememberable features necessary and sufficient to determine the industrial design and distinguish one industrial design from another for products of the same type.
Non-fundamental design features that fail to meet the above conditions are called "non-fundamental design features".
34. Formal examination and publication of applications for registration of industrial designs Procedures for formal examination and publication of applications for registration of industrial designs shall comply with the general procedures prescribed in Points 13 and 14 of this Circular.
35. Content appraisal of industrial design registration applications
35.1 Assessment of similarity between industrial designs:
a) Two industrial designs shall be considered identical when they are used for products of the same type and have the same collection of fundamental and non-fundamental design characteristics;
b) Two industrial designs shall be considered similar when they are used for products of the same type and have some fundamental design characteristics being the same;
c) Two industrial designs shall be considered most similar when they have more fundamental design characteristics being the same than all other similar industrial designs.
35.2 Order of carrying out procedures for content appraisal of industrial design registration applications The content appraisal of industrial design registration applications shall be carried out in accordance with the general order specified in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific provisions at this Point.
35.3 Assessment of compatibility between subject matters stated in applications and types of industrial design protection titles
Subject matters stated in applications shall be considered incompatible with types of industrial design protection titles if:
a) The subject matter is not the appearance of the product;
b) The subject matter stated in the application is:
(i) The appearance of the product which is required by its technical characteristics; (ii) The appearance of a civil or industrial construction work;

(iii) The internal appearance (the invisible part) during the use of the product (exploitation of the utility of the product in the usual way, performed by any user, excluding maintenance, repair, or repair of the product).
35.4 Information lookup
a) Purpose of information lookup
The purpose of information lookup is to search among the mandatory minimum sources of information for industrial designs identical or similar to those stated in the application.
b) Mandatory minimum sources of information used during the content appraisal of the application include the following documents:
(i) Industrial design registration applications that have been received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and have an earlier publication date than the submission date or priority date of the application currently undergoing appraisal (if the application is eligible for priority rights);
(ii) Applications for registration of industrial designs and protection titles of industrial designs filed by other organizations or countries within 25 years before the filing date or the priority date of the application which is being examined (if the application enjoys priority right) shall be kept in the existing database on industrial designs at the National Office of Intellectual Property;
(iii) Other information relating to industrial designs collected and stored by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam;
(iv) The industrial design registration applications received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with submission dates or priority dates earlier than those of the applications currently under appraisal (used to check the first-to-file rule prescribed in Point 35.9 of this Circular).
c) In necessary and possible cases, the search may be expanded beyond the minimum compulsory sources of information.
35.5 Search report
Search results must be presented in search reports, specifying the searched field, scope of search and search results within such scope (statistics and identification of found control industrial designs, information sources, date of publication of corresponding information) and full names of report preparers (searchers).
In this point, “industrial design used for comparison” means an industrial design identical or similar to the industrial design stated in the application, which is compared with the industrial design stated in the application when assessing novelty and creativity.
35.6 Assessment of industrial applicability of industrial designs according to Article 67 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) Industrial designs stated in applications shall be considered having industrial applicability if persons with average knowledge in the respective fields can use such industrial designs as samples for manufacturing products with appearance identical to such industrial designs by using industrial or handicraft methods.
The concept of "person with average knowledge" in the respective field is understood according to Point 23.6.a of this Circular.
b) In the following cases, subjects stated in applications shall be considered having no industrial applicability:
(i) Subjects stated in applications are forms of products with unstable existence states (products in solid form, liquids, etc.);
(ii) Only products with forms similar to those stated in applications can be created by using special skills or it is impossible to repeatedly create products with forms similar to those stated in applications; (iii) Other cases with legitimate reasons.
35.7 Assessment of novelty of industrial designs according to Article 65 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
a) Method of assessing novelty of industrial designs
In order to evaluate the novelty of the industrial design stated in the application, it is required to compare the set of fundamental design characteristics of such industrial design with the set of fundamental design characteristics of the closest identical/similar industrial design used as the control industrial design found during the information search.

b) Conclusion on the novelty of the industrial design
The industrial design stated in the application shall be considered novel if:
(i) No control industrial design is found in the mandatory information sources; or (ii) Although a control industrial design is found in the mandatory information sources, the industrial design stated in the application has at least one fundamental design characteristic not present in (not belonging to) the set of fundamental design characteristics of the control industrial design, or (iii) The control industrial design is actually the industrial design stated in the application that was disclosed/announced and falls under the cases specified in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 65 of the Law on Intellectual Property. 35.8 Evaluation of the creativity of industrial designs according to Article 66 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) Method for evaluating the creativity of industrial designs
In order to evaluate the creativity of the industrial design stated in the application, it is required to compare the set of fundamental design characteristics of such industrial design with the set of fundamental design characteristics of each identical or similar control industrial design found during the information search.
b) Conclusion on the creativity of the industrial design
In the following cases, the industrial design stated in the application shall be considered non-creative:
(i) Industrial design means a simple combination of known design features (design features that have been publicly disclosed and arranged or assembled in a simple manner such as replacement, change of position, increase or decrease in quantity, etc.);
(ii) Industrial design means a shape that is partially or entirely replicated or simulated from the inherent natural shape of trees, fruits, animals, etc., or the shape of geometric figures (circle, ellipse, triangle, square, rectangle, regular polygon, prism with cross-section being one of the above shapes) that are widely known;
(iii) Industrial design means a simple replication of the shape of a product or work that is well known or widely recognized in Vietnam or the world;
(iv) Industrial design means a simulation of an industrial design in another field if such simulation has been widely recognized in reality (for example: toy simulating cars, motorcycles, etc.). If not falling into the above cases, the industrial design shall be considered creative.

35.9 Examination of the first-to-file principle according to Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) To examine the first-to-file principle, it is required to search information in the mandatory sources specified at Point 35.4.b(iv) of this Circular.
b) The industrial design stated in the application shall be considered satisfying the first-to-file principle if no identical or significantly different industrial design is found in the applications that meet the conditions for the issuance of the industrial design patent obtained during the information search process.
c) The industrial design of a product part stated in the application shall also be considered satisfying the first-to-file principle if no identical or significantly different industrial design of the product part and/or product stated in the applications that meet the conditions for the issuance of the industrial design patent obtained during the information search process is found.
d) In case there are many different applications for registration of industrial designs which are identical or insignificantly different from each other, and all of them satisfy the conditions for grant of an industrial design patent and have the earliest priority date or earliest filing date, the industrial design stated in the application shall still be considered to satisfy the first-to-file principle prescribed in Clause 2 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property if all applicants reach agreement on the name of the applicant in a single application for grant of an industrial design patent.
35.10 Notification of content appraisal results
The notification of content appraisal results of an industrial design registration application shall comply with Point 15.7.a of this Circular.
36. Grant, registration and publication of industrial design patents

Procedures for issuing decisions on grant, registration and publication of industrial design patents shall comply with general procedures prescribed in Points 18 and 19 of this Circular.
Section 5. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF MARKS
37. Requirements for mark registration applications
37.1 A mark registration application must meet the general requirements regarding application documents prescribed in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and specific requirements prescribed in this Point.
37.2 The application shall ensure consistency with Clause 1 and Clause 4 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property. Each application may request registration of one mark for one or more goods or services.

37.3 In case of doubt about the authenticity of information stated in the application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam may request the applicant to submit the following documents within 01 month to verify such information:
a) Documents proving the applicant’s status:
(i) Business registration certificate, contract or other documents confirming the production of products or provision of services by the applicant according to Clause 1 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; (ii) Agreement or letter confirming that the manufacturer does not use the mark and does not object to the mark registration by the person conducting commercial activities of the manufacturer according to Clause 2 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(iii) Decision or establishment license, charter of the organization confirming the function and authority to manage collective marks, quality certification marks, geographical origin marking marks for goods or services according to Clause 3 and Clause 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; (iv) Agreement, business registration certificate, and documents related to the mark registration of co-owners according to Clause 5 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(v) Documents confirming that the applicant has received the mark registration right from another person according to Clause 6 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(vi) Agreement or letter of consent of the mark owner confirming the mark registration right of the representative or agent according to Clause 7 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property and Article 6septies of the Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property.
b) Documents proving the representative status of the applicant: original power of attorney of the applicant; documents confirming that the representative of the applicant is the legal representative of the mark registration organization or the person authorized by such organization; documents confirming that the authorized person of the applicant meets the requirements to act as the representative of the applicant as prescribed in Point 3 of this Circular.
c) Documents proving the right to use/register marks containing special signs for: (i) Names, symbols, flags, and badges of domestic and international agencies and organizations, or certification seals, inspection seals, warranty seals of international organizations as prescribed in Clauses 2 and 4 Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(ii) Names of characters and images of works under copyright protection that are widely known, or trade names, trade indications, origin indications, prizes, medals, or distinctive symbols of certain products that may cause confusion as prescribed in Clause 5 Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(iii) Signs under industrial design protection of others as prescribed in Point n Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
d) Documents proving priority rights;
e) Information necessary to clarify or confirm the contents stated in the regulations on use of collective marks, regulations on use of certification marks, or other application documents.
37.4 Requirements for declarations
The applicant must submit 02 declarations made according to Form No. 04-NH specified in Appendix A of this Circular, with the following notes:
a) The description of a mark in the declaration must specify the type of registered mark (common mark, collective mark, integrated mark, certification mark);
b) For a mark registered as an integrated mark, the applicant must specify the integrated elements of the mark or of goods or services in accordance with the following provisions:
(i) In case the integrated element is a mark (similar to another mark used by the same applicant for the same goods or service or for similar goods or services), it must be specified whether among such integrated marks, there is any basic mark or not, if so, which one is it; if one or several of these marks have been registered or mentioned in a previous application, the protection title number and the previous application number must be specified; (ii) In case the integrated element is a good or service (one mark used for similar or related goods or services), it must be specified whether among such goods or services, there is any basic one or not, if so, which one is it; if one of such goods or services has been registered or mentioned in a previous application, the protection title number and the previous application number must be specified;
(iii) If the applicant fails to specify the basic mark or basic goods or service, all marks and all goods or services related to the mark stated in the application shall be considered independent of each other. The assessment of distinguishability of the mark stated in the application shall not be subject to the exception for integrated marks prescribed at Point e, Clause 2, Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property but must comply with the general provisions on assessment of distinguishability prescribed at Point 39 of this Circular.
c) For a certification mark, the applicant must specify in the declaration the purpose, contents and method of certification (certifying what: quality, origin, geographical origin or combination of these purposes; certification contents: specific conditions on the subject, goods or service; certification how: the order and procedures for grant of certification license, methods of inspection and maintenance of certification purpose and contents).
d) The declaration must contain the sample of mark and description in words of such mark according to the following regulations:
(i) If the mark is composed of multiple elements, it must specify the elements and their combination; if the mark contains graphic elements, it must clearly state the content and meaning of such graphic elements;
(ii) If the protection request for a color mark is made, it must specify such request and name of the color shown on the mark;
(iii) If the mark contains letters or words other than Vietnamese, it must specify the Vietnamese pronunciation of such letters or words and, if such letters or words have meanings, they must be translated into Vietnamese;

(iv) If the mark contains numerals other than Arabic numerals or Roman numerals, it must be translated into Arabic numerals.
e) The section "List of goods and services bearing the mark" in the declaration must be grouped in accordance with the International Classification of Goods and Services under the Nice Agreement published by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam in the Industrial Property Gazette.
37.5 Requirements for sample of mark
In addition to the sample of mark affixed on the declaration, the application must be accompanied by 09 identical samples of mark which must meet the following requirements:
a) The sample of mark must be presented clearly with the size of each component of the sample not exceeding 80mm and not smaller than 8mm, the overall sample must be presented in the sample frame with size of 80mm x 80mm printed on the declaration;
b) For a three-dimensional mark, the mark sample must be accompanied by a photograph or drawing showing the perspective view and may also be accompanied by a descriptive model in the projection form;
c) For a mark with color protection requirements, the mark sample must be presented in the exact colors required for protection. If no color protection is required, the mark sample must be presented in black and white.
37.6 Requirements on regulations on use of collective marks and certification marks Regulations on use of collective marks and certification marks must contain the contents corresponding to those prescribed in clauses 4 and 5 Article 105 of the Law on Intellectual Property and must clarify the following issues:
a) Summary information on the mark, mark owner, goods or service bearing the mark;
b) Conditions for obtaining permission from the mark registrant to use the mark and conditions for terminating the right to use the mark;
c) Obligations of the mark user (ensuring quality and particular characteristics of goods or services bearing the mark, subject to the control of the mark registrant, paying mark management fees, etc.);

d) Rights of the mark registrant (controlling compliance with regulations on use of the mark, collecting mark management fees, terminating the right to use the mark of persons who fail to meet the conditions prescribed in regulations on use of the mark, etc.);
e) Mechanisms for licensing, controlling and inspecting the use of the mark and ensuring the quality and reputation of goods or services bearing the mark;
g) Dispute settlement mechanism.
37.7. Requirements on documents certifying the permission to register and use geographical origin marking trademarks
a/ For a geographical origin marking, in addition to the regulations on use of the mark and necessary documents evidencing the registration right, the application must be enclosed with a license from the concerned local authority permitting the applicant to register the geographical origin marking (geographical name, symbol or map of the area or locality) for goods or services bearing such mark.
b/ In case it is impossible to identify a region or locality bearing a geographical origin indication based on the administrative boundaries and license of the local authority specified at Point 37.7.a above, the National Office of Industrial Property shall request the applicant to provide a geographical map clearly indicating the scope of the region or locality bearing the geographical origin indication of goods or services with certification by the concerned local authority.
38. Formal examination and publication of mark registration applications
The procedures for formal examination and publication of mark registration applications shall comply with the general provisions prescribed in clauses 13 and 14 of this Circular.
39. Substantive examination of applications for registration of marks
39.1 The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall be responsible for conducting content appraisal of valid applications in accordance with the general procedures specified in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific procedures prescribed in this Point.
39.2 Assessment of the conformity between subject matters stated in applications for mark registration and requirements for grant of mark registration certificates
a/ Under Clause 1, Article 72 of the Law on Intellectual Property, signs registered as marks must be visible signs in the form of letters, numerals, words, drawings or images, including three-dimensional figures or combinations thereof, expressed in one or more colors.
b/ The following types of signs shall not be protected as marks:
(i) Signs which are merely colors and not combined with signs in the form of letters or figures or not expressed in the form of signs in the form of letters or figures;
(ii) Signs which fall within the subject matters ineligible for protection as marks under Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(iii) Signs which are contrary to social order or harmful to national security.
39.3 Assessment of the distinguishability of signs in the form of written characters or numerals (hereinafter referred to as “written signs”) under Clause 2, Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
Except for exceptions specified at Point 39.5 of this Circular, the following written signs shall be considered indistinguishable:
a) Characters belonging to languages which Vietnamese consumers have common knowledge of which they cannot recognize or remember (cannot read, understand or remember) such as characters not derived from Latin: Arabic, Slavic, Sanskrit, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, etc.; except for characters belonging to the above-mentioned languages accompanied by other components forming an overall whole capable of being distinguished or presented in graphic form or in other special forms;
b) Although they are characters derived from Latin, signs consist of only one letter or only numbers, or although there are two letters but they cannot be read as a word - even when accompanied by numbers; except for cases where these signs are presented in graphic form or in other special forms;
c) A collection of too many letters or words making it impossible to recognize and remember as a string of too many characters not arranged in a definite order or rule, or a document or a paragraph of a document;
d) Although they are characters derived from Latin, they are a word with meaning and the meaning of the word has been widely used and is common in Vietnam in the relevant field to the extent of losing the ability to distinguish;
e) A word or a collection of words used in Vietnam as the common name of the goods or service in question;

g) A word or a collection of words with content describing the main goods or service bearing a trademark as a sign indicating time, location, geographical origin (except for cases where the trademark is registered as a trademark certifying the geographical origin of goods or collective trademark), production method, category, quantity, quality, characteristics (except for cases where the trademark is registered as a trademark certifying the quality of goods or services), composition, utility, value of goods or services;
h) A word or a collection of words with meaning describing the legal form, business sector of the trademark owner;
i) Signs of letters identical or similar to one of the objects within the scope of industrial property rights protection of others according to the provisions of Points e, g, h, i, k, l, m Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
k) Signs that are misinterpreted or misleading consumers about origin, features, uses, quality, value or other characteristics such as composition, production process, raw materials, superiority of goods and services according to Clause 5 Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
l) Signs that are identical or confusingly similar to real names, aliases, pseudonyms of leaders, national heroes, famous personalities of Vietnam or foreign countries; identical or confusingly similar to names of characters, images in works protected by copyright of others that have been widely known, except for cases permitted by owners of such works.
39.4 Assessment of distinguishability of signs in form of drawings and images (hereinafter referred to as “visual signs”) according to Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property
Except for exceptions specified in Point 39.5 of this Circular, visual signs shall be considered indistinguishable if:
a) Visual signs are common geometric shapes such as circles, ellipses, triangles, quadrilaterals, etc., or simple drawings; drawings and images are used only as backgrounds or decorative lines of products or product packages;
b) Drawings and images are too complicated for consumers to easily recognize and remember their characteristics such as having too many combined or overlapping images and lines;
c) Drawings, images, symbols, and signs have been widely used;
d) Drawings and images mainly describe goods or services bearing marks; locations, methods of production, geographical origins, categories, quantity, quality, properties, components, uses, value or other characteristics of goods or services bearing marks;
e) Signs which are identical or insignificantly different from the industrial designs of others;
g) Signs which are identical or confusingly similar to the image of a leader, national hero or famous person of Vietnam or foreign country; identical or confusingly similar to the image of characters and images in works protected by copyright of others who have been widely known, except for cases permitted by the owners of such works.
39.5 The following exceptions shall be applied when assessing the ability to distinguish between letters and figures:
a/ Signs falling into the cases mentioned at Points 39.3.a, b, c, g, h and Points 39.4.a, b, c, d, e of this Circular which have been used with the function of marks and are widely known by consumers, so that these marks have achieved the distinguishing capability for related goods or services.
b/ To be entitled to this exception, the applicant shall provide evidence of the extensive use of such mark (the starting time, scope and extent of current use, etc.) which is considered being used only when such use is made in lawful production, business, commercial or advertising activities, and evidence of the distinguishability of the mark for related goods or services of the mark owner. In this case, such mark shall be recognized as having a distinguishability only when it is shown in the form in which it has been continuously and widely used in practice.
39.6 Assessment of distinctiveness of signs combined between characters and images (hereinafter referred to as “combined signs”)

A combined sign is considered distinguishable when the sign of letters and signs of figures combine into a distinguishable whole, specifically:
a) The letter and figure signs are distinguishable and form an overall distinguishable sign;
b) The strong components of a mark (elements that strongly affect the consumer's perception, causing attention and impression about the mark when observed) are words or graphic signs with distinguishable capability, even though the remaining components have little or no distinguishable capability;
c) In case a combination of words and graphic signs has little or no distinguishable capability but the unique way of combination creates a distinctive impression, such overall combination is still considered distinguishable;
d) A combination of words and graphic signs has little or no distinguishable capability but the overall combination has achieved distinguishable capability through use according to Point 39.5 of this Circular.
39.7 Minimum information sources
a) To assess the confusion-creating capacity of signs stated in an application, at least the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall conduct a search in the following minimum information sources:
(i) Mark registration applications filed with the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with submission dates or priority dates earlier than the submission date or priority date of the application currently being appraised, and international mark registration applications with designation of Vietnam notified by WIPO to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with submission dates or priority dates earlier than the submission date or priority date of the application currently being appraised for identical or similar goods or services;
(ii) Marks registered for protection or recognized for protection that are still valid in Vietnam (including well-known marks) used for identical, similar or related goods or services; (iii) Marks registered which have their validity terminated within 5 years, except for marks whose validity is terminated due to non-use as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 95 of the Law on Intellectual Property, used for identical or similar goods or services;
(iv) Geographical indications currently protected in Vietnam;
(v) Geographical origins of goods and services; geographical names, quality marks, inspection marks; national flags and national emblems of countries; flags, names and symbols of agencies and organizations of Vietnam and the world; names and images of leaders, national heroes, names and images of Vietnamese and foreign celebrities, etc., which are collected and preserved by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
b) In necessary cases, reference sources may be consulted in addition to the minimum sources mentioned in Point 39.7.a above, such as applications for registration of industrial designs, trade names, etc.; 39.8 Assessment of similarity to the point of confusion between a registration request mark and another mark
a) To assess whether a registration request mark stated in an application is identical or similar to the point of confusion with another mark (hereinafter referred to as "control mark"), it is necessary to compare the structure, content, pronunciation (for written signs), meaning and form of expression of the sign (for both written and graphic signs), and at the same time compare goods or services bearing the sign with those bearing the control mark according to the provisions of this Point.
b) Sign identical to the control mark: A sign shall be considered identical to the control mark if it is identical to the control mark in terms of structure, content, meaning and form of expression.
c) A sign shall be considered similar to the point of confusion with the control mark if:
(i) The sign is similar to the control mark in terms of structure or content or pronunciation or meaning or form of expression to the point of confusion, making consumers mistakenly believe that these two objects are one or that this object is a variant of that one or that these two objects have the same origin;
(ii) The sign is merely an transcription or translation from the control mark if the control mark is a well-known mark.
39.9 Assessment of similarity of goods and services
a/ Two goods or two services are regarded as identical (of the same type) when they have the following characteristics:
(i) Having the same nature (components, structure, etc.) and the same function and use purpose; or (ii) Having nearly the same nature and the same function and use purpose;

b/ Two goods or two services are regarded as similar when they have the following characteristics:
(i) Being similar in nature; or (ii) Being similar in function and use purpose; and (iii) Being marketed through the same commercial channel (being distributed by the same mode, being sold together or next to each other in the same kind of shop, etc.);
c/ One good and one service are regarded as similar if they fall into one of the following cases: (i) There is a relationship between them in terms of nature (the one is composed of the other); or (ii) There is a relationship between them in terms of function (the one is used for the performance of the function of the other or they are often used together); or (iii) There is a close relationship between them in terms of mode of performance (the one is the result of the use or exploitation of the other).
39.10 Examination of the principle of first-to-file
Before issuing a decision on grant of a certificate of registered mark, the National Office of Industrial Property shall examine to ensure the principle of first-to-file as provided for in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property.

39.11 Conclusion on the possibility of confusion between signs and control marks Signs are regarded as identical or confusingly similar to control marks used for identical, similar or related goods in the following cases:
(i) Signs are identical to control marks and goods or services bearing signs identical or similar to those bearing control marks;
(ii) Signs identical with the control mark and goods or services identical with those bearing the control mark of the same owner of the mark;
(iii) Signs similar to the point of confusion with the control mark and goods or services bearing signs identical or similar to those bearing the control mark, except for exceptions where similarity in goods or services and similarity in signs is insufficient to create the possibility of confusion when using similar signs;
(iv) Signs identical or similar to the control mark being a well-known mark and goods or services bearing signs not identical or similar to those bearing such mark, but the use of signs as marks may cause consumers to mistakenly believe that there is a relationship between goods or services bearing such signs and the owner of the well-known mark, which may actually reduce the distinguishability of the well-known mark or harm the reputation of the well-known mark.
39.12 Assessment of other points of confusion of signs
The assessment of other points of confusion of signs shall comply with Article 73 and Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following specific regulations.
a) Signs are considered causing confusion regarding the origin of goods or services in the following cases:
(i) Signs identical or similar to the name or symbol of a country or territory (national flag, national emblem, national name, local name), or confusingly similar to the name or symbol of a country or territory, leading consumers to believe that goods or services bearing such marks originate from such country or territory but actually originate from another country or territory; (ii) Signs identical or similar to protected geographical indications where the use of such signs may mislead consumers as to the geographical origin of goods; signs identical to or containing geographical indications or being translated or transcribed from protected geographical indications for wines or spirits if the signs requesting registration as marks for wines or spirits do not originate from the geographical areas bearing such geographical indications;
(iii) Signs being words identical or similar to the trade names of others which have been lawfully used for the same type of goods or services and which may mislead consumers as to the fact that goods or services bearing such signs were produced or provided by persons with such trade names; signs being images identical or similar to commercial symbols of others which have been lawfully used for the same type of goods or services and which may mislead consumers as to the fact that goods or services bearing such signs were produced or provided by persons with such commercial symbols;

(iv) Signs identical or similar to real names, aliases, pen names, or images of Vietnamese or foreign leaders, national heroes, or famous personalities; signs identical or similar to names or images of characters or typical images of works which are widely known if the use of such signs may mislead consumers as to the fact that goods or services bearing such signs were produced or provided by owners of such works;
(v) Signs identical or insignificantly different from industrial designs of others protected on the basis of applications for registration of industrial designs with submission dates or priority dates earlier than those of applications for registration of marks.
b) In the following cases, signs shall be considered likely to cause confusion or misunderstanding as to the nature and value of goods or services:
(i) Signs which are words, drawings, images, symbols, etc., and which create misleading impressions about the features and utility of goods or services, such as signs identical with or similar to marks or other signs widely used to the extent that they are considered associated with a function or utility of a certain type of goods or service, causing consumers to mistakenly believe that goods or services bearing such signs also have such features or utilities;
(ii) Signs which are words or images and which create misleading impressions about the composition or structure of goods or services, such as descriptions of other goods or services related to goods or services bearing such signs, creating the impression that goods or services bearing such signs are composed of or of the same nature as the described goods or services.
40. Granting, registering and publishing mark registration certificates The procedures for issuing decisions on grant, registration and publication of mark registration certificates shall comply with the general procedures prescribed in clauses 18 and 19 of this Circular.
41. Processing applications for international registration of Vietnamese origin marks and applications for international registration of marks designated to Vietnam
41.1 Procedures applicable to applications for international registration of Vietnamese origin marks Regulations on procedures for processing mark registration applications prescribed in this Circular shall also apply to processing applications for international registration of marks at the National Office of Intellectual Property.
41.2 Rights to international registration of marks based on base mark registration in Vietnam
a) Persons who have been granted mark registration certificates in Vietnam may register internationally corresponding marks under the Madrid Agreement;
b) Any person who has filed an application for registration of a mark in Vietnam and is granted a certificate of registration of the mark in Vietnam may apply for international registration of the corresponding mark under the Madrid Protocol. 41.3 Applications for international registration of marks originating from Vietnam
a) An application for international registration of a mark which designates Vietnam as a country to be registered for protection as a member of the Madrid Agreement but does not designate any other country as a member of the Madrid Protocol must be made in French.
b) An application for international registration of a mark which designates at least one country as a member of the Madrid Protocol, including concurrently designates Vietnam as a member of the Madrid Agreement, must be made in English or French.
c) The applicant shall submit a declaration requesting international registration of the mark, made according to Form No. 06-DKQT in Appendix C of this Circular, and an application for international registration of the mark, made according to the form provided free of charge by the National Office of Intellectual Property. The declaration shall specify the countries being members of the Madrid Agreement (which may concurrently be members of the Madrid Protocol) and the country designated as a member of the Madrid Protocol for which the applicant wishes to register for protection of the mark. The application for international registration of the mark must be made by accurately and fully filling in information in the fields reserved for applicants and must be enclosed with the mark samples registered in Vietnam.
d) The applicant shall preliminarily calculate the total fees and charges according to the fee schedule on the application form or may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to notify the exact amount of fees and charges payable to the International Office. The applicant shall directly pay such fees and charges to the International Office and additionally pay relevant charges and fees to the National Office of Intellectual Property.
e) The applicant must ensure that the information declared in the international mark registration application is accurate, including the language and translation, and consistent with the information stated in the certificate of base mark registration or the corresponding base mark registration application. The applicant shall pay all fees related to amendments to the international mark registration application due to inaccurate or inconsistent declarations as notified by the International Office.

g) All correspondence and transactions related to the international trademark registration application shall be conducted through the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall promptly notify the applicant's requests to the International Office and vice versa, and comply with relevant international treaties.
41.4 Agencies receiving applications for international registration of marks of Vietnamese origin
a) An application for international registration of a mark shall be submitted to the International Office through the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall transfer the application within 30 days from the date of receipt of complete and valid documents as prescribed.
b) The date on which the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam receives the application for international registration of a mark shall be considered the filing date if the International Office receives such application within 2 months from the date indicated on the receipt seal of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. If the application is not completed by the applicant for submission to the International Office within the aforementioned time limit, the date of receipt at the International Office shall be considered the filing date.
41.5 Supplementation, amendment and transfer of applications for international registration of marks of Vietnamese origin
a/ After an application is filed with the International Office, all transactions between the applicant and the International Office related to the modification of the name, address or limitation of the list of goods or services stated in the application for international registration of marks, for extension of validity of international mark registrations shall be made according to Form No. 08-SDQT in Annex C of this Circular and through the National Office of Intellectual Property. The applicant shall pay fees and charges for these transactions according to regulations.
b) Owners of marks must, through the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam (or directly request International Office for international mark registration in a country being a signatory to the Madrid Protocol), request International Office to record the transfer of ownership of marks according to international mark registration and comply with relevant international treaties and pay fees and charges as prescribed.
a) After receiving a notice of the International Office on an international mark registration application with designation of Vietnam, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall conduct a substantive examination of the application according to procedures applicable to applications for mark registration submitted directly to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. Within 12 months from the date of the International Office's notice, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall conclude on the protectionability of the mark.
b/ For a mark which is eligible for protection under Vietnamese law, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to accept the protection of the internationally registered mark and publish it in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property and record it in the National Register of Marks within one month after the date of issuance of the decision. The scope (volume) of protection shall be certified according to the contents of the international registration application for the mark already recorded by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and certified by the National Office of Industrial Property.
c) For marks which are ineligible for protection or partially rejected, before the expiry of the above-said 12-month time limit, the National Office of Industrial Property shall send a written notice on rejection of the application to the International Office for notification to the applicant, clearly stating the reason and contents of rejection.
d) Within 3 months after the National Office of Industrial Property sends the rejection notice, the applicant may lodge a complaint about such notice. The procedures for lodging and settling complaints are the same as those applicable to mark registration applications filed directly with the National Office of Industrial Property. Results of complaint settlement shall be notified by the National Office of Industrial Property to the International Office and to the applicant.
e) From the date of international registration of a Vietnamese origin mark recognized, it shall take effect in Vietnam. At the request of the mark owner, the National Office of Industrial Property shall grant a certificate of protection of the internationally registered Vietnamese mark, provided that the requester pays the prescribed fee.
41.7 Extension of validity of internationally registered Vietnamese origin marks Within 6 months before the expiry of the validity of an internationally registered mark (20 years from the filing date of the international registration of a mark in a country being a contracting party to the Madrid Agreement, including also countries being concurrently members of the Madrid Protocol; 10 years from the filing date of the international registration of a mark in a country being a contracting party to the Madrid Protocol), the mark owner shall pay the fee for validity extension of the internationally registered mark according to the notice of the International Office.
a) In case of international registration of a mark in Vietnam by its owner who is a member of the Madrid Protocol but such mark has lost its validity according to Article 9 quinquies of the Madrid Protocol, such owner may file an application for conversion of the mark using Form No. 07-DKCD specified in Appendix C of this Circular with the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to register protection for such mark for part or all of goods or services included in the list of goods and services recorded in the international registration of the invalidated mark.

b) An application for conversion of a mark shall be accepted if it satisfies the following conditions: (i) It is filed within 03 months from the date of international registration of the corresponding mark which has lost its validity;
(ii) The goods or services described in the application for conversion of the mark are included in the list of goods and services recorded in the international registration of ordinary marks;
(iii) The application for conversion of the mark satisfies all requirements regarding form and content as prescribed by Vietnamese law and the applicant fully pays fees and charges as prescribed.
c) The application for conversion of a mark shall have the priority right over the international registration of the corresponding mark (if the international registration of the mark has the priority right according to an international treaty).
d) The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall process the application for conversion of a mark like that of an ordinary mark.
42. Recognition of well-known marks
42.1 Well-known marks are protected under Vietnamese law as prescribed in Article 75 of the Law on Intellectual Property and in accordance with Article 6bis of the Paris Convention for Industrial Property Protection.

42.2 The right to a well-known mark shall be protected and belong to its owner without registration procedures. The mark owner may use the documents specified in Point 42.3 of this Circular to prove his/her ownership of the mark and to prove that the mark satisfies the conditions for being considered well-known.
42.3 Documents proving the ownership of a mark and proving its popularity include information on the scope, scale, level and continuity of use of the mark, including descriptions of the origin, history and period of continuous use of the mark; the number of countries where the mark has been registered or recognized as a well-known mark; the list of goods and services bearing the mark; the territorial scope within which the mark is circulated, sales volume of products or provision of services; the quantity of goods and services bearing the mark that have been produced and sold; the value of the mark's assets, the price of transfer or licensing of rights, the investment capital contribution value of the mark; investments and costs for advertising and marketing of the mark, including participation in national and international exhibitions; infringement cases, disputes, decisions and judgments of courts or competent authorities; survey data on consumers' knowledge of the mark through purchase, use and advertising and marketing; rankings and assessments of the reputation of the mark by national and international organizations and mass media; prizes and medals won by the mark; assessment results of intellectual property assessment organizations.
42.4 In case a well-known mark is recognized according to civil proceedings or a decision of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, such well-known mark shall be recorded in the List of well-known marks kept at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam.
Section 6. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATIONS
43. Requirements for applications for geographical indications registration
43.1 An application for geographical indication registration must satisfy the general requirements prescribed in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements prescribed in this Point.
43.2 The application must ensure consistency as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property, and each application may register only one geographical indication for a product.
43.3 Application documents: Declaration - made according to Form 05-CDDL prescribed in Appendix A of this Circular; Description of properties/quality/reputation of the product; Map of the geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication (both must be made in 02 copies) and 10 samples showing the presentation of the geographical indication shall be used with size not larger than 80mm x 80mm and not smaller than 20mm x 20mm (in case the geographical indication is not a word).
43.4 Requirements for the description of properties/quality/reputation of the product
a) The description of properties/quality/reputation of products must contain the following main information: (i) List of particular properties/quality of products bearing GIs determined by geographical conditions - determined by perceptible, qualitative, quantitative physical, chemical and biological indicators which can be tested by technical means or experts according to a testing method; and/or (ii) Reputation of products bearing GIs determined by geographical conditions - determined through widespread knowledge about such products among relevant consumers which can be verified; and (iii) Geographical conditions creating particular properties/quality, reputation of products bearing GIs, including unique elements of meteorology, hydrology, geology, topography, ecosystems and other natural conditions; unique elements of skills and techniques of producers, including traditional production processes of local areas (which may include one, several or all stages from production of raw materials, processing of raw materials to creation of products and may also include product packaging stages if such stages affect properties/quality, reputation of products), if such processes are factors creating and maintaining particular properties/quality, reputation of products bearing GIs, in which there is clear and detailed information which can be checked (if among the above-mentioned information there is any secret or technical secret not yet disclosed or widely known outside of local areas, the applicant may refuse to provide details about such secrets or techniques if he/she does not commit to keep such information confidential at his/her request); and (iv) Organic relationship between particular properties/quality, reputation of products bearing GIs mentioned in Points 43.4.a(i) and (ii) with geographical conditions mentioned in Point 43.4.a(iii) above.
b) The description of properties/quality/reputation of products must be enclosed with documents certifying that information on properties/quality/reputation is well-grounded and authentic (test results, research results, investigation results, etc.).
43.5 Requirements for maps of geographical areas corresponding to geographical indications Maps of geographical areas corresponding to geographical indications must fully present information to the extent that they can accurately identify geographical areas meeting natural conditions creating particular characteristics/quality and reputation of products. Maps may be submitted together with documents describing geographical areas corresponding to geographical indications.
44. Formal examination and publication of applications for registration of geographical indications Procedures for formal examination and publication of applications for registration of geographical indications shall comply with general procedures prescribed in clauses 13 and 14 of this Circular.
45. Content examination of applications for registration of geographical indications
45.1 Order of carrying out content examination procedures
Content examination of an application for registration of a geographical indication shall be carried out according to the general order prescribed in clause 15 of this Circular and specific provisions in this clause.
45.2 Assessment of compatibility between subject matters stated in the application and the certificate of geographical indication registration Subject matters stated in an application for registration of a geographical indication shall be considered incompatible with the type of geographical indication protection title if such subject matters are not visible signs used to indicate products originating from a specific area, locality, territory or country as prescribed in clause 22 Article 4 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
45.3 Assessment of geographical indications according to protection conditions
a) Subject matters stated in the application shall be accepted for registration and recorded in the National Register of Geographical Indications if they fully satisfy the conditions prescribed in Article 79 of the Law on Intellectual Property and do not fall into the cases prescribed in Article 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property, specifically, they must fully demonstrate all conditions:
(i) There is a geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication stated in the application;
(ii) Products originating from the above-mentioned geographical area;
(iii) Such products have particular characteristics/quality and/or reputation decided by geographical conditions of such geographical area according to Article 82 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) In the following cases, geographical indications mentioned in applications shall not be registered:
(i) Geographical indications which have become common names of goods in Vietnam;
(ii) A geographical indication of a foreign country which is not protected, has been terminated or no longer used in such country;
(iii) The geographical indication is identical or similar to a mark currently protected in Vietnam, where such use would cause confusion as to the origin of products;

(iv) The geographical indication misleads consumers as to the true geographical origin of products bearing such geographical indication.
c) Method of assessment of geographical indications according to protection conditions

The assessment of a geographical indication shall be carried out according to the protection conditions specified in Points 45.3.a and b above on the basis of information provided by the applicant and information obtained from the following mandatory sources:
(i) Marks currently protected in Vietnam for products identical or similar to products bearing the geographical indication, with the starting date of protection earlier than the date of filing the application for registration of the geographical indication, including marks protected under international treaties to which Vietnam is a signatory;
(ii) Marks recognized as popular marks by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam.
In case a mark identical or similar to a geographical indication is found, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the mark owner of such finding for obtaining their opinion within one month from the date of signing the notice, clearly stating the right to object to the registration of the geographical indication if there are sufficient grounds to prove that the geographical indication falls under the case specified in Clause 3 Article 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property. The consideration of the mark owner's opinions shall comply with regulations on the consideration of third-party opinions prescribed in Point 6 of this Circular. A geographical indication may mislead consumers about the true geographical origin of products bearing such geographical indication when it is considered identical or similar to the point of indistinguishability from a sign known by Vietnamese consumers as the geographical origin of goods or services.
45.4 Notification of results of content appraisal
Results of content appraisal of applications for registration of geographical indications shall comply with general provisions in Point 15.7 of this Circular.
46. Issuance, registration and publication of certificates of geographical indication registration Procedures for issuance, registration and publication of certificates of geographical indication registration shall comply with general provisions in Points 18 and 19 of this Circular.
Chapter II
    PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF CONTRACTS FOR TRANSFER OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS AND PROCEDURES FOR RULING ON FORCED TRANSFER OF LICENSING RIGHTS
Section 1. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF CONTRACTS FOR TRANSFER OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
47. Application for registration of contracts for transfer of industrial property rights 47.1 An application for registration of a contract for transfer of industrial property rights must include the following documents:
a) 2 copies of the declaration for registration of a contract for transfer of industrial property rights, made according to Form No. 01-HDC prescribed in Appendix D of this Circular;
b) 2 copies of the contract (original or valid copy); if the contract is made in another language other than Vietnamese, it must be enclosed with a Vietnamese translation; if the contract has many pages, each page must bear the signatures of the parties or be affixed with a seal on every two adjacent pages;
c) Original copy of the protection title;
d) Written consent of co-owners regarding the transfer of industrial property rights, if the corresponding industrial property rights are under joint ownership;
e) Power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative);
g) Documents on payment of fees and charges.
47.2 An application for registration of a contract for transfer of the right to use an industrial property object (industrial property license contract) shall include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the declaration for registration of a contract for transfer of the right to use an industrial property object, made according to Form No. 02-HDS prescribed in Appendix D of this Circular;
b) 02 copies of the contract (original or valid copy); if the contract is made in another language other than Vietnamese, it must be enclosed with a Vietnamese translation; if the contract has many pages, each page must bear the signatures of the parties or be affixed with a seal on every two adjacent pages;
c) Written consent of co-owners regarding the transfer of the right to use the industrial property object, if the corresponding industrial property right is under joint ownership;
d) Power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative);
e) Documents on payment of charges.

48. Procedures for processing applications
48.1 In case an application for registration of an industrial property right transfer contract does not contain any of the deficiencies specified in Clause 48.3 of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall perform the following tasks:
a) Issue a decision to grant a certificate of registration of an industrial property right transfer/licensing contract;
b) (For an industrial property right transfer contract): Record the new owner in the protection title; in case of partial transfer of the list of goods or services bearing protected marks, issue a certificate of registration of new marks to the transferee and determine the scope of the transferred list of goods/services in the original protection title; or (for a licensing contract): Grant a certificate of registration of the industrial property right licensing contract to the applicant; affix the registration seal on 02 copies of the contract and give 01 copy to the applicant and keep 01 copy;
c) Record the industrial property right transfer in the National Register of Industrial Property Right Transfers;
d) Publish the decision to grant a certificate of registration of the industrial property right transfer contract in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 02 months from the date of signing of the decision.

48.2 In case an application for registration of an industrial property right transfer contract contains deficiencies as prescribed in Clause 48.3 of this Circular, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall perform the following procedures:
a) Issue a notice of intended refusal to register the contract, clearly stating the deficiencies in the application, and impose a time limit of 01 month from the date of signing of the notice for the applicant to correct the deficiencies or provide opinions opposing the intended refusal to register the contract;
b) Issue a notice of refusal to register the contract if the applicant fails to correct the deficiencies or provides inadequate corrections, no opinion opposing, or provides unjustifiable opinions opposing the intended refusal to register the contract within the imposed time limit.
48.3 An application for registration of an industrial property right transfer contract shall be deemed to contain errors in one of the following cases:
a/ The declaration is invalid;
b/ One of the documents on the list of required documents is missing;
c/ The power of attorney is invalid;
d/ The copy of the contract is not certified as valid;
e/ The name and address of the assignor in the contract do not match the corresponding information in the protection title or in the contract which is the basis for the emergence of the right to transfer, the power of attorney or the declaration; the name and address of the transferee in the contract do not match the name and address stated in the power of attorney or the declaration;
f/ The contract does not have full signatures (and seals, if any) of the assignor and the transferee;
g/ The assignor is not the owner of the protection title;
i) The industrial property subject matter is no longer within the term of protection or is in dispute;
k) The transfer contract lacks the prescribed contents according to Article 140 or Clause 1 Article 144 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
l) The contract has contents not conforming to regulations on conditions restricting the transfer of industrial property rights in Article 139 of the Law on Intellectual Property or contains unreasonable clauses restricting the rights of the transferee of the right to use the industrial property subject matter as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 144 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
m) There are grounds to confirm that the transfer of industrial property rights infringes upon the industrial property rights of a third party.
48.4 The time limit for processing applications for registration of contracts for transfer of industrial property rights shall be 02 months (excluding the time reserved for applicants to correct errors).
49. Recording of amendments to contents, extension or premature termination of validity of contracts for transfer of the right to use industrial property subject matters
49.1 Any amendment to contents, extension or premature termination of validity of a registered contract for transfer of the right to use an industrial property subject matter must be recorded at the National Office of Intellectual Property under this Point.

49.2 Applications for recording of amendments to contents, extension or premature termination of validity of contracts
a) Application for recording of amendments to contents, extension or premature termination of contracts for transfer of the right to use industrial property subject matters must be made in writing and include the following documents:
(i) 02 copies of declaration requesting recording of amendments to contents, extension or premature termination of contracts for transfer of the right to use industrial property subject matters, made according to Form No. 03-SDHĐ specified in Annex D of this Circular;
(ii) Original copy of certificate of registration of contract for transfer of the right to use industrial property subject matters (in case of registration of amendment to contents or extension of validity of contract);
(iii) Documents proving the amendment to names and addresses of parties to the contract;
(iv) Agreement and documents recording specific articles in the contract which need to be amended, including the extension or premature termination of the contract;
(v) Power of attorney (if the application is filed by a representative);
(vi) Fee receipt.
b) Application for extension of contract must be submitted at least 01 month before the expiry date of the contract stated in the certificate of registration of contract for transfer of the right to use industrial property subject matters.
49.3 Within 01 month from the date of receipt of an application for recording of changes in content, extension or termination of a contract, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall review the application as follows:
a) In case of valid application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a decision to record changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract; record changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract in the Certificate of registration of the industrial property object licensing contract; record changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract in the National Register of Industrial Property Licensing; and publish the decision on recording of changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 02 months from the date of signing of the decision;
b) In case of invalid application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intended refusal to record changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract, clearly stating the deficiencies in the application and setting a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice of intended refusal for the applicant to correct the deficiencies or provide feedback on the intended refusal to register the contract; and issue a notice of refusal to record changes in content, extension or termination of the industrial property object licensing contract if the applicant fails to correct the deficiencies or provides inadequate corrections, no objection or unjustified objection within the prescribed time limit.
Section 2. PROCEDURES FOR RULING ONMANDATORY LICENSING OF INVENTIONS
50. Applications for issuance of decisions on compulsory licensing of inventions 50.1 Organizations and individuals capable, tasked with or wishing to use inventions specified at Points a, b and c or subject to anti-competitive practices specified at Point d, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property may request competent persons specified in Clause 1, Article 147 of the Law on Intellectual Property to issue decisions on compulsory licensing of inventions under specific provisions in this Section.

50.2 An application for issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention shall include the following documents:
a/ Two copies of the written request for compulsory licensing of the invention, made according to Form No. 04-CGBB in Appendix D of this Circular;
b/ Documents evidencing that the request for issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of the invention is well-grounded in accordance with law, specifically:
(i) In case of requesting issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention pursuant to Point a, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the application must contain documents evidencing that at the time of filing the application, there is a need to use the invention for public purposes, non-commercial purposes, national defense and security, disease prevention, treatment and nutrition of the people or to meet other urgent social needs but the owner of the invention has not used it and such non-use will affect the achievement of the above-mentioned objectives;

 (ii) In case of requesting issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention pursuant to Point b, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the application must contain documents evidencing that the owner of the invention has failed to fulfill his/her obligation to use the invention as prescribed in Clause 1, Article 136 and Clause 5, Article 142 of the Law on Intellectual Property and at the time of filing the application, the 4-year period from the filing date of the invention registration application has expired and the 3-year period from the grant date of the invention patent has expired;
(iii) In case of requesting issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention pursuant to Point c Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, documents proving that the person wishing to use such invention has failed to reach agreement with the holder of the exclusive right to use such invention on the conclusion of an invention license contract despite having made reasonable efforts to negotiate a satisfactory price and commercial terms, including the indication of the need to use the invention, the period during which negotiations were conducted, the specific price and commercial terms proposed by the person wishing to use the invention;
(iv) In case of requesting issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention pursuant to Point d Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, documents proving that the holder of the exclusive right to use such invention has committed acts deemed to be anti-competitive restrictions prohibited under the law on competition;
(v) In case of requesting issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of an invention in the field of semiconductor technology, documents proving that the use of such invention is for public and non-commercial purposes or presenting documents proving that the holder of the exclusive right to use such invention has committed acts deemed to be anti-competitive restrictions prohibited under the law on competition;
c) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
d) Fee payment document.
51. Procedures for processing applications for issuance of decisions on compulsory licensing of inventions 51.1 Applications shall be filed as follows:
a) Applications falling within the cases specified at Points b, c and d Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be filed with the National Office of Intellectual Property;
b) Applications falling within the case specified at Point a Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be filed with ministries or ministerial agencies managing inventions.
51.2 Appraisal of applications
When there are applications falling into the cases specified in Point 51.1.b of this Circular, ministries and ministerial-level agencies shall designate an agency to receive and appraise the applications.
Within 02 months after receiving the applications, the National Office of Intellectual Property or the agency appraising applications of ministries and ministerial-level agencies (hereinafter referred to as “applications-appraising agency”) shall consider the applications according to the following provisions:
a) In case the application is valid: Within 15 days after receiving the application, the application-appraising agency shall issue a notice requesting the compulsory licensing of the invention to the holder of the exclusive right to use the invention and request such person to give his/her opinion within 01 month after the date of issuance of the notice; request the involved parties to re-negotiate in order to overcome their differences for signing an invention licensing contract, if necessary; in case no agreement is reached among the parties and it is considered unreasonable to refuse to sign the invention licensing contract, it shall report the results of the application consideration and propose the Minister of Science and Technology or the Minister or Head of a ministerial-level agency to issue a decision on compulsory licensing of the invention.
If the request falls into the case specified at Point a, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the invention is used for public and non-commercial purposes, the ministries and ministerial-level agencies may issue decisions on compulsory licensing of the invention without requesting the holder of the exclusive right to use the invention to give his/her opinion and without requesting the involved parties to negotiate.
b) In case the request for issuance of a decision on compulsory licensing of the invention has no ground of justification as prescribed in Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the application-appraising agency shall report the results of the application consideration and propose the Minister of Science and Technology or the Minister or Head of a ministerial-level agency to issue a notice of intended refusal, clearly stating the reason for refusal and fixing a time limit of 01 month after the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to give his/her opinion on the intended refusal.
The time limit for applicants to correct errors in their applications or to provide opinions shall not be included in the time limit for application review.

c) For applications falling under the case specified at Point a, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the appraising agencies of ministries and ministerial-level agencies shall send copies of the applications for consultation to the Ministry of Science and Technology (via the National Office of Intellectual Property) before submitting them to the Minister or the Head of the ministerial-level agency for decision as prescribed in Points a and b above. Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall review it and report to the Minister of Science and Technology for issuance of a written request to the Minister or the Head of the ministerial-level agency to issue a decision to enforce the licensing of the invention or to issue a notice of refusal.
51.3 Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the report on the results of the review of the application by the National Office of Intellectual Property, the Minister of Science and Technology shall review it and issue a decision to enforce the licensing of the invention, or shall send a notice of refusal to enforce the licensing of the invention to the applicant, clearly stating the reasons for refusal.
Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the written request from the Minister of Science and Technology, the Minister or the Head of the ministerial-level agency shall review it and issue a decision to enforce the licensing of the invention, or shall send a notice of refusal to enforce the licensing of the invention to the applicant, clearly stating the reasons for refusal.
In the event of disagreement with the request of the Minister of Science and Technology, the Minister or the Head of the ministerial-level agency shall issue a written notice, clearly stating the reasons therefor.
51.4 The decision to enforce the licensing of the invention shall be sent by the Minister or the Head of the ministerial-level agency to the licensee, the holder of the exclusive right to use the invention and the National Office of Intellectual Property.
The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall record the decision in the National Register of Industrial Property Transfer within 01 month and publish it in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property within 02 months from the date of signing the decision.
52. Request for termination of invention use right under compulsory decisions 52.1 The termination of invention use right under a compulsory decision must be made by the Minister or Head of a ministerial agency who issued the compulsory transfer decision. 52.2 A request for termination of invention use right under a compulsory decision includes the following documents:
a) Written request for termination of invention use right under a compulsory decision;
b) Documents proving that the grounds leading to the compulsory transfer of the invention use right no longer exist and are unlikely to reappear, and that the termination of the use of the invention does not cause damage to the person receiving the compulsory transfer of the invention use right;
c) Power of attorney (if the request is submitted through a representative);
d) Fee receipt.
52.3 Procedures for receiving and processing requests for termination of invention use right under compulsory decisions and issuing termination decisions shall be carried out in the same manner as procedures for receiving and processing applications for compulsory transfer of invention use right under Point 51 of this Circular.
Chapter III
                            INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION
Section 1. ISSUANCE AND REVOCATION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION SERVICE LICENSES
53. Issuance of practice certificates
53.1 Conditions for issuance of practice certificates
Only persons who fully satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 2, Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be issued with industrial property representation service practice certificates. To be issued with a certificate, such person must submit an application dossier to the National Office of Intellectual Property under this Point.
53.2 Application dossiers for issuance of practice certificates
An application dossier for issuance of an industrial property representation service practice certificate must include the following documents:
a/ Two copies of the declaration requesting the issuance of an industrial property representation service practice certificate (practice certificate), made according to Form No. 01-CCHN in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) Copies of the notice of satisfactory performance in the industrial property representation examination organized by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam;
c) 02 3x4 photos;

d/ Documents proving payment of fees.
53.3 Processing of applications for issuance of practicing certificates:
In case of a valid application, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, based on the review of the application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a decision to grant the practicing certificate and issue the practicing certificate to the applicant; record the issuance of the practicing certificate in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives; publish the issuance of the practicing certificate in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property within 2 months from the date of signing the decision; or notify deficiencies in the application and impose a 1-month time limit for the applicant to correct such deficiencies; or issue a notice of refusal to grant the practicing certificate, clearly stating the reasons for refusal. If the applicant fails to correct deficiencies or fails to meet requirements or the application is invalid.
54. Revocation of practicing certificates
If the industrial property representative commits a violation and is sanctioned by revocation of his/her professional practice certificate, on the basis of the decision to revoke the industrial property representation service professional practice certificate issued by the competent state agency handling the administrative violation, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to revoke the professional practice certificate; delete the name of the industrial property representative from the list of industrial property representatives of the industrial property representation service organization; and publish the revocation of the professional practice certificate in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property within 2 months after the date of signing the decision.
55. Re-grant of practicing certificates
The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall carry out procedures for reissuing industrial property representation service practice certificates at the written request made using Form No. 02-CLCC specified in Appendix E of this Circular by industrial property representatives in case their practice certificates are lost or damaged (torn, dirty, faded, etc.) to the extent that they cannot be used.
Procedures for reissuing practice certificates shall be carried out similarly to procedures for issuing practice certificates specified in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
Section 2. RECORDING, AMENDING AND DELETING NAMES OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION SERVICE PROVIDERS
56. Recording industrial property representation service providers
56.1 In order to officially exercise the right to represent applicants before the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, industrial property representation service providers must carry out procedures for recording in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives as prescribed in this Point.
56.2 A dossier requesting recording of an industrial property representation service provider in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives shall include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the declaration requesting recording of the industrial property representation service provider, made using Form No. 03-YCGN specified in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) List of members holding industrial property representation service practice certificates of the organization, enclosed with copies of decisions on recruitment/employment contracts for each of the above-mentioned members;
c) Copies of business registration certificates/operation registration certificates of the organization;
d) List of charges for industrial property representation services of the organization;
e) A written authorization for representation issued by the head of the organization to one of the members in the list mentioned in Point 56.2.b above (if necessary);
g) Charges paid.
56.3 Within 01 month after receiving the dossier, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider the dossier requesting the recognition of an industrial property representation service provider similar to the procedures for granting industrial property representation practice certificates prescribed in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
57. Recognition of changes in information on industrial property representation service providers 57.1 Industrial property representation service providers may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to recognize changes related to their names and addresses or names and addresses of their members on the list of industrial property representatives.
57.2 A dossier requesting the recognition of changes in information on an industrial property representation service provider must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the declaration requesting the recognition of changes in information on the industrial property representation service provider, made according to Form No. 04-YCSD specified in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) Copies of the amended business registration certificate/operation registration certificate of the industrial property representation service organization (in case of change of name or address of the organization);

c) Decision on employment or decision on termination of labor contract for industrial property representatives being members of the organization (in case of change of members in the list of industrial property representatives of the organization);
d) Fee payment documents.
57.3 Procedures for processing applications for amendment of information on industrial property representation service organizations shall be carried out similarly to the procedures specified in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
58. Deletion of names of industrial property representation service organizations
In cases specified in Clause 2 Article 156 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a decision on deletion of names of industrial property representation service organizations; record the deletion of names of industrial property representation service organizations in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives; notify the deletion of names of industrial property representation service organizations to the competent authority issuing the business registration certificate or operation registration certificate of such organizations; and publish the deletion on the Industrial Property Gazette within 02 months from the date of signing the decision.
Section 3. INSPECTION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION OPERATIONS
59. Inspection of industrial property representation operations
The examination of industrial property representation practice (hereinafter referred to as "examination") under Article 28 of the Decree on Industrial Property shall be conducted in accordance with specific regulations below.
59.1 The Council for Examination of Industrial Property Representation Practice:
The Council for Examination of Industrial Property Representation Practice (hereinafter referred to as "Examination Council") is established by the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property and has the tasks of issuing examination questions, organizing the examination and marking examination papers according to the Regulation on Examination of Industrial Property Representation Practice promulgated by the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property (hereinafter referred to as "Regulation on Examination").
The Examination Council consists of the President - the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property, Vice Presidents, Secretary and other members designated by the President from the list of experts who may participate in the Examination Council. The list of experts who may participate in the Examination Council includes experts familiar with law and practical implementation of law on establishment of industrial property rights under the National Office of Intellectual Property and professional organizations on industrial property representation approved by the Minister of Science and Technology on the basis of proposals of such agencies and organizations. Decisions of the Council are adopted on the principle of voting with two-thirds of the consent of the members of the Council.
Remuneration for the work of members of the Council shall be paid from the revenue of fees for industrial property representation practice examination under the Regulation on Examination.
59.2 Registration for examination:
a) Only persons who fully satisfy the conditions specified at Points a through dd Clause 2 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property may register for examination under this Point.
b) The dossier of registration for examination shall be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property, including the following documents: (i) 02 copies of the declaration for registration for examination (made according to Form 05-KTNV specified in Appendix E of this Circular);
(ii) Copies of university degrees;
(iii) Documents proving that the applicant has received training in industrial property law or has experience in this field: Copies of certificates of completion of training courses on industrial property law recognized by the Ministry of Science and Technology or written certification from the training institution where the applicant has completed their undergraduate or postgraduate dissertation on industrial property; or documents certified by competent authorities that the applicant has directly worked as an examiner of industrial property registration applications at national or international industrial property offices for 5 years or more, or has directly worked in the field of industrial property law as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property (including inspection, examination, supervision, adjudication, legal affairs, and legal consultancy on industrial property; scientific research with the title of researcher or lecturer on industrial property);
(iv) 2 3x4 photos;
(v) Receipt of examination fee payment.
59.3 In case the application is valid, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant of their eligibility to participate in the examination and specify the time, location, and schedule of the examination. 59.4 Examination content and questions:

a) Examination content includes:
(i) Vietnamese industrial property law and international treaties to which Vietnam is a signatory; (ii) Professional practices in preparing, filing, and pursuing industrial property registration applications;
(iii) Professional practices in searching and extracting information on industrial property;
(iv) Other contents if necessary.
b) The examination papers shall be enclosed with answers and score scales prepared by the Examination Council, approved by the President of the Examination Council and kept confidential until the start of the examination time.
59.5 Examination organization
a) The examination shall be organized in sessions (not on a regular basis) when there are at least 05 persons registering for examination.
b) The Examination Council shall mark the examination papers according to the approved answers and score scales.
c) The results of the examination shall be notified by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to examinees. Examinees may request the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam to conduct a final review and the Examination Council shall conduct a final review according to regulations of the Director of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam.
The examination results shall be valid within 02 years for application for industrial property representation practice certificates.
Chapter IV
                     PROTECTION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY INFORMATION
60. Development and management of national database on industrial property 60.1 The National Office of Intellectual Property shall develop and manage the national database on industrial property in order to promptly, fully and accurately meet the needs of information on industrial property for research, application, development and other socio-economic activities.
60.2 The national database on industrial property includes the following information which has been published, compiled, selected and systemized for search purposes:
a) Applications for registration of industrial property;
b) Protection titles already granted and industrial property rights already recognized for protection in Vietnam;
c) Patent titles issued by countries/regions with the most advanced science and technology;
d) Other types of protection titles of industrial property depending on the purpose of using information.
61. Access to and exploitation of industrial property information in the national database 61.1 All organizations and individuals have the right to access and exploit information in the national database on industrial property after registering their use of information with the National Office of Intellectual Property.
61.2 Persons wishing to access and exploit industrial property information ("information users") shall have to search for information themselves in the national database.
Information users may request the National Office of Industrial Property to search for information in the national database and shall pay a search fee according to regulations.
62. Industrial property information search services
62.1 When requesting the National Office of Industrial Property to search for industrial property information, information users shall make written requests for searching (according to the forms 01-YCTC, O2-YCTC and 03-YCTC specified in Annex F of this Circular), which clearly state the purpose and scope of the search (field, type of documents carrying information, time, country or region to be searched, etc.).
62.2 Within one month after receiving a written request for searching, the National Office of Industrial Property shall reply to the information user.
In case the request for searching is valid (having a valid written request for searching as prescribed in Point 62.1 of this Circular and paying a search fee), the National Office of Industrial Property shall reply by sending a "search report" to the information user, which clearly states the results of the search and information retrieval according to the information user's request.

In case the request for searching is invalid (having an invalid written request for searching, unclear purpose and scope of the search, failure to pay a search fee, etc.), the National Office of Industrial Property shall notify the refusal to perform the search request, clearly stating the reason for refusal.
62.3 A search report shall contain only the found information and indications of the sources of such information. If no information is found in the requested sources, it must also be clearly stated in the search report.
Reports on search shall not contain comments or assessments about found information.

62.4 Reports on search must clearly state full name of persons performing the search and at the same time take responsibility for search results.
63. Assurance of industrial property information in localities 63.1 Depending on their conditions and capabilities, the State administrative body for industrial property (the provincial/municipal Department of Science and Technology) may establish and manage the industrial property database to ensure industrial property information for research, application, development of production, business and protection of industrial property rights in localities.

63.2 The State administrative body for industrial property in provinces and centrally run cities shall have the responsibility and right to conduct activities to assure industrial property information as prescribed in this Circular.
64. Issuance of copies of documents
64.1 All relevant organizations and individuals have the right to request the National Office of Industrial Property to issue copies of documents issued or kept by the National Office of Industrial Property, including certification of the copy from the original or the archive copy. Only applicants have the right to request issuance of copies of documents related to unannounced applications. Applicants shall pay fees as prescribed.
64.2 Documents requested for issuance of copies of include:
a/ Written request for issuance of copies of documents, including 02 copies;
b/ Original copies of documents issued by the National Office of Industrial Property (in case documents are not kept at the National Office of Industrial Property);
c/ Fee payment vouchers.
64.3 Within 03 working days from the date of receipt of a request for issuance of copies, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue copies or notify the refusal and provide an explanation.
Chapter V
                                   IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS
65. Regulations on carrying out procedures for industrial property
The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall promulgate regulations on procedures for industrial property in accordance with the Decree on Industrial Property and this Circular.
66. Transitional provisions
66.1 An invention registration application as stipulated in this Circular shall be understood as including both an application for issuance of an invention patent and an application for issuance of a utility solution patent in accordance with the previous regulations.
66.2 For internationally registered marks already recognized for protection in Vietnam under previous regulations, the WIPO Official Gazette or the Industrial Property Official Gazette of the National Office of Intellectual Property announcing such internationally registered marks shall be valid as basis for certification of rights to such marks.

66.3 Before new regulations on fees and charges are promulgated, the fee and charge rates prescribed in this Circular shall apply according to the Circular No. 132/2004/TT-BTC of December 30, 2004 of the Ministry of Finance guiding the collection, payment, management and use of industrial property fees and charges.
66.4 The documents mentioned above shall continue to be used until the National Office of Intellectual Property fully satisfies the technical conditions and issues an official notice on the application of the documents mentioned in this Circular.
67. Effect
67.1 This Circular replaces the following Circulars:
a) Circular No. 3055/TT-SHCN of December 31, 1996 of the Ministry of Science, Technology and Environment providing guidance on the implementation of regulations on procedures for establishment of industrial property rights and other procedures in Decree No. 63/CP of October 24, 1996 of the Government detailing industrial property;

b) Circular No. 29/2003/TT-BKHCN of November 5, 2003 of the Ministry of Science and Technology providing guidance on the implementation of procedures for establishment of industrial property rights for industrial designs;
c) Circular No. 30/2003/TT-BKHCN of November 5, 2003 of the Ministry of Science and Technology providing guidance on the implementation of procedures for establishment of industrial property rights for inventions/utility solutions.
67.2 This Circular takes effect 15 days after its publication in the Official Gazette.
