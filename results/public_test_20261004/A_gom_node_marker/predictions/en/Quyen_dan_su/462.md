                                                        LAW

                                           ON MARRIAGE AND FAMILY

  The family is the cell of society. A good family means a good society and a good society means a good family. In a socialist family, wives and husbands are equal, love and help each other to make progress, actively participate in the cause of building socialism and defending the Fatherland, and jointly raise and educate their children into citizens useful to the society.
  To inherit and develop the 1959 Marriage and Family Law in order to further build and consolidate socialist families, preserve and promote fine national customs and practices, and eliminate backward practices and vestiges of the anti-queen bee marriage and family regime, and resist the influence of the bourgeois marriage and family regime; Pursuant to Articles 64 and 65 of the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;
  This Law provides for the marriage and family regime.

  Chapter I

                                         GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1

  The State shall ensure the genuine implementation of the regime of voluntary, progressive and monogamous marriage, with husband and wife equality, aiming to build democratic, harmonious, happy and sustainable families.

  Marriage between Vietnamese citizens of different ethnicities and religions, or between religious believers and non-believers shall be respected and protected.

  Article 2

  Husband and wife have the obligation to have children by planned conception.

  Parents have the obligation to raise their children to become citizens useful for society.

  Children have the obligation to respect, care for and nurture their parents.

  Article 3

  The State and society shall protect mothers and children, assist mothers in performing their noble functions as mothers.

  Article 4

  To prohibit child marriage, forced marriage, obstruction of voluntary marriage, progress and property claims in marriage proposals; to prohibit forced divorce.

  To prohibit married persons from marrying or cohabiting with other persons.

  To prohibit discrimination against, persecution of fathers, mothers, wives, husbands and children.

  Chapter 2

                                                     MARRIAGE

  Article 5

  Men must be twenty years of age or older and women must be eighteen years of age or older to get married.

  Article 6

  Marriage shall be decided voluntarily by men and women; neither party may coerce the other party, nor anyone may force or obstruct the marriage.

  Article 7

  To prohibit marriage in the following cases:

  a/ Being married;

  b/ Suffering from mental diseases which render them incapable of perceiving their acts; suffering from venereal diseases;

  c/ Among persons of the same direct blood line; among siblings of the same father or mother or of different fathers or mothers; among persons of different generations who are related by blood;

  d) Between adoptive parents and adopted children.

  Article 8

  The marriage shall be recognized and recorded in the marriage register by the People's Committee of the commune, ward or township where either of the partners resides according to the procedures prescribed by the State.

  The marriage between Vietnamese citizens living abroad shall be recognized by diplomatic missions of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam.

  All other marriage ceremonies are not legally valid.

  Article 9

  The marriage that violates one of Articles 5, 6 and 7 of this Law is illegal.

  One or both partners of a marriage contracted in contravention of law, wife, husband or children of persons who are married, and who marry other persons, the People's Procuracy, the Vietnam Women's Union, the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union and Vietnam Trade Union have the right to request the People's Court to cancel such illegal marriage.

  The property of persons whose marriage is canceled shall be settled on the principle that their personal property still belongs to them; common property shall be divided according to each partner's contribution; the legitimate interests of the partner who is deceived or forced into marriage shall be protected.

  The interests of children shall be settled as in cases where parents divorce.

  Chapter III

                                   OBLIGATIONS AND RIGHTS OF WIFE AND HUSBAND

  Article 10

  Wife and husband have equal obligations and rights in all aspects of the family.

  Article 11

  Husband and wife have the obligation to be faithful to each other, love, respect, care for and help each other to make progress and jointly carry out planned childbearing.

  Husband has the obligation to create conditions for his/her wife to well perform the mother’s function.

  Article 12

  Husband and wife have the right to freely select legitimate occupations and participate in political, economic, cultural and social activities.

  Article 13

  The domicile of husband and wife shall be selected by them and not bound by customs and practices.

  Article 14

  Common property of husband and wife includes property created by either of them, income from their occupations and other lawful incomes during the marriage period, property jointly inherited or given to them.

  Article 15

  Common property is used to meet common family needs.

  Husband and wife have equal rights and obligations to common property. The purchase, sale, exchange, lending, borrowing and other transactions related to property of a value of big must be agreed upon by husband and wife.

  Article 16

  For property acquired before marriage or separately inherited or given during the marriage period, the person who has such property may put it in or not put it in the common property of husband and wife.

  Article 17

  When one spouse dies first, if it is necessary to divide the common property of husband and wife, it shall be divided into two parts. The divided property of the deceased spouse shall comply with the provisions of law on inheritance.

  Husband and wife have the right to inherit each other's property.

  Article 18

  When marriage exists, if one party makes a request and has a plausible reason, the common property of husband and wife may be divided according to Article 42 of this Law.

  Chapter IV

                               OBLIGATIONS AND RIGHTS OF PARENTS AND CHILDREN

  Article 19

  Parents have the obligation to love, nurture and educate their children, and to care for their physical, intellectual and moral development.

  Parents must not discriminate among their children.

  Parents must set good examples for their children in all aspects and closely coordinate with schools and social organizations in educating their children.

  Article 20

  Parents have the obligation to raise their adult children who are unable to work for themselves.

  Article 21

  Children have equal obligations and rights in the family.

  Children have the obligation to respect, care for and support their parents and listen to their advice.

  Article 22

  Adult children who still live with their parents have the right to choose their professions and participate in political, economic, cultural and social activities.

  Article 23

  Children have their own property.

  Children aged 16 or older who are still living with their parents have the obligation to take care of their family's life and, if they have their own income, must contribute to the family's needs.

  Article 24

  Parents represent their minor children before law.

  Parents manage the property of their minor children.

  Article 25

  Parents shall pay compensation for damage caused by illegal acts of their under-16 children. In cases where parents have no means but their children have their own property, the property of the children shall be used for compensation.

  Under-16 children shall pay compensation with their own property for damage caused by their illegal acts. If their children have no property, their parents shall pay compensation.

  Article 26

  A father or mother who is sanctioned for one of the crimes of infringing upon the body or dignity of a minor child, seriously ill-treating or persecuting a minor child may be decided by the People's Court not to let him/her look after and educate the child, manage the property of the child or represent the child for between one and five years. For such person who has been reformed, the People's Court may shorten this term.

  The above-mentioned fathers and mothers shall still have the obligation to contribute the costs of raising their children.

  Article 27

  Grandparents have the obligation to rear and educate their minor grandchildren in cases where the children have no parents left. Adult grandchildren have the obligation to rear their grandparents in cases where the grandparents have no children left. Brothers and sisters have the obligation to support one another in cases where they have no parents left.

  Chapter 5.

                                        DETERMINING PARENTS

  Article 28.-

  A child born during the marriage period or conceived by the wife during such period is the common child of the husband and wife.

  In case there is a request for re-determination of this matter, there must be other evidence.

  Article 29

  The person declared as father or mother of a child may request to determine that the child is not his/her offspring.

  The person who is not declared as father or mother of a child may request to determine that the child is his/her offspring.

  Article 30

  The adoption of an out-of-wedlock child shall be recognized by the People's Committee of commune, ward or township where the child resides and recorded in the birth register.

  Article 31

  A child out of wedlock may apply for recognition as his/her parent even in case the parent has died.

  The mother or father or patron may request the identification of the parent for an out-of-wedlock child who is a minor.

  The People’s Procuracy, Vietnam Women’s Union, Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union and Vietnam Trade Union have the right to request the identification of the parent for an out-of-wedlock child who is a minor.

  Article 32

  A child out of wedlock recognized by his/her parents or by the People’s Court has all rights and obligations like a child in wedlock.

  Article 33

  Disputes over the recognition of children or parents shall be settled by the People’s Court of the place where the child resides.

  Chapter VI

                                                CHILD CARE

  Article 34

  The adoption of a child aims to establish a close bond between the adopter and the adopted child in the parent-child relationship, ensuring that the minor adopted child is well nurtured, cared for and educated.

  Between the adopter and the adopted child, there are obligations and rights of parents and children as prescribed in Articles 19 through 25 of this Law.

  Article 35

  A person aged 15 years or younger may adopt a child. In case the adopted child is a war invalid or a disabled person or an old and weak lonely person, the adopted child may be over 15 years old.

  The adopter must be 20 years older than the adopted child.

  Article 36

  The adoption of a child must be agreed upon by both spouses of the adopter, by the natural parents or by the guardian of the minor adopted child. If adopting a person aged 9 years or older, his/her consent is also required.

  Article 37.-

  The adoption of a child shall be recognized by the People's Committee of the commune, ward or township where the adopter or adoptee resides and recorded in the civil status book.

  Article 38.-

  The State and society encourage the adoption of orphans.

  Orphans who are adopted shall still enjoy all rights and interests of the fallen heroes.

  Article 39.-

  The adoption may be terminated when the adopter or the adoptee or both commit acts of seriously infringing upon the body and/or dignity of each other or other acts which make the affection between them no longer exist.

  The termination of the adoption shall be decided by the People's Court at the request of the adoptee or the adopter. In case the adoptee is a minor, his/her natural parents or tutor, the People's Procuracy, the Vietnam Women's Union, the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union or the Vietnam Trade Union have the right to request the termination of the adoption.

  Chapter VII

                                                     LY HÔN

  Article 40.-

  When a spouse or both spouses apply for divorce, the People’s Court shall conduct an investigation and mediation.

  In case both spouses apply for divorce, if the mediation fails and it is well established that the two parties really voluntarily apply for divorce, the People’s Court shall recognize the voluntary divorce.

  In case either spouse applies for divorce, if the mediation fails, the People’s Court shall conduct a trial. If it deems that the situation is serious, their common life cannot be prolonged and the marriage purpose cannot be achieved, the People’s Court shall grant permission for the divorce.

  Article 41.-

  In case the wife is pregnant, her husband may apply for divorce only after she has given birth for one year.

  This restriction does not apply to the application for divorce by the wife.

  Article 42.-

  Upon their divorce, the division of property shall be agreed upon by the two parties and recognized by the People's Court. If the two parties fail to reach agreement, the People's Court shall make decision.

  The division of property upon divorce must, in principle, comply with the following regulations:

  a/ Private property of one party shall still belong to that party;

  b/ The common property of husband and wife shall be divided into two parts, but with consideration given to the property situation, the specific condition of the family and each party’s contribution;

  c/ In case the couple’s personal property is unidentified while they are still living together in the family, the wife or husband shall be entitled to a part of the family’s common property, based on his/her contribution to the maintenance and development of the common property as well as to the family life. Labor in the family shall be regarded as productive labor;

  d/ When dividing property, the interests of the wife and minor children must be protected, as well as the legitimate interests of production and profession.

  Article 43. Transfer of title to a foreigner

  When divorcing, if the poorer spouse requests alimony, the richer spouse shall pay alimony according to his/her capability.

  The level and duration of support shall be agreed upon by the two parties. If they fail to reach agreement, the People's Court shall make decision.

  When circumstances change, the supported person or the person who is supported may request a change in the level or duration of support. If the supporting person marries another person, he/she will no longer be supported.

  Article 44

  Divorced couples still have all obligations and rights toward their children.

  Article 45

  Upon divorce, the custody of minor children must be based on the child's interests in all aspects. In principle, breastfed children shall be placed under the care of their mothers.

  Persons who do not raise children have the obligation and right to visit and care for them and pay expenses for their rearing and education. If they delay or shirk such payment, the People's Court shall decide to deduct such expenses from their income or force them to pay such expenses.

  For the benefit of the children, when necessary, the custody of children or the level of payment of expenses for their rearing and education may be changed.

  Chapter VIII

                                                SALVAGE REGIMES

  Article 46

  The tutorship shall be provided in cases where it is necessary to ensure the care for, education and protection of rights and interests of minors whose parents have died or are alive but cannot perform such tasks.

  Article 47

  Parents may appoint a tutor for their minor children. If they fail to appoint a tutor, relatives may appoint a tutor for such person. The appointment of tutors shall be recognized by the People’s Committees of communes, wards or townships.

  In cases where parents or relatives fail to appoint tutors, competent State agencies or social organizations shall act as tutors for their minor children.

  Article 48

  Citizens acting as tutors must be aged 21 years or older, have good moral qualities and practical conditions to act as tutors.

  Article 49

  Citizens or organizations acting as patrons have the following tasks and powers:

  a/ To care for and educate their patrons;

  b/ To manage the assets of their patrons;

  c/ To represent their patrons before law and protect their interests.

  Article 50.-

  Citizens acting as patrons shall be subject to the supervision of the People's Committees of communes, wards or townships regarding patronage work.

  The patrons' relatives, the People's Procuracies, the Vietnam Women's Union, the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union and the Vietnam Trade Union may request the commune, ward or town People's Committees to appoint substitutes for the patrons if such persons fail to fulfill their tasks or cause serious damage to the patrons' interests.

  Citizens acting as patrons may request the appointment of substitutes if they deem themselves no longer eligible to act as patrons.

  Article 51

  The tutorship shall terminate when the minor is returned to his/her parents, adopted by them or reaches full 18 years of age.

  Chapter IX

      MARRIAGE AND FAMILY RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN VIETNAMESE CITIZENS AND FOREIGNERS

  Article 52

  In the marriage between a Vietnamese citizen and a foreigner, each party shall comply with regulations of law of their country on marriage.

  If the marriage between a Vietnamese citizen and a foreigner takes place in Vietnam, the foreigner must also comply with the provisions of Articles 5, 6 and 7 of this Law. Marriage procedures shall be prescribed by the Council of Ministers.

  Article 53

  The State Council shall stipulate matters concerning husband and wife relations, property relations, parent-child relations, marriage annulment, divorce, child adoption and patronage between Vietnamese citizens and foreigners.

  Article 54

  Where there is a legal and judicial assistance agreement on marriage and family between Vietnam and a foreign country, such agreement shall prevail.

  Chapter 10

                                          FINALE PROVISIONS

  Article 55

  For ethnic minority groups, the State Council shall, based on this Law and specific circumstances, make appropriate provisions.

  Article 56

  The Council of Ministers, the Supreme People’s Court and the Prosecutor General of the Supreme People’s Procuracy shall, within their respective functions, guide the implementation of this Law.

  The Vietnam Fatherland Front, the Vietnam Women’s Union, the Ho Chi Minh Communist Youth Union and the Vietnam Trade Union shall educate and mobilize people to strictly implement this Law.

  Article 57

  This Law replaces the Marriage and Family Law of 1959.

  This Law was passed on December 29, 1986, by the National Assembly of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam at its 12th session.
