                                                       LAW

                                           NARCOTIC SUBSTANCE ADDICTION PREVENTION

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam;
  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Drug Prevention and Fight.

  Chapter I

                                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for prevention and control of narcotic substances; management of illicit drug users; narcotic substance rehabilitation; responsibilities of individuals, families, agencies and organizations in prevention and control of narcotic substances; state management and international cooperation on prevention and control of narcotic substances.

  Article 2. Interpretation of terms

  For the purposes of this Law, the terms below shall be construed as follows:

  1. Narcotic substances are addictive or psychotropic substances specified in the list of narcotic substances issued by the Government.

  2. Addictive substances are stimulants or depressants which easily cause addiction to users.

  3. Psychotropic substances are stimulants or depressants which cause hallucinations or, if used repeatedly, may lead to addiction to users.

  4. Precursor chemicals are chemicals indispensable to the process of manufacturing narcotic substances and specified in the list of precursors issued by the Government.

  5. Veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors are veterinary drugs containing substances prescribed in Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article.

  6. Plants containing narcotic substances are opium poppies, coca plants, cannabis plants and other plants containing narcotic substances as regulated by the Government.

  7. Drug prevention and control means preventing, combating and fighting drug-related crimes and vices; controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances.

  8. Drug-related vices mean illegal use of narcotic substances, narcotic addiction and violations of the law on narcotic substances which are not serious enough to be examined for penal liability or are not examined for penal liability.

  9. Controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances means permitting, monitoring, inspecting and supervising legal activities related to narcotic substances and preventing and stopping the abuse of such activities for other purposes.

  10. Illegal drug users are those who use narcotic substances without permission of competent persons or authorities and whose drug test results are positive.

  11. Drug test means the application of specialized techniques to determine narcotic substances in the human body through urine samples, blood samples or other samples of the human body.

  12. Drug addicts are those who use narcotic substances, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and become dependent on these substances.

  13. Rehabilitating drug addicts means the process of providing medical, psychological and social support to help drug addicts stop using narcotic substances, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, physically and mentally recover, raise awareness and change behavior in order to stop illegally using these substances.

  14. Drug rehabilitation facilities are established to fully implement rehabilitation procedures as prescribed by this Law, including public drug rehabilitation facilities and voluntary drug rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 3. State policies on prevention and combat against narcotic substances

  1. Implement synchronous measures for prevention and combat against narcotic substances; combine with HIV/AIDS prevention and combat and other social evils.

  2. Intensify activities of communication and education on prevention and combat against narcotic substances; encourage organizations and individuals to participate in communication and education on prevention and combat against narcotic substances.

  3. Prioritize resources for prevention and combat against narcotic substances in ethnic minority areas, mountainous areas, remote areas, islands, border areas and areas with complex drug-related issues.

  4. Officials and soldiers under agencies specialized in prevention and combat against drug-related crimes, and persons engaged in narcotic substance rehabilitation work in public drug rehabilitation facilities shall be entitled to preferential treatment and policies appropriate to their nature of duties and operating areas according to regulations of the Government.

  5. Protect and support individuals, families, agencies and organizations participating in prevention and combat against narcotic substances.

  6. Strictly manage illicit drug users and addicts; encourage addicts to voluntarily detoxify and treat opium addiction with substitution therapy; encourage individuals, families, agencies, and organizations to participate in and support rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  7. Ensure funding for compulsory narcotic rehabilitation; provide support for voluntary narcotic rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  8. Domestic and foreign organizations and individuals investing in narcotic rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management, and prevention of relapse shall be exempted from or reduced land rent and corporate income tax in accordance with the law.

  9. Encourage scientific research, technological development, and high technology application in drug prevention and control.

  10. Commend and reward individuals, organizations, and agencies achieving outstanding performance in drug prevention and control.

  Article 4. Financial sources for drug prevention and control

  1. State budget.

  2. Donations, aid, investment, and gifting from domestic and foreign organizations and individuals.

  3. Spending of families and drug addicts.

  4. Other lawful financial sources.

  Article 5. Prohibited acts

  1. Planting narcotic-containing plants or providing instructions on planting narcotic-containing plants.

  2. Researching, examining, testing, inspecting, producing, storing, transporting, preserving, storing, trading, distributing, processing, exchanging, exporting, importing, temporarily importing for re-exporting, temporarily exporting for re-importing, illegally transporting narcotics, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotics, precursors.

  3. Appropriating narcotics, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotics, precursors.

  4. Delivering, receiving, managing, controlling, storing, issuing, preserving narcotics, precursors in contravention of the law; allowing users of narcotics, addictive drugs or psychotropic medicines to use narcotics, addictive drugs or psychotropic medicines in contravention of the law.

  5. Illegally using narcotics; forcing or persuading other people to illegally use narcotics; harboring or supporting illegal use of narcotics.

  6. Producing, storing, transporting, or trading in instruments and equipment used for illegally producing or using narcotic substances.

  7. Opposing or obstructing the testing of narcotic substances in the body, determining narcotic substance dependence, managing individuals illegally using narcotic substances, narcotic substance rehabilitation, and post-rehabilitation management.

  8. Taking revenge on or obstructing individuals performing drug prevention and control duties or participating in drug prevention and control activities.

  9. Abusing positions, powers, or occupations to violate the law on drug prevention and control.

  10. Illegally guiding the production or use of narcotic substances; advertising or marketing narcotic substances.

  11. Criticizing individuals illegally using narcotic substances, narcotic substance rehabilitating individuals, or post-rehabilitation individuals.

  12. Other prohibited acts related to narcotic substances as prescribed by law.

  Chapter II

                                  RESPONSIBILITY FOR NARCOTIC PREVENTION AND COMBAT

  Article 6. Responsibilities of individuals and families

  1. Propagate and educate family members and relatives about the harms of narcotic substances and comply with regulations on narcotic prevention and combat; manage and prevent family members from violating regulations on narcotic prevention and combat.

  2. Comply with competent persons’ instructions on using addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors.

  3. Cooperate with authorities in combating drug-related crimes and evils; participate in supporting community-based and family-based voluntary rehabilitation, rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities, and substitution therapy for narcotic substance addiction; monitor and assist individuals post-rehabilitation to reintegrate into the community; prevent and combat relapse.

  4. Promptly provide information on drug-related crimes, evils, and narcotic-containing plants to public security forces or competent authorities; participate in removing narcotic-containing plants organized by local authorities.

  Article 7. Responsibilities of regulatory agencies

  1. Organize narcotic prevention and combat within their agencies and units; prevent and stop officials, public employees, and employees under their management and officers, soldiers under people’s armed forces from violating regulations on narcotic prevention and combat; propagate and mobilize the public to detect, denounce, and combat drug-related crimes and evils.

  2. Implement state policies on planning and socio-economic development to replace planting of narcotic-containing plants in areas where such plants are removed.

  Article 8. Responsibilities of educational institutions

  1. Organize implementation of education programs on drug prevention and control; disseminate and educate students about the law on drug prevention and control; strictly manage and prevent students from violating the law on drug prevention and control.

  2. Cooperate with families, agencies, organizations and local authorities in managing and educating students on drug prevention and control.

  3. Cooperate with competent agencies, organizations and individuals in organizing drug test when necessary to detect students who illegally use narcotic substances.

  Article 9.- Responsibilities of the press bodies

  Press agencies shall coordinate with relevant agencies and organizations in propagating policies and laws on drug prevention and combat.

  Article 10. Responsibilities of the Vietnam Fatherland Front, its member organizations and other organizations

  1. To organize and coordinate with competent agencies in propagating and popularizing knowledge about and laws on drug prevention and combat among the people; to mobilize the people to participate in drug prevention and combat and carry out campaigns on drug prevention and combat.

  2. To prevent and stop members of their organizations and all citizens from violating the law on drug prevention and combat.

  3. To coordinate with local governments at all levels and competent agencies in mobilizing drug addicts to implement rehabilitation measures and treatment of narcotic substances with substitution drugs; to participate in reforming, educating, providing vocational training and job creation for, and assisting post-rehabilitation individuals in community reintegration; to prevent and combat relapse into drug addiction.

  Article 11. Drug-related crime preventing authorities

  1. Drug-related crime preventing authorities include:

  a) Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to the People’s Public Security;

  b) Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to Vietnam Border Guard, Vietnam Coast Guard and Customs authorities.

  2. Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to the People’s Public Security shall, within their tasks and powers, take charge and coordinate with relevant agencies and organizations in preventing, stopping and combating drug-related crimes.

  3. Drug-related crime preventing authorities affiliated to Vietnam Border Guard, Vietnam Coast Guard, customs authorities, within their tasks and powers, shall take charge and cooperate with public security authorities and other relevant agencies in preventing, combating drug-related crimes in areas or regions under their management.

  4. In case of detecting violations related to multiple agencies’ tasks and powers within the same area, the authority that detects the violation first shall handle it within its jurisdiction as prescribed by law; in case where the violation does not fall within its jurisdiction, the violation dossier, offender, exhibits and instrumentalities of the violation shall be transferred to the competent authority for settlement.

  5. The Government shall regulate cooperation among drug-related crime preventing authorities.

  Chapter III

                  CONTROL OF LEGITIMATE ACTIVITIES RELATED TO DRUGS

  Article 12. Legitimate activities related to narcotic substances

  1. Legitimate activities related to narcotic substances are those permitted by competent state authorities, including:

  a) Research, assessment, testing, inspection, production of narcotic substances (excluding cultivation of plants containing narcotic substances), precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor medicines, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines and veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors;

  b) Transport, preservation, storage, trading, distribution, use, processing and exchange of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines and veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors;

  c) Import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines and veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances and precursors.

  2. Legal activities related to narcotic substances specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall be strictly controlled in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 13. Control of research, assessment and production of narcotic substances and precursors

  1. Research, assessment and production of narcotic substances (excluding cultivation of plants containing narcotic substances) and precursors must be monitored, inspected and supervised by competent regulatory authorities.

  2. The Government shall provide for competent authorities’ licensing, procedures for licensing, monitoring, inspection and supervision prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 14. Control of transport of narcotic substances and precursors

  1. Agencies, organizations and individuals that have been licensed by competent regulatory authorities may transport narcotic substances and precursors; when transporting, they must package and seal them in accordance with regulations of competent authorities; take responsibility for quantity, quality, adopt measures to ensure safety and prevent loss during transportation and be subject to monitoring, inspection and supervision by competent regulatory authorities.

  2. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 15. Control of activities related to addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursors and medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or precursors used for production of medicines

  Control of activities related to addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursors and medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or precursors used for production of medicines shall comply with regulations of law on pharmaceuticals, except for activities specified in Point b Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 17 hereof.

  Article 16. Control of activities related to veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors

  Control of activities related to veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances and precursors shall comply with regulations of the Government.

  Article 17. Control of import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursors and medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or precursors used for production of medicines

  1. The following activities must be permitted by competent authorities:

  a) Import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import of narcotic substances and precursors;

  b) Temporarily import, re-export, temporarily export, re-import addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances and medicinal ingredients which are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or pre-substances used for production of medicines.

  2. The transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs and medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients or precursors used for production of medicines shall be carried out according to the route specified in the transit permit. The agency or organization that performs the transit shall carry out procedures and submit to the control of customs agencies and competent authorities of Vietnam.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 18. Preparation of documents on legal activities related to narcotic substances

  Agencies, organizations and individuals shall, when conducting activities specified in Clause 1 Article 12 of this Law, prepare dossiers and submit reports as prescribed by the Government.

  Article 19. Control of lawful activities related to narcotic substances for national defense and security

  Research, appraisal, production, transportation, storage, preservation, use, import, export, temporary import, re-export, temporary export, re-import, transit of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, precursor drugs, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary drugs containing narcotic substances, precursors for national defense and security shall comply with regulations of the Government.

  Article 20. Control of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for purposes of first aid and emergency treatment during international journeys, travel and self-treatment of persons entering, exiting or transiting through Vietnam

  1. The carrying of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for first aid and emergency treatment on board a ship, aircraft, train, automobile or other means of transport during international journeys or tours shall not be regarded as carrying exported, imported or transited goods in the territory of Vietnam.

  The commander or driver of a means of transport shall declare to the Vietnamese customs agency, explain the quantity of drugs used, apply appropriate safety measures to prevent the use for wrong purposes or illegal transportation of such drugs and submit to the control of the competent authority of Vietnam.

  2. The carrying of addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines and pre-substances for self-treatment of persons entering, exiting or transiting through the territory of Vietnam shall comply with regulations of the Government of Vietnam and submit to the control of the competent authority of Vietnam.

  Article 21. Handling of narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances or precursors seized in cases of violation of law Narcotic substances, precursors, addictive drugs, psychotropic medicines, pre-substances, medicinal ingredients that are addictive active ingredients, psychotropic active ingredients, precursors used for production of medicines, veterinary medicines containing narcotic substances or precursors seized in criminal cases or administrative violations shall be handled according to regulations of law on criminal procedure and law on handling of administrative violations.

  Chapter IV

                          MANAGEMENT OF ADDICTS TO NARCOTIC SUBSTANCES

  Article 22. Drug test in body

  1. Drug test in body shall be conducted for the following persons:

  a) Individuals found using narcotic substances illegally;

  b) Individuals whose agencies or competent persons have grounds to believe they are using narcotic substances illegally;

  c) Individuals illegally using narcotic substances under management;

  d) Individuals subject to educational administrative measures at communes, wards, towns due to illegal use of narcotic substances; rehabilitation; substitution therapy for narcotic substance addiction; post-rehabilitation management.

  2. Heads of rehabilitation facilities, facilities providing substitution therapy for narcotic substance addiction; individuals with authority to impose penalties for illegal use of narcotic substances shall conduct narcotic substance testing within their jurisdiction or request specialized agencies and individuals to test narcotic substances in the body in cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article.

  If positive results are obtained, send them immediately to the President of the People’s Committee of the commune where the individual lives, except for cases where such individuals are undergoing compulsory rehabilitation.

  3. The State ensures funding for narcotic substance testing in the body in cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 23. Management of individuals illegally using narcotic substances

  1. Managing illicit drug users is a preventive measure to help illicit drug users not continue using illicit drugs and prevent their violations of law.

  Managing illicit drug users is not an administrative handling measure.

  2. The duration of managing illicit drug users is 01 year from the date on which the President of People’s Committee of commune issues the decision on management.

  3. Management of illicit drug users includes:

  a) Counseling, encouraging, educating, assisting illicit drug users to not continue using illicit drugs;

  b) Drug test;

  c) Preventing illicit drug users from committing acts of social order and safety loss.

  4. Within 03 working days from the date on which positive test results of local residents are received, Presidents of People’s Committees of communes shall issue decisions and organize management of local illicit drug users.

  5. Within the management period, the President of the commune-level People’s Committee shall issue a decision to stop managing illicit drug users in the following cases:

  a) The illegal drug user is determined to be a drug addict;

  b) The illicit drug user shall be subject to administrative handling measures of education at the commune, ward or township for using illicit narcotic substances;

  c) The illegal drug user shall be subject to administrative measures such as being sent to a compulsory educational institution or reformatory;

  d) The illegal drug user must serve their prison sentence;

  dd) The illegal drug user on the list of drug users dies or is declared missing by the court.

  6. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 24. Responsibility of illegal drug users

  1. Fully and accurately provide information on their illegal use of narcotic substances to the commune-level police authority where they reside.

  2. Comply with management by the commune-level People’s Committee.

  Article 25. Responsibilities of families, agencies, organizations, and communities in managing illicit drug users

  1. Families of illicit drug users have the following responsibilities:

  a) Manage and educate illicit drug users; prevent illicit drug use;

  b) Provide information on illicit drug use of illicit drug users to the commune-level police authority where such persons reside;

  c) Prevent illicit drug users from committing acts that cause social disorder or insecurity;

  d) Cooperate with competent authorities in taking illicit drug users for drug test.

  2. Agencies and organizations where illicit drug users work or live shall:

  a) Encourage, assist, and educate illicit drug users; prevent illicit drug use;

  b) Cooperate with families and competent authorities in taking illicit drug users for drug test.

  Article 26. List of illicit drug users

  1. Commune-level police authorities shall assist People’s Committees at the same level in listing local illicit drug users.

  2. Where illicit drug users change their residence, commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move to shall notify commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move from within 5 working days from the date on which they leave the administrative divisions to include them in the list and continue to manage them.

  3. Commune-level police authorities shall assist People’s Committees at the same level in removing illicit drug users from the list in the following cases:

  a) Illicit drug users do not commit illicit drug use during the management period under Clause 2 Article 23 hereof;

  b) Illegal drug users subject to management suspension under Clause 5 Article 23 hereof;

  c) Illegal drug users relocating to reside in other local areas.

  Chapter V

                                              CRYSTALINE NARCOTIC ADDICTION

  Article 27. Determination of narcotic substance dependence

  1. Determination of narcotic substance dependence shall be carried out for individuals in the following cases:

  a) Individuals illegally using narcotic substances are found to be using narcotic substances during management period;

  b) Individuals illegally using narcotic substances do not have stable residence;

  c) Individuals subject to administrative measures at communes, wards or townships due to illegal use of narcotic substances or within 01 year from the date on which they completely serve administrative measures at communes, wards or townships due to illegal use of narcotic substances are found to be using narcotic substances illegally;

  d) Individuals under post-rehabilitation management period are found to be using narcotic substances illegally;

  dd) Individuals voluntarily determining narcotic substance dependence.

  2. Commune-level police authorities where individuals under Points a, b, c and d Clause 1 of this Article are found shall prepare documents requesting competent medical facilities to determine narcotic substance dependence.

  3. Where a district-level police authority or provincial police authority directly detects or during the investigation and handling of a violation case detects the situation specified in Points a, b, c, and d Clause 1 of this Article, the police authority handling the case shall prepare a request for the competent health facility to determine narcotic substance dependence.

  4. Upon obtaining results of determination of narcotic substance dependence, the health facility shall promptly send such results to the requesting agency and the person determined to have narcotic substance dependence.

  5. A person proposed for determination of narcotic substance dependence has the following rights and responsibilities:

  a) Have his/her honor and dignity assured; receive travel allowances, accommodation, and treatment for withdrawal syndrome and accompanying diseases during the period of determination of narcotic substance dependence;

  b) Comply with the internal regulations and rules of the facility determining narcotic substance dependence; truthfully declare to health workers information on drug use history and signs of drug use;

  c) A person aged 18 years or older or his/her parent, guardian, or legal representative of a person aged between 12 years and under 18 years as prescribed in Points a, b, c, and dd Clause 1 of this Article, upon receiving the result of determination of narcotic substance dependence, shall register for voluntary rehabilitation according to Article 28 of this Law or register for treatment of opium addiction with substitution drugs in case of opium addiction and submit a written request to the People’s Committee of the commune where he/she resides.

  6. The Government shall provide that health facilities are eligible to determine narcotic substance dependence; and documents, procedures, and processes for determining narcotic substance dependence.

  7. The Minister of Health shall provide standards for diagnosis and professional procedures for determining narcotic substance dependence.

  8. The State shall ensure funding for determination of narcotic substance dependence in cases specified in points a, b, c and d clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 28. Rehabilitation measures

  1. Rehabilitation measures include:

  a) Voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) Compulsory rehabilitation.

  2. Voluntary rehabilitation shall be implemented at family, community or rehabilitation facilities; compulsory rehabilitation shall be implemented at public rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 29. Rehabilitation process

  1. Rehabilitation process includes the following stages:

  a) Reception and classification;

  b) Treatment of withdrawal, detoxification, treatment of mental disorders, treatment of other medical conditions;

  c) Education, counseling, behavior and personality rehabilitation;

  d) Occupational therapy, vocational education;

  dd) Preparation for community reintegration.

  2. Compulsory rehabilitation must ensure all stages specified in Clause 1 of this Article; voluntary rehabilitation must ensure completion of 3 stages specified in Points a, b, and c Clause 1 of this Article.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 30. Voluntary rehabilitation at home or community

  1. Community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation means that drug addicts voluntarily undergo rehabilitation at their families or community with professional support from organizations and individuals providing rehabilitation services, cooperation and assistance from families and communities, and under the management of commune-level People’s Committees.

  2. Duration of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation is between 6 months and 12 months.

  3. Persons undergoing community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation who complete at least 3 stages prescribed in Points a, b and c Clause 1 Article 29 hereof shall be provided with financial support.

  4. Persons undergoing community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation have the following responsibilities:

  a) Comply with regulations on voluntary rehabilitation and comply with guidance of specialized agencies;

  b) Pay expenses related to rehabilitation as prescribed.

  5. Chairpersons of commune-level People’s Committees have the following responsibilities:

  a) Receive registration for community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) Provide guidance and manage community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service providers;

  c) Issue certificates of completion of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation.

  6. Chairpersons of district-level People’s Committees shall:

  a) Assign public service providers within their jurisdiction to provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services;

  b) Receive applications for provision of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services and announce list of organizations and individuals eligible to provide such services;

  c) Notify commune-level People’s Committees of list of organizations and individuals providing community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation services;

  d) Allocate funding for support of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation;

  dd) Direct, guide, and inspect community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation.

  7. Rehabilitation facilities and organizations or individuals eligible to provide one or multiple rehabilitation activities according to rehabilitation procedures under Clause 1 Article 29 hereof may provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service and have the responsibility to:

  a) receive and organize provision of community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service;

  b) comply with professional procedures as prescribed by competent authorities;

  c) inform commune-level People’s Committees where they register community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation within 5 working days from the date on which rehabilitating individuals use or arbitrarily terminate use of rehabilitation service or complete rehabilitation service.

  8. Organizations and individuals eligible to provide community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation service may register with Chairpersons of district-level People’s Committees.

  9. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 31. Voluntary rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities

  1. Duration of voluntary rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities shall be between 6 months and 12 months.

  2. Voluntary rehabilitation facility attendants who have completed at least 3 stages prescribed in Points a, b and c Clause 1 Article 29 hereof shall be provided with financial assistance.

  3. Voluntary rehabilitation facility attendants have the responsibility to:

  a) comply with regulations on voluntary rehabilitation and guidelines of specialized agencies;

  b) pay expenses related to rehabilitation as per the law.

  Voluntary rehabilitation facility attendants at public rehabilitation facilities who face financial difficulties may apply for reduction or exemption from expenses payable.

  4. Rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to issue certificates of completion of voluntary rehabilitation to rehabilitating individuals.

  5. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 32. Entities subject to administrative measures of placement in compulsory rehabilitation facilities

  Drug addicts aged 18 years or older who are subject to administrative handling shall be sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers in accordance with the Law on Handling of Administrative Violations in one of the following cases:

  1. Failing to register, failing to implement, or arbitrarily terminating voluntary rehabilitation;

  2. Using narcotic substances illegally during voluntary rehabilitation period;

  3. Drug addicts using opium-based substances without registering, implementing, or arbitrarily terminating substitution therapy for opium-based substances or having substitution therapy terminated due to violations against regulations on addiction treatment;

  4. Relapsing during post-rehabilitation management period.

  Article 33. Rehabilitation for drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age

  1. Drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age shall be sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers in one of the following cases:

  a) Failing to register, failing to implement, or arbitrarily terminating voluntary rehabilitation;

  b) During voluntary rehabilitation period, they are found to have illegally used narcotic substances;

  c) Drug addicts who use opium preparations without registration, fail to implement or terminate treatment of opium preparations with substitution drugs without permission or have their treatment terminated due to violations against regulations on narcotic substance treatment.

  2. Drug addicts aged from full 12 years to under 18 years who are sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers shall:

  a) Comply with regulations on compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation, internal rules and regulations, and submit to management, education and treatment by compulsory rehabilitation centers;

  b) Participate in activities of treatment, medical treatment, education, counseling, general education, vocational education, occupational therapy and behavior and personality rehabilitation.

  3. Duration of compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation for drug addicts aged from full 12 years to under 18 years is from 6 months to 12 months.

  4. The sending of drug addicts aged from full 12 years to under 18 years to compulsory rehabilitation centers shall be decided by district-level People’s Courts and is not an administrative handling measure.

  5. The Standing Committee of the National Assembly shall stipulate procedures for People’s Courts to consider deciding the sending of drug addicts aged from full 12 years to under 18 years to compulsory rehabilitation centers.

  Article 34. Preparation of request for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities for drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age

  1. The preparation of request for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities for drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age shall be carried out as follows:

  a) Chairpersons of People’s Committees of communes where such persons reside or commit violations in case their residence is not identifiable shall prepare request for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities;

  b) In case drug addicts are detected by district-level police authorities or provincial police authorities or during investigation and handling of cases of violation of law where subjects are persons aged from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age who are subject to compulsory rehabilitation facilities, police authorities currently handling the cases shall conduct verification, collect documents and prepare request for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities for such persons;

  c) Request includes violation records, résumé, documents proving current narcotic substance dependence of such persons, report of drug addicts or legal representatives thereof, opinions of parents, guardians or legal representatives;

  d) Commune-level police authorities shall assist chairpersons of the same-level People’s Committees in collecting documents and preparing request specified in point a clause 1 of this Article.

  2. Agencies and persons preparing request specified in clause 1 of this Article shall take responsibility for legality of the request. After completing the preparation of the request, agencies and persons preparing the request must notify in writing persons subject to compulsory rehabilitation facilities, parents, guardians or legal representatives thereof of the preparation of the request. These persons have the right to read the request and provide opinions on the preparation of the request within 3 working days from the date of receipt of the notice.

  3. Within 1 working day from the expiry date of application, agencies and persons who have prepared the application shall submit it to the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division where the person concerned resides or commits violations in case his/her place of residence cannot be identified. Within 2 working days from the receipt of the application, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall decide to transfer the application to the district-level People’s Court for decision on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation establishment. In case the application is incomplete, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall return it to the agency or person who has prepared the application for supplementation within 2 working days from the receipt of the application. Within 2 working days from the receipt of the supplemented application, the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division shall decide to transfer the application to the district-level People’s Court for decision on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation establishment.

  4. An application for consideration by the district-level People’s Court for decision on admission to a compulsory rehabilitation establishment includes:

  a) The application specified in Point c Clause 1 of this Article;

  b) A document of the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division proposing consideration of admission to a compulsory rehabilitation establishment.

  The document of the Head of the district-level Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs Division must contain opinions on child protection.

  5. Applications for admission of drug addicts aged between full 12 years and under 18 years to compulsory rehabilitation establishments must be numbered and archived in accordance with the law on archives.

  6. The Government shall elaborate the preparation of applications for admission of drug addicts aged between full 12 years and under 18 years to compulsory rehabilitation establishments; and rehabilitation regimes applicable to persons aged between full 12 years and under 18 years.

  Article 35. Public rehabilitation facilities

  1. Public rehabilitation facilities shall be established under decisions of Chairpersons of provincial People’s Committees.

  2. Public rehabilitation facilities must arrange the following areas:

  a) Temporary accommodation area for individuals proposed for determination of narcotic substance dependence and those subject to administrative handling measures who are admitted to compulsory rehabilitation facilities during preparation of request documentation;

  b) Compulsory rehabilitation area;

  c) Voluntary rehabilitation area;

  d) Rehabilitation area for individuals from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age;

  dd) Rehabilitation area for individuals with Group A or Group B infectious diseases in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases;

  e) Rehabilitation area for individuals causing disturbances, violating internal regulations, and regulations of rehabilitation facilities.

  3. In areas under Clause 2 of this Article, separate areas shall be arranged for men and women. Individuals having differences between their identity and gender stated in their dossiers or records shall be managed at separate areas according to gender indicated on their identity documents.

  4. Public rehabilitation facilities shall carry out the following activities:

  a) Receive and arrange rehabilitation for rehabilitating individuals placed in compulsory rehabilitation facilities and voluntary rehabilitating individuals;

  b) Determine narcotic substance dependence;

  c) Receive, manage, educate, counsel, and treat withdrawal syndrome, mental disorders, and other diseases for individuals who are preparing documents for placement in compulsory rehabilitation facilities.

  5. Public rehabilitation facilities have the following rights:

  a) Receive rehabilitating individuals for voluntary rehabilitation depending on capacity of rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Figureheads of public rehabilitation facilities may decide appropriate measures for managing, educating, and treating rehabilitating individuals.

  6. Public rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to:

  a) comply with regulations on determination of narcotic substance dependence, rehabilitation process, and management of individuals during preparation for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities;

  b) respect life, health, honor, dignity, and property of rehabilitating individuals, individuals proposed to be determined as narcotic substance dependent, and individuals during preparation for admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities;

  c) ensure rights of drug addicts during rehabilitation period and issue written confirmation of completion of compulsory rehabilitation for individuals from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age;

  d) figureheads of public rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to ensure operational conditions of rehabilitation facilities; publicly post costs of rehabilitation at facilities as per the law;

  dd) prevent drug trafficking into facilities;

  e) within 5 working days from the date in which voluntary rehabilitating individuals receive or discontinue service without permission or complete rehabilitation process, inform People’s Committees of communes where they register for voluntary rehabilitation.

  7. The Government shall stipulate conditions regarding facilities, equipment, personnel of public rehabilitation facilities, and management at public rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 36. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities

  1. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities shall be established by individuals and organizations providing rehabilitation services. The Government shall stipulate conditions, authority, procedures for issuance and revocation of operating license of voluntary rehabilitation facilities.

  2. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities have the right to:

  a) Receive rehabilitating addicts in voluntary rehabilitation according to capacity of rehabilitation facilities;

  b) Collect expenses related to rehabilitation;

  c) Receive incentives as per the law.

  3. Voluntary rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to:

  a) Comply with rehabilitation process;

  b) Respect for life, health, honor, dignity and property of rehabilitating individuals;

  c) Ensure rights of drug addicts from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age during rehabilitation period;

  d) Figureheads of rehabilitation facilities have the responsibility to ensure operational conditions of rehabilitation facilities; publicly post costs related to rehabilitation at facilities as per the law;

  dd) Prevent and control drug trafficking into facilities;

  e) Arrange separate areas or rooms when receiving entities who are people from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age, people with infectious diseases group A, group B according to regulations of Law on Prevention and Control of Infectious Diseases, women; people having differences between entities and gender written in documents, personal background managed in separate rooms in area according to gender represented on learner’s identity;

  g) Within 5 working days from the date on which rehabilitating individuals are received or arbitrarily terminate use of services or complete rehabilitation process, they must inform People’s Committees of communes where they register for voluntary rehabilitation.

  Article 37. Application of rehabilitation measures to Vietnamese people expelled by foreign countries for using illegal narcotic substances, addicted to narcotic substances; foreigners addicted to narcotic substances living in Vietnam

  1. Vietnamese people expelled by foreign countries to Vietnam for using illegal narcotic substances, addicted to narcotic substances upon returning to Vietnam must undergo determination of narcotic substance addiction. In case of being determined to be addicted to narcotic substances, they must implement rehabilitation measures according to this Law.

  2. Foreign drug addicts living in Vietnam shall register for voluntary rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities according to this Law and pay all costs related to rehabilitation. If they fail to voluntarily undergo rehabilitation, they shall be handled as prescribed by law.

  Article 38. Rehabilitation for persons held in temporary detention, inmates, inmates of compulsory educational establishments, and students of reformatories

  1. The State shall apply appropriate rehabilitation measures to drug addicts who are persons held in temporary detention, inmates, inmates of compulsory educational establishments, and students of reformatories.

  2. Superintendents of prisons, detention centers, directors of custody centers, directors of compulsory educational establishments, and principals of reformatories shall closely cooperate with local medical facilities in implementing Clause 1 of this Article.

  Article 39. Exemption from serving the remaining period under decision on admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities for individuals aged between full 14 years and under 18 years currently undergoing compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation

  Individuals aged between full 14 years and under 18 years who are currently undergoing compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation but are found to have committed criminal acts before or during the period of serving the decision on compulsory narcotic substance rehabilitation and are sentenced to imprisonment by the Court without being granted suspended sentences shall be exempted from serving the remaining period under the decision on admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities.

  Article 40. Post-rehabilitation management at residence

  1. Individuals who have completed voluntary rehabilitation, individuals who have completed treatment for narcotic substances in the form of opium substitutes, and individuals aged between full 12 years and under 18 years who have completely served the decision on admission to compulsory rehabilitation facilities shall be subject to post-rehabilitation management for 1 year from the date of completion of rehabilitation or from the date of completion of serving of the decision.

  2. Individuals who have completely served decisions on administrative measures and been placed in compulsory rehabilitation facilities shall be subject to post-rehabilitation management for 2 years from the date of completion of serving such decisions.

  3. Post-rehabilitation management includes:

  a) Making list of individuals under post-rehabilitation management;

  b) Counseling, assisting, preventing relapse;

  c) Monitoring, detecting, preventing illegal use of narcotic substances.

  4. Social support during post-rehabilitation management includes:

  a) Educational support for individuals from 12 years of age to less than 18 years of age;

  b) Vocational education, loans, job hunting, participation in social activities for community reintegration.

  5. Chairpersons of commune-level People’s Committees shall issue decisions and organize management and social assistance after rehabilitation.

  6. The Government shall provide regulations on application, procedures, management regime and support policies for individuals under post-rehabilitation management.

  Article 41. Interventions to reduce harms to illicit drug users and drug addicts

  1. Interventions to reduce harms to illicit drug users and drug addicts are measures to reduce harms related to illicit drug use causing harm to themselves, their families and community.

  2. Interventions to reduce harms to illicit drug users and drug addicts include:

  a) Treatment for narcotic substance addiction with substitution therapy;

  b) Other measures as prescribed by the Government.

  Article 42. Responsibilities of families of drug addicts and community

  1. Drug addicts’ families shall:

  a) Support drug addicts during rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation and community reintegration;

  b) Cooperate with competent agencies and persons in preparing dossiers for compulsory rehabilitation; cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations and units in supporting drug addicts during rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management and community reintegration.

  2. Communities where drug addicts reside shall:

  a) Encourage and assist drug addicts;

  b) Cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations and units in supporting drug addicts during rehabilitation, post-rehabilitation management and community reintegration.

  Article 43. Preparation of lists of drug addicts and persons placed under post-rehabilitation management

  1. Commune-level police authorities shall assist People’s Committees at the same level in preparing lists of drug addicts and persons placed under post-rehabilitation management residing in their communes.

  2. When rehabilitating addicts or persons placed under post-rehabilitation management change their residence, commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move to shall notify commune-level police authorities of administrative divisions where they move from within 5 working days from the date on which they leave the administrative divisions to include them in the list and cooperate in management.

  3. Commune-level police authorities shall assist People’s Committees at the same level in removing individuals from the list of rehabilitating addicts or persons placed under post-rehabilitation management in the following cases:

  a) During post-rehabilitation management period specified in Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 40 hereof, if such individuals are not found using narcotic substances illegally;

  b) Rehabilitating addicts or persons placed under post-rehabilitation management must serve prison sentences;

  c) Rehabilitating addicts or persons placed under post-rehabilitation management move to reside in other administrative divisions;

  d) Rehabilitating addicts or persons placed under post-rehabilitation management die or are declared missing by the Court.

  Chapter VI

                            STATE MANAGEMENT OF NARCOTIC SUBSTANCE PREVENTION AND COMBAT

  Article 44. Contents of state management of drug prevention and combat

  1. Formulate and implement strategies, policies and plans on drug prevention and combat.

  2. Promulgate and organize implementation of legal documents on drug prevention and combat.

  3. Organize apparatus, train and nurture persons engaged in drug prevention and combat.

  4. Control lawful activities related to narcotic substances.

  5. Implement narcotic substance rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  6. Organize drug prevention and combat.

  7. Perform state statistics on drug prevention and combat.

  8. To conduct research and application of scientific and technological advances in drug prevention and combat.

  9. To organize communication and education on drug prevention and combat.

  10. To undertake international cooperation on drug prevention and combat.

  11. To commend, inspect, examine, settle complaints and denunciations, and handle violations of law on drug prevention and combat.

  Article 45. State management responsibility for drug prevention and combat

  1. The Government shall perform the unified state management of drug prevention and combat.

  2. The Ministry of Public Security shall act as the focal agency to assist the Government in performing the state management of drug prevention and combat.

  3. Ministries, ministerial-level agencies and Government-attached agencies shall, within the scope of their tasks and powers, implement and coordinate with relevant agencies and organizations in drug prevention and combat.

  4. People’s Committees of all levels shall, within their tasks and powers, perform state management of drug prevention and control in localities.

  Article 46. Responsibilities of the Ministry of Public Security

  1. Promulgate or submit to competent regulatory authorities for promulgation legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies and organizations in drug prevention and control; receive and handle information on drug-related crimes; control legal activities related to narcotic substances within its competence.

  3. Organize assessment of narcotic substances and precursors.

  4. Provide training and advanced training for persons engaged in investigation and combat against drug-related crimes.

  5. Guide the compilation of lists of illicit drug users, drug addicts, and individuals under post-rehabilitation management.

  6. Take charge of conducting state statistics on drug prevention and control; manage information on drug-related crimes, illicit drug users, drug addicts, individuals under post-rehabilitation management, and results of controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances.

  7. Implement international cooperation in drug prevention and control.

  Article 47. Responsibilities of Ministry of Labor, War Invalids and Social Affairs

  1. Promulgate or request competent authorities to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies and organizations and local governments in guiding community-based and family-based voluntary rehabilitation, rehabilitation at rehabilitation facilities and post-rehabilitation management.

  3. Guide establishment, dissolution and operation of public rehabilitation facilities.

  4. Provide training for persons engaged in rehabilitation and post-rehabilitation management.

  5. Guide and direct implementation of communication and education on drug prevention and control in vocational education and training institutions.

  6. Implement international cooperation in rehabilitation.

  7. Statistics on rehabilitating drug addicts at their own homes, in community, or at rehabilitation centers.

  Article 48. Responsibilities of Ministry of National Defense

  1. Promulgate according to its competence or request competent regulatory authorities to promulgate legislative documents on prevention and control of narcotic substances.

  2. Organize implementation of plans for prevention and control of narcotic substances in border areas on land, at sea, contiguous zones, exclusive economic zones and continental shelves.

  3. Direct Vietnam Border Guard and Coast Guard forces to take charge and cooperate with relevant agencies, organizations and competent forces in receiving and handling information on narcotic substance-related crimes, detecting, preventing and combating violations against regulations on narcotic substances, and controlling legal activities related to narcotic substances in the areas and localities specified in Clause 2 of this Article in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  4. Provide training and advanced training for persons engaged in prevention and combat of narcotic substance-related crimes of Vietnam Border Guard and Coast Guard forces.

  5. Produce statistics on drug addicts in prisons, detention camps and custody houses under management of Ministry of National Defense.

  Article 49. Responsibilities of Ministry of Health

  1. Promulgate or request competent regulatory agencies to promulgate legislative documents on drug prevention and control.

  2. Research narcotic substances and rehabilitation methods; issue and revoke licenses for narcotic substance circulation and rehabilitation methods; provide personnel and medical technical expertise for narcotic substance rehabilitation.

  3. Statistics on persons undergoing treatment for addiction to opium substitutes.

  Article 50. Responsibilities of Ministry of Finance

  Direct customs authorities to implement drug prevention and control activities in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Chapter VII

                             INTERNATIONAL COOPERATION ON NARCOTIC PREVENTION AND COMBAT

  Article 51. Principles in international cooperation on drug prevention and combat

  1. The State of Vietnam shall implement international treaties on drug prevention and combat and other relevant international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory on the principles of respecting independence, sovereignty, territorial integrity and mutual benefit; cooperate with foreign countries, international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals in drug prevention and combat.

  2. On the basis of international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory, this Law and other relevant laws, and concluded international agreements, competent authorities of Vietnam shall implement cooperation programs on drug prevention and combat with relevant agencies of foreign countries, international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals.

  Article 52. International cooperation policies on drug prevention and combat

  The State of Vietnam shall implement multilateral and bilateral international cooperation policies on drug prevention and combat on the principles of respecting national independence and sovereignty with other countries; encourage international organizations, foreign organizations and individuals to cooperate with Vietnamese agencies and organizations in supporting each other in terms of material facilities, enhancing legal, information and technological training and medical capabilities for drug prevention and combat.

  Article 53. Controlled goods transfer

  The State of Vietnam shall comply with requests for controlled goods transfer on the basis of international treaties to which the Socialist Republic of Vietnam is a signatory to detect and criminally prosecute persons committing drug-related crimes. The decision to apply and implement this measure shall be made under agreement between competent authorities of Vietnam and relevant countries.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS

  Article 54. Entry into force

  1. This Law comes into force as of January 01, 2022.

  2. The Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 ceases to be effective from the effective date of this Law.

  Article 55. Transitional provisions

  1. From the effective date of this Law:

  a) Persons who are currently receiving community-based or family-based voluntary rehabilitation shall continue to receive voluntary rehabilitation until the end of their registered period in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 and placed under post-rehabilitation management in accordance with this Law;

  b) Persons placed under post-rehabilitation management shall continue to comply with decisions on post-rehabilitation management until the end of the post-rehabilitation management period in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12. Where a person placed under post-rehabilitation management is 18 years of age or older from the effective date of this Law and relapses, they shall be subject to administrative measures for placement in a compulsory rehabilitation facility;

  c) Persons currently serving a community-based compulsory rehabilitation in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 may register for voluntary rehabilitation in accordance with this Law. The period served under the decision on community-based compulsory rehabilitation shall be included in the period of voluntary rehabilitation;

  d) Individuals subject to mandatory rehabilitation in accordance with the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 during preparation of documents for admission to compulsory rehabilitation centers shall undergo rehabilitation in accordance with this Law;

  dd) Licenses related to lawful activities involving narcotic substances issued shall remain valid until expiry date stated on the license.

  2. Within 02 years from the effective date hereof, compulsory rehabilitation facilities established under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 shall satisfy all conditions of public rehabilitation facilities according to this Law.

  Other rehabilitation facilities established under the Law on Prevention and Control of Narcotic Substances No. 23/2000/QH10 amended by the Law No. 16/2008/QH12 and issued with operation licenses before the effective date hereof shall continue operating until expiry date of the licenses.
