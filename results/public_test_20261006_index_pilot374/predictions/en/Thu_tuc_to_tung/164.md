                                                       THE LAW

                                              JUDICIAL REVIEW

  Pursuant to the Constitution of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam of 1992, amended by Resolution No. 51/2001/QH10;

  The National Assembly promulgates the Law on Judicial Expertise.

  Chapter I

                                          GENERAL PROVISIONS

  Article 1. Scope

  This Law provides for judicial experts, judicial assessment organizations, case-specific judicial experts and case-specific judicial assessment organizations, judicial assessment activities, judicial assessment costs, regimes and policies in judicial assessment activities, and responsibilities of state agencies towards judicial assessment organizations and activities.

  Article 2. Interpretation of terms

  In this Law, the following terms are construed as follows:

  1. Judicial assessment means the use of scientific, technical and professional knowledge, means and methods by a judicial expert to conclude on professional issues related to investigation, prosecution, trial and enforcement of criminal judgments, settlement of civil cases and administrative cases at the request of a procedural body, a procedural person or at the request of a person requesting assessment as prescribed by this Law.

  2. Persons requesting assessment include procedural bodies and procedural persons.

  3. Persons requesting assessment are persons who may request assessment themselves after requesting a procedural body or procedural person to request assessment but their request is not accepted. Persons who may request assessment themselves include litigants in civil cases and administrative cases, civil plaintiffs, civil defendants, persons with interests and obligations related to criminal cases or their lawful representatives, except for cases where the request for assessment relates to determination of criminal liability of suspects or defendants.

  4. Judicial assessment organizations include judicial experts, case-specific judicial experts, public judicial assessment organizations, non-public judicial assessment organizations and case-specific judicial assessment organizations.

  5. Judicial experts include judicial experts and case-specific judicial experts.

  6. Judicial experts are persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1, Article 7 of this Law and are appointed by competent regulatory agencies to perform judicial expertise.

  7. Case-specific judicial experts are persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2, Article 18 and Article 20 of this Law and are solicited or requested to perform judicial expertise.

  8. Case-specific judicial expertise organizations are agencies or organizations that meet the criteria specified in Article 19 and Article 20 of this Law and are solicited or requested to perform judicial expertise.

  Article 3. Principles of judicial expertise

  1. Compliance with law and professional regulations.

  2. Truthfulness, accuracy, objectivity, impartiality and timeliness.

  3. Concluding only on professional issues within the scope of request.

  4. To take responsibility before law for the conclusion of assessment.

  Article 4. Responsibilities of individuals and organizations for judicial assessment activities

  1. Individuals and organizations that are solicited or requested to conduct judicial assessment shall have to receive and conduct judicial assessment in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  2. Other individuals and organizations shall have to create conditions for judicial assessors to conduct assessment in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  Article 5. State policies on judicial assessment activities

  1. The State shall invest in and develop the system of public judicial assessment organizations in fields with large and regular assessment needs to meet the requirements of legal proceedings; have policies to create favorable conditions for the development of private judicial assessment organizations.

  2. The State shall have policies to prioritize the training and professional retraining of judicial assessors.

  Article 6. Prohibited acts

  1. Refusing to issue judicial expertise conclusions without a plausible reason.

  2. Intentionally issuing false judicial expertise conclusions.

  3. Intentionally prolonging the time for judicial expertise.

  4. Taking advantage of judicial expertise to seek personal benefits.

  5. Disclosing confidential information known during judicial expertise.

  6. Inciting or forcing judicial experts to issue false conclusions.

  7. Interfering with or obstructing the performance of judicial expertise by judicial experts.

  Chapter II

                                           JUDICIAL EXPERTS

  Article 7. Criteria for appointment of judicial experts

  1. Vietnamese citizens permanently residing in Vietnam who meet the following criteria may be considered for appointment as judicial experts:

  a) Good health and moral qualities;

  b) Having a university degree or higher and having at least 05 years of professional experience in the field of training.

  In case of persons proposed for appointment as forensic, forensic psychiatric, or criminal technical experts, they must have at least 03 years of professional experience in judicial expertise at forensic, forensic psychiatric, or criminal technical expertise organizations.

  c) Persons proposed for appointment as judicial experts in the fields of forensic, forensic psychiatric, and criminal technical expertise must have certificates of professional training or refresher courses.

  2. Persons falling into one of the following cases shall not be appointed as judicial experts:

  a) Having lost or restricted civil act capacity;

  b) Being investigated for penal liability; having been convicted but not yet having their criminal record erased for unintentional or less serious intentional crimes; having been convicted of serious, very serious or particularly serious crimes intentionally;

  c) Being subject to administrative education measures in communes, wards or townships, being sent to compulsory rehabilitation centers or compulsory education centers.

  3. The Minister or the head of a ministerial agency shall elaborate Clause 1 of this Article for judicial experts in the field under their management after reaching agreement with the Minister of Justice.

  Article 8. Dossier for request for appointment of judicial experts

  1. A written request for appointment of a judicial expert.

  2. A copy of a university degree or higher diploma relevant to the professional field for which the appointment is requested.

  3. A resume and a criminal record certificate.

  4. Certificates of actual professional working time of agencies and organizations where the proposed appointees work.

  5. Training or refresher certificates for the proposed judicial assessors in the fields of forensic medicine, forensic psychiatry and criminal technical assessment.

  6. Other papers proving that the proposed appointees meet the criteria prescribed by the Minister or the head of the ministerial-level agency competent to manage the field of assessment.

  Article 9. Competence, order and procedures for appointment of judicial assessors

  1. The Minister of Health shall appoint forensic medical assessors and forensic psychiatry assessors working at central agencies.

  The Minister of Public Security shall appoint criminal technical assessors working at central agencies.

  The Minister or the head of the ministerial-level agency shall appoint judicial assessors working in other fields at central agencies under their management.

  The President of the People’s Committee of the province or centrally-run city (hereinafter referred to as the provincial level) shall appoint judicial assessors in the locality.

  2. The Ministry of National Defense and the Ministry of Public Security shall select persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law and request the Minister of Health to appoint forensic experts affiliated to their ministries.

  The Ministry of National Defense shall select persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law and request the Minister of Public Security to appoint criminal technical experts affiliated to their ministries.

  The heads of units affiliated to ministries and ministerial agencies assigned to manage judicial expertise activities shall select persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law and request the ministers and heads of ministerial agencies to appoint judicial experts in the fields under their management.

  The heads of specialized agencies of provincial People’s Committees managing the field of judicial expertise shall assume the prime responsibility and coordinate with the directors of provincial Departments of Justice in selecting persons who meet the criteria specified in Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law, receive applications for appointment of judicial experts specified in Article 8 of this Law, and request the presidents of provincial People’s Committees to appoint judicial experts in their localities.

  Within 20 days from the date of receipt of valid applications, the ministers, heads of ministerial agencies, and presidents of provincial People’s Committees shall decide to appoint judicial experts. In case of refusal, they must notify the applicants in writing and clearly state the reasons.

  3. Ministries, ministerial agencies, and provincial People’s Committees shall prepare and post lists of judicial experts on the websites of their ministries, ministerial agencies, and provincial People’s Committees, and simultaneously send them to the Ministry of Justice for compilation of a general list of judicial experts.

  Article 10. Dismissal of judicial experts

  1. Cases of dismissal of judicial experts:

  a) No longer meets the criteria specified in Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law;

  b) Falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 Article 7 of this Law;

  c) Is disciplined in the form of warning or higher or is administratively sanctioned for intentionally violating regulations of the law on judicial expertise;

  d) Commits one of the acts specified in Article 6 of this Law;

  dd) At the request of the judicial expert who is a civil servant, public employee, military officer, people's public security officer, professional soldier, or national defense worker who has received a decision to retire or resign.

  2. An application for dismissal of a judicial expert shall include:

  a) A written request for dismissal of the judicial expert issued by the agency or organization that requested the appointment of such person;

  b) A written document or paper proving that the judicial expert falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article.

  3. The Minister of Public Security and the Minister of National Defense shall consider and propose the Minister of Health to dismiss the forensic experts under their management.

  The Minister of National Defense shall consider and propose the Minister of Public Security to dismiss the criminal technical experts under their management.

  The Minister or the head of a ministerial agency shall dismiss the judicial experts operating at central agencies under their management at the proposal of the head of the unit under the Ministry or the ministerial agency assigned to manage judicial expertise.

  The President of the provincial People’s Committee shall dismiss the judicial experts in the locality at the proposal of the head of the specialized agency of the People’s Committee after the head of the specialized agency has reached an agreement with the Director of the Department of Justice.

  4. Within 10 days from the date of receipt of a valid dossier, the Minister, the head of a ministerial agency, or the President of the provincial People’s Committee shall consider and decide to dismiss the judicial experts and adjust the list of judicial experts on the website of the Ministry, the ministerial agency, or the provincial People’s Committee, and simultaneously send it to the Ministry of Justice for adjustment of the general list of judicial experts.

  Article 11. Rights and obligations of judicial experts

  1. To conduct expertise at the request of the requester or the person requesting the expertise or at the assignment of the agency or organization requested or requested.

  2. To refuse to conduct expertise in cases where the content of the expertise is beyond their professional capacity; the subjects of expertise and related documents provided are insufficient or not valid for conclusion of expertise; the time is insufficient for conducting expertise or there are other legitimate reasons. In case of refusal to conduct expertise, within 05 working days from the date of receipt of the request or request for expertise, they must notify the requester or the person requesting the expertise in writing and clearly state the reason.

  3. Attending professional training courses on assessment and legal knowledge.

  4. Setting up a judicial assessment office when the conditions specified in Article 15 of this Law are met.

  5. Setting up and participating in judicial assessor associations in accordance with the law on associations.

  6. Enjoying benefits and policies in accordance with this Law and other relevant laws.

  7. The rights and obligations specified in Article 23 and Clause 1, Article 34 of this Law.

  Chapter III

                                       JUDICIAL ASSESSMENT ORGANIZATIONS

  Section 1. PUBLIC JUDICIAL ASSESSMENT ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 12. Public forensic examination organizations

  1. Public forensic examination organizations shall be established by competent state agencies in the fields of forensic medicine, forensic psychiatry and criminal techniques.

  Where necessary, the Minister, the head of a ministerial agency or the President of the provincial People’s Committee shall consider and decide on the establishment of or propose to a competent agency to establish public forensic examination organizations in other fields after reaching agreement with the Minister of Justice.

  2. Public forensic examination organizations in the field of forensic medicine include:

  a) The National Forensic Institute affiliated to the Ministry of Health;

  b) The provincial forensic centers;

  c) The military forensic institutes affiliated to the Ministry of National Defense;

  d) The forensic examination centers affiliated to the Institute of Criminal Sciences, the Ministry of Public Security.

  3. Public forensic psychiatric assessment organizations include:

  a) The Central Forensic Psychiatry Institute affiliated to the Ministry of Health;

  b) The Regional Forensic Psychiatry Center affiliated to the Ministry of Health.

  Based on the forensic psychiatric assessment requests of procedural activities and the actual conditions of regions and areas nationwide, the Minister of Health shall consider and decide on the establishment of the regional forensic psychiatry center after reaching agreement with the Minister of Justice.

  4. Public forensic criminal technical assessment organizations include:

  a) The Criminal Science Institute affiliated to the Ministry of Public Security;

  b) The Criminal Technical Division affiliated to the provincial-level Public Security;

  c) The Criminal Technical Assessment Division affiliated to the Ministry of National Defense.

  5. Based on the actual needs and conditions of the locality, the criminal technical division of the provincial-level police authority shall have a forensic expert to conduct the forensic examination of the corpse.

  6. Public judicial assessment organizations shall have their own seals and accounts as prescribed by law.

  7. The Government shall elaborate the functions, tasks, organizational structure and working regime of public judicial assessment organizations prescribed in this Article.

  Article 13. Ensuring material facilities for public judicial assessment organizations

  1. Public judicial assessment organizations shall be provided with material facilities, funding, equipment, means and other necessary conditions for the performance of judicial assessment by the State.

  2. Funding for operation of public judicial assessment organizations shall be provided by the state budget and other sources of revenue as prescribed by law.

  3. The Ministry of Health shall elaborate the conditions for material facilities, equipment and means of assessment for public judicial assessment organizations in the fields of forensic medicine and forensic psychiatry.

  The Ministry of Public Security shall elaborate the conditions for material facilities, equipment and means of assessment for public judicial assessment organizations in the field of criminal technical assessment.

  Section 2. NON-GOVERNMENTAL JUDICIAL ASSESSMENT ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 14. Judicial assessment offices

  1. A judicial assessment office is a non-governmental judicial assessment organization established in the financial, banking, construction, antiques, relics, and copyright fields.

  2. A judicial assessment office established by 01 judicial assessor shall be organized and operate as a private enterprise. A judicial assessment office established by 02 or more judicial assessors shall be organized and operate as a partnership company.

  The legal representative of a judicial assessment office is its head. The head of a judicial assessment office must be a judicial assessor.

  Article 15. Conditions for establishment of a judicial assessment office

  1. A judicial assessor may establish a judicial assessment office when he/she meets the following conditions:

  a) Having been a judicial assessor in the field of establishment of the office for at least 05 years;

  b) Having a plan for establishment as prescribed in Point d, Clause 2, Article 16 of this Law.

  2. Officials, civil servants, public employees, military officers, public security officers, professional soldiers, and national defense workers are not allowed to establish judicial assessment offices.

  Article 16. Granting of licenses for establishment of judicial assessment offices

  1. The President of the People’s Committee of the province where the judicial assessment office is located shall consider and decide to grant the establishment of a judicial assessment office at the request of the Director of the Department of Justice.

  2. A judicial assessor who wishes to establish a judicial assessment office shall submit an application for establishment to the Department of Justice. The application shall include:

  a) An application for establishment;

  b) A copy of the decision on appointment of the judicial assessor;

  c) A draft regulation on organization and operation of the judicial assessment office.

  d) The plan for establishment of the judicial assessment office must clearly state the purpose of establishment; the proposed name, personnel, location of the head office; conditions on material facilities, equipment and assessment means according to regulations of ministries and ministerial-level agencies managing professional fields of assessment and implementation plans.

  3. Within 30 days from the date of receipt of a complete and valid dossier, the Director of the Department of Justice shall consider and appraise the dossier for permission to establish the judicial assessment office, reach an agreement with the head of the professional agency of the provincial People’s Committee managing the judicial assessment field, and submit it to the President of the provincial People’s Committee for consideration and decision.

  Within 15 days from the date of submission of the dossier by the Department of Justice, the President of the provincial People’s Committee shall consider and decide to permit the establishment of the judicial assessment office. In case of refusal, a written notice must be issued, clearly stating the reasons. The rejected party has the right to lodge a complaint or file a lawsuit according to law.

  Article 17. Registration of operation of the judicial assessment office

  1. Within 1 year from the date on which the President of the provincial People’s Committee decides to permit the establishment, the judicial assessment office shall register its operation at the Department of Justice.

  After 1 year from the date on which the President of the provincial People’s Committee decides to permit the establishment, if the judicial assessment office fails to register its operation, the Decision on permit to establish the judicial assessment office shall become invalid.

  2. The judicial assessment office shall send its application for registration of operation to the Department of Justice. The application shall include:

  a) An application for registration of operation;

  b) Regulations on organization and operation of the judicial assessment office;

  c) Papers proving that the judicial assessment office is eligible for operation as prescribed in the establishment plan specified in Point d, Clause 2, Article 16 of this Law;

  d) Copies of the decision on permission for establishment of the judicial assessment office.

  3. Within 30 days from the date of receipt of a complete and valid dossier, the Department of Justice shall take charge and cooperate with the specialized agency of the provincial People’s Committee managing the judicial assessment field to check whether the conditions specified in the establishment plan specified in Point d, Clause 2, Article 16 of this Law are satisfied and issue the Certificate of operation; in case of refusal, it must notify in writing and clearly state the reasons, and report to the President of the provincial People’s Committee for consideration and decision on revocation of the decision on permission for establishment. The person who is refused may lodge a complaint or file a lawsuit according to law.

  4. The judicial assessment office may operate from the date on which it is issued with the Certificate of operation.

  Chapter IV

   CASE-BASED JUDICIAL ASSESSORS AND CASE-BASED JUDICIAL ASSESSMENT ORGANIZATIONS

  Article 18. Case-based judicial assessors

  1. Vietnamese citizens permanently residing in Vietnam who meet the following criteria may be selected as case-specific judicial assessors:

  a) Good health and moral qualities;

  b) Holding a university degree or higher and having at least 5 years of professional experience in the field of training.

  2. In cases where a person does not hold a university degree but has in-depth knowledge and extensive practical experience in the field to be assessed, they may be selected as a case-specific judicial assessor.

  3. Case-specific judicial assessors shall conduct assessments at the request or order of the judicial assessor as prescribed in this Law. Case-specific judicial assessors have the rights and obligations specified in Clauses 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 Article 11 of this Law.

  Article 19. Case-specific judicial assessment organizations

  1. Case-specific judicial assessment organizations must meet the following conditions:

  a) Having legal personality;

  b) Having professional activities suitable for the contents of the request for judicial expertise;

  c) Having conditions for professional staff and material facilities to ensure the performance of judicial expertise.

  2. Case-based judicial expertise shall be carried out in accordance with the provisions of this Law. The head of the organization shall be responsible for receiving and assigning persons to perform judicial expertise.

  3. Ministries, ministerial-level agencies, Governmental agencies and provincial-level People’s Committees shall only perform judicial expertise at the request of the judicial expertise requester.

  Article 20. Compilation and publication of lists of case-based judicial experts and case-based judicial expertise organizations

  1. The Ministry of Construction, the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, the Ministry of Information and Communications, the Ministry of Planning and Investment, the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment, the Ministry of Transport, the Ministry of Science and Technology, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, the State Bank of Vietnam, other ministries, ministerial-level agencies and provincial-level People’s Committees shall be responsible for selecting, compiling and annually publishing lists of case-based judicial experts and case-based judicial expertise organizations in the fields under their management to meet the requirements for expertise of procedural activities.

  The list shall be accompanied by information on the expertise specialty, experience and capacity of the case-based judicial expert or the case-based judicial expertise organization and shall be posted on the website of the ministry, ministerial-level agency or provincial-level People’s Committee, and sent to the Ministry of Justice for compilation of a general list.

  2. In special cases, the judicial expertise requester may request a professional individual or organization that is qualified but not on the published list to perform the expertise, but must clearly state the reason.

  At the request of the procedural authority, the procedural officer, the ministry, the ministerial agency, or the specialized agency of the provincial People’s Committee managing the field of expertise shall have to introduce individuals and organizations that are eligible to conduct expertise outside the list of those already published.

  Chapter V

                                     JURISPRUDENTIAL EXPERTISE

  Article 21. Rights and obligations of the judicial expert solicitor

  1. The judicial expert solicitor has the right to:

  a) Solicit individuals and organizations specified in Clause 4 Article 2 of this Law to conduct expertise;

  b) Request individuals and organizations specified in Point a Clause 1 of this Article to provide the expertise conclusion in accordance with the requested content and time limit;

  c) Request individuals and organizations that have conducted the judicial expertise to explain the expertise conclusion.

  2. The person requesting the assessment shall have the following obligations:

  a) Select an organization or individual to conduct the assessment in accordance with the nature and requirements of the case to be assessed;

  b) Issue a written decision to request the assessment;

  c) Provide information and documents related to the subject matter of the assessment at the request of the judicial assessment organization or individual;

  d) Make advance payment for judicial assessment expenses when requesting the assessment; promptly and fully pay the judicial assessment expenses to the judicial assessment organization or individual upon receiving the assessment conclusion;

  dd) Ensure the safety of the judicial assessor during the assessment process or when participating in legal proceedings as a judicial assessor.

  Article 22. Rights and obligations of the person requesting the judicial assessment

  1. The person requesting the assessment may send a written request to the procedural body or the procedural person to request the assessment. If the procedural body or the procedural person does not accept the request, it must notify the person requesting the assessment in writing within 07 days. Upon the expiration of the above-mentioned time limit or from the date of receipt of the notice of refusal to request the assessment, the person requesting the assessment may request the assessment on their own.

  2. The person requesting the assessment has the right to:

  a) Request the judicial assessor to provide the assessment conclusion within the agreed time limit and according to the requested content;

  b) Request the judicial assessor to explain the assessment conclusion;

  c) Request the court to summon the judicial assessor who has conducted the assessment to participate in the court hearing to explain and present the assessment conclusion;

  d) Request the procedural body or the procedural person to request the re-assessment; request the additional assessment according to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 29 of this Law.

  3. The person requesting the judicial assessment has the obligation to:

  a) Provide information and documents related to the subject of assessment at the request of the judicial assessor and take responsibility for the accuracy of the information and documents provided by themselves;

  b) Pay the advance payment for judicial assessment costs when requesting the assessment; pay the assessment costs in a timely and full manner to the judicial assessor upon receiving the assessment conclusion.

  4. The requesters shall only exercise the right to request the assessment before the court issues a decision to bring the case to first-instance trial.

  Article 23. Rights and obligations of judicial assessors when conducting judicial assessment

  1. Judicial assessors have the right to:

  a) Select necessary and appropriate methods for conducting assessment according to the contents of the assessment request;

  b) Use experimental results, additional tests or professional conclusions made by other individuals or organizations for the purpose of assessment;

  c) Independently make assessment conclusions.

  2. Judicial assessors have the obligation to:

  a) Comply with the principles of conducting judicial assessment;

  b) To conduct the assessment in accordance with the assessment request;

  c) To make and return the assessment conclusion within the prescribed time limit; in case of necessity, to notify the assessor or the assessor’s requestor thereof;

  d) To prepare the assessment dossier;

  dd) To preserve the assessment specimen and documents related to the assessment case;

  e) To refrain from notifying the assessment result to other persons, unless otherwise agreed in writing by the assessor or the assessor’s requestor;

  g) To bear personal responsibility for the assessment conclusion. In case of intentionally making a false assessment conclusion, causing damage to an individual or organization, the assessor shall also pay compensation and reimbursement in accordance with the law.

  3. Apart from the rights and obligations prescribed in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the judicial assessor shall have other rights and obligations in accordance with the law on proceedings.

  Article 24. Rights and obligations of the organization requested or requested to conduct the judicial assessment

  1. Organizations solicited or requested for judicial expertise shall have the right to:

  a) Request the soliciting or requesting person to provide information and documents necessary for the expertise;

  b) Refuse to perform the expertise if they do not have sufficient conditions for the performance of the expertise;

  c) Be entitled to advance payment of judicial expertise costs when solicited or requested for judicial expertise; and be entitled to timely and full payment of judicial expertise costs upon the return of the expertise results.

  2. Organizations solicited or requested for judicial expertise shall have the obligation to:

  a) Receive and assign persons with appropriate professional competence to perform the expertise according to the solicitation or request, and take responsibility for the professional competence of such persons; assign persons responsible for coordinating the performance of the expertise in cases where multiple persons are required to perform the expertise;

  b) Ensure the necessary equipment, facilities and other conditions for the performance of the expertise;

  c) Pay compensation for damage in cases where the persons assigned to perform the expertise by them intentionally make incorrect conclusions, causing damage to individuals or organizations.

  d) To notify the person requesting the assessment in writing within 05 working days from the date of receipt of the decision to request the assessment and clearly state the reason for refusal in case of refusal to accept the request for assessment.

  Article 25. Judicial assessment request

  1. The person requesting the assessment shall decide to request the judicial assessment in writing and send the decision together with the subject matter of assessment and relevant documents and objects (if any) to the person or organization performing the assessment.

  2. The decision to request the assessment must contain the following contents:

  a) Name of the requesting agency; full name of the person competent to request the assessment;

  b) Name of the organization; full name of the person requested for assessment;

  c) Name and characteristics of the subject matter of assessment;

  d) Name of the relevant document or the comparison sample enclosed (if any);

  d) Contents of the request for assessment;

  e) Date of request for assessment and time limit for return of assessment conclusion.

  3. In case of request for additional assessment or re-assessment, the decision on request for assessment must clearly state that it is a request for additional assessment or re-assessment.

  Article 26. Request for judicial assessment in civil cases, administrative cases and criminal cases

  1. The person requesting assessment must send a written request for assessment together with the subject matter of assessment, relevant documents and objects (if any) and a copy of documents proving that he/she is a party to the civil case, administrative case, civil plaintiff, civil defendant, person with relevant interests and obligations in the criminal case or their lawful representative to the individual or organization performing the assessment.

  2. The written request for judicial assessment must contain the following contents:

  a) Name of the organization or full name of the person requesting assessment;

  b) Contents of the request for assessment;

  c) Name and characteristics of the subject matter of assessment;

  d) Name of relevant documents or comparison samples enclosed (if any);

  dd) Date of request for assessment and time limit for return of assessment conclusions;

  e) Signature, full name of the requester for assessment.

  Article 27. Delivery and receipt of dossiers and subjects of request for assessment

  1. Dossiers and subjects of request for assessment shall be delivered or received in person or sent to the assessor by post.

  2. The delivery and receipt of dossiers and subjects of request for assessment shall be made in writing. The written record of delivery and receipt shall contain the following information:

  a) Time and place of delivery and receipt of assessment dossiers;

  b) Full names of representatives of the assessor and the assessed;

  c) The request for assessment or written request for assessment; the assessed; relevant documents and objects;

  d) The method of preserving the assessed, relevant documents and objects when handing over and receiving them;

  dd) The condition of the assessed, relevant documents and objects when handing over and receiving them;

  e) The signature of the representative of the assessor and the assessed.

  3. The postal delivery of dossiers and assessed objects shall be made using a service with a number. The recipient shall be responsible for preserving the service with a number and making a record upon opening the seal as prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article.

  4. In case of handing over and receiving human forensic or psychiatric assessed objects, the assessor shall take charge and coordinate with the requested individual or organization in managing the assessed object during the assessment.

  5. Upon completion of the assessment, the assessor shall hand over the assessed object to the assessor, unless otherwise provided by law.

  1. The person requesting or requesting the assessment shall have to take back the assessed object as prescribed by law.

  The delivery and return of the assessed object after the assessment is completed shall comply with the provisions of Clauses 2 and 3 of this Article.

  Article 28. Individual assessment and collective assessment

  1. Individual assessment means the assessment performed by 1 person. Collective assessment means the assessment performed by 2 or more persons.

  2. In case of individual assessment, the assessor shall perform the assessment, sign the assessment conclusion and take personal responsibility for the assessment conclusion.

  3. In case of collective assessment in a professional field, the assessors shall perform the assessment together, sign the joint assessment conclusion and take joint responsibility for the assessment conclusion; if they have different opinions, the assessor shall record his/her opinion in the assessment conclusion and take responsibility for that opinion.

  In case of collective assessment in different professional fields, each assessor shall perform the assessment part belonging to his/her professional field and take personal responsibility for that assessment conclusion.

  Article 29. Additional assessment and re-assessment

  1. Additional assessment shall be conducted in cases where the contents of the assessment conclusion are unclear or insufficient or when new issues arise related to the circumstances of the case or matter that has been assessed previously. The request for additional assessment shall be made as the first assessment.

  2. Reassessment shall be conducted in cases where there are grounds to believe that the first assessment conclusion is inaccurate or in cases specified in Clause 2 Article 30 of this Law.

  3. The assessor shall decide on the request for reassessment either on their own or at the request of the assessor. If the assessor does not accept the request for reassessment, they must notify the requester in writing and clearly state the reasons.

  Article 30. Assessment council

  1. In cases where there is a difference between the first assessment conclusion and the reassessment conclusion regarding the same assessment content, the second assessment shall be decided by the assessor. The second assessment must be conducted by the assessment council.

  The Minister or the head of a ministerial agency managing the field requiring assessment shall decide to establish a council to conduct the second assessment. The assessment council consists of at least 03 members who are highly professional and reputable in the field requiring assessment. The assessment council operates under the collective assessment mechanism specified in Clause 3 Article 28 of this Law.

  2. In special cases, the Chief Procurator of the Supreme People’s Procuracy or the Chief Justice of the Supreme People’s Court shall decide on the reassessment after the assessment council has issued its conclusion.

  Article 31. Written record of the judicial assessment process

  1. The judicial expert must promptly, fully and honestly record the entire process and results of the judicial expertise in writing.

  2. The written record of the judicial expertise process must be kept in the judicial expertise file.

  Article 32. Judicial expertise conclusions

  1. A judicial expertise conclusion is a written comment and assessment of the judicial expert on the subject matter of the judicial expertise according to the contents of the request for judicial expertise. A judicial expertise conclusion must include the following contents:

  a) Full name of the judicial expert; organization conducting the judicial expertise;

  b) Name of the procedural body; full name of the procedural body requesting the judicial expertise; number of the written request for judicial expertise or full name of the person requesting the judicial expertise;

  c) Information identifying the subject matter of the judicial expertise;

  d) Time of receipt of the written request for judicial expertise;

  d) Contents of the request for assessment;

  e) Methods of assessment;

  g) Conclusion on the subject of assessment;

  h) Time and place of assessment.

  2. In case of solicitation or request for assessment by an individual, the signature of the assessor must be authenticated in accordance with the law on authentication.

  In case of solicitation or request for assessment by an organization, the head of the organization must sign and affix the seal to the assessment conclusion, and the solicited or requested organization shall be responsible for the assessment conclusion.

  In case the assessment council specified in Clause 1 Article 30 of this Law carries out the assessment, the person who decides on the establishment of the council must sign and affix the seal to the assessment conclusion, and shall be responsible for the legal status of the assessment council.

  3. In case the assessment is carried out before the decision on initiation of a criminal case is issued, in accordance with the order and procedures prescribed by this Law, the procedural body may use the assessment conclusion as a judicial assessment conclusion.

  Article 33. Judicial expertise dossier

  1. A judicial expertise dossier shall be prepared by the judicial expert and include:

  a) The request for expertise, the written request for expertise and enclosed documents (if any);

  b) The record of handover and receipt of the dossier and the subject matter of the request for expertise;

  c) The written record of the expertise process;

  d) Photographs of the expertise (if any);

  dd) Previous expertise conclusions or results of the expertise tests or experiments performed by other persons (if any);

  e) Other documents related to the expertise (if any);

  g) Judicial expertise conclusions.

  2. Judicial expertise dossiers must be prepared using a uniform form. The Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of Health and other ministries and ministerial agencies shall, within the scope of their tasks and powers, coordinate with the Supreme People’s Court and the Supreme People’s Procuracy in formulating a uniform form for judicial expertise dossiers.

  3. Individuals and organizations that perform judicial expertise shall be responsible for preserving and keeping the expertise dossiers in accordance with the law on archives.

  4. Judicial expertise dossiers shall be presented upon request of procedural bodies or competent procedural persons who settle criminal, administrative or civil cases.

  Article 34. Cases where judicial expertise is not permitted

  1. Individuals in the following cases are not permitted to perform judicial expertise:

  a) Individuals in the cases where the law on procedures provides for refusal to participate in proceedings or replacement;

  b) Individuals who are requested to re-perform the same content of expertise in the same case or matter as the one they have performed, unless otherwise provided for by law.

  2. Organizations in the following cases shall not be permitted to conduct judicial expertise:

  a) Having interests or obligations related to the case or matter as prescribed by the law on proceedings;

  b) Having other clear grounds to believe that such organization may not be objective and impartial in conducting the expertise.

  Article 35. Judicial assistance in judicial expertise

  1. The request for foreign individuals or organizations to conduct judicial expertise shall only be made if the subject matter of the expertise is located abroad or if the professional competence, equipment and facilities of domestic judicial expertise individuals or organizations are unable to meet the requirements for the expertise.

  2. Judicial expertise individuals or organizations shall receive and conduct judicial expertise at the request of competent foreign procedural authorities.

  3. The order, procedures and costs of judicial assistance in judicial expertise between Vietnam and foreign countries shall comply with the law on judicial assistance.

  Chapter VI

     JURISDICTIONAL ASSESSMENT EXPENSES AND REGIME AND POLICY FOR JURISDICTIONAL ASSESSMENT

  Article 36. Jurisdictional assessment expenses The requesters and assessors shall pay the jurisdictional assessment expenses to the individuals and organizations that conduct the jurisdictional assessment in accordance with the law on jurisdictional assessment expenses.

  Article 37. Regime for jurisdictional assessors and participants in jurisdictional assessment

  1. Jurisdictional assessors, case-specific jurisdictional assessors, persons assisting the case-specific jurisdictional assessors who are paid by the state budget, and persons assigned by competent state agencies to be present and perform tasks during the assessment of the post-mortem examination, autopsy, and exhumation shall be entitled to jurisdictional assessment allowances according to the jurisdictional assessment cases.

  2. Apart from the jurisdictional assessment allowances according to the jurisdictional assessment cases prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article, full-time jurisdictional assessors affiliated to public jurisdictional assessment organizations shall be entitled to professional allowances and other allowances.

  3. The Government shall elaborate this Article.

  Article 38. Policy for jurisdictional assessment

  1. Non-public jurisdictional assessment organizations shall be entitled to preferential policies according to the Government’s regulations.

  2. Judicial assessors, judicial assessment organizations, and case-based judicial assessment organizations that make positive contributions to judicial assessment activities shall be honored and rewarded in accordance with the law on emulation and rewards.

  3. Ministries, ministerial-level agencies, and provincial People’s Councils shall, based on their actual capabilities and conditions and their respective competence, provide other regulations and policies to attract capable experts and organizations to participate in judicial assessment activities.

  Chapter VII

     RESPONSIBILITIES OF STATE AGENCIES FOR JUDICIAL ASSESSMENT ORGANIZATIONS AND ACTIVITIES

  Article 39. State management agencies of judicial assessment

  1. The Government shall uniformly manage judicial assessment.

  2. The Ministry of Justice shall assist the Government in uniformly managing judicial assessment.

  3. The Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Public Security, the Ministry of National Defense, the Ministry of Finance, the Ministry of Construction, the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, the Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment, the Ministry of Transport, the Ministry of Science and Technology, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development, the State Bank of Vietnam, and other ministries and ministerial-level agencies shall manage judicial assessment in the fields under their management and take responsibility before the Government; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in uniformly managing judicial assessment.

  4. The provincial People’s Committees shall, within the scope of their tasks and powers, exercise the state management over judicial expertise in their localities.

  Article 40. Tasks and powers of the Ministry of Justice

  1. To promulgate or submit to competent state agencies for promulgation legal documents on judicial expertise and guide the implementation of such documents.

  To take charge of the formulation and submission to the Prime Minister of the general strategy, planning and development plan on judicial expertise; to coordinate with ministries and ministerial-level agencies in formulating strategies, plans and development plans for each field of judicial expertise.

  2. To provide written opinions on the establishment of public judicial expertise organizations under the decision-making competence of ministries, ministerial-level agencies managing professional fields and provincial People’s Committees; where necessary, to request ministries, ministerial-level agencies and provincial People’s Committees to consider establishing public judicial expertise organizations to meet the requirements for expertise of procedural activities.

  3. To formulate programs for legal knowledge training for judicial experts; to coordinate with ministries and ministerial-level agencies in organizing professional, professional and legal knowledge training for judicial experts.

  4. To compile, prepare and publish a list of judicial expertise organizations and individuals on the website of the Ministry of Justice.

  5. To urge the performance of the state management over judicial expertise of ministries, ministerial-level agencies and provincial People’s Committees; to request ministries, ministerial-level agencies and provincial People’s Committees to report on judicial expertise organizations and activities; to report to the Government on judicial expertise organizations and activities nationwide.

  6. To take charge or request relevant ministries, ministerial-level agencies, and provincial-level People’s Committees to inspect and supervise judicial expertise organizations and activities.

  7. To exercise state management over international cooperation in judicial expertise.

  Article 41. Tasks and powers of ministries and ministerial-level agencies in managing judicial expertise

  1. To promulgate or submit to competent state agencies for promulgation legal documents on judicial expertise in the fields under their management and guide the implementation of such documents.

  2. To promulgate judicial expertise regulations or guide the application of professional regulations to judicial expertise activities according to the requirements and specific characteristics of the fields under their management.

  3. To take charge and cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in deciding on the establishment, consolidation, and improvement of public judicial expertise organizations under their management in accordance with this Law.

  4. To appoint and dismiss judicial experts within their competence; to compile and publish lists of case-specific judicial experts and case-specific judicial expertise organizations specified in Clause 1 Article 20 of this Law.

  5. To ensure funding, equipment, and other necessary material conditions for public judicial expertise organizations under their management to meet assigned tasks.

  6. Annually, assess the quality of operation of judicial assessment organizations, case-based judicial assessment organizations, and judicial assessors in the fields under their management.

  7. Define conditions for facilities, equipment, and means of assessment of judicial assessment offices in the fields under their management.

  8. Develop and implement programs and plans for professional training and legal knowledge for judicial assessors in the fields under their management.

  9. Inspect, examine, and resolve complaints and denunciations about judicial assessment organizations and activities in the fields under their management; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in inspecting and examining judicial assessment organizations and activities in accordance with Clause 6, Article 40 of this Law.

  10. Cooperate internationally in judicial assessment in the fields under their management.

  11. Annually, summarize judicial assessment organizations and activities in the fields under their management and send reports to the Ministry of Justice for compilation and submission to the Government.

  Article 42. Tasks and powers of the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Public Security, and the Ministry of National Defense

  In addition to the tasks and powers specified in Article 41 of this Law, the Ministry of Health, the Ministry of Public Security, and the Ministry of National Defense shall have the following tasks and powers:

  1. The Ministry of Health shall have the following tasks and powers:

  a) To perform the state management of the field of forensic examination and forensic psychiatric examination;

  b) To promulgate professional regulations in the field of forensic examination and forensic psychiatric examination;

  c) To specify the standards for forensic examination experts and forensic psychiatric examination experts;

  d) To provide training, retraining and issue professional certificates in forensic examination and forensic psychiatric examination as prescribed in Point c Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law.

  2. The Ministry of Public Security shall have the following tasks and powers:

  a) To perform the state management of the field of criminal technical examination;

  b) To promulgate professional regulations in the field of criminal technical examination;

  c) To specify the standards for criminal technical assessors;

  d) To provide training, retraining and certification in criminal technical assessment as prescribed in Point c Clause 1 Article 7 of this Law;

  dd) To annually compile statistics on the solicitation, evaluation of the performance of assessment and use of judicial assessment conclusions in the system of investigation agencies under their management;

  e) To guide investigation agencies under their management in applying the law provisions on solicitation of assessment and evaluation, use of judicial assessment conclusions;

  g) To ensure funding and guide the payment of costs for judicial assessment in the system of investigation agencies under their management;

  h) To annually summarize and send reports to the Ministry of Justice on the solicitation, evaluation of the performance of assessment and use of judicial assessment conclusions in the system of investigation agencies under their management.

  3. The Ministry of National Defense shall have the tasks and powers prescribed in Points dd, e, g and h Clause 2 of this Article.

  Article 43. Tasks and powers of provincial People’s Committees

  1. The provincial People’s Committees shall have the following tasks and powers:

  a) Establish public judicial expertise organizations; decide to permit the establishment of judicial expertise offices; compile and publish lists of judicial expertise organizations and case-based judicial expertise organizations in their localities;

  b) Appoint and dismiss judicial experts within their competence; compile and publish lists of judicial experts in their localities;

  c) Ensure funding, operational means, material facilities and other necessary conditions for public judicial expertise organizations in their localities;

  d) Organize professional, vocational and legal knowledge training for judicial experts in their localities;

  dd) Annually assess the organization and quality of judicial expertise activities in their localities; ensure the quantity and quality of judicial experts and case-based judicial experts, promptly and qualitatively meet the requirements for expertise of local proceedings;

  e) Inspect, examine and settle complaints and denunciations about judicial expertise within their competence; cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in inspecting and examining judicial expertise organizations and activities in accordance with Clause 6, Article 40 of this Law;

  g) Report to the Ministry of Justice on judicial expertise organizations and activities in their localities, and simultaneously send reports to relevant ministries and ministerial-level agencies for state management as prescribed in Articles 40, 41 and 42 of this Law.

  2. The Department of Justice shall assist the provincial People’s Committees in exercising the state management over judicial expertise in the localities; take charge and cooperate with the specialized agencies in assisting the provincial People’s Committees in managing the operation of the judicial expertise offices.

  The specialized agencies of the provincial People’s Committees shall assist the People’s Committees of the same level in exercising the state management over the specialized judicial expertise, and shall be accountable to the provincial People’s Committees for the organization and operation of judicial expertise in the fields under their management; cooperate with the Departments of Justice in assisting the People’s Committees of the same level in exercising the state management over judicial expertise in the localities.

  Article 44. Responsibilities of the Supreme People’s Court and the Supreme People’s Procuracy

  1. To guide the application of the provisions of law on solicitation of judicial expertise and evaluation, and use of judicial expertise conclusions in the system of the People’s Courts and People’s Procuracies.

  2. To implement the regime of statistics on solicitation, evaluation of the performance of judicial expertise and use of judicial expertise conclusions in the system of the People’s Courts and People’s Procuracies and report to the National Assembly in the annual work reports.

  3. To cooperate with the Ministry of Justice in implementing the regime of statistics and reporting on solicitation, evaluation of the performance of judicial expertise and use of judicial expertise conclusions.

  4. To ensure funding and guide the payment of expenses for judicial expertise in the system of the People’s Courts and People’s Procuracies.

  Chapter VIII

                                           IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS

  Article 45. Entry into force

  1. This Law comes into force on January 1, 2013.

  2. The 24/2004/PL-UBTVQH11 Ordinance on Judicial Expertise shall cease to be effective from the date of entry into force of this Law.

  3. From the date of entry into force of this Law, if the provisions of the Criminal Procedure Code, the Civil Procedure Code, or the Law on Administrative Procedure relating to judicial expertise are different from those of this Law, the provisions of this Law shall apply.

  Article 46. Elaboration and guidance on implementation

  The Government, the Supreme People’s Court, and the Supreme People’s Procuracy shall elaborate and guide the implementation of the articles and clauses assigned in this Law.

  This Law was passed by the XIIIth National Assembly of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam at its 3rd session on June 20, 2012.
