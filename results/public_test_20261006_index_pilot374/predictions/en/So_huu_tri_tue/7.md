Chapter I
                                    GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article 1. Scope of regulation
1. This Circular elaborates Clause 2 Article 67, Clause 3 Article 74, Clause 4 Article 89, Clause 4 Article 94, Clause 8 Article 95, Clause 7 Article 96, Clause 4 Article 97, Clause 8 Article 100, Clause 1 Article 108, Clause 6 Article 109, Clause 3 Article 112a, Clause 4 Article 113, Clause 4 Article 114, Clause 5 Article 115, Clause 4 Article 116, Clause 7 Article 119a, Clause 3 Article 120, Clause 2 Article 150, Clause 3 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property; Clause 5 Article 14a, Clause 2 Article 48, Points a and c Clause 4 Article 62, Points a and c Clause 4 Article 108a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
2. Guidance on implementation of the following contents of the Law on Intellectual Property and Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP:
a) Authorization to perform procedures related to industrial property rights as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 107 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) Industrial property gazette, national register of industrial property, industrial property representative;
c) Ensuring industrial property information.
Article 2. Regulated entities
Organizations and individuals performing procedures related to industrial property rights, procedures for filing complaints and settling complaints related to industrial property procedures, organizations and individuals related to ensuring industrial property information, organizations and individuals related to training in industrial property law, training in industrial property assessment, and other relevant organizations and individuals.
Article 3. Interpretation of terms
In this Circular, the following terms are construed as follows:
1. “Applicant” performing procedures related to industrial property rights (hereinafter referred to as “applicant”) means an organization or individual that files an application for registration of an invention, industrial design, layout, or industrial style, a mark, a geographical indication, an international application, an application for extension or maintenance of validity of a protection title, an application for termination or cancellation of a protection title, an application for modification of a protection title, or an application for registration of a contract for transfer of industrial property rights.
2. “Procedures related to industrial property rights” in this Circular include procedures for registration of establishment of rights to inventions, industrial designs, layouts, marks, geographical indications, procedures for maintenance and extension of validity of protection titles, procedures for termination and cancellation of protection titles, procedures for modification of protection titles, and procedures for transfer of industrial property rights. Requests arising from procedures related to industrial property rights include opposition applications, amendments, additions, recognition of changes to applicants, division of applications, withdrawal of applications, conversion of applications, and requests for expedited examination of contents, which are part of the procedures specified in this Clause.
3. “Complainant” means an organization or individual that files a complaint related to industrial property procedures as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property.
4. “Law on Intellectual Property” means the Law on Intellectual Property No. 50/2005/QH11, amended by the Law No. 36/2009/QH12, the Law No. 42/2019/QH14, the Law No. 07/2022/QH15, the Law No. 93/2025/QH15, and the Law No. 131/2025/QH15.
5. “Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP” means the Government’s Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP detailing a number of articles and measures for implementation of the Law on Intellectual Property regarding industrial property, protection of industrial property rights, rights to plant varieties, and state management of intellectual property, amended by the Decree No. 15/2026/ND-CP, the Decree No. 33/2026/ND-CP, and the Decree No. 100/2026/ND-CP.
6. “Paris Convention” means the 1883 Paris Convention for the Protection of Industrial Property, amended in 1967 and 1979.
7. “PCT Treaty” means the 1970 Patent Cooperation Treaty, amended in 1979, 1984, and 2001.
8. “Budapest Convention” means the 1977 Budapest Convention for the International Recognition of the Effect of Microorganisms Submitted for Invention Procedures, amended in 1980.
9. “The Madrid Convention” means the 1891 Convention on International Registration of Marks, amended in 1979.
10. “The Madrid Protocol” means the 1989 Protocol to the Madrid Convention, amended in 2006 and 2007.
11. “The Hague Convention” means the 1999 Hague Convention on International Registration of Industrial Designs.
12. “PCT application” means an application for patent filed under the PCT Convention.
13. “PCT application designating or selecting Vietnam” means a PCT application filed with any member of the PCT Convention, including Vietnam, in which Vietnam is designated or selected.
14. “PCT application in national phase” means a PCT application designating or selecting Vietnam, filed with the Intellectual Property Office.
15. “PCT application originating from Vietnam” means a PCT application filed from Vietnam, in which protection is requested in any member of the PCT Convention, including Vietnam.
16. “Madrid application” means an application for international registration of a mark filed under the Madrid Convention or the Madrid Protocol.
17. “Vietnam-originated Madrid application” means a Madrid application for protection of a mark in other members of the Madrid Convention or the Madrid Protocol filed from Vietnam.
18. “Vietnam-registered Madrid application” means a Madrid application for protection of a mark in Vietnam, which is originated from other members of the Madrid Convention or the Madrid Protocol.
19. “La Hay application” means an application for registration of an industrial design under the La Hay Convention.
20. “Vietnam-registered La Hay application” means a La Hay application for protection of an industrial design in Vietnam, which is originated from any member of the La Hay Convention, including Vietnam.
21. “Vietnam-originated La Hay application” means a La Hay application filed from Vietnam, which includes a request for protection of an industrial design in any member of the La Hay Convention, including Vietnam.
22. “International Office” means the International Office of the World Intellectual Property Organization.
Article 4. Procedures for handling applications for registration of inventions, industrial designs, marks, geographical indications and layout designs
1. Applications for registration of inventions, industrial designs, marks, geographical indications and layout designs shall be received and processed in the following order, except for cases where each type of application is subject to separate regulations as prescribed in this Circular and relevant legal documents:
a) Receipt in accordance with the corresponding provisions of Articles 13, 47, 70, 95 of this Circular;
b) Formal assessment in accordance with the provisions of Articles 14, 48, 71, 96 of this Circular;

c) Publication in accordance with the provisions of Articles 15, 49, 72, 97 of this Circular, except for the case where a confidential invention application is not published in accordance with the provisions of Article 49 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;
d) Content assessment in accordance with the provisions of Articles 17, 50, 73, 98 of this Circular. The content assessment of an invention application shall be carried out upon request;
dd) In case a third party objects to the application, the handling of the objection shall comply with the provisions of Articles 27, 59, 82, 103 of this Circular;
e) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to grant or refuse to grant a protection title based on the results of the content assessment of the application. The decision to grant a protection title shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette;
g) During the processing of an application, the applicant may amend, supplement, withdraw, split, or convert the application in accordance with the provisions, provided that the corresponding fees and charges are paid.
2. An application for registration of a layout design shall be received, formally assessed, and published in accordance with the provisions of Articles 40, 41, 42 of this Circular. During the processing of an application, the applicant may amend, supplement, or withdraw the application in accordance with the provisions, provided that the corresponding fees and charges are paid. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to grant or refuse to grant a protection title based on the results of the formal assessment of the application for registration of a layout design. The decision to grant a protection title shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette.
Article 5. Calculation of time limits in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint procedures
1. The calculation of time limits in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint procedures shall comply with Article 15 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
2. The fixed time (including the extended time as prescribed) for applicants, complainants, and relevant parties to respond to notices, amend or supplement documents as prescribed shall not be included in the time limit for handling corresponding applications or requests as prescribed, including:
a) The time from the date of issuance of the notice to the date on which the applicant, complainant, or relevant party responds to the notice; or the fixed time in the notice, in case the applicant, complainant, or relevant party fails to respond to the notice;
b) The time from the date of filing of the application to the date on which the applicant, complainant, or relevant party files the supplementary documents as prescribed in this Circular; or the time limit for the applicant, complainant, or relevant party to file supplementary documents as prescribed in this Circular, in case the applicant, complainant, or relevant party fails to file supplementary documents.
Article 6. Method of filing applications for procedures related to industrial property rights and filing of complaints
1. Applicants and complainants may file applications for procedures related to industrial property rights or file complaints either themselves or through their legal representatives in Vietnam as prescribed in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 89 and Clause 2 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property and this Article.
2. Legal representatives of individuals as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article and Articles 89 and 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be determined as follows:
a) For Vietnamese individuals, foreign individuals permanently residing in Vietnam or having production and business establishments in Vietnam, legal representatives include: legal representatives or authorized representatives; authorized industrial property representation service providers;
b) For foreign individuals not permanently residing in Vietnam or having no production and business establishments in Vietnam, legal representatives are authorized industrial property representation service providers.
3. Legal representatives of organizations as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article and Article 89, Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be determined as follows:
a) For Vietnamese organizations, legal representatives include: representative offices or branches in Vietnam; authorized industrial property representation service providers;
b) For foreign organizations having production and business establishments in Vietnam, legal representatives include: representative offices or branches in Vietnam or foreign-invested organizations in Vietnam established in accordance with laws on investment; authorized industrial property representation service providers;
c) For foreign organizations without production and business establishments in Vietnam, legal representatives are authorized industrial property representation service providers.
4. Vietnamese individuals, foreign individuals permanently residing in Vietnam shall submit applications for industrial property rights procedures or complaint applications in accordance with Clause 1 Article 89, Clause 2 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property by signing their names on the application forms.
5. Vietnamese organizations, foreign organizations having production and business establishments in Vietnam shall submit applications for industrial property rights procedures or complaint applications in accordance with Clause 1 Article 89, Clause 2 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property through their legal representatives or persons authorized by their legal representatives.
6. When conducting procedures related to industrial property rights, the National Office of Intellectual Property is permitted to transact with applicants, complainants or their lawful representatives as prescribed in Clauses 2, 3, 4 and 5 of this Article. The transaction with the above-mentioned entities shall be referred to as a transaction with applicants or complainants.
Article 7. Forms of filing applications
1. Applications in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint applications shall be filed in the form of paper or electronic documents through the electronic application system in accordance with the provisions of Articles 89 and 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property and this Circular.
2. Electronic applications prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article are data messages created or digitized from paper documents, accurately and completely reflecting the content and format of the paper documents, and having electronic authentication in accordance with the law on electronic transactions. Electronic applications in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint applications filed through the electronic application system shall have the same legal validity as paper applications in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint applications.
3. Applications shall be received by the National Office of Intellectual Property at the One-Stop Division of the Ministry of Science and Technology.
Article 8. Authorization in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint procedures
1. Authorization for representation, including reauthorization and execution of authorization for representation in procedures related to industrial property rights (hereinafter referred to as “authorization”), must comply with the authorization provisions of the Civil Code, Article 107 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the guidance in this Circular.
Applicants and complainants may change authorized representatives. The change of authorized representatives shall terminate the authorization relationship between applicants or complainants and the authorized persons. The termination of authorization with the authorized persons and the change of new authorized representatives must be declared in writing by applicants or complainants (immediately in the authorization documents or separate documents).
The authorized person may re-authorize another person in accordance with the Civil Code, and the re-authorized person must satisfy the requirements for lawful representation in accordance with Article 6 of this Circular. When the re-authorized organization or individual conducts transactions with the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints, they must submit the original authorization document, unless such authorization document has already been submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property.
2. The date on which the authorization document is recognized in transactions with the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints is the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints receives the valid authorization document. In case of change of authorized representative or re-authorization or modification of information related to change of authorization scope, termination of authorization before the deadline, or change of address of the authorized party, the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints receives the corresponding valid documents is the effective date.
3. In case the authorization document is submitted later than the date of filing but before the date on which the application is validly accepted or accepted, the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints shall conduct transactions with the person who claims to be the representative of the applicant or complainant (in the application or complaint) in order to carry out the formal assessment procedure to conclude whether the application is valid or invalid, whether it is accepted or not, including the conclusion on the legality of the representative status.
4. Any transaction of any authorized party within the scope of authorization at any time shall be considered a transaction in the name of the applicant or complainant, giving rise to the rights and obligations of the applicant or complainant. In case of change of authorized representative or re-authorization, the new authorized representative or re-authorized person shall continue the representation in all matters arising from the previous authorized person's transactions with the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints.
5. If the authorization document includes a scope of authorization covering multiple procedures that are independent of each other and the original authorization document has been submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property or the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints, when proceeding with subsequent procedures, the authorized party must submit a copy of the authorization document and provide an exact reference to the application number containing the original authorization document in the application or document of the subsequent procedure.

6. In case the authorization document clearly states that the authorization only terminates when one of the parties provides a written declaration of termination or when another authorization document replaces it, such authorization document shall be considered to have a time limit as prescribed in Point c, Clause 2, Article 107 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
7. In case of authorization to an organization or individual that is not permitted to act as a representative or authorization to multiple organizations or individuals at the same time, including organizations or individuals that are not permitted to act as representatives, the application shall be deemed invalid.
8. The provisions on authorization in this Article shall also apply to the implementation of administrative procedures prescribed in Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP and the complaint against the results of the settlement of such administrative procedures.
Article 9. Responsibilities of applicants, complainants and representatives
1. Applicants, complainants and representatives of applicants and complainants shall ensure the truthfulness of information and documents provided to the National Office of Intellectual Property and the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in settling complaints in accordance with the following regulations:
a) All transaction documents must be self-certified by the applicant, complainant or representative of the applicant or complainant with their signature and the seal of the organization (if any). In cases where the law stipulates that documents must be notarized or authenticated, such regulations must be followed.
b) All Vietnamese translations of documents made in other languages must be accompanied by a commitment from the applicant, complainant or representative of the applicant or complainant to ensure that the translation is a literal translation of the document, except for cases where the Vietnamese translation has been notarized to confirm the translation;
c) In cases where the representative of the applicant or complainant is an industrial property representation service organization, the representative signing the transaction document must have an industrial property representation service certificate.
2. Applicants or complainants shall be responsible for all consequences and obligations arising from the representative of the applicant in transactions with the National Office of Intellectual Property and the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in settling complaints in accordance with law.
3. Representatives of applicants and complainants shall be held responsible before applicants and complainants in accordance with law.
Article 10. Industrial property charges and fees
1. Applicants shall carry out procedures related to industrial property rights and other related procedures in accordance with this Circular, and industrial property service users shall pay charges and fees in accordance with regulations of the Minister of Finance and other service charges in accordance with regulations.
2. Collection of charges and fees shall be carried out as follows:
a) Applicants shall pay charges and fees to the National Office of Intellectual Property in accordance with regulations;
b) Upon collecting charges and fees, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue receipts for collection of charges and fees as proof of payment of charges and fees, clearly stating the amounts and levels of charges and fees collected, and keep them in the application dossier for the purpose of examining the application;
c) In case of payment of charges and fees via postal services or direct payment into the account of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the collection of charges and fees shall be determined through documents proving the payment of charges and fees in the application.
Chapter II
                               PROCESSING OF INVENTION APPLICATIONS
Section 1. INVENTION APPLICATIONS
Article 11. Invention application
1. An invention application comprises the following documents:
a) An application for invention registration made using Form No. 01 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) An invention description;
c) An invention abstract;
d) A power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative);
(d) Documents proving the right to register, if the applicant is entitled to the right of another person;
e) Documents proving the priority right if the priority right is requested, including:

e1) A copy of the application or the first application of the agency that received the first application; e2) The transfer of priority rights if the rights are received from another person;
g) Explanatory documents on the origin of the genetic source or traditional knowledge on the genetic source for inventions directly created based on the genetic source or traditional knowledge on the genetic source;
h) Other documents supplementing the application (if any).
2. An application for a confidential patent shall include the following documents:
a) The documents specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall be stamped with the seal of secrecy as prescribed by the law on protection of state secrets;
b) A written confirmation that the subject of the application is a state secret in accordance with the law on protection of state secrets.
Article 12. Requirements for documents in an invention registration application
1. Documents of an invention registration application must satisfy the specific requirements corresponding to the provisions in Clauses 2 through 12 of this Article and satisfy the general requirements for form as prescribed below:
a) Documents must be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables may be presented horizontally) on an A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), with margins on all four sides, each 20mm wide, using Times New Roman font, with a font size of not less than 13, except supplementary documents whose origins are not intended to be included in the application;
b) If a document consists of multiple pages, each page must be numbered with Arabic numerals;
c) Documents must be typed or printed with a fade-resistant ink, clearly and cleanly presented, without erasure or correction. For documents requiring signatures and stamps, if the document consists of 02 pages or more, each page must be signed or stamped by the applicant or the issuing authority (if any), except for electronic documents signed with digital signatures. Before receiving the application, if minor spelling errors are detected, the applicant may correct them and must sign (and stamp, if any) at the correction location; the signature and stamp must not obscure the contents of the document;
d) Documents of an application submitted in electronic form via the electronic application submission system must be formatted as “.pdf”; the name of the electronic document must correspond to the name of the paper type as prescribed; individuals signing documents of an invention registration application may use digital signatures to sign directly on electronic documents or directly on paper documents and scan (scan) the paper documents in color mode for cases of application submission via the VNeID application;
dd) Terms used in the application must be consistent and common (not using local languages, rare words, or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts, and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese national standards;
e) Documents and papers of transactions between the applicant and the National Office of Intellectual Property must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents under Points d, dd, e, and h Clause 1 Article 11 of this Circular, which may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese when requested;
g) The application documents must contain all mandatory information and information must be consistent.
2. The application documents must specify the protected subject matter as a product or process in accordance with Clause 12 Article 4 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. The invention application must comply with the provisions of Clauses 3 and 4 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property. A group of inventions that are closely related in terms of technology and aim to achieve a common inventive concept in accordance with Clause 4 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be classified as a single invention if any of the following conditions are met:
a) One subject matter is used to create (produce, manufacture, prepare) another subject matter;
b) One subject matter is used to implement another subject matter;
c) One subject matter is used to use another subject matter;
d) The subject matters are of the same form and have the same function to achieve the same result.
4. The invention application must contain all required information in the appropriate sections of the application. In the section “International classification of inventions”, the applicant must specify the classification number of the technical solution to be protected according to the latest International Classification of Inventions (according to the latest Strasbourg Convention on International Classification of Inventions) published in the Industrial Property Gazette (complete classification, including parts, classes, subclasses, groups (main groups or subgroups)). If the applicant fails to classify or classifies incorrectly, the Intellectual Property Office will classify the application and the applicant must pay the classification fee as prescribed.
5. The invention summary shall briefly (not more than 150 words) describe the invention subject matter to be protected. The summary must disclose the main contents of the technical solution and is only for information purposes. The summary may include drawings and distinctive formulas. All drawings and distinctive formulas (if any) must be presented on half of an A4 page and must be clear. The summary must be presented in a separate document.
6. The invention description shall include the description of the invention and the protection request (the scope of protection) of the invention and must comply with the provisions of Clauses 7, 8, 9 and 10 of this Article. The invention description may include drawings (if any) to illustrate the invention.
7. The description section of the invention description must fully and clearly disclose the nature of the technical solution of the invention to the extent that a person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field can implement the invention; must clarify the novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability of the technical solution (if the protection title granted is an invention patent); clarify the novelty and industrial applicability of the technical solution (if the protection title granted is an utility solution patent). If the application includes illustrative drawings, the description must be consistent with the drawings.
The description must include the following contents in the following order:
a) Invention title: briefly describe one or several protected subject matters (hereinafter referred to as “subject matter”); the invention title must be concise, accurate and not exaggerated or advertising;
b) Invention use field: the field in which the subject matter is used or related. If the invention is used or related to many fields, all such fields must be indicated. The above fields must be consistent with the invention classification result;
c) Technical state of the invention: the technical state of the invention in the field of use at the time of filing (known similar subject matters (if any)). If there is no information on the technical state of the invention, this must be clearly stated;
d) Technical nature of the invention: the nature of the protected object, which must specify the following contents: the technical problem to be solved (the purpose of the invention), which must specify the purpose that the invention aims to achieve or the task (problem) that the invention aims to solve (for example, to overcome the disadvantages or limitations of the technical solution indicated in the technical state of the invention). The purpose or task of the invention must be presented objectively, specifically, without exaggeration or advertising; the technical signs (features) that form the protected object, i.e. the technical signs (features) that constitute the solution to achieve the purpose of the invention (called basic technical signs) and must specify the technical signs (features) that are new compared to similar known technical solutions; the benefits (efficacy) that can be achieved compared to the technical state (if any), this content can be described in a separate section, as prescribed in point h of this clause;
dd) Brief description of the accompanying drawings (if any);
e) Detailed description of the invention's implementation options: a detailed description of one or some of the invention's implementation options so that a person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field can implement the invention. A person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field is a person with ordinary practical skills and is well aware of the general and common knowledge in the corresponding technical field;
g) Examples of the invention's implementation (if any): specify one or some specific implementation options of the invention. If the invention is characterized by quantifiable signs, the specific value of such signs must be specified; if not quantifiable, the specific state of such signs must be specified. In addition, specific results related to the functions and purposes that the corresponding object allows to be achieved must be provided;
h) Benefits (efficacy) that can be achieved (if any and not stated in the technical nature section of the invention): may be expressed in terms of improved productivity, quality, accuracy or efficiency; saving energy consumption, raw materials; simplifying or creating convenience in handling, operation, management or use; overcoming environmental pollution; etc. If the benefits (efficacy) that can be achieved are based on statistical results from experimental data, the applicant must provide the necessary experimental conditions and methods;
i) Illustrative drawings (if any) must be numbered and grouped together on separate pages dedicated to drawings and placed at the end of the description. If the drawings contain guiding symbols for description, the guiding symbols must be used consistently in all documents of the application.
8. The protection request must be presented concisely, clearly, in accordance with the description and drawings, on separate pages, including the protection request points, which must specify the new signs of the protected object and comply with the following regulations:
a) The protection request must be fully described in the description, including the basic technical signs necessary and sufficient to identify the subject matter, to achieve the stated purpose and to distinguish the subject matter of the protection request from the prior art;
b) The technical signs in each protection claim must be clear, precise and accepted in the corresponding technical field; the terms used in the protection request must be clear and consistent with the terms used in the description;
c) The protection request must not refer to the description and drawings, except for the parts which cannot be accurately described in words, such as the nucleotide sequence and amino acid sequence, diffraction patterns, state diagrams, etc.;
d) If the application contains drawings illustrating the protection request, the signs stated in the protection request may be accompanied by reference numbers, but they must be enclosed in brackets. These reference numbers are not considered to limit the scope of protection;
dd) The protection claim should (but not necessarily) be expressed in two parts: “the limiting part” and “the differentiating part”, in which: “the limiting part” includes the name of the subject matter and the signs of that subject matter which coincide with the signs of the nearest prior art and are connected to the “differentiating part” by the phrase “differing in” or “characterized by” or equivalent words; “the differentiating part” includes the differentiating signs of the subject matter compared with the nearest prior art and these signs, together with the signs of the “limiting part”, constitute the subject matter of the protection request;
e) The protection request may include one or more claims. In which the protection request for multiple claims may be used to represent a subject matter to be protected, with the first claim (called the independent claim) and the subsequent claims (called the dependent claims) used to specify the independent claim; or to represent a group of protected subject matters, with a number of independent claims, each independent claim representing a protected subject matter in that group, each independent claim may have dependent claims. Each protection claim may only refer to one protected subject matter and must be expressed in a single sentence;
g) The claims of the protection request must be numbered in sequence with Arabic numerals, ending with a dot;
h) The protection request for multiple claims used to represent a group of subject matters must satisfy the following requirements: the independent claims, representing separate subject matters, must not refer to other protection claims, except for the case where such reference allows the avoidance of the complete repetition of the content of other claims; the dependent claims must be expressed after the independent claims to which they depend.
9. For an application for an invention relating to pharmaceuticals, in addition to the general requirements for the description of the invention specified in Clause 7 of this Article, the description must state the results of clinical trials and/or the pharmacological effects of the pharmaceutical, including at least the following information:
a) The substance or mixture used;
b) The method (system) of trial used;
c) Information on the trial results;
d) The correlation between the pharmacological effects obtained in the trial and the practical application of the pharmaceutical in the diagnosis and treatment of diseases.
10. For an application for an invention relating to biotechnology, in addition to the general requirements for the description of the invention specified in Clause 7 of this Article, the application must comply with the following requirements:
a) For an application for an invention relating to a gene sequence or a part of a gene sequence, the description must include a list of the gene sequence expressed according to the standards of the World Intellectual Property Organization for the expression of nucleotide sequence and amino acid sequence in the application for an invention. The list of the sequence must be expressed in a separate section and placed at the end of the description;
b) Include an electronic data carrier (e.g. floppy disk, optical disk, etc.) readable by common electronic devices on which the nucleotide sequence and amino acid sequence are inscribed, which are identical to the list of the sequence stated in the description, if so requested by the Intellectual Property Office.
c) For inventions relating to or involving biological materials, if the biological material cannot be described or fully described to the extent that a person of average knowledge in the field of biotechnology can perform it, the patent application must satisfy the following conditions: c1) The biological material specimen has been submitted for deposit at the competent agency for biological material deposit no later than the date of filing the patent application relating to that biological material; c2) The description clearly states the necessary information about the properties of the biological material that the applicant can obtain; c3) The declaration clearly states the biological material depositing agency, the deposit number of the biological material specimen submitted for deposit issued by the depositing agency, and documents certifying this information, which are submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property within 16 months from the priority date, or no later than the date of filing the request for early publication of the application (if any) depending on which date is earlier, except for cases where the deposit is made in accordance with the regulations of the PCT Treaty implementation regulations;
d) If the applicant is not the biological material depositor, the declaration must clearly state the name and address of the depositor and documents certifying the lawful use of the biological material, which must be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property within 16 months from the priority date, or no later than the date of filing the request for early publication of the application (if any) depending on which date is earlier, except for cases where the deposit is made in accordance with the regulations of the PCT Treaty implementation regulations.
11. For inventions relating to or involving biological materials under the case specified in Point c Clause 10 of this Article, the deposit of the biological material specimen at the competent agency in accordance with the provisions of Point c1 Clause 10 of this Article must satisfy the following requirements:
a) The competent agency for biological material deposit is an agency in Vietnam or a foreign country on the list of international competent agencies for biological material deposit under the Budapest Treaty or recognized by a competent agency in Vietnam for its function of biological material deposit;
b) For biological material specimens deposited at foreign depositing agencies, if it is deemed necessary to clarify the nature of the protected subject matter or satisfy the requirements of third parties for access to such subject matter, the National Office of Intellectual Property may do the following: b1) Request the applicant to additionally deposit the biological material specimen at a competent agency in Vietnam in case the biological material specimen is not deposited at the international competent agency for biological material deposit under the Budapest Treaty; b2) Request the biological material specimen depositing agency to provide the specimen in case the biological material specimen is deposited at the international competent agency for biological material deposit under the Budapest Treaty.
12. For an invention registration application directly created based on genetic resources or traditional knowledge on genetic resources, if the applicant fails to submit a document explaining the origin of the genetic resources or traditional knowledge on genetic resources, and the inventor or applicant has accessed them as prescribed in Point g Clause 1 Article 11 of this Circular due to the inability to determine the origin of the genetic resources or traditional knowledge on genetic resources, such failure must be clearly stated and the applicant shall take responsibility for the truthfulness of such statement.
Article 13. Receipt of invention registration applications
1. An invention application shall be accepted when it fully satisfies the following conditions:
a) The minimum documents, including: a1) The registration application; a2) The description, including the protection request;
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
2. A confidential patent application shall be accepted when it fully satisfies the following conditions:
a) Having sufficient minimum documents as prescribed in Point a, Clause 1 of this Article and documents prescribed in Point b, Clause 2, Article 11 of this Circular;
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
3. In case the application fully meets the conditions specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and confirm the date of filing and the application number for such application, and return to the applicant a receipt for the application, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the receiving officer.
For electronic applications, in case the application is received, the Intellectual Property Office shall return to the applicant a receipt for the application, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the receiving officer via the electronic application submission system.
4. In case the application does not fully meet the conditions specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2 of this Article, the application shall be rejected (if the application is submitted via postal services, electronic applications submitted via the electronic application submission system, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a notice of rejection to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for rejection). For applications rejected via postal services, the Intellectual Property Office shall not return the application documents to the applicant, except for the original submitted for comparison.
5. For documents proving priority rights, applicants may submit additional documents within 03 months from the date of filing as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
6. For secret patent applications, the receipt of applications as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 of this Article must comply with the law on protection of state secrets.
Section 2. ASSESSMENT OF APPLICATION FORMS FOR PATENT APPLICATIONS
Article 14. Assessment of application forms for patent applications
1. The assessment of application forms for patent applications as prescribed in Article 109 of the Intellectual Property Law refers to the examination of compliance with regulations on form of applications, thereby concluding whether the application is considered valid or not.
2. An application for an invention registration shall be deemed invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:

a) There are grounds to assert that the applicant does not have the right to register according to the provisions of Article 86 of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The application is filed contrary to the provisions of Articles 89 and 89a of the Intellectual Property Law;
c) There are grounds to assert that the subject matter stated in the application is a subject matter not protected according to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 8 and Article 59 of the Intellectual Property Law;
d) There are grounds to assert that the inventor does not satisfy the provisions of Clause 1 Article 122 of the Intellectual Property Law;
dd) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed in Article 10 of this Circular (including cases where the application fee, the fee for publication of the application, the fee for examination of the application and the fee for information search for examination purposes have not been paid in full, except for the fee for information search for examination purposes and the fee for examination of contents if the application does not request examination of contents);
e) The application fails to satisfy the formal requirements specified in Article 12 of this Circular (the application is defective) including: failing to satisfy the requirements for presentation; failing to classify or incorrectly classifying the invention for which the applicant has not paid the classification fee; failing to provide a translation of the document proving the priority right (if required), a translation of the document proving the registration right if the applicant has received the registration right from another person; information about the applicant in the documents is inconsistent or has been erased or not certified in accordance with regulations, the application form is incomplete with information about the inventor, applicant, representative, and lacks the signature and/or seal (if any) of the applicant or representative; for a divisional application, the specification of the subject matter of the protection request in the divisional application is missing from the initial application; documents in the invention registration application are not sealed with a seal as prescribed, etc.;
g) There is no valid authorization document (if the application is filed through a representative).
3. In case there are grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of information stated in the application or information stated in the application is unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to submit documents to verify or clarify such information within 2 months from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice. The above-mentioned documents may include documents certifying the lawful registration rights if the applicant is entitled to the right to submit the application of another person (documents proving inheritance rights according to the provisions of civil law, documents proving the transfer of the right to submit the application; contracts for assignment of tasks or labor contracts, etc.); documents showing the results of pharmaceutical testing on human, animal or plant bodies stated in the description section (when the subject matter of protection is a pharmaceutical for human, animal or plant use); etc.
In case the above-mentioned fixed time limit expires and the applicant fails to submit additional documents to satisfy the requirements, the application shall continue to be assessed based on the documents in the application.
4. The notification of assessment results shall be carried out as follows:
a) If the application falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notification of assessment results, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the protection title, the reason for refusal or the deficiencies of the application (including the request for priority rights) and fix a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the deficiencies.
b) If the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause expires and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the remedy is not satisfactory or there is no objection or there is an objection but it is not reasonable, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the protection title and send it to the applicant.
c) If the application does not fall into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article or the applicant remedies the deficiencies satisfactorily or provides a reasonable explanation within the prescribed time limit, the application shall be deemed valid and transferred for publication and content assessment according to regulations (in case the application contains a request for content assessment). If the request for priority rights is not accepted, the application shall still be considered valid and transferred for publication according to regulations.
5. Every week, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall post on the website the list of patent applications transferred for publication, clearly stating the application number, the date of application and the date of publication of each application.
6. The date of application for patent registration at the National Office of Intellectual Property is the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Intellectual Property according to the provisions of Clause 3 Article 13 of this Circular.
In case the application is submitted through the electronic application submission system after working hours or on a holiday or public holiday, the date of submission shall be the next working day.
7. The priority date of an invention registration application shall be determined as follows:
a) If the application does not request the right to priority or, although it requests the right to priority, the Intellectual Property Office does not approve it, the application shall be considered as having no priority date;
b) If the application requests the right to priority, the priority date (or priority dates) is the date stated in the above-mentioned request and approved by the National Office of Intellectual Property;
c) The determination of the priority date according to the request for priority rights based on the first (first) application (applications) filed in Vietnam shall comply with the principles specified in Article 91 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the corresponding provisions in Points b, c and dd Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
8. If the applicant requests the priority right but there are no documents proving the priority right at the time of filing the invention registration application, the examination of the application form shall start from the time the Intellectual Property Office receives additional documents proving the priority right or the 3-month time limit specified in Clause 5 Article 13 of this Circular expires.
Article 15. Publication of invention registration applications
1. A valid application for an invention shall be published in the form prescribed in Article 110 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. Contents of the invention registration application published on the Industrial Property Gazette include: applicant, representative (if any); application number, date of filing, priority date (if any); name and nationality of the inventor; information on the origin of the application (converted application, separated application, original application of the separated application/converted application, etc.); invention summary; distinctive drawing enclosed with the summary (if any); date of request for examination of contents (if any); date of request for early publication (if any); international classification of the invention; and other information (if any).
3. The invention description shall be published on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
Section 3. EXAMINATION OF INVENTION REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS

1. An applicant may request examination of contents of an invention registration application in accordance with Article 113 of the Intellectual Property Law and the following regulations:
a) The request for examination of contents of an invention registration application shall be made in writing in Form No. 02 in Appendix I of this Circular or in the application registration declaration (if the request is made immediately upon filing the application) or Form No. 13 in Appendix I of this Circular in case of request for expedited examination of contents;
b) The time limit for filing a request for examination of contents of an invention registration application shall comply with the provisions of Clause 1 and 2 Article 113 of the Intellectual Property Law; a request for expedited examination of contents of an invention registration application shall be filed immediately upon filing the application in accordance with the provisions of Clause 3 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;

If the applicant fails to pay the above-mentioned fees, the request for examination of contents shall be deemed invalid and the National Office of Intellectual Property shall not conduct examination of contents of the application.
2. A third party may request examination of the contents of an invention registration application filed after the application is published in the Industrial Property Gazette in the second month from the date of receipt of the request. 3. A third party may request examination of the contents of an invention registration application in accordance with Article 113 of the Intellectual Property Law and the following regulations:
a) The request for examination of the contents of the invention registration application shall be made using Form No. 02 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) The time limit for filing a request for examination of the contents of an invention registration application shall comply with Clause 1 and 2 Article 113 of the Intellectual Property Law;

Article 17. Examination of contents of invention registration applications
1. Examination of contents of invention registration applications according to Article 114 of the Law on Intellectual Property means assessment of the possibility of protection of technical solutions stated in the application according to protection conditions and determination of corresponding scope of protection according to the provisions of this Article.
2. Assessment of the possibility of granting protection titles to invention registration applications includes:
a) Assessment of the suitability of the subject matter stated in the invention registration application and the type of protection title according to Article 19 of this Circular;
b) Assessment of the subject matter stated in the invention registration application according to each protection condition according to Articles 20, 21 and 22 of this Circular. The assessment shall be carried out in order according to each point stated in the protection request;
c) Assessment of the principle of filing the first application according to Article 23 of this Circular.
3. The assessment of the possibility of granting protection titles according to the provisions of Point b Clause 2 of this Article shall be considered completed when the subject matter of the invention stated in the application has been assessed under all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy or satisfies the protection conditions, specifically:
a) Finding reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy one or some or all protection conditions; or b) Finding no reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy at least one protection condition.
4. Based on the results of assessment of the ability to grant protection titles specified in Clauses 2 and 3 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall determine the corresponding scope of expected protection.
5. Before issuing a notice of assessment results with the intention of granting an invention/ utility solution patent according to the provisions of point a, d2 clause 3 Article 25 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall check the first application submission according to the provisions of Article 23 of this Circular.
6. The notification of the results of the content assessment to the applicant for the grant of the patent/ utility solution grant specified in Point a, d2 Clause 3 Article 25 of this Circular shall be made for the following patent applications:
a) Applications not falling under the cases specified in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) An application with the earliest date of filing or earliest date of priority among the applications for invention registration under the case specified in Clause 1 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) Application made under the agreement specified in Clause 3 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
7. An application for an invention registration not falling under the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article shall be handled as follows:
a) The application is rejected for granting an invention/ utility solution patent because it fails to satisfy the first-filing principle, if the application has a filing date or earliest priority date that is granted an invention/ utility solution patent; or b) The application is considered to have a filing date or earliest priority date and is handled as the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article, if all other invention registration applications with earlier filing dates or earliest priority dates are rejected for granting invention/ utility solution patents or withdrawn, or are considered withdrawn.
8. During the process of examining the contents of the application, if the application does not fully reveal the nature of the subject matter, the Intellectual Property Office may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application and provide information within the scope of the nature of the subject matter stated in the application to fully reveal the nature of the subject matter and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
9. During the process of examining the contents of the application, depending on the complexity of the assessment of the protection conditions, the Intellectual Property Office may consult the opinions of independent consultants and the Invention Assessment Advisory Council.
The Invention Assessment Advisory Council shall be established and operate on the basis of the principles specified in the decision of the Director of the Intellectual Property Office.
Independent consultants shall be selected from the list of industrial property consultants compiled by the Intellectual Property Office and from other sources (if there are no suitable consultants in that list). The applicant may request to participate in meetings of the Invention Assessment Advisory Council or with independent consultants to clarify information stated in the application, serving the assessment of the Council or the consultant.
Article 18. Minimum information sources
1. To assess the novelty and inventive step of the technical solution stated in the application, information must be searched in the following mandatory sources (but not limited to these minimum sources):
a) All patent applications received by the Intellectual Property Office with the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter being examined - up to the third level classification index (the third level classification index) and having a priority date or an earlier filing date than the priority date or filing date of the application being examined, except for applications that have been or will not be published (when assessing novelty) or applications that have not been published as of the priority date or filing date of the application being examined (when assessing inventive step);
b) Patent applications or patent protection titles published by other organizations or countries within 25 years as of the filing date or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority) are stored in the patent database at the Intellectual Property Office and other information sources specified by the Intellectual Property Office, with the scope of search specified in point a of this clause;
c) Confidential patent protection titles granted by the National Office of Intellectual Property up to the date of filing or priority date of the application being examined.
Where necessary and possible, the search shall be extended to the national database of science and technology and other accessible information sources, including information on the Internet.
2. The purpose of the information search is to find technical solutions which are similar or identical to the technical solutions stated in the application. For the purposes of this Clause:
a) Two technical solutions are considered identical when all the basic signs (features) are identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
b) Two technical solutions are considered similar when most of the basic signs (features) are identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
c) “Contrasting technical solution” means the technical solution which is identical or most similar to the technical solution stated in the application;
d) “Contrasting document” means a document which describes the contrasting technical solution or evidence which proves that the contrasting technical solution has been publicly disclosed.
3. The information search result must be expressed in a search report, which must clearly state the search field, scope of search, search results within that scope (statistics of the found contrasting technical solutions, clearly stating the identical signs, names of the contrasting documents, page numbers, line numbers, source of the documents and the date of publication of the corresponding documents) and must state the name of the person who prepares the report (the searcher).
Article 19. Assessment of the suitability of objects specified in the invention application
1. An object specified in an invention application shall be considered unsuitable for the type of invention protection title granted to the applicant (invention patent or utility solution patent) if it is not a technical solution, specifically not a product or process.
2. A technical solution (object protected under the name of an invention) specified in Clause 1 of this Article is a set of sufficient information on the technical method and/or technical means (applying natural laws) for solving a specific task (problem). A technical solution may take one of the following forms:
a) A product in the form of an object, such as a tool, machine, equipment, component, electrical circuit, etc., expressed by a set of information defining an artificial product characterized by technical signs (features) of structure, the product having a function (use) as a means to satisfy a certain human need; or a product in the form of a substance (consisting of a single substance, compound and mixture of substances), such as a material, material, food, pharmaceutical, etc., expressed by a set of information defining an artificial product characterized by technical signs (features) of the presence, ratio and state of elements, having a function (use) as a means to satisfy a certain human need; or a product in the form of a biological material, such as a gene, genetically modified plant/animal, etc., expressed by a set of information on a product containing genetically modified information under human influence, capable of self-reproduction;
b) A process (technology process; diagnostic, forecasting, inspection, processing method, etc.) expressed by a set of information defining the manner of carrying out a specific process or task, characterized by signs (features) of order, conditions, participating components, measures, means for performing operations to achieve a specific purpose.
3. An object specified in the application shall not be considered a technical solution in the following cases:
a) The object specified in the application is only an idea or intention, only stating (posing) the problem without providing a solution to the problem, not answering the question “how” or/and “by what means”;
b) The problem (task) posed for solution is not a technical problem and cannot be solved by a technical method;
c) Natural products, which are not human-made inventions.
Article 20. Assessment of novelty

1. To assess the novelty of the technical solution stated in the application, the following basic signs (features) of that technical solution must be compared with the signs of the comparative technical solution found during the information search as prescribed in Article 18 of this Circular, including:
a) The basic technical signs of the technical solution may be features of the physical structure (details, clusters of details, connections, etc.) or the composition of the substance (components (presence, ratio), state of the elements, etc.) together with other basic technical signs that form a sufficient and necessary set to determine the nature (content) of the object.
The basic technical signs mentioned above may be expressed in the form of the technical function of a component in the structure or composition of the product (called functional signs), provided that this expression is sufficient for an average person in the field to easily understand the technical means or method to perform that function under normal conditions without any invention. The function or utility of the protected object is not a basic technical sign but may be the purpose or result achieved by that object;
b) The basic technical signs of the technical solution stated in the application, in the protection titles stated in the invention protection request, the description or drawings;
c) The basic technical signs of the technical solution stated in other documents that are expressed and discovered according to the descriptive documents or the actual form of expression of that technical solution.
2. To avoid being considered as losing novelty for inventions already published in the exceptional cases specified in Clause 3 and Clause 4 Article 60 of the Intellectual Property Law, applicants must submit documents related to the publication to prove eligibility for the exception. The above documents must be submitted together with the application or as supplements according to the regulations on amendment and supplementation of applications.
3. Corresponding to one point of the protection request, the technical solution stated in the application is considered new compared to the technical state in the world if:
a) No technical countermeasures are found in the course of information search; or b) Technical countermeasures are found but at least one basic technical sign in the application is absent in the technical countermeasures (and this sign is called a different basic sign).
Article 21. Assessment of creativity level
1. To assess the creativity of the technical solution stated in the application, information in mandatory sources (but not limited to those sources) shall be searched according to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 18 of this Circular.
2. The assessment of the inventive level of the technical solution specified in the application shall be carried out by assessing the basic distinguishing features specified in the protection request in order to draw conclusions:
a) Whether the basic distinctive signs are considered to have been disclosed in the minimum mandatory information sources; and b) Whether the set of basic distinctive technical signs are considered obvious to persons of average knowledge in the respective technical field.
To satisfy a protection requirement, a technical solution is considered inventive if the inclusion of a basic technical sign differing from the set of basic technical signs of the technical solution is the result of an inventive activity and not an obvious result of ordinary knowledge in the corresponding technical field.
3. In the following cases (but not limited to these cases), a technical solution is considered lacking in inventive step for each protection claim:
a) A set of basic, distinct technical signs that are obvious (any person with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field knows that a set of such signs is necessary to perform the defined function or achieve the defined purpose, and vice versa, that using such a set of signs will necessarily achieve the corresponding purpose or perform the corresponding function);
b) A set of basic, distinct technical signs that are disclosed in the form of identity or equivalence in one or more known technical solutions in the minimum mandatory information source. Two signs are considered identical if they have the same nature; two signs are considered equivalent if they have similar nature, the same purpose, and the same method of achieving the basic purpose;
c) A technical solution is a simple combination of known technical solutions with the same function, purpose, and effectiveness, or a simple combination of the function, purpose, and effectiveness of each known technical solution.
Article 22. Assessment of applicability for industrial use

1. A technical solution stated in the application is considered “feasible” under Article 62 of the Law on Intellectual Property if:
a) Information on the nature of the solution, together with instructions on necessary technical conditions, is presented clearly and sufficiently so that a person with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field can create, produce, use, exploit, or implement the solution;
b) The creation, production, use, exploitation, or implementation of the above-mentioned solution is repeated with the same results as those stated in the invention description.
2. A technical solution is considered not applicable for industrial use in the following cases:
a) The nature of the object or the instructions for the implementation of the object are contrary to the basic principles of science (e.g. not following the principle of conservation of energy, etc.);
b) The object comprises elements or components that are not technically related to each other or cannot be related to each other (coupling, binding, dependency, etc.);
c) The object contains internal contradictions;
d) The instructions for the implementation of the object can only be carried out a limited number of times (cannot be repeated);
dd) To be able to implement the solution, the implementer must have special skills and these skills cannot be taught or taught to others;
e) The results obtained from the implementation are inconsistent with each other;
g) The results obtained are different from the results stated in the description;
h) There are no or insufficient important instructions for the implementation of the solution;
i) Other cases with plausible reasons.
Article 23. Assessment of the first-filing principle
For an invention application that has been concluded to satisfy the protection conditions, before issuing a notice of the result of the content examination with the intention of granting an invention patent or utility solution patent as prescribed in point a, d2 clause 3 Article 25 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall check to ensure the first-filing principle prescribed in clause 1 and clause 3 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law according to the following regulations:
1. To assess the first-filing principle, at least the information in the following mandatory sources must be searched (but not limited to the minimum sources): All invention applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property (as of the examination date) having the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application being examined - up to the classification index (third class index) and having a filing date or priority date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority) and has not been published or has a publication date later than the filing date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) of the application being examined.
2. The search is to find cases where multiple applications (including the application being examined) register inventions that are identical or equivalent to each other and determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date.
3. If there are multiple applications under the cases specified in clause 2 of this Article, the invention patent or utility solution patent can only be granted to the invention in the valid application with the earliest filing date or priority date among the applications that satisfy the conditions for granting the protection title.
4. Among the applications under the cases specified in clause 2 of this Article, if there are multiple applications with the earliest filing date or priority date, the invention patent or utility solution patent can only be granted to one application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement is reached, all those applications will be rejected for granting the protection title.
Article 24. Use of the results of the information search and the results of the content examination of the invention application of a foreign patent office
1. During the examination of the contents of an invention application, the Intellectual Property Office may refer to the results of the search of information and the examination of the contents of the corresponding application filed abroad.
2. The search results and examination results specified in Clause 1 of this Article include one of the following documents:
a) Search report, examination report or examination result notification;
b) The declaration of the invention patent or protection title.

3. Before the Intellectual Property Office issues a notification of examination results, the applicant may request the Intellectual Property Office to use the examination results of an invention application filed abroad to assess the protection possibility, if the following conditions are satisfied:
a) The examination results of the contents in the documents specified in Clause 2 of this Article must be issued by the national or regional intellectual property offices selected as the preliminary search and examination authorities under the PCT Treaty;
b) The examination results of the contents of the invention application filed abroad mentioned above contain at least one protection claim assessed as satisfying the protection conditions;
c) The protection claims of the application filed in Vietnam initially or after amendment must coincide with the protection claims assessed as satisfying the protection conditions in the examination results of the contents of the invention application filed abroad mentioned above;
d) The applicant shall submit the following documents to the National Office of Intellectual Property: a request for use of foreign assessment results made according to Form No. 12 in Appendix I of this Circular; a copy of the assessment results; a Vietnamese translation of the assessment results, the protection claim points assessed by the foreign patent office as meeting the protection conditions, and the Vietnamese translation of the documents cited in the foreign patent office’s decision on the application; a description of the amendment, a detailed explanation of the amendment compared to the initial description (if any); and the fees as prescribed.
4. If the conditions specified in Clause 3 of this Article are satisfied, the request for use of foreign assessment results shall be accepted, and the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment results within 8 months from the date of receipt of the applicant’s request. Any amendment to the patent application within the 8-month period mentioned above, if it causes the conditions specified in Clause 3 of this Article to no longer be satisfied, shall be assessed according to the normal procedures.
5. If the conditions specified in Clause 3 of this Article are not satisfied, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the rejection of the request for use of foreign assessment results, and the patent application shall be assessed according to the normal procedures.
Article 25. Grant or rejection of patent/ utility solution certificates
1. A patent application shall be rejected for grant of a patent or a utility solution certificate if it falls under the cases specified in Points a, b, c, d and dd Clause 1, Clauses 1a, 1b Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. A patent application shall be granted a patent or a utility solution certificate if it meets the requirements specified in Article 118 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. For a patent application falling under the cases specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks:
a) If the subject matter specified in the application meets the protection conditions and the application does not fall under the cases specified in Points b, c, d and dd Clause 1, Clauses 1a, 1b Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment results, stating the intention to grant the protection certificate and setting a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection certificate grant fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection certificate, the fee for registration of the decision on grant of the protection certificate, the fee for maintenance of validity, and the first-year fee for use of the protection certificate;
b) If the subject matter specified in the application does not satisfy the protection conditions or the application falls into the cases specified in Points b, c, d and dd Clause 1, Clauses 1a and 1b Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law or if the subject matter specified in the application satisfies the protection conditions but the application has deficiencies, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of the assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse the grant of the protection title, the entire reasons for refusal or deficiencies of the application, and may guide the modification of the scope (volume) of protection and set a time limit of 03 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or make corrections to the deficiencies;
c) If the applicant fails to correct the deficiencies or provide any objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 15 working days from the expiration of the time limit specified above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the grant of the protection title;
d) In case the applicant makes corrections or provides objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 04 months from the date of receipt of the written correction or objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall assess the application as follows:

d1) If the correction does not meet requirements or if the objection is unfounded, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the protection title;

d2) If the correction meets requirements or if the objection is well-founded, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment results, stating the intention to grant the protection title and setting a time limit of 02 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection title grant fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, the fee for registration of the decision on grant of the protection title, the fee for maintenance of validity and the first year's fee for use of the protection title;
(d) If the applicant fails to pay the fee for grant of protection title, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of protection title, and the fee for registration of the decision on grant of protection title within the time limit specified in Points a and d2 of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on refusal to grant the protection title within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit;
e) If the applicant pays all fees and charges in full and on time within the time limit specified in Point a and d2 of this Clause, within 1 month from the date of payment of fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to grant the protection title. If within the prescribed time limit the applicant has paid the fee for grant of the protection title, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, the fee for registration of the decision on grant of the protection title but has not paid the fee for maintenance of validity and the first year's fee for use of the protection title, the protection title shall still be granted but its validity shall be terminated immediately after its grant.
4. The decision to grant the invention patent or utility solution patent shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision. The publication includes the information stated in the corresponding decision (including the decision number and date) and the information specified in Clause 2 Article 15 of this Circular.
The full description of the granted invention patent or utility solution patent shall be published on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
5. The invention patent or utility solution patent shall be granted in electronic and paper form (if the applicant requests paper form) according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP. In case of industrial property rights to inventions held in common ownership, the invention patent or utility solution patent shall be granted to the first person on the list of common applicants.
The duplicate of the invention patent according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be granted by the National Office of Intellectual Property in case the applicant requests a duplicate (stated in the invention registration application) for other co-owners.
Section 4. HANDLING OTHER REQUESTS RELATED TO INVENTION REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Article 26. Handling of requests for expedited examination of invention registration applications
1. A request for expedited examination of an invention registration application shall be received when the applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed and has provided the following documents:
a) An application for expedited examination according to Form No. 13 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) Documents proving that the invention has been commercially exploited in accordance with Point b Clause 1 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP within 12 months as of the date of filing;
2. The Intellectual Property Office shall receive and process all documents required for expedited content assessment in accordance with Clause 1 of this Article as follows:
a) If the expedited content assessment request does not meet the requirements of Clause 1 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the result of the assessment, clearly stating the reason for the proposed rejection or deficiencies, and set a 10-working-day deadline from the date of notification for the applicant to provide comments or remedy deficiencies;
b) If, after the deadline specified in Point a of this Clause, the applicant fails to remedy deficiencies or remedies are inadequate, or fails to provide comments or provides unfounded comments, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the rejection of the expedited content assessment request within 10 working days from the date of the deadline, and the corresponding invention registration application shall continue to be processed in accordance with regulations;
c) If the expedited content assessment request meets the requirements of Clause 1 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, the expedited content assessment request shall be published at the same time as the publication of the invention registration application and content assessment shall be conducted in accordance with this Circular within the time limit specified in Clause 2a Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law, unless the invention registration application is deemed invalid in accordance with Article 14 of this Circular.
Article 27. Handling of comments on invention registration applications
1. Within 6 months from the date of publication of the invention registration application or 3 months from the date of publication of the invention registration application in the case of expedited content assessment in accordance with Clause 2a Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law, any third party may object to the grant of the protection title for that invention registration application and must pay fees and charges.
The objection must be made in writing in Vietnamese, accompanied by documents or information sources. Documents or information sources may be in another language but must be translated into Vietnamese upon request.
2. In case the objection to the invention registration application complies with the provisions of Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall receive and handle the objection according to the following regulations, except for the case specified in Clause 3 of this Article:
a) Within 01 month from the date of receipt of the objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of the objection and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to respond in writing;
b) After receiving the applicant's response within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector of the response and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the objector to respond in writing to the response;
c) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall organize dialogue between the objector and the applicant to clarify the issue of objection if deemed necessary or at the request of both parties;
d) Based on the information, evidence, arguments provided by the parties, the results of dialogue (if any) as prescribed in Points a, b and c of this Clause and the documents in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall handle the objection and notify the results of handling the objection together with the results of examination of the contents of the application to the objector, except for the case specified in Point dd of this Clause.
dd) In case the invention registration application has been withdrawn or considered withdrawn according to the provisions of Article 116, Clause 3 Article 113 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector of the suspension of handling the objection within 01 month from the date of notification of acceptance of the withdrawal of the application or from the date of expiration of the time limit for requesting examination of the contents of the invention registration application specified in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 113 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. In case the objection of the objector relates to the invention registration rights, the objection shall be handled as follows:
a) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector to file a lawsuit with the competent court according to the provisions of the law on civil procedure and set a time limit of 02 months from the date of notification for the objector to send a copy of the court's notice of acceptance of the lawsuit;
b) If the deadline specified in Point a of this Clause is expired and the object of opposition does not send a copy of the notice of acceptance of the case or the opposition opinion, the opposition is considered withdrawn and the application for registration of the invention shall be processed as if there is no opposition.
c) If the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a copy of the notice of acceptance of the case from the Court within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the processing of the application for registration of the invention to wait for the result of the Court’s dispute resolution, except for the cases specified in Point dd Clause 2 of this Article. The processing of the application shall be continued immediately after the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the legally effective judgment or decision of the Court.
4. The time limit for the applicant to reply to the opposition and the time limit for the object of opposition to reply to the applicant’s reply shall not be counted in the time limit for the examination of the application.
Article 28. Amendments to the application for registration of the invention
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant the patent/ utility solution, the applicant may proactively or based on the notice of examination result amend the information and documents in the application for registration of the invention.
2. The request for amendment of the application for registration of the invention must comply with the following regulations:
a) If the amendment is based on the notice of examination result of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the request for amendment shall be expressed in the written reply to the notice, clearly stating the amendment. If the applicant proactively amends the application, the request for amendment shall be made using the Declaration Form No. 10 in Appendix I of this Circular, clearly stating the amendment;
b) If the amendment is related to the description or the abstract, the applicant must submit a part or the whole of the amended description or abstract and enclose a detailed explanation of the amendment. The amendment to the description and the abstract of the application for registration of the invention must not expand the scope (volume) disclosed in the initial description and must not change the nature of the invention subject matter stated in the application;
c) In case of change of industrial property representative, the applicant shall submit a written declaration on termination of authorization for the old industrial property representative and change of the new industrial property representative as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 8 of this Circular;
d) In case of changing the name, address or country code of the applicant, the applicant must submit a certified document (original or certified copy) or a legal document (certified copy) proving the change (decision on name or address change; certificate of business registration with name or address change, etc.) unless the National Office of Intellectual Property can proactively access and use information already available in databases published by the managing agencies of the databases. In case where information cannot be accessed or the accessed information is incomplete or inaccurate, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a written request to the individual or organization to supplement the application and clearly state the reason. The time for requesting and supplementing the application shall not be counted in the time limit for handling administrative procedures;
(d) Where other information is amended, apart from points b, c and d of this Clause, documents proving the lawful amendment or the applicant’s commitment are required;
e) In case of amendment or supplementation of information in Points c, d and dd of this Clause, the applicant may request amendment or supplementation of the same content related to multiple invention registration applications in one amendment request;
g) The applicant shall pay the fee for examination of the request for amendment to each amendment to each invention registration and the fee for publication of the amendment if the amendment must be published according to the provisions of Point a, Clause 4 of this Article, except for the case where the amendment must be made to correct errors due to the fault of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
3. In case the applicant actively requests amendment of the contents specified in Points b, c and d of Clause 2 of this Article before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the application or refuses to grant the protection title due to the application being invalid in form, such request shall be handled during the examination of the application and the result of handling this request (acceptance or rejection of the request for amendment) shall be stated in the notification of the examination result or the result of handling the application for registration of the invention.
4. Requests for amendment or supplementation that are not related to the description and abstract submitted after the application has been published shall be handled as follows:
a) If the request for amendment or supplementation of the invention registration application complies with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks: a1) Issue a notice of acceptance of the request for amendment or supplementation of the application; a2) Publish the amendment or supplementation on the Industrial Property Gazette;
b) If the request for amendment or supplementation of the application does not comply with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the request for amendment or supplementation of the application, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections;
c) If, after the expiration of the set time limit, the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the remedy of the deficiencies does not meet the requirements, or there are no objections or objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal of the request for amendment or supplementation of the application.
5. Requests for amendment or supplementation related to the description or abstract submitted after the application has been published shall be handled as follows:
a) In case the applicant actively makes amendments or supplements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of recognition of the request for amendment or supplementation and publish the content of the amendment or supplement on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property;
b) Requests for amendment or supplementation shall be handled during the examination of the application and the result of handling this request (acceptance or rejection of the request for amendment or supplementation) shall be stated in the notice of examination result or the result of handling the application for invention registration;
c) In case the request for amendment or supplementation of the application related to the nature of the invention stated in the description is submitted after the notice of intention to grant the patent/utility solution, the application for invention registration shall be re-examined within the time limit prescribed in Clause 3 Article 119 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the applicant shall pay the fee as prescribed.
Article 29. Recognition of change of applicant of invention registration
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant the patent/ utility solution, the applicant may request recognition of the change of applicant based on transfer, inheritance, or inheritance or according to the decision of the competent authority.
2. The application for recognition of change of applicant must comply with the following regulations:
a) The application for recognition of change of applicant shall be made using Form No. 11 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to transfer of application, the applicant must submit documents showing the transfer of the invention registration application (original or certified copy), which mainly include the names and addresses of the transferring and transferees; the number of the transferred application or sufficient information to identify such application;
c) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to inheritance, succession or according to the decision of a competent authority, the applicant must submit documents proving the inheritance or succession of property when consolidating, merging, dividing, splitting legal entities, joint ventures, alliances, establishing new legal entities of the same owner, changing business form or according to the decision of the Court or another competent authority or inheritance due to the applicant's waiver of registration rights;
d) The applicant must pay the examination fee for the recognition of change of applicant and the fee for publication of information on the recognition of change of applicant;
dd) The recognition of change due to transfer of multiple applications of the same applicant may be made in the same declaration, provided that the examination fee is paid according to the regulations corresponding to the number of applications for recognition of transfer.
3. The recognition of change of applicant shall be handled as follows:
a) If the recognition of change of applicant complies with the regulations in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of recognition of change of applicant and publish it on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property (except for cases where the amendment or supplementation of the application is made before the National Office of Intellectual Property publishes the application or refuses to grant the protection title due to the application being invalid in form);
b) If the applicant’s request for recognition of change does not comply with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse recognition of the applicant’s change and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiency or provide an objection. If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiency or the remedy fails to satisfy the requirements within the set time limit, or if there is no objection or the objection is not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to recognize the applicant’s change.
Article 30. Separation of invention application
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant an invention patent/ utility solution patent, the applicant may separate one or several technical solutions from the invention application (hereinafter referred to as the initial application) into one or more new invention applications (hereinafter referred to as the separated application). The separation must comply with the following regulations:
a) The request for separation must be made in writing, clearly stating the number of the initial application, explaining the subject matter of protection requested in the separated application, and the changes to the separated application compared with the initial application;
b) The applicant must submit a new invention application for the separated technical solution and a description, abstract, and authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative);
c) The applicant must pay the application fee and all fees and charges for procedures performed for the separated application other than those already performed in the initial application, which are not required to be performed again in the separated application, and are not subject to the priority right examination fee;
d) The subject matter of protection requested in the separated application must be the same as that in the initial application and must be different from the subject matter of protection requested in the initial application after separation;
dd) The separated application must not expand the scope (volume) of protection beyond what is disclosed in the description section and must not alter the nature of the subject matter stated in the initial application.
3. The application for division shall bear the number of the new application and the date of filing of the initial patent application and (the) priority date(s) of the initial patent application (if any).
4. The application for division shall be examined for form and continued to be processed according to the procedures that have not been completed for the initial application. The application for division must be published according to regulations.
5. The initial application (after being divided) shall continue to be processed according to the procedures that are being processed if the initial application is not amended when filing the application for division or shall be processed according to the procedures for amendment of the application according to regulations in Article 28 of this Circular if the initial application is amended when filing the application for division.
Article 31. Conversion of patent applications
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant a patent or utility solution patent, the applicant may convert the request for grant of a patent into a request for grant of a utility solution patent or vice versa for the whole or part of the patent application.
2. The request for conversion of a patent application must satisfy the following regulations:
a) The request for conversion of the application (for the initial application) must be expressed in writing, clearly stating the request for conversion;
b) The applicant must submit a declaration, which indicates the number of the initial application, the application fee for the converted application according to regulations, and pay fees and charges and a written authorization (if the application is filed through a representative);
c) In case of partial conversion of an invention application, the applicant shall have to carry out the procedures for separation of the application as prescribed in Article 30 of this Circular before requesting the conversion of the application.
3. After receiving a valid request for application conversion, the National Office of Industrial Property shall continue to process the converted application in accordance with the applicable regulations, but shall not re-perform the procedures that have been carried out for the original application before the request for conversion is received.
The converted application shall bear a new application number and shall be granted the filing date of the original application or the priority date of the original application.
4. In case of request for conversion of an application from an application for grant of an exclusive right to a utility solution into an application for grant of an exclusive right to an invention, which is filed after the notification of the result of the examination of the contents of the proposed grant of a protection title, the application shall be re-examined and the applicant shall have to pay the fees and charges as prescribed.
5. The original application (after conversion) shall be considered withdrawn at the time of filing the request for application conversion, except for the case where only a part of the application is converted as prescribed in Point c, Clause 2 of this Article.
Article 32. Withdrawal of an invention application
1. Before the National Office of Industrial Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant an invention/exclusive right to a utility solution title, the applicant may declare the withdrawal of the invention application.
2. The withdrawal of an invention application shall be carried out by the applicant himself/herself or by his/her authorized representative through a written declaration. For applications filed through representatives, the authorization document must clearly state the authorization for withdrawal of the application or be accompanied by a written order specifying the application number to be withdrawn.
3. Within 2 months from the date of receipt of the declaration of withdrawal of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the withdrawal request as follows:
a) In case the withdrawal request complies with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice accepting the withdrawal request and terminate the processing of the corresponding application. The withdrawn application cannot be restored and can only be used as a basis for requesting priority rights.
b) In case the withdrawal request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to reject the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the time limit is expired and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of rejection of the withdrawal request.
Article 33. Re-examination of the application
1. An application shall be re-examined in the following cases:
a) There is a written opinion of a third party opposing the intention to grant the invention patent in the notice of examination result together with credible evidence proving that due to force majeure events or objective obstacles, the applicant could not oppose the application within the time limit prescribed in Article 112a of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The applicant requests amendments to the nature of the invention stated in the application after the National Office of Intellectual Property has notified the examination result, which includes the intention to grant the protection title as prescribed in Point c Clause 5 Article 28 of this Circular;
c) The application for patent registration that is requested to be converted falls under the case specified in Clause 4 Article 31 of this Circular.
2. The re-examination of the application shall be carried out as follows:
a) The time limit for re-examining the patent registration application shall comply with the provisions of Clause 3 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
For complex cases with many details that need verification or expert opinion solicitation, the re-examination time limit may be extended but shall not exceed the time limit for the first examination as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
b) The re-examination content shall comply with the corresponding provisions in Section 3 Chapter II of this Circular;
c) For each case specified in Point a Clause 1 of this Article, the re-examination of the patent registration application shall be carried out only once for each third party.
3. After the re-examination results are available, the patent registration application shall be handled as follows:
a) For the case specified in Point a Clause 1 of this Article, based on the re-examination results, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall continue to handle the application in accordance with the provisions of this Circular;
b) In cases specified in Points b and c of Clause 1 of this Article, the handling shall comply with Article 25 of this Circular.
Section 5. PCT APPLICATIONS AND THEIR HANDLING
Article 34. PCT Application

1. A PCT application comprises a PCT application originating in Vietnam and a PCT application for national stage.
2. For a PCT application originating in Vietnam, the applicant may submit the application through the Intellectual Property Office or directly to the International Office. The application submitted directly to the International Office must be made in the language specified in the PCT Treaty and must satisfy the requirements for form and content specified in the PCT Treaty.
An application submitted through the Intellectual Property Office must be made in English, each application must be made in 01 copy and must satisfy the requirements for form and content specified in the PCT Treaty and the applicant must pay the preliminary examination fee, the fees and charges specified in the Regulations on Implementation of the PCT Treaty and the laws on fees and charges of the member countries specified in the PCT application.
3. For a PCT application designated or selected for Vietnam, in order to enter the national stage, the applicant must pay the Intellectual Property Office within 31 months from the priority date (if the application requests the right to priority) or from the date of submission of the following documents for the international application:
a) The invention registration declaration made according to Form No. 01 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) Copies of the international application (if the applicant applies for national phase before the international phase);
c) Vietnamese translations of the description and abstract in the international application (the original application if not yet published or the amendments and explanations of amendments if the international application is amended under Article 19 and/or Article 34.2(b) of the PCT Treaty);
d) Power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative).
4. The date of filing of the international application is the date of filing of the international application if the international application is designated or selected Vietnam.
Article 35. Processing of PCT applications originating from Vietnam filed through the National Office of Intellectual Property
1. Upon receipt of a PCT application originating from Vietnam, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following procedures:
a) Collect the fee for preliminary examination of the application form;
b) Check the compliance with the security control requirements for inventions under Article 89a of the Law on Intellectual Property and Article 14 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
c) Notify fees as prescribed for the applicant to transfer to the International Office and the international search agency as prescribed by the PCT Treaty;
d) Examine and process the application as prescribed by the PCT Treaty;
dd) Transfer the application to the International Office and the international search agency in case the application meets preliminary requirements regarding form and fees as prescribed by national law, is fully and on time, and the subject matter of protection requested in the application is not a state secret;
e) Issue a notice of refusal to proceed with further work in case the application fails to meet the security control requirements for the invention as prescribed in Article 89a of the Law on Intellectual Property and Article 14 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
2. After a Vietnamese-originate PCT application is transferred by the National Office of Intellectual Property to the International Office, all transactions related to the application shall be carried out directly by the applicant with the International Office or the competent authority of the member states of the PCT Treaty specified in the application as prescribed by the PCT Treaty.
Article 36. Processing of PCT applications in the national phase
PCT applications in the national phase shall be processed as follows:
1. The right to priority in a PCT application in the national phase shall be granted in accordance with the PCT Treaty and the Regulations on Implementation of the PCT Treaty. To be entitled to the right to priority, the applicant must:
a) Reaffirm the request for priority in the application;
b) Pay the fee for examination of the request for priority;
c) Submit the Vietnamese translation of the documents submitted to the International Office at the request of the Intellectual Property Office and necessary documents under Rule 17.1(a) of the PCT Treaty Implementation Regulations.
2. The applicant may amend or supplement the documents in the application. The amendment or supplement of the documents in the application must comply with the following regulations:
a) Article 28 and 41 of the PCT Treaty, Rule 52.1(b) and 78.1(b) of the PCT Treaty Implementation Regulations, Article 115 of the Intellectual Property Law and Article 28 of this Circular;
b) The authorization document and the document on transfer of the right to file the application in the international phase (if any) must be submitted within 34 months from the priority date (if the application requests priority) or from the date of filing the international application;

c) The amendment or supplement documents submitted by the applicant to the Intellectual Property Office must be made in Vietnamese.
3. The time of commencement of processing of the PCT application in the national phase is the first day of the thirty-second month from the priority date (if the application requests priority) or from the date of filing the international application. If the applicant submits a written request for early processing of the PCT application in the national phase, the PCT application will be processed before the time limit specified in this Clause in accordance with Article 23.2 of the PCT Treaty.
4. The PCT application shall be examined for form and content in accordance with the procedures prescribed for the national application and published within 1 month from the date on which the application is deemed valid, provided that such publication does not take place earlier than the date on which the application is published under the PCT Treaty, unless the applicant requests an earlier publication.
Chapter III
                         PROCESSING OF DESIGN PATENT APPLICATIONS
Section 1. DESIGN PATENT APPLICATIONS
Article 37. Design patent application
A design patent application comprises the following documents:
1. The application for design patent made using Form No. 06 specified in Appendix I of this Circular.
2. A set of photographs or drawings of the design (4 sets if the application is made in paper form).
3. Semiconductor integrated circuits manufactured according to the layout design, including 04 samples, if the layout design has been commercially exploited.
4. Description of semiconductor integrated circuits manufactured according to the layout design, including information on the functions and structure of semiconductor integrated circuits manufactured according to the layout design in order to clarify the nature of the layout design.
5. Authorization document (if the application is filed through a representative).
6. Documents proving the registration rights, if the applicant is entitled to such rights from another person.
7. Other documents supplementing the application (if any).
Article 38. Requirements for documents in a layout design registration application
1. Documents of a layout design registration application must satisfy the specific requirements corresponding to the provisions in Clauses 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 of this Article and satisfy the general requirements on form as prescribed below:
a) Documents must be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented horizontally) on an A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), with margins on all four sides, each margin being 20mm wide, using Times New Roman font, with a font size of not less than 13, except supplementary documents whose origin is not intended to be included in the application;
b) For documents consisting of multiple pages, each page must be numbered with Arabic numerals;
c) Documents must be typed or printed with fade-resistant ink, clearly and cleanly presented, without erasure or correction. Documents requiring signatures and stamps must have each page signed or the joint seal of the applicant/issuing authority (if any) affixed, except for electronic documents signed with digital signatures. Before receiving an application, if minor spelling errors are detected, the applicant may correct them and must sign (and affix a seal, if any) at the corrected location; the signature and seal must not obscure the document’s contents;
d) Electronic applications submitted via the electronic application system must be formatted as “.pdf”; the name of the electronic document must correspond to the name of the paper document as prescribed; individuals signing documents of design layout registration applications may use digital signatures to sign directly on electronic documents or sign directly on paper documents and scan (scan) the paper documents in color photo mode for applications submitted via the VNeID application;
dd) Terms used in applications must be consistent and common (not using local languages, rare words, or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts, and spelling rules used in applications must comply with Vietnamese national standards;
e) Documents and papers exchanged between applicants and the National Office of Intellectual Property must be in Vietnamese, except for documents specified in Clauses 5, 6, and 7 Article 37 of this Circular, which may be in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese upon request;
g) Documents of applications must contain all mandatory information and information must be consistent.
2. Design layout registration applications must comply with the requirements specified in Clause 3 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. Design layout registration declarations must contain all information as requested in appropriate places in the declaration. If the applicant requests confidentiality of information, the documents to be kept confidential must be clearly stated in the declaration.
4. A set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design must fully show the spatial structure of the elements of the circuit and the connections between these elements in the semiconductor integrated circuit so that, based on such set of photographs and/or drawings, a unique layout design can be identified. For the purposes above, the set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design must satisfy the conditions specified in Clauses 5, 6 and 7 of this Article.
5. A set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design must include at least one of the following three types of documents:
a) Layout design drawings made by computer for each layer of the integrated circuit;
b) Drawings or photographs of photolithography masks for the production of each layer of the integrated circuit;
c) Photographs of each layout design layer shown in the integrated circuit.
6. In case the application is submitted in paper form, the applicant shall submit a set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design together with supplementary documents which are electronic data carriers of a part or the whole set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design.
7. Forms of a set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design:
a) A set of photographs and/or drawings of layout design must include separate photographs and/or drawings for each layout design layer, accompanied by the symbol of each layer, the size of the integrated circuit and magnification;
b) All photographs and design drawings of layout must be in the same scale. For paper documents, each photograph and design drawing of layout must be at least 20 times magnified from the size in the integrated circuit so that the basic circuit design can be seen by the naked eye;
c) Each photograph and design drawing of layout may be presented on A4 paper or larger paper, provided that it must be folded into A4 size;
d) Photographs and design drawings of layout must be clear and sharp.
8. The semiconductor integrated circuit sample produced according to the layout design must be the whole or a part of the integrated circuit corresponding to the layout design stated in the application. If the semiconductor integrated circuit produced according to the layout design is an inseparable part of another product, the product must be enclosed with a document indicating precisely the semiconductor integrated circuit produced according to that layout design. In case the layout design has been commercially exploited anywhere in the world before the application is filed, the applicant must submit the first such commercially exploited sample.
9. The description of the semiconductor integrated circuit produced according to the layout design must include the following details about the semiconductor integrated circuit produced according to the layout design for which the protection is requested:
a) Name/notation: a set of letters and/or numbers used to distinguish this integrated circuit from other integrated circuits when it is put into circulation on the market;
b) Description of the basic functions of the integrated circuit (e.g. memory or logic functions or other functions);
c) Description of the basic structure of the integrated circuit (e.g. bipolar or MOS, or Bi-MOS, or optoelectronic or other structure);
d) Description of technology for manufacturing integrated circuits (e.g., TTL, DTL, ECL, ITL, CMOS, NMOS, PMOS or other technology);
dd) Description of main characteristics distinguishing the integrated circuit from other semiconductor integrated circuits on the market at the time of filing or first commercial exploitation in the world, whichever is earlier.
10. Confidentiality-protected documents must be separated into a set of confidential documents and may be submitted in the following forms:
a) Microfilm or analog form for documents showing the size of the design on computer drawings;
b) Electronic data;
c) Drawings or photographs with obscured parts, provided that the characteristics of the layout design are basically visible.
The maximum level of confidentiality protection allowed for an uncommercialized layout design is 50% of the surface area of each layer; for a commercialized layout design, 2 layers in every 5 layers from top to bottom.
Article 39. Confidentiality of information in layout design registration applications The National Office of Intellectual Property and organizations and individuals involved in the receipt and processing of layout design registration applications are obliged to keep confidential information in layout design registration applications that applicants request to be kept confidential under the provisions of Clause 3, Article 38 of this Circular.

Article 40. Receipt of application for design arrangement
1. An application for registration of a design arrangement shall be accepted when the following conditions are fully satisfied and:
a) The minimum documents, including: a1) The registration application; a2) A set of photographs or drawings of the layout; and a semiconductor integrated circuit design according to the layout, if the layout has been commercially exploited; a3) A description of the semiconductor integrated circuit according to the layout.
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
2. In case the application fully meets the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and confirm the date of filing and the application number for such application, and return the receipt of application to the applicant, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application.
For electronic applications, upon receipt, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue the applicant a receipt of application containing information on the date of application, the application number and the result of the examination of the application dossier, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application through the electronic application system.
3. If an application does not satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the application shall be rejected. If the application is submitted via postal service, electronic application submission system, or the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam sends a notice of rejection to the applicant, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall return the application documents to the applicant, except for the original document submitted for comparison.
Section 2. DETERMINATION OF THE FORM OF APPLICATION FOR DESIGN PATENT
Article 41. Formal assessment of application for design layout registration
1. After receipt, an application for design layout registration shall be formally assessed in accordance with Article 109 of the Intellectual Property Law to evaluate its validity and not assessed for its content.
2. An application for design layout registration shall be deemed invalid if it falls into any of the following cases:
a) There are grounds to assert that the applicant does not have the right to register as prescribed in Article 86 of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The application is filed in contravention of Article 89 of the Intellectual Property Law;
c) There are grounds to assert that the subject matter stated in the application is not protected as prescribed in Articles 8 and 69 of the Intellectual Property Law;
d) There are grounds to assert that the designer of the design layout does not satisfy the requirements specified in Clause 1 Article 122 of the Intellectual Property Law;
dd) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed in Article 10 of this Circular (including cases where the application fee, the fee for publication of the application, and the assessment fee have not been paid in full);
e) Applications that fail to meet the formal requirements specified in Article 38 of this Circular (incomplete applications) include: failure to meet the formal presentation requirements; translation of documents proving registration rights if the applicant is entitled to registration rights from another person; information about the applicant in documents is inconsistent or erased or not certified as required, the application form is incomplete with information on the author, applicant, representative (if any), lacks the signature and/or seal (if any) of the applicant or representative; etc.
g) No valid authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative).
3. Where there are grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of the information stated in the application or the information in the application is unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to submit documents to verify or clarify such information within 1 month from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice. The above-mentioned documents may be documents certifying the lawful registration rights, if the applicant is entitled to submit the application of another person (documents proving inheritance rights, documents proving the transfer of application rights; contracts for assignment of work or labor contracts), etc. Where, past the above-mentioned fixed time limit, the applicant fails to submit additional documents to meet the requirements, the application may continue to be assessed based on the documents in the application.
4. The notification of the assessment result of the design registration application shall be carried out as follows:
a) If the application falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notification of the assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the Design Registration Certificate, the reason for refusal or the deficiencies of the application and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the deficiencies;

b) If the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause expires and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the remedy is not satisfactory or there is no objection or there is an objection but not reasonable, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the Design Registration Certificate and send it to the applicant;
c) If the application does not fall into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article or the applicant remedies the deficiencies satisfactorily or provides reasonable explanations within the prescribed time limit, within 1 month from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to grant the Design Registration Certificate.
5. The date of submission of the design registration application is the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Intellectual Property as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 40 of this Circular.
6. The date of submission of the application according to the provisions of this Clause shall be the next working day in case the application is submitted through the electronic application submission system after working hours or on a public holiday or a public holiday.
6. The certificate of registration of the arrangement design according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be issued in electronic and paper form (if the applicant requests the paper form). In case the industrial property rights to the arrangement design are jointly owned, the certificate of registration of the arrangement design shall be issued only to the first person on the list of joint applicants.
A copy of the certificate of registration of the arrangement design according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property in case the applicant requests a copy (stated in the application for registration of the arrangement design) for other co-owners.
Article 42. Publication of the application for registration of the arrangement design
1. The application for registration of the arrangement design shall be published according to the provisions of Clause 4 Article 110 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. From the date of publication of the application, everyone can access the detailed information about the nature of the arrangement design stated in the published application, except for the information that is kept confidential according to the provisions of Clause 3 Article 38 and Article 39 of this Circular.
3. For the confidential information about the arrangement design specified in Clause 3 Article 38 and Article 39 of this Circular, only the competent authority that carries out the procedures for cancellation of the protection title or the procedures for handling the infringement of the rights to the arrangement design shall be allowed to access it.
Section 3. HANDLING OF OTHER REQUESTS RELATED TO THE APPLICATION FOR REGISTRATION OF THE ARRANGEMENT DESIGN
Article 43. Amendments to applications and recognition of changes to applicants registering layout designs
1. Before the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant a certificate of registration of a layout design, the applicant may proactively or based on the notification of assessment results amend the documents in the application for registration of the layout design.
2. An application for amendment of the application for registration of a layout design shall include the following documents:
a) In case of amendment of the application by the applicant, the amendment request shall be made in a Declaration using Form No. 10 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of amendment of the application based on the notification of assessment results of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, the amendment shall be made in a written reply clearly stating the amendment contents;
c) The applicant may request amendment of the same contents related to multiple applications for registration of layout designs in a Declaration or a written request for amendment;
d) In case of amendment related to a set of photographs or a set of drawings or descriptions of integrated circuits according to the layout design, the applicant shall submit a set of photographs or a set of drawings (4 sets for paper applications), the description after amendment, together with a detailed explanation of the amendment contents compared with the initial submitted documents;
dd) In case of amendment of the applicant's name, address, country code, the applicant shall submit a certified document (original or certified copy) or a legal document (certified copy) proving the change (decision on name change, address change; certificate of business registration with name and address change, etc.). The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall proactively access and use information available in databases published by the database management agencies. In case of inability to access information or insufficient or inaccurate information, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send a written request to individuals and organizations to supplement the application components and clearly state the reasons. The time for requesting and supplementing the application shall not be counted towards the time limit for administrative procedure settlement;
e) In case of amendment of industrial property representative, the applicant shall submit a written declaration of termination of authorization to the old industrial property representative and change of the new industrial property representative as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 8 of this Circular.
3. The applicant shall pay the assessment fee for each amendment and the fee for publication of information on amendments if the amendment must be published as prescribed in Point a Clause 5 of this Article, except for the case where the amendment must be made to remedy errors due to fault of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
4. The amendment to the design registration application shall not expand the scope (volume) of protection already disclosed or stated in the application and shall not change the nature of the design subject matter stated in the application.
5. In case of amendment of the application by the applicant, the amendment request shall be handled as follows:
a) If the amendment request complies with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the amendment request.
The amendment to the application shall be published when the design registration application is published as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 110 of the Law on Intellectual Property and Article 42 of this Circular.
b) If the amendment request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the amendment request, clearly stating the reason and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiency or provide an objection. If the time limit is expired and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiency or the remedy fails to meet the requirements, or there is no objection or the objection is not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal of the amendment request.
6. In case of amendment of the application by the applicant based on the notice of assessment result of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the acceptance or refusal of the amendment request of the applicant shall be handled by the National Office of Intellectual Property in the notice of assessment result or the corresponding decision during the handling of the design registration application.
7. In case the request for amendment or supplementation of the application includes amendment or supplementation contents made by the applicant and amendment or supplementation contents based on the notification of assessment results, the request for amendment or supplementation must be made into a Declaration as prescribed in Point a Clause 2 of this Article. The handling of the request for amendment or supplementation in this case shall comply with Clause 5 of this Article.
8. Before the National Office of Industrial Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant the Certificate of registration of design arrangement, the applicant may request the registration of the change of the applicant based on the assignment, inheritance or the decision of the competent authority.
9. The request for registration of the change of the applicant must satisfy the following requirements:
a) The request for registration of the change of the applicant shall be made according to Form No. 11 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of registration of the change of the applicant due to the assignment of the application, the applicant must submit documents showing the assignment of the design arrangement registration application (original or certified copy), which mainly include the names and addresses of the assignor and assignee; the number of the application being assigned or information sufficient to identify such application;
c) In case of registration of the change of the applicant due to inheritance or the decision of the competent authority, the applicant must submit documents proving the inheritance of the property upon consolidation, merger, division, separation of legal entities, joint venture, association, establishment of a new legal entity of the same owner, conversion of business form or according to the decision of the Court or another competent authority or inheritance due to the waiver of registration rights by another applicant;
d) The applicant must pay the assessment fee for the request for registration of the change of the applicant and the fee for the announcement of the information on the registration of the change of the applicant;
dd) The request for registration of the change due to the assignment of multiple applications of the same applicant may be made in the same declaration, provided that the assessment fee is paid according to the number of applications requested for registration of the assignment.
10. Requests for recognition of change of applicant shall be handled as follows:
a) If the request for recognition of change of applicant complies with the provisions of Clause 9 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of recognition of change of applicant and publish it on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property (except for cases where the amendment or supplementation of the application is made before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice of application or refuses to grant the Certificate of Design Registration due to the application being invalid in form);
b) If the request for recognition of change of applicant does not comply with the provisions of Clause 9 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse recognition of change of applicant and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If the time limit is expired and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the deficiencies are not remedied to the satisfaction, or if there are no objections or the objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to recognize the change of applicant.

Article 44. Withdrawal of application for design registration
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the decision to grant or refuse to grant the Certificate of Design Registration, the applicant may declare the withdrawal of the application for design registration.
2. The withdrawal of the application for design registration must be made by the applicant or his/her authorized representative through a written declaration. For applications submitted through representatives, the authorization document must clearly state the authorization for withdrawal of the application or be accompanied by a letter specifying the number of the application to be withdrawn.
3. Within 02 months from the date of receipt of the declaration of withdrawal of the application for design registration, the withdrawal request shall be handled as follows:
a) If the withdrawal request complies with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the withdrawal request and terminate the handling of the corresponding application;
b) In case the request for withdrawal of application does not satisfy the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the request for withdrawal of application and fix a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to satisfy the requirements after the expiry of the fixed time limit, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of refusal of the request for withdrawal of application.
Chapter IV
                          PROCESSING OF INDISTRIAL DESIGN APPLICATIONS
Section 1. INDISTRIAL DESIGN REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Article 45. Application for registration of industrial design
An application for registration of industrial design comprises the following documents:
1. An application for registration of industrial design made according to Form No. 03 in Appendix I of this Circular.
2. Documents to identify the industrial design to be protected are a set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design (4 sets of photographs or drawings must be submitted in case the application is filed in paper form).
3. Description of the industrial design.
4. Authorization document (if the application is filed through a representative).
5. Documents proving the registration right, if the applicant is entitled to that right from another person.
6. Documents proving the priority right, if the applicant requests the priority right, including:
a) Copies of the application or the first application of the agency that received the first application;
b) Transfer papers of the priority right, if the right is received from another person.
7. Other documents supplementing the application (if any).
Article 46. Requirements for documents in an application for industrial design registration
1. The application for industrial design registration must satisfy the specific requirements prescribed in Clauses 2, 3, 4 and 5 of this Article and the general requirements on form as prescribed below:
a) Documents must be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented horizontally) on an A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), with margins of 20mm on all four sides, using Times New Roman font, not smaller than size 13, except supplementary documents whose origins are not intended to be included in the application;
b) Each document, except for the set of photographs and industrial design drawings, if it comprises multiple pages, must have the page number written in Arabic numerals;
c) Documents must be typed or printed with permanent ink, clearly and cleanly presented, without erasure or correction. Documents that require signatures and stamps must have each page signed or the joint seal of the applicant or the issuing agency (if any) made, unless the document is an electronic document signed with a digital signature. Before receiving the application, if there are minor spelling errors, the applicant may correct them and must sign (and make a seal, if any) at the corrected position; the signature and seal must not obscure the contents of the document;
d) Documents of applications submitted in electronic form via the electronic application submission system must be in the “.pdf” format, except for sets of photographs, industrial design drawings, and documents which may be submitted in common image file formats (such as “.jpg” or “.png”, etc.); the name of the electronic document must correspond to the name of the paper document as prescribed; individuals signing documents of industrial design registration applications may use digital signatures to directly sign electronic documents or directly sign paper documents and scan (scan) paper documents in color mode for cases of application submission via applications
VNeID;

d) Terms used in the application must be uniform and common (not using local dialects, rare words, or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts, and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese national standards;
e) Documents and papers on transactions between applicants and the National Office of Intellectual Property must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents in Clauses 4, 5, 6 and 7 Article 45 of this Circular which may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese when requested;
g) Declarations and other documents of applications must ensure the completeness and consistency of mandatory information.
2. An application for registration of an industrial design must ensure consistency as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 5 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property. In case an application requests protection of an industrial design of the whole product or a part of the product together with one or more variant solutions of that industrial design, the variant solutions of an industrial design must not be significantly different from the basic solution and not significantly different from each other.
Here, a product is understood as follows:
a) A physical product is an object existing in the form of a tangible material such as an object, tool, equipment, means, produced by industrial or handicraft methods, with a clear structure and function;
b) A non-physical product is an object that does not exist in the form of a tangible material, created by projecting or displaying a design on a surface or into the environment (including air) and having a practical function for operation, interaction or performance of a specific task;
c) A part of a product is the part on the product that the applicant requests protection, including the separable part and the inseparable part;
d) A separable part of a product is a part of the product capable of independent circulation used for assembling or combining the product if independently registered, also referred to as the product, except for separate regulations.
3. The application for registration of industrial design must contain all the information required in the appropriate sections and satisfy the following requirements:
a) In the section “Name of industrial design”, the name of the product bearing the industrial design must be written in a concise manner using common words, without any advertising, commercial signs, or indications;
b) In the section “International classification of industrial design”, the international classification code of the industrial design to be protected must be stated in accordance with the International Classification of Industrial Designs (according to the Locarno Convention on International Classification of Industrial Designs). If the applicant fails to classify or classifies incorrectly, the Intellectual Property Office will classify the application and the applicant must pay the classification fee as prescribed;
c) In the section “Domain of use of industrial design”, the specific domain of use of the product bearing the industrial design must be clearly stated, including the purpose, utility, and function of the product;
d) In the section “Type of industrial design”, one of the two sections “Industrial design of the whole product” or “Industrial design of a part of the product” must be selected. In case of industrial design of a part of the product, the method of representing the part to be protected must be clearly stated on the set of photographs or drawings.
4. The set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design must satisfy the following requirements:
a) The set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design must fully represent the design features of the industrial design to be protected to the extent that a person with average knowledge in the relevant field can identify the industrial design. A person with average knowledge in the relevant field as prescribed in this point is a person with ordinary practical skills and clear knowledge of common and general knowledge in the relevant field;
b) The set of photographs or drawings must represent the subject matter to be protected in a consistent and accurate manner. In case of application submitted in paper form, the set of photographs or drawings must be printed or affixed on A4 paper without a frame;
c) Photographs and drawings must be clear and sharp; the background color of photographs and drawings must be uniform and contrast with the color of the industrial design product; photographs and drawings must only show the industrial design product to be protected (not accompanied by other products), except for the cases specified in Points k and l of this Clause; must not contain technical drawing instructions or instructions explaining the industrial design, except for brief and necessary instructions to indicate cross-sections and magnified images;
d) The set of photographs or drawings must clearly show the protected and non-protected parts of the product in one of the following ways:

d1) The protected part is shown with a continuous line, and the non-protected part is shown with a dashed line; d2) The protected part is shown in a contrasting color with the non-protected part;
(d) Photographs or drawings must show the industrial design in the same scale. In case of paper applications, the industrial design product must be no larger than 190mm x 277mm and the largest dimension must be no smaller than 120mm;
e) Photographs and drawings must show the industrial design in the same direction and be numbered in the following order: perspective (three-dimensional) view of the industrial design, front, rear, right, left, top and bottom views; the front views must be in the front view;
g) Photographs or projections which are identical or symmetrical with existing photographs or projections, photographs or projections of the bottom surfaces of large-sized products, photographs or perspective drawings of products which are too thick or thin, photographs or projections of surfaces which are too thick or thin of industrial designs, photographs or projections which do not show the protected parts and are not required to be included in the application, provided that this is clearly stated in the list of photographs or drawings in the description;
h) For industrial designs of the whole product or a part of the product which can be unfolded into a plane (e.g. boxes, packages, etc.), the drawings of the industrial design may be replaced by photographs or drawings of the industrial design in the unfolded state;
i) Depending on the complexity of the industrial design, additional photographs, perspective drawings from other angles, cross-sections, enlarged drawings, separate detailed drawings or separate representations of a part of the product, photographs or drawings illustrating the installation and use of parts on the complete product may be required, in order to clarify the nature and characteristics of the industrial design (but not for establishing industrial property rights to the industrial design of the part or such part);
k) For the industrial design of the whole product, photographs or drawings of each part or section on the product may be provided but only for illustrative purposes and not for establishing industrial property rights to the industrial design of the part or section;
l) Photographs or drawings must show the industrial design in the same state of use selected; photographs or drawings showing other states may be provided to clarify the nature of the industrial design. In case the industrial design of a non-material product changes its shape or moves, this effect may be shown by a series of images, each image in the series showing a successive state of the transformation process;
m) For applications with multiple solutions, the basic solution must be presented first. Each solution of an industrial design must be presented by a set of photographs and drawings in full accordance with the provisions of this Clause; the photographs and drawings must be numbered to show the order of the corresponding solution and the order of the photographs and drawings in that solution;
n) For a set of products, the set must have a perspective of the whole set and a set of photographs and drawings of each product in the set as prescribed in this Clause.
5. The industrial design description must include the following contents:
a) To fully list the basic design features that show the nature of the industrial design, in accordance with the design features shown in the photo set or drawing in the following ways: a1) The design features of the industrial design to be protected shall include: the design features of the shape, line, the correlation between the design features of the shape and/or line, the design features of the color (if any), the shape, arrangement, the correlation of the ratio, the order of display and the visual effect of the graphic elements on the non-material product; a2) For a product having different states of use (e.g. a product with a lid or foldable, etc.), list the basic design features of the product in different states; a3) If the industrial design comprises multiple solutions, clearly specify the design features of each variant solution compared with the basic solution; a4) If the industrial design is a design of a set of products, list the basic design features of each product in that set;
b) The most similar industrial design (if any): specify whether there is or is not a similar industrial design, if any, indicate the industrial design that is least different from the industrial design of the same type of product stated in the application, which has been widely known before the date of filing of the application or the priority date (if the application requests the right of priority), and specify the source of information that publicly disclosed the most similar industrial design;
c) List of photographs or drawings: list of photographs, perspective drawings (three-dimensional), projections, sections, etc. of the industrial design, in accordance with the order of the photographs or drawings.
Article 47. Receipt of applications for industrial design registration
1. An application for registration of an industrial design shall be accepted when it fully satisfies the following conditions:
a) The minimum documents, including: a1) The registration application; a2) A set of photographs and/or drawings of the industrial design;
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
2. In case the application fully meets the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and confirm the date of filing and the application number for such application, and return the receipt of application to the applicant, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application.
For electronic applications, upon receipt, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue the applicant a receipt of application containing information on the date of application, the application number and the result of the examination of the application dossier, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application through the electronic application system.
3. If an application does not satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the application shall be rejected (if the application is submitted via postal services, electronic applications submitted via the electronic application submission system, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a notice of rejection to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for rejection). For applications rejected via postal services, the Intellectual Property Office shall not return the application documents to the applicant, except for the original documents submitted for comparison.
4. For documents proving the grounds for entitlement to priority, applicants may submit additional documents within 03 months from the date of filing the application as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
Section 2. ASSESSMENT OF THE FORM OF INDISTRIAL DESIGN REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Article 48. Assessment of the form of industrial design registration applications
1. The assessment of the form of industrial design registration applications according to the provisions of Article 109 of the Law on Intellectual Property is the examination of compliance with the form requirements of the application, and the conclusion whether the application is valid or not.
2. An industrial design registration application shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) There is a basis to affirm that the applicant does not have the right to register according to the provisions of Article 86 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) The application is filed contrary to the provisions of Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) There is a basis to affirm that the subject matter stated in the application is a subject matter not protected according to the provisions of Articles 8 and 64 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
d) There is a basis to affirm that the industrial design author does not satisfy the provisions of Clause 1 Article 122 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
d) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges prescribed in Article 10 of this Circular (including cases where the applicant has not yet paid the application fee, the fee for publication of the application, the fee for examination of the application, and the fee for information retrieval for examination);
e) The application fails to meet the formal requirements prescribed in Article 46 of this Circular (the application is defective), including: failing to meet the requirements for presentation; failing to classify or incorrectly classifying the industrial design, for which the applicant has not paid the classification fee; failing to provide translations of documents proving priority (if required), translations of documents proving registration rights if the applicant is entitled to registration rights from another person; information about the applicant in documents being inconsistent or erased or not certified as required, the application form being incomplete with information about the author, applicant, representative, and lacking the signature and/or seal (if any) of the applicant or representative; etc.;
g) There is no valid authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative).
3. Where there is a basis (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of the information stated in the application or the information stated in the application is unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to submit documents to verify or clarify such information within 2 months from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice. The above-mentioned documents may be documents certifying the lawful registration rights if the applicant is entitled to the application fee of another person (documents proving inheritance rights, documents proving the transfer of application rights; contracts for assignment of work or labor contracts, etc.). If the applicant fails to submit additional documents meeting the requirements within the above-mentioned time limit, the application shall continue to be examined based on the documents in the application.

4. The notification of the examination result shall be carried out as follows:
a) If the application falls into one of the cases prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notification of the examination result, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the industrial design exclusive right certificate, the reasons for refusal or defects of the application (including the request for priority rights) and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the defects;
b) If the time limit prescribed in Point a of this Clause expires and the applicant fails to remedy the defects or remedies the defects do not meet the requirements or there is no objection or there is an objection but it is not reasonable, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the industrial design exclusive right certificate and send it to the applicant;
c) If the application does not fall into one of the cases prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article or the applicant remedies the defects to meet the requirements or there is a reasonable objection within the prescribed time limit, the application shall be considered valid in terms of form and shall be forwarded for publication and examination of the contents as prescribed. If the request for priority rights is not accepted, the application shall still be considered valid and forwarded for publication as prescribed.
5. Every week, the National Office of Industrial Property shall post on its website a list of applications for industrial design registration that are eligible for publication, clearly stating the application number, the date of filing, and the date of publication of each application.
6. The date of filing an application for industrial design registration is the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Industrial Property as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 47 of this Circular.
In case an application is filed via the electronic application system after working hours or on a public holiday or a public holiday or a public holiday as prescribed, the date of filing as prescribed in this Clause is the next working day.
7. The priority date of an application for industrial design registration shall be determined as follows:
a) If the application does not request the right to priority or, although the application requests the right to priority, the National Office of Industrial Property does not approve it, the application shall be considered as having no priority date;
b) If the application requests the right to priority, the priority date (or the priority dates) is the date stated in the above-mentioned request and approved by the National Office of Industrial Property;
c) The determination of the priority date according to the request for the right to priority based on the first application (applications) filed in Vietnam shall comply with the principles prescribed in Article 91 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the corresponding provisions in Points b, c, and dd Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
8. In case the applicant requests the right to priority but at the time of filing the application for industrial design registration there are no documents proving the right to priority, the assessment of the application form shall start from the time the National Office of Industrial Property receives additional documents proving the right to priority or the 3-month time limit prescribed in Clause 4 Article 47 of this Circular expires.
Article 49. Publication of applications for industrial design registration
1. A valid application for industrial design registration shall be published as follows:
a) The application shall be published within 1 month from the date on which it is deemed valid if the applicant does not request late publication or requests late publication but the application is deemed valid after the expiration of the time limit for late publication;
b) In case the applicant requests late publication and the application is deemed valid before the expiration of the time limit for late publication, the application shall be published in the following month of the month in which the time limit for late publication expires.
2. The publication of an application for industrial design registration in the Industrial Property Gazette shall include the following information: applicant, representative (if any); application number, date of filing, priority date (if any); name and nationality of the author of the industrial design; information on the origin of the application (separate application, original application number of the separated application, etc.); set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design; number of solutions/products for which protection is requested; international classification of the industrial design; and other information (if any).
Section 3. ASSESSMENT OF CONTENT OF APPLICATIONS FOR INDUSTRIAL DESIGN REGISTRATION
Article 50. Assessment of content of applications for industrial design registration
1. The assessment of content of an application for industrial design registration shall comply with Article 114 of the Law on Intellectual Property, which means evaluating the eligibility of the subject matter specified in the application for protection under the protection conditions and determining the corresponding scope of protection under the provisions of this Article.
2. Assessment of the possibility of granting protection titles of industrial design registration applications includes:
a) Assessment of the conformity of the subject matter specified in the industrial design registration application with the requirements for granting the industrial design exclusive title according to Article 52 of this Circular;
b) Assessment of the subject matter specified in the industrial design registration application according to each protection condition as prescribed in Articles 54, 55 and 56 of this Circular. The assessment shall be carried out in order for each product design (if the application refers to a set of products); in case of referring to multiple solutions, the assessment shall be carried out in order for each solution, starting from the basic solution (the first solution specified in the application);
c) Assessment of the principle of filing the first application according to Article 57 of this Circular.
3. The assessment of the possibility of granting protection titles according to point b clause 2 of this Article shall be completed when the industrial design subject matter specified in the application has been assessed under all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that such subject matter does not meet or meets the protection conditions, specifically:
a) Finding reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not meet one or some or all protection conditions; or b) Finding no reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not meet at least one protection condition.
4. Before issuing the notice of the result of the content examination with the intention of granting the industrial design exclusive title according to clause point a, d2 clause 3 Article 58 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the principle of filing the first application according to Article 57 of this Circular.
5. Based on the results of the assessment of the possibility of granting protection titles specified in clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall determine the corresponding expected scope of protection.
6. The notification of the assessment result of the contents of the application for the grant of the industrial design exclusive right specified in clause a and d2, section 3, Article 58 of this Circular shall be made for the following industrial design registration applications:
a) Applications not falling under the cases specified in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) An application with the earliest date of filing or earliest date of priority among applications for registration of industrial designs under the case specified in Clause 1 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) Application made under the agreement specified in Clause 3 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
7. Applications for registration of industrial designs not falling under the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article shall be handled as follows:
a) The application for the industrial design exclusive right certificate is rejected because it fails to satisfy the first-filing principle, if the application has the earliest filing date or priority date for the issuance of the industrial design exclusive right certificate; or b) The application is considered to have the earliest filing date or priority date and is handled as the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article, if all other industrial design registration applications with earlier filing dates or priority dates are rejected for the issuance of the industrial design exclusive right certificate, withdrawn or considered withdrawn.
8. During the process of examining the contents of the application, if the application does not fully reveal the nature of the subject matter, the National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application and provide information within the scope of the nature of the subject matter stated in the application to fully reveal the nature of the subject matter and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
Article 51. Minimum information sources
1. The purpose of information search is to search for industrial designs identical or similar to the industrial designs specified in the application in the minimum mandatory information sources. The minimum mandatory information sources used in the process of examining the application’s contents include the following documents:
a) Industrial design registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property and having application publication dates earlier than the application filing date or the priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority);
b) Industrial design registration applications and industrial design protection titles published by other organizations or countries within 25 years before the application filing date or the priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority), which are kept in the existing industrial design database at the National Office of Intellectual Property;
c) Other information related to the industrial design collected and kept by the National Office of Intellectual Property;
d) Industrial design registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property, international industrial design registration applications designated for Vietnam, and having application filing date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) earlier than or on the same day as the application filing date or priority date of the application being examined (used to check the first application filing principle specified in Article 57 of this Circular).
2. Where necessary and possible, the information search shall be expanded beyond the minimum mandatory information sources specified in Clause 1 of this Article, such as the national database on inventions, trademarks and other accessible information sources.

3. The search result shall be presented in a search report, which must clearly state the search field, scope, and search results within that scope (statistics and specify the found industrial designs in question, information source, and date of publication of corresponding information), and must state the full name of the report preparer (the searcher).
“Industrial designs in question” means industrial designs identical or similar to the industrial designs specified in the application, which are compared with the industrial designs specified in the application when assessing novelty and inventive step.
Article 52. Assessment of the suitability of the subject matter stated in the application for industrial design registration
1. The subject matter stated in the application for industrial design registration shall be considered unsuitable for the industrial design exclusive right certificate if it is not the external shape of the whole product or a part of the product.
The external shape of the whole product or a part of the product is the set of characteristics that create the shape (shape, lines, colors or the combination of these elements) of the whole product or a part of the product that can be seen during the exploitation of the product. The exploitation of the product means the use of the product according to its functions and uses, excluding the maintenance, repair or repair of the product. For the industrial design of a separate part registered independently, the assessment of visibility must be considered on the product assembled from that part.
2. The subject matter stated in the application according to Clause 1 of this Article is:
a) The external shape of the whole product or a part of the product due to the technical characteristics of the product;
b) The external shape of a civil or industrial construction work, except for the external shape of separate modules or units that can be used independently or assembled to form a construction work such as shops, kiosks, mobile houses or similar products.
Article 53. Assessment of the degree of difference of industrial designs The assessment of the degree of difference between two industrial designs shall comply with the following regulations:
1. Two industrial designs are considered identical when they are used for the same type of product, have the same set of basic and non-basic design characteristics.
2. Two industrial designs are considered not substantially different from each other when they are used for products of the same kind and have the same set of basic design features.
3. Two industrial designs are considered similar when they are used for products of the same kind and have at least one basic design feature that is identical or substantially different from each other.
4. Two industrial designs among similar industrial designs are considered most similar when they have the most identical or substantially different number of basic design features compared to all other similar industrial designs.
5. Two industrial designs are considered substantially different from each other when they are used for products of different kinds, or used for products of the same kind but have at least one basic design feature that is different.
6. Design features of an industrial design are understood as follows:
a) Design features of an industrial design are elements expressed in the form of lines, shapes, colors, positional or size correlations of the protected part on the whole product, form, arrangement, scale correlation, display order and visual effects of graphic elements on the non-material product in order to form the industrial design when combined with other features (signs);
b) Basic design features are design features that are easily recognizable/remembered, necessary and sufficient to determine the nature of the industrial design and distinguish the industrial design from other industrial designs used for products of the same kind.
Products of the same kind are products with the same or similar purposes or functions. Products assembled from multiple parts and parts used to assemble such products are products of different kinds.
c) The following elements shall not be considered as basic design features of an industrial design: c1) The shape and lines are determined by the technical functions of the product (e.g. the flat shape of a data disk is determined by the relative movement between the disk and the read head, etc.); c2) The presence of an element in the set of signs is insufficient to create an aesthetic impression (the impression of the product's shape does not change when the element is present or absent; e.g. a familiar shape or line changes but the change is insufficient to be recognized, so the changed shape or line is still recognized as the old one); c3) The material used to manufacture the product; c4) Information displayed inside the non-material product (e.g. text, data, etc.); signs attached, glued, etc. to the product for the purpose of providing information, guidance on the origin, characteristics, structure, use, and method of use of the product; Examples include information on the label of goods (such as manufacturer, trade description, country of origin, barcode, etc.), trademark, geographical indication, etc.; c5) Size of the product, except for the case of change in decorative size of fabric pattern and similar materials; c6) Unvisible shaping characteristics during the exploitation of the product. For industrial design of a separately registered part, the determination of unvisible shaping characteristics must be considered on the assembled product from that part; c7) Other elements that do not satisfy the conditions specified in point b of this clause.
Article 54. Assessment of novelty
1. To assess the novelty of the industrial design specified in the application, it is necessary to compare the set of basic design features of such industrial design with the set of basic design features of each of the industrial design counterparts found during the information search.
2. The industrial design specified in the application shall be considered new if:
a) The industrial design in question is not found in the minimum information source; or b) The industrial design in question is found in the minimum information source but is considered significantly different from the industrial designs in question; or c) The industrial design in question is the industrial design in question that is published or disclosed under the provisions of Clauses 3 and 4 Article 65 of the Intellectual Property Law.
Article 55. Assessment of creativity
1. To assess the creativity of the industrial design specified in the application, it is necessary to compare the set of basic design features of such industrial design with the set of basic design features of the industrial designs in evidence found during the information search.
2. Industrial designs specified in the application shall be considered as creative if they do not fall into any of the following cases:
a) Industrial design is a simple combination of known design features (design features that have been publicly disclosed are arranged or assembled in a simple way, such as replacement, repositioning, increase or decrease in quantity, etc.);
b) Industrial design is a copy/mimic of a part or whole of the natural form of trees, fruits, animals, etc., or a known geometric shape (circle, ellipse, triangle, square, rectangle, regular polygon, prisms with cross-sections of the above shapes, etc.);
c) Industrial design is a simple copy/mimic of the whole or part of the form of products or works that are famous or known in Vietnam or the world;
d) Industrial design is a copy/mimic of an industrial design in another field, if such copy/mimic is known in practice (e.g., toy cars, motorcycles, etc.).
Article 56. Assessment of applicability for industrial use
1. An industrial design stated in the application is considered applicable for industrial use if, based on the information about the industrial design presented in the application, a person with average knowledge of the relevant field can use that industrial design as a model to create a series of products with the same or similar external shape, by producing physical products using industrial or artisanal methods or by recreating identical non-physical products.
2. In the following cases, the subject matter stated in the application is considered not applicable for industrial use:
a) The subject matter stated in the application is the form of the whole product or part of the product with an uncertain state of existence (products in gaseous, liquid, or interface forms with images displayed randomly, etc.);
b) Only the whole product or a part of the product with the shape of the object specified in the application can be created by special skills or the whole product or a part of the product with the shape of the object specified in the application cannot be manufactured repeatedly;
c) Other cases with valid reasons.
Article 57. Assessment of the first application submission principle
For industrial design registration applications that have been concluded to meet the protection conditions, before issuing the notice of assessment results with the intention of granting the industrial design exclusive patent according to the provisions of point a, d2 clause 3 Article 58 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall check to ensure the first application submission principle specified in clause 1 and clause 3 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property according to the following regulations:
1. To check the first application submission principle, information in the mandatory sources specified in point d clause 1 Article 51 of this Circular must be searched.
2. The search is to find industrial design registration applications of the same product type that are identical or not significantly different from each other or to find industrial design registration applications of products containing parts with industrial designs that are identical or not significantly different from the registered industrial designs or to find industrial design registration applications of products including parts with external shapes that are identical or not significantly different from the registered industrial designs and determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date.
3. If there are many applications under the cases specified in clause 2 of this Article, the industrial design exclusive patent can only be granted to the valid application with the earliest filing date or priority date among the applications that meet the conditions for granting the industrial design exclusive patent.
4. Among the applications under the cases specified in clause 2 of this Article registered for the same product type, if there are many applications with the earliest filing date or priority date, the industrial design exclusive patent can only be granted to one application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement is reached, all applications will be rejected for granting the industrial design exclusive patent.
Article 58. Grant and refusal of industrial design exclusive rights
1. An application for registration of an industrial design shall be rejected for issuance of an industrial design exclusive right certificate if it falls under any of the cases specified in Points a, b, c, d, dd Clause 1 and Clause 1b Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. An application for registration of industrial design shall be granted an industrial design exclusive right certificate when it meets the provisions of Article 118 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. For industrial design registration applications under the cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks:
a) If the subject matter specified in the application meets the protection conditions and the application does not fall into any of the cases specified in Points b, c, d and dd Clause 1, Clause 1b Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of the assessment result, which includes the intention to grant the protection title and a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection title grant fee, the decision on grant of the protection title publication fee, and the registration fee for the decision on grant of the protection title;
b) If the subject matter specified in the application for industrial design protection does not satisfy the protection conditions or the application falls into the cases specified in Points b, c, d and dd Clause 1, Clause 1b Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property or if the subject matter specified in the application satisfies the protection conditions but the application has deficiencies, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse the grant of the protection title, the entire reason for refusal or the deficiencies of the application, and may guide the modification of the scope (volume) of protection and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the deficiencies;
c) If the applicant fails to correct the deficiencies or provide any objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 15 working days from the expiration of the time limit specified above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the grant of the protection title;
d) In case the applicant makes corrections or has objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 50 days from the date of receipt of the written correction or objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall assess the application as follows:

d1) If the correction does not meet the requirements or there are reasonable objections, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the protection title;

d2) If the correction meets the requirements or there are reasonable objections to the assessment results and/or the amendment to the application meets the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment results, stating the intention to grant the protection title and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection title grant fee, the fee for publication of the decision to grant the protection title, and the fee for registration of the decision to grant the protection title;
(d) If the applicant fails to pay the fee for grant of protection title, fee for publication of decision on grant of protection title, and fee for registration of decision on grant of protection title within the time limit specified in Points a and d2 of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on refusal to grant the protection title within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit;
(e) If the applicant pays all fees and charges within the time limit specified in Points a and d2 of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on grant of the protection title within 1 month from the date of payment of all fees and charges.
4. The decision on grant of the industrial design exclusive right shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision. The publication shall include information stated in the decision (including the decision number and date) and other information specified in Clause 2 Article 49 of this Circular.
5. The industrial design exclusive right shall be granted in the form of an electronic copy and a paper copy (if the applicant requests a paper copy) according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP. In case the industrial design is jointly owned, the industrial design exclusive right shall be granted to the first person on the list of applicants.
A duplicate of the industrial design exclusive right granted in the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be granted by the National Office of Intellectual Property if the applicant requests a duplicate (stated in the industrial design registration application) for other co-owners.
Section 4. HANDLING OTHER REQUESTS RELATED TO INDUSTRIAL DESIGN REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Article 59. Handling objections to industrial design registration applications
1. Within 3 months from the date of publication of the industrial design registration application, any third party may object to the grant of the industrial design exclusive right for that industrial design registration application and shall pay the fee and charge.
Opposition opinions must be made in Vietnamese and accompanied by documents or information sources. Documents or information sources may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese when requested.
2. In case the opinion on opposition to the application for industrial design complies with the provisions of Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall receive and process the opinion on opposition according to the following regulations, except for the case specified in Clause 3 of this Article:
a) Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of the objection and set a 1-month time limit from the date of notification for the applicant to provide a written response.
b) After receiving the feedback from the applicant within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the feedback to the opponent and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for the opponent to reply in writing to the feedback;
c) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall organize dialogue between the objector and the applicant to clarify the issue of objection if deemed necessary or at the request of both parties;
d) Based on information, evidence, arguments provided by the parties, the results of dialogue (if any) as prescribed in Points a, b and c of this Clause and documents in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the objection and notify the result of processing the objection together with the result of assessment of the application’s contents to the objector, except for the case prescribed in Point dd of this Clause;
(d) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the opposition of the decision to withdraw the application for industrial design registration in accordance with Article 116 of the Law on Intellectual Property within 01 month from the date of issuance of the decision to withdraw the application.
3. In case of opinions of opponents related to the right to register industrial designs, the opinions of opponents shall be handled as follows:
a) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objecting person to file a lawsuit with the competent court in accordance with the law on civil procedure and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of notification to the objecting person to send a copy of the court's notice of acceptance of the case;
b) If the objecting person fails to send a copy of the court's notice of acceptance of the case within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, the objection shall be deemed withdrawn and the industrial design registration application shall be processed as if no objection exists;
c) If the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a copy of the court's notice of acceptance of the case within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the processing of the industrial design registration application to wait for the court's dispute resolution result, except for the case specified in Point dd Clause 2 of this Article. The application shall be processed immediately after the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the court's legally effective judgment or decision.
4. The time limit for the applicant to respond to the objection and the time limit for the objecting person to respond to the applicant's response shall not be counted in the time limit for the assessment of the application's contents as prescribed in Points a and b Clause 2 of this Article.
Article 60. Amendments to industrial design registration applications
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant an industrial design exclusive right certificate, the applicant may proactively or based on the assessment result notification amend the documents in the industrial design registration application.
2. Requests for amendment of industrial design registration applications include the following documents:
a) If the amendment is made by the applicant, the amendment request must be made using Declaration Form No. 10 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of amendment or supplementation of the application based on the notification of assessment results of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the amendment or supplementation shall be made in the form of a written feedback clearly stating the contents of the amendment or supplementation;
c) The applicant may request amendment or supplementation of the same contents related to multiple industrial design registration applications in one application or a written request for amendment or supplementation;
d) In case of amendment or supplementation related to a set of photographs, a set of drawings or descriptions, the applicant must submit a set of drawings, a set of photographs (4 sets for paper application) or the amended description together with a detailed explanation of the amendment or supplementation compared to the initial submitted document;
dd) In case of amendment of the applicant's name, address, country code, the applicant must submit a certified document (original or certified copy) or a legal document (certified copy) proving the change (decision on name change, address change; business registration certificate with name change, address change, etc.). The National Office of Intellectual Property shall take initiative to access and use information available in databases published by the database management authority to replace the components of the application for administrative procedure settlement. In case of inability to access information or insufficient or inaccurate information, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a written request to the individual or organization to supplement the application components and clearly state the reason. The time for requesting and supplementing the application shall not be counted towards the time limit for administrative procedure settlement;
e) In case of amendment of the industrial property representative, the applicant must submit a written declaration terminating the authorization to the old industrial property representative and changing the new industrial property representative as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 8 of this Circular;
3. The applicant must pay the assessment fee for each amendment or supplementation and the information disclosure fee for the amendment or supplementation if the amendment or supplementation must be disclosed as prescribed in Point a Clause 5 of this Article, except for cases where the amendment or supplementation must be made to correct errors due to fault of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
4. The amendment or supplementation of the industrial design registration application shall not expand the scope (volume) of protection already disclosed in the set of photographs, drawings and descriptions in the application and shall not change the nature of the industrial design subject matter in the application.
5. In case of amendment or supplementation of the application made by the applicant, the amendment or supplementation request shall be handled as follows:
a) If the amendment request complies with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the amendment request (except for cases where the amendment request is made before the National Office of Industrial Property issues a notice of rejection of the application for the industrial design exclusive right certificate due to the invalidity of the application in terms of form).
The amendment request shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette in the case where the amendment request includes information related to the application in terms of form, such as the name, nationality and address of the industrial design author, the set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design;
b) If the amendment request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to reject the amendment request, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the deficiencies are not remedied to the satisfaction of the National Office of Industrial Property within the set time limit, or if there are no objections or the objections are not justified, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of rejection of the amendment request;
c) In case the amendment request relates to the nature of the industrial design stated in the set of photographs, drawings or descriptions submitted after the notice of assessment result is issued and the intention to grant the industrial design exclusive right certificate is granted, the application shall be re-assessed and the applicant shall pay the fee as prescribed.
6. In case the amendment request is made by the applicant based on the notice of assessment result issued by the National Office of Industrial Property, the acceptance or rejection of the amendment request shall be made by the National Office of Industrial Property in the notice of assessment result or the corresponding decision during the processing of the industrial design registration application.
7. In case the amendment request includes the amendment request made by the applicant and the amendment request based on the notice of assessment result, the amendment request shall be made into a Declaration as prescribed in Point a Clause 2 of this Article. The processing of the amendment request in this case shall comply with the provisions of Clause 5 of this Article.
Article 61. Recording of change of applicant of industrial design registration application
1. Before the National Office of Industrial Property issues a decision to grant or reject the industrial design exclusive right certificate, the applicant may request the recording of the change of applicant based on the transfer, inheritance or the decision of the competent authority.
2. The application for recognition of change of applicant must comply with the following regulations:
a) The application for recognition of change of applicant shall be made using Form No. 11 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to transfer of application, the applicant must submit documents showing the transfer of the application for registration of industrial design (original or certified copy), which mainly include the names and addresses of the transferring and transferees; the number of the application being transferred or information sufficient to identify such application;
c) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to inheritance, succession or according to the decision of a competent authority, the applicant must submit documents proving the inheritance or succession of property when consolidating, merging, dividing, splitting legal entities, joint ventures, associations, establishing new legal entities of the same owner, changing business form or according to the decision of the Court or another competent authority or inheriting due to the applicant’s waiver of registration rights;
d) The applicant must pay the assessment fee for the application for recognition of change of applicant and the fee for publication of information on recognition of change of applicant;
dd) The application for recognition of change due to transfer of multiple applications of the same applicant may be made in the same declaration, provided that the assessment fee is paid according to the regulations corresponding to the number of applications for recognition of transfer.
3. The application for recognition of change of applicant shall be handled as follows:
a) If the application for recognition of change of applicant complies with the regulations in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of recognition of change of applicant and publish it on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property (except for cases where the amendment or supplementation of the application is made before the National Office of Intellectual Property publishes the application or refuses to grant the industrial design exclusive right certificate due to the application being invalid in form);
b) If the applicant’s request for recognition of the change does not comply with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse recognition of the applicant’s change and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiency or provide an objection. If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiency or the remedy fails to meet the requirements within the set time limit or provides no objection or provides an objection but the objection is unfounded, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of refusal to recognize the applicant’s change.
Article 62. Separation of an application for registration of an industrial design
1. Before the National Office of Industrial Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant an industrial design exclusive right certificate, the applicant may, on his/her own initiative or based on the results of the assessment notification of the National Office of Industrial Property, separate one or several industrial designs from the application for registration of the industrial design (hereinafter referred to as the initial application) into one or more new applications for registration of industrial designs (hereinafter referred to as the separation application).
2. The applicant shall submit a new registration declaration for the separated industrial design. The separation application shall bear the number of the new application and shall be filed on the date of filing of the initial application for registration of the industrial design or the priority date of the initial application for registration of the industrial design (if any).
The applicant shall pay the application fee and all fees and charges for procedures performed for the separation application other than those already performed for the initial application, which are not required to be performed again for the separation application, and shall not pay the assessment fee for the priority right unless the separation application is required to be performed because the initial application fails to meet the requirements for uniformity of the application.
3. For the initial application, the applicant shall amend the application as prescribed in Article 60 of this Circular.
4. The separation application and the initial application shall be handled as follows:
a) The separation application shall be assessed for form and then handled according to the procedures that have not been completed for the initial application. The separation application shall be published as prescribed.

b) The initial application shall continue to be processed in accordance with Article 60 of this Circular.
Article 63. Withdrawal of application for registration of industrial design
1. Before the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant an exclusive industrial design certificate, the applicant may declare the withdrawal of the application for registration of industrial design.
2. The withdrawal of the application for registration of industrial design must be carried out by the applicant or his/her authorized representative through a written declaration. For applications submitted through representatives, the authorization document must clearly state the authorization for withdrawal of the application or be accompanied by a letter specifying the number of the application to be withdrawn.
3. Within 2 months from the date of receipt of the declaration of withdrawal of the application for registration of industrial design, the withdrawal request shall be as follows:
a) In case the withdrawal request complies with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice accepting the withdrawal request and terminate the processing of the application. The withdrawn application for registration of industrial design cannot be restored and can only be used as a basis for requesting priority rights.
b) In case the withdrawal request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If, after the expiration of the set time limit, the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice refusing the withdrawal request.
Article 64. Re-examination of industrial design registration applications
1. The Intellectual Property Office shall re-examine an industrial design registration application in the following cases:
a) There is a written opinion of a third party opposing the intention to grant an industrial design exclusive right certificate in the notice of the examination result together with convincing evidence proving that due to force majeure events or objective obstacles, the applicant could not oppose the application within the time limit prescribed in Article 112a of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The applicant requests amendment or supplementation of the application after the notice of the examination result has been issued, which includes the intention to grant a protection title as prescribed in Point c Clause 5 Article 60 of this Circular.
2. The re-examination of the application shall be carried out as follows:
a) The time limit for re-examination of an industrial design registration application is prescribed in Clause 3 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
For complex cases with many details that need verification or expert opinion solicitation, the re-examination time limit may be extended but not exceed the time limit for the first examination as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
b) The re-examination content shall comply with the corresponding provisions in Section 3 Chapter IV of this Circular.
c) In each case specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the re-assessment of the industrial design registration application shall only be carried out once for each third party.
3. After the re-assessment results are available, the industrial design registration application shall be handled as follows:
a) In the case specified in Point a, Clause 1 of this Article, based on the results of re-assessment, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall continue to process the case in accordance with this Circular;
b) In the case specified in Point b Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the application according to the provisions of Article 58 of this Circular.
Section 5. Complaints and handling of complaints
Article 65. La Hay applications 1. A La Hay application includes a La Hay application specifying Vietnam and a La Hay application of Vietnamese origin.
2. For Vietnamese-origin La Hay applications, applicants may submit them to the National Office of Intellectual Property or directly to the International Office. Applications submitted to the International Office must be made in the language specified in the La Hay Convention and must comply with the formal and substantive requirements specified in the La Hay Convention.
3. The La Haye application must be made in English (each application must be made in 2 copies for paper application) and must satisfy the requirements on form and content specified in the La Haye Convention. The applicant must pay the international transfer fee, fees and charges specified in the La Haye Convention and the laws on fees and charges of the designated member countries.

4. For a La Hay application designated for Vietnam, the date of submission of the application shall be the date of international registration.
Article 66. Processing of La Hay applications originating from Vietnam submitted through the National Office of Intellectual Property
1. In case a La Hay application originating from Vietnam is submitted through the National Office of Intellectual Property, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall be responsible for performing the following procedures:
a) Collecting the fee for international transfer of the application;
b) Notifying the applicant of the fee to be paid directly to the International Office in accordance with the La Hay Convention within 20 working days from the date of receipt of the application;
c) Preliminarily examining the form of the application within 12 working days from the date of receipt of the application;
d) In case the application has deficiencies, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of the deficiencies and set a time limit of 10 working days from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies;
dd) Transferring the La Hay application originating from Vietnam to the International Office within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application.
2. The date on which the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam receives the La Hay application originating from Vietnam shall be considered the date of filing of the application for international registration of industrial design in case the International Office receives such application within 01 month from the date inscribed on the receipt stamp of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam.
3. After the La Hay application originating from Vietnam has been filed with the International Office, all transactions related to the application shall be carried out directly by the applicant with the International Office or the competent authority of the member states of the La Hay Convention designated in the application according to the provisions of the La Hay Convention.
Article 67. Processing of La Hay applications designated for Vietnam
After receiving the notice of the International Office, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall process the La Hay application designated for Vietnam as follows:
1. The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall conduct the examination of the contents of the application as for the application for international registration of industrial design filed under the national form, except for the cases specified in Clauses 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 of this Article. Within 06 months from the date on which the La Hay application is published by the International Office, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall conclude on the protection possibility of the industrial design in the application.
2. In case the industrial design in the application meets the protection conditions according to the provisions of Vietnamese law and the application is without any defect, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall carry out the following procedures:
a) Before the expiration of the 06-month period specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a decision to accept the protection of the industrial design registered under the international application, record it in the National Register of Industrial Property (Section on International Registration of Industrial Designs) and send it to the International Office for declaration of acceptance of the protection of the industrial design registered under the international application using the form of the International Office;
b) Publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette within 01 month from the date of issuance of the decision.
3. In case the industrial design does not satisfy the protection conditions or the application has deficiencies (lacks photographs/drawings which fail to fully reveal the design’s distinctive features or the international registration does not satisfy the declarations of Vietnam or contains information that needs verification, etc.), before the 06-month time limit specified in Clause 1 of this Article expires, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a refusal notice using the form of the International Office, clearly stating the contents and reasons for refusal and send such notice to the International Office.
4. In case some registered industrial designs do not satisfy the protection conditions or the application has deficiencies regarding some industrial designs (lacks photographs/drawings which fail to fully reveal the design’s distinctive features or the international registration does not satisfy the declarations of Vietnam or contains information that needs verification, etc.), before the 06-month time limit specified in Clause 1 of this Article expires, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) Issue a refusal notice regarding the industrial design that does not satisfy the protection conditions or has deficiencies using the form of the International Office, clearly stating the contents and reasons for refusal and send such notice to the International Office;
b) Issue a decision on granting protection regarding the industrial design that satisfies the protection conditions and has no deficiencies, record it in the National Register of Industrial Property (Section on International Registration of Industrial Designs) and send the Declaration of Granting Protection for the International Registration of Industrial Design to the International Office using the form of the International Office, clearly stating the industrial design granted protection;
c) Publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette within 01 month from the date of issuance of the decision.

5. Within 03 months from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a refusal notice as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 of this Article, the applicant has the right to remedy the deficiencies or object to the refusal opinion of the National Office of Intellectual Property. The remedy of deficiencies or objection to the refusal opinion stated in the notice shall be carried out according to the procedures for applications for industrial design registration submitted under the national form, including the regulations on the method of application submission.
In case the application is proposed to be refused due to failure to satisfy the requirements for uniformity of the application as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 5 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the applicant may remedy the above deficiencies by requesting the separation of one or some industrial designs in the above international registration into one or more new applications. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the separation of the application and issue decisions and notices of the new application independently of the initial application.
6. In case the applicant remedies the deficiencies and meets the requirements and/or has a valid objection within the time limit specified in Clause 5 of this Article, within 02 months the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) To issue a decision on acceptance of protection of an industrial design registered internationally for industrial designs that satisfy the protection conditions, record it in the National Register of Industrial Property (International Registration of Industrial Designs section) and send it to the International Office for declaration of acceptance of protection of the international registration of industrial design after refusal according to the form of the International Office, clearly stating the industrial design accepted for protection;
b) To publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette within 1 month from the date of issuance of the decision.
7. In case the deadline specified in clause 5 of this Article expires and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies do not satisfy the requirements, or there is no objection or objection is not reasonable to the industrial designs notified of refusal, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the above deadline, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on refusal of protection of the international registration of industrial design for those industrial designs.
8. In case the deadline of 3 months from the date of publication of the La Haye application by the International Office for designation of Vietnam expires and the applicant fails to submit documents proving the priority right or submits documents proving the priority right but is not approved by the National Office of Intellectual Property, the application shall be considered as having no priority right claim.
9. The procedures for filing complaints and settling complaints against the decisions specified in clauses 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 of this Article shall be carried out as for the industrial design registration application filed under the national form. In case, after the result of complaint settlement, some or all of the industrial designs refused in the refusal decisions are accepted for protection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send it to the International Office for declaration of acceptance of protection after refusal according to the form of the International Office, clearly stating the industrial design accepted for protection.
10. In case a third party has an opinion on the La Haye application for designation of Vietnam before the date of issuance of the decision on acceptance of protection, the opinion of the third party shall be considered as a reference source for the process of handling the La Haye application for designation of Vietnam.
11. The contents of publication according to the provisions of point b clause 2, point c clause 4 and point b clause 6 of this Article include basic information recorded in the corresponding decision (including the decision number and date of decision) and other information according to the provisions of clause 2 Article 49 of this Circular.
Chapter V
                            PROCESSING OF MARK REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Section 1. MARK REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS
Article 68. Mark registration application
A mark registration application comprises the following documents:
1. The mark registration application made using Form No. 04 in Appendix I of this Circular.
2. The mark form enclosed (5 mark forms are enclosed if the application is made in paper form).
3. The collective mark use regulations or the certification mark use regulations for the mark registered as a collective mark or a certification mark.
4. The authorization document (if the application is made through a representative).
5. Documents proving the right to register a mark if the applicant is entitled to the registration right of another person.
6. Documents proving the permission for registration of collective marks, certification marks containing local geographical names or other signs indicating the origin of local specialties of Vietnam as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
7. Documents proving the priority right if the applicant requests the priority right, including:

a) Copies of the application or the first application of the agency that received the first application;
b) Transfer papers of the priority right if the right is entitled to by another person.
8. Documents proving the right to use the image of an individual for mark registration, including:
a) Copies of personal documents (with the image of the individual) in case the applicant uses the image of himself/herself;
b) Written consent for the use of the image of an individual in accordance with the provisions of civil law in case the applicant uses the image of another individual and copies of personal documents (with the image of the individual) of that person;
c) The applicant shall not have to submit copies of personal identification documents as prescribed in Points a and b of this Clause if the National Population Database provides such information.
9. Other documents supplementing the application (if any).
Article 69. Requirements for documents in a trademark application
1. Documents of a trademark application shall comply with the requirements specified in Clauses 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 of this Article and general requirements on form as prescribed below:
a) Documents shall be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented horizontally) on an A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), except for maps of geographical areas which may be presented on an A3-sized paper (420mm x 297mm), with margins of 20mm on all four sides, using Times New Roman font, with a font size of at least 13, except for supplementary documents whose origins are not intended to be included in the application;
b) If a document comprises multiple pages, each page shall bear the page number in Arabic numerals;
c) Documents must be typed or printed with permanent ink, clearly and cleanly presented, without erasure or alteration. For documents requiring signatures and stamps, if they comprise 02 pages or more, each page must be signed or stamped by the applicant or the issuing authority (if any), except for electronic documents signed with digital signatures. Before receiving the application, if minor spelling errors are detected, the applicant may correct them and must sign (and stamp, if any) the corrected positions; the signature and stamp must not obscure the contents of the document;
d) Documents of an application submitted in electronic form via the electronic application submission system must be formatted as “.pdf”; the name of the electronic document must correspond to the name of the paper document as prescribed; individuals signing documents of a trademark application may use digital signatures to sign directly on electronic documents or directly on paper documents and scan (scan) the paper documents in color mode for applications submitted via the VNeID application;
d) Terms used in the application must be uniform and common (not using local dialects, rare words, or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts, and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese national standards;
e) Documents and papers on transactions between applicants and the National Office of Intellectual Property must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents under Clauses 4, 5, 7, 8 and 9 Article 68 of this Circular which may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese when requested.
g) The declaration and other documents of the application must contain all mandatory information and be consistent.
2. The trademark application must comply with the provisions of Clause 3 and Clause 6 Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. The trademark registration application must contain all the information required in the appropriate sections of the application and satisfy the following requirements:
a) In the “Non-traditional marks” section, if the applicant requests registration of a sound mark or a three-dimensional mark, the applicant must mark the corresponding box;
b) In the “Trademark sample” section, a sample must be included as prescribed in the section on trademark samples in Clause 4 of this Article;
c) In the “Mark description” section, each element of the mark and the overall meaning of the mark (if any) must be clearly stated, and the following contents must be described:

c1) If the mark contains words in a language other than Vietnamese, the pronunciation (transliteration into Vietnamese) must be clearly stated, and if those words have meaning, they must be translated into Vietnamese;

c2) If the mark contains non-Arabic numerals or Roman numerals, they must be translated into Arabic numerals;

c3) If the mark is a shape or contains a shape element, the contents and meaning of the shape element must be clearly stated;

c4) If the mark expresses a color and the color mark is protected, such request must be clearly stated, and the color expressed on the mark must be stated.

c5) If the mark is a sound mark, the sound mark must be fully, clearly and completely described (what kind of musical instrument is used, whether it is accompanied by words, etc.). This description may be included in the documents attached to the application;
d) In the “Exclusion factors” section, the applicant may exclude one or more factors of the mark form which he/she does not wish to claim as exclusion factors;
dd) In the “Applicant” section, if the applicant is a foreign individual or foreign organization, the name and address must be expressed in Latin letters in the international form of the original name, without translation or transliteration into Vietnamese;
e) In the “List and classification of goods and services bearing the mark” section, the applicant must classify according to the International Classification of Goods and Services under the Nice Agreement published by the National Office of Industrial Property in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property. If the applicant fails to classify or classifies incorrectly, the list of goods and services will be re-classified, and the National Office of Industrial Property will classify it, and the applicant must pay the classification fee as prescribed.
4. The mark form must meet the following requirements:
a) The mark form must be clearly presented with the size of each component of the mark not exceeding 80mm and not less than 8mm, the overall mark must be presented in the mark form with a size of 80mm x 80mm on the application;
b) For 3D marks, the mark form must be accompanied by a photograph or drawing showing the perspective and may be accompanied by a description in the form of a projection;
c) For marks requiring color protection, the mark form must be presented in the color required for protection. If color protection is not required, the mark form must be presented in black and white;
d) For a sound mark, the mark sample must be an MP3 digital audio file with a size of not more than 5 MB and the audio represented in the form of a musical score with 5 lines.
The musical score with 5 lines must be clear, accurate, complete and consistent with the audio file, which may include a key, a staff, a note, a rhythm, a tempo, a musical note, etc.
5. The collective mark use regulations shall include the following main contents:
a) Information on the mark and goods and services bearing the mark;
b) Name, address, establishment and operation bases of the collective mark owner organization when the protection title is granted;
c) Criteria for becoming a member of the collective mark owner organization;
d) List of members of the collective mark owner organization;
dd) Conditions for use of the mark. For a collective mark containing a geographical origin indicator, the conditions for use of the collective mark shall include the geographical origin condition and the characteristics of the goods and services;
e) Measures to handle violations against the regulations on use of the mark.
6. The mark certification regulations must contain the following main contents:
a) Information on the mark and the goods and services certified by the mark;
b) The organization that is the mark owner when the protection title is granted;
c) Conditions for use of the mark. For a certified mark containing a geographical indication, the conditions for use of the certified mark must include the geographical origin conditions and characteristics of the goods and services;
d) Characteristics of the goods and services certified by the mark;
dd) Methods for assessing the characteristics of the goods and services and methods for controlling the use of the mark;
e) Costs that the mark user must pay for mark certification and protection, if any.
7. Documents proving the permission for registration of collective marks, certification marks containing localities or other signs indicating the origin of local specialties of Vietnam as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property, including the written permission for use in accordance with Point a and the corresponding geographical area map in accordance with Point b of this Clause.
a) A written permission to use a geographical name or other sign indicating the geographical origin of a local specialty for registration of a collective trademark or certification mark containing such element, issued by the following competent authority:

a1) People’s Committees of provinces and centrally-run cities where the geographical area corresponding to the local specialty’s name or other geographical origin-indicating signs is located (in case the geographical area belongs to one locality); a2) All People’s Committees of provinces and centrally-run cities where the geographical area corresponding to the local specialty’s name or other geographical origin-indicating signs is located (in case the geographical area belongs to multiple localities).
b) Maps of geographical areas corresponding to localities or other signs indicating the geographical origin of local specialties must contain sufficient information to enable the geographical area to be precisely identified and must be certified by the competent authority specified in Point a of this Clause.
8. In case there are grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of information stated in the application or information stated in the application is unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant to submit documents to verify or clarify such information within 02 months from the date of notification. In case the applicant fails to submit additional documents to meet the requirements within the above-mentioned time limit, the application shall continue to be assessed based on the documents in the application. Documents specified in this clause may be the following documents:
a) Documents proving the applicant’s eligibility: a1) Certificate of business registration, contract or other document certifying the applicant’s production of products or provision of services in accordance with Clause 1 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; a2) Agreement or written confirmation that the producer does not use the mark and does not object to the registration of the mark by the person conducting the commercial activity of the producer’s products in accordance with Clause 2 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; a3) Decision or license for establishment, articles of association of the organization in which information on the function and competence to manage collective marks, quality certification marks, geographical origin certification marks of goods and services is stated in accordance with Clause 3 and Clause 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; a4) Agreement, certificate of business registration, documents related to the registration of marks of co-owners in accordance with Clause 5 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; a5) Document certifying that the applicant is entitled to the right to register marks from another person in accordance with Clause 6 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; a6) Agreement or written consent of the mark owner certifying the right to register marks of the representative or agent in accordance with Clause 7 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property and Article 6septies of the Paris Convention. a7) Document proving permanent residence in Vietnam that is currently valid for applicants who are foreign individuals permanently residing in Vietnam.
b) Documents proving the applicant’s eligibility as a representative: original power of attorney of the applicant; documents certifying that the applicant is the legal representative of the mark registration organization or the person authorized by such organization; documents certifying that the authorized person of the applicant meets the requirements to act as the representative of the applicant in accordance with Article 6 of this Circular;
c) Documents proving the right to use or register marks containing special signs in accordance with Clauses 2, 4 and 7 Article 73, Point p Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property; documents proving the use of trade indications, indications of origin, awards, medals or distinctive signs of a certain product on the mark without misleading, confusing or deceiving consumers;
d) Documents proving the right of priority;
dd) Documents proving the control and certification functions of the certification mark registration organization;
e) Necessary information to clarify or affirm the contents stated in the collective mark use regulations, the certification mark use regulations or other application documents;
g) Other appropriate documents to clarify the authenticity of information in the application.
Article 70. Receipt of mark registration applications
1. An application for registration of a mark shall be accepted when it fully satisfies the following conditions:
a) The minimum documents, including: a1) The registration application; a2) The label form enclosed (5 label forms are enclosed for paper application);

a3) Regulations on use of collective marks and regulations on use of certification marks for marks registered as collective marks or certification marks; a4) Documents proving the permission for registration of collective marks or certification marks containing local geographical names or other signs indicating the origin of local specialties of Vietnam specified in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
2. In case the application fully meets the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and confirm the date of filing and the application number for such application, and return the receipt of application to the applicant, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application.
For applications submitted via the electronic application system, upon receipt of an application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue the applicant a receipt of application containing information on the date of application submission, the application number, and the result of the dossier check, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application via the electronic application system.
3. If an application does not satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the application shall be rejected (if the application is submitted via postal services, electronic applications submitted via the electronic application submission system, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a notice of rejection to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for rejection). For applications rejected via postal services, the Intellectual Property Office shall not return the application documents to the applicant, except for the original documents submitted for comparison.
4. For documents proving the grounds for entitlement to priority, applicants may submit additional documents within 03 months from the date of filing the application as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
Section 2. DETERMINATION OF THE FORM OF THE APPLICATION FOR STAMPING EFFECT
Article 71. Assessment of the form of a trademark registration application
1. The assessment of the form of a trademark registration application according to the provisions of Article 109 of the Law on Intellectual Property is the examination of compliance with the form requirements of the application, and the conclusion whether the application is valid or not.
The assessment of the form of application for certification mark registration and collective mark registration does not include the evaluation of specific characteristics of goods and services certified by the mark, the method of evaluation of characteristics of goods and services and the method of control of the use of the mark, the cost that the mark user has to pay for the certification and protection of the mark (if any) and the conditions for the use of the mark, and measures to handle violations of the mark use regulations specified in the mark use regulations.
2. An application for registration of a mark shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) There are grounds to affirm that the applicant has no right to register as prescribed in Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) Applications submitted in contravention of Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) There are grounds to clearly affirm that the subject matter mentioned in the application is not protected by the State according to the provisions of Articles 8 and 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
d) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed;
dd) The application fails to satisfy the formal requirements specified in Article 69 of this Circular (the application is defective), including: failing to satisfy the requirements for the quantity of one of the mandatory documents; failing to satisfy the requirements for the presentation; the declaration does not clearly state the type of mark to be registered, lacks the description of the mark; fails to classify the goods and services bearing the mark, or classifies them incorrectly and the applicant fails to pay the classification fee; lacks the translated documents proving the priority right (if any), the translated documents proving the registration right if the applicant is entitled to the registration right from another person; the information about the applicant in the documents is inconsistent or erased or not certified as prescribed, the declaration does not contain sufficient information about the applicant, the representative, and the signature and/or seal (if any) of the applicant or the representative; etc.;
e) There is no valid authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative).
3. The notification of the formal assessment result shall be carried out as follows:
a) If the application falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notification of the formal assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the Certificate of mark registration, the reason for refusal or the defects of the application (including the request for priority right) and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notification for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the defects.

b) If the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause expires and the applicant fails to remedy the defects or the remedy is unsatisfactory or there is no objection or there is an objection but it is not reasonable, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the above time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the Certificate of mark registration and send it to the applicant.
c) If the application does not fall into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article (or the applicant remedies the defects satisfactorily or provides a reasonable explanation), the application is deemed valid and its contents shall be published and assessed as prescribed. If the request for priority right is not accepted, the application shall still be considered valid and its contents shall be published as prescribed.
4. Weekly, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall post on the website the list of mark registration applications to be published, clearly stating the application number, the date of application and the date of publication of each application.
5. The date of filing of a trademark application shall be the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Intellectual Property in accordance with Clause 2 Article 70 of this Circular.
In case the application is filed via the electronic application system after working hours or on a public holiday or a public holiday, the date of filing of the application in accordance with this Clause shall be the next working day.
6. The priority date of a trademark application shall be determined as follows:
a) If the application does not request the priority right or, although the application requests the priority right, the National Office of Intellectual Property does not approve the request, the application shall be considered as having no priority date;
b) If the application requests the priority right, the priority date (or the priority dates) shall be the date stated in the above-mentioned request and approved by the National Office of Intellectual Property;
c) The determination of the priority date in accordance with the request for the priority right shall be based on the first (first) application(s) filed in Vietnam in accordance with the principles specified in Article 91 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the corresponding provisions in Points b, c and dd Clause 1 Article 12 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
7. In case the applicant requests the priority right but there are no documents proving the priority right at the time of filing the trademark application, the examination of the application form shall commence from the time the National Office of Intellectual Property receives additional documents proving the priority right or the 3-month time limit specified in Clause 4 Article 70 of this Circular expires.
Article 72. Public disclosure and publication of trademark applications
1. An application for registration of a mark shall be published immediately after its receipt as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 70 of this Circular.
2. A valid application for registration of a mark shall be published as prescribed in Article 110 of the Law on Intellectual Property and this Article.
3. The contents of a published application for registration of a mark on the Industrial Property Gazette shall include information on the applicant, the representative (if any), the application number, the date of filing, the priority date (if any); information on the mark sample and the list of goods and services bearing the mark; the international classification of goods and services; information on the origin of the application (conversion, separation, the original number of the separated application); and other information (if any).
The regulations on use of collective marks and the regulations on use of certification marks shall be published on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
Section 3. ASSESSMENT OF CONTENTS OF AN APPLICATION FOR REGISTRATION OF A MARK
Article 73. Assessment of contents of an application for registration of a mark
1. The assessment of contents of an application for registration of a mark as prescribed in Article 114 of the Law on Intellectual Property means the evaluation of the eligibility of the subject matter specified in the application for protection under the protection conditions and the determination of the corresponding scope of protection as prescribed in this Article.
2. The evaluation of the eligibility of an application for registration of a mark for grant of a protection title includes:
a) To assess the suitability of the subject matter specified in the trademark registration application to the requirements for granting the trademark registration certificate as prescribed in Article 75 of this Circular;
b) To assess the subject matter specified in the trademark registration application according to the protection conditions as prescribed in Articles 76, 77 and 78 of this Circular. The assessment shall be carried out in order of each component of the sign for each goods or service specified in the list of goods and services;
c) To assess the first-filing principle as prescribed in Article 79 of this Circular.
3. The assessment of the possibility of granting the protection title as prescribed in Point b Clause 2 of this Article shall be completed when the subject matter specified in the application has been assessed under all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy or satisfies the protection conditions, specifically:
a) Finding reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy one or some or all of the protection conditions; or

b) Finding no reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy at least one of the protection conditions.
4. Before issuing the notice of the result of the content examination with the intention of granting the trademark registration certificate as prescribed in Points a, d2, dd, g1, g2 Clause 3 Article 80 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall check the first-filing principle as prescribed in Article 79 of this Circular.
5. Based on the results of the assessment of the possibility of granting the protection title specified in Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall determine the corresponding scope of expected protection.
6. The notification of the result of the content assessment for the intention to grant the Certificate of registration of a mark according to the provisions of Points a, d2, dd, g1, g2 Clause 3 Article 80 of this Circular shall be made for the following mark registration applications:
a) Applications not falling under the cases specified in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) An application with the earliest date of filing or earliest date of priority among the applications for registration of a mark under the case specified in Clause 2 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) Application made under the agreement specified in Clause 3 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
7. Applications for registration of marks not falling under the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article shall be handled as follows:
a) The application is rejected for non-compliance with the first-filing principle, if the application has the earliest filing date or priority date for the Certificate of registration of the mark; or b) The application is considered as having the earliest filing date or priority date and is handled as the cases specified in Clause 6 of this Article, if all other applications for registration of the mark having the earlier filing date or priority date are rejected for the Certificate of registration of the mark, withdrawn or considered as withdrawn.
8. During the process of examining the contents of the application, if the application does not fully reveal the nature of the subject matter, the National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application and provide information within the scope of the nature of the subject matter stated in the application to fully reveal the nature of the subject matter and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
Article 74. Minimum information sources
1. To assess the potential for confusion of the sign specified in the trademark application, the Intellectual Property Office shall, at least, search the following minimum sources of information:
a) Trademark applications filed at the Intellectual Property Office with filing dates or priority dates earlier than or on the same date as the filing date or priority date of the application being examined and international trademark applications designated for Vietnam which the Intellectual Property Office has been notified by the International Office with filing dates or priority dates earlier than or on the same date as the filing date or priority date of the application being examined for goods or services identical or similar;
b) Trademarks which are protected or recognized for protection and are still valid in Vietnam for goods or services identical, similar or related;
c) Protected trademarks which have expired for a period not exceeding 3 years, except for cases where the trademarks have expired due to non-use as prescribed in Point d, Clause 1, Article 95 of the Intellectual Property Law, for goods or services identical or similar;
d) Geographical indications which are protected in Vietnam;
dd) Well-known trademarks which have been recognized as prescribed;
e) Denominations, symbols or other signs indicating the geographical origin of goods or services; geographical names, types of quality marks, inspection marks, warranty marks of international organizations; national flags, national emblems, national anthems of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam and of other countries and international anthems; flags, names, symbols of state agencies, political organizations, socio-political organizations, socio-political-occupational organizations, social organizations, socio-occupational organizations of Vietnam and the world; names and images of national leaders and heroes, names and images of Vietnamese and foreign celebrities, etc., which the Intellectual Property Office has collected and preserved.
2. Where necessary, the Intellectual Property Office may search for reference information outside the minimum sources of information specified in Clause 1 of this Article, such as trade names, industrial designs, names of protected plant varieties in Vietnam; names and images of characters and figures in works under the scope of copyright protection, etc.
Article 75. Assessment of the suitability of objects specified in the trademark registration application The assessment of the suitability of the objects specified in the trademark registration application and the request for issuance of the trademark registration certificate shall be carried out as follows:

1. According to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 72 of the Intellectual Property Law, a sign registered as a trademark must be a visible sign in the form of letters, numbers, words, drawings, images, including 3D shapes or combinations of these elements expressed in one or a number of specific colors or an audio sign expressed in the form of a graphic (specifically, a 5-line musical staff).
2. The following types of signs shall not be protected as trademarks:
a) Signs which are only colors without being combined with a sign of letters (the sign in the form of letters or numbers) or signs of shapes or not expressed in the form of a sign of letters or signs of shapes; or audio signs which cannot be expressed in the form of a graphic (specifically, a 5-line musical staff);
b) Signs which are objects not protected as trademarks according to the provisions of Article 73 of the Intellectual Property Law;
c) Signs contrary to social ethics, public order, harmful to national defense and security according to the provisions of Article 8 of the Intellectual Property Law.
Article 76. Assessment of the distinctiveness of signs specified in the application
1. The assessment of the distinctiveness of a sign of letters according to the provisions of Clause 2 Article 74 of the Intellectual Property Law shall be carried out as follows:
Except for the exceptional cases specified in Clause 5 of this Article, the following signs are considered incapable of distinguishing:
a) Characters of languages which are not recognized and remembered by Vietnamese consumers with normal knowledge (cannot be read, understood, or remembered) such as non-Latin characters: Arabic, Slavic, Sanskrit, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, etc.; unless such characters are accompanied by other components which make up a whole capable of distinguishing or are presented in a graphic or other special form;
b) Latin characters which consist of only one letter or only numbers or, although having two letters, cannot be read as a syllable (for Vietnamese) or are not a word for other common languages (English, French) - even when accompanied by numbers, unless such signs are presented in a graphic or other special form;
c) A set of too many letters or words which makes it impossible to recognize and remember as a sequence of too many characters not arranged in a definite order or law or a text, a passage of text;
d) Latin characters which are a word with meaning which has been widely used and common in Vietnam in the field related to the level of loss of distinguishability;
dd) A word or a set of words used in Vietnam as the common name of the concerned goods or services;
e) A word or a set of words containing descriptions of the main goods or services bearing the mark as a sign indicating time, place, method of production, type, quantity, quality, nature, composition, utility, value of goods or services;
g) A word or a set of words containing descriptions of the geographical origin of goods or services;
h) A word or a set of words that describes the legal form or business domain of the trademark owner;
i) A sign that is a letter in the cases specified in Points e, g, h, i, k, l, m, o, p Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. The assessment of the distinguishability of a sign (including a sign that is an image, drawing, shape, etc.) under Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be carried out as follows: Except for the exceptional cases specified in Clause 5 of this Article, a sign that is a shape is considered to be non-distinguishable if:
a) The sign that is a shape is a common shape such as a circle, ellipse, triangle, quadrilateral, etc. or a simple drawing; a drawing or image is only used as a background or decorative line of a product or product packaging;
b) The sign that is a shape is too complicated and confusing, making it difficult for consumers to recognize and remember the characteristics of the shape, such as too many images, lines combined or overlapped;
c) The drawing, image, symbol, emblem, common shape of goods or a part of goods, common shape of packaging or goods container has been used regularly and widely recognized;
d) The sign that is a shape describes the goods or services bearing the trademark, such as location, production method, type, quantity, quality, properties, composition, utility, value or other characteristics of the goods or services bearing the trademark, or a sign that significantly increases the value of the goods;
dd) A drawing or image describes the geographical origin of goods or services;
e) The sign is identical or substantially identical to the industrial design already protected or being protected by another person;
g) The sign is one of the cases specified in Points e, g, h, i, l, m and p, Clause 2, Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
3. The ability of a sound sign to distinguish goods or services is assessed as follows:
Except for the exceptional cases specified in Clause 5 of this Article, a sound sign is considered incapable of distinguishing goods or services if:
a) The sound is common and widely used for goods or services or in the production and business sectors related to goods or services and has become familiar to the relevant public;
b) The sound has too many melodies, tones or is too long to be recognized and remembered;
c) The sound is too short and simple, consisting of only 1 or 2 musical notes;
d) The sound contains descriptions of the main goods or services bearing the mark, such as a sign indicating time, place, production method, type, quantity, quality, characteristics, composition, use, value or other properties of the goods or services bearing the mark;
d) Sound containing descriptions or indicating the geographical origin of goods or services;
e) Sound containing descriptions of legal forms or business sectors;
4. Where a sound mark contains language, the language shall be assessed for its ability to distinguish as the language mark is assessed for its ability to distinguish as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article.
5. The following exceptions shall apply when assessing the ability to distinguish of a language mark, a figurative mark or a sound mark:
a) The mark in the cases specified in Points a, b, c, e, g Clause 1, Points a, b, d, dd Clause 2, Points d, dd Clause 3 of this Article has been and is being used as a mark and is widely recognized by consumers as having acquired the ability to distinguish for goods or services related to it before the date of filing. To apply the exceptions prescribed in this Clause, the applicant shall provide evidence of the mark’s use as a mark (the number of consumers who are aware of it, the time of use, the scope and extent of use before the date of filing, sales from the sale of goods or provision of services, etc.) and thus the mark is capable of distinguishing for goods or services related to the applicant’s goods or services. In this case, the mark must be continuously and widely used in lawful production, business, trade, advertising and marketing activities in the form as shown in the application.
b) The mark specified in Point g Clause 1, Point dd Clause 2, Point dd Clause 3 of this Article is registered as a certification mark or collective mark containing a mark indicating the geographical origin of goods or services.
c) The mark specified in Point g Clause 1, Point dd Clause 2, Point dd Clause 3 of this Article is registered as a certification mark for the geographical origin of goods or services or a collective mark.
6. The ability to distinguish of a mark combining a language mark and a figurative mark (hereinafter referred to as “combined mark”) shall be assessed as follows:
A combined sign is considered capable of distinguishing when the combined sign of the word and the figurative sign together form a distinctive whole, specifically:
a) The word and the figurative sign are both capable of distinguishing and form a distinctive whole;
b) The dominant component of the mark (the element that strongly affects the consumer's sense, causing attention and impression of the mark when hearing or seeing) is the word or the figurative sign capable of distinguishing, although the remaining components have no or little distinguishing ability;
c) In case the combined sign consists of the word and the figurative sign having no or little distinguishing ability but the unique way of combining these signs creates a distinct impression, the combined whole is still considered capable of distinguishing;
d) The combined sign consisting of the word and the figurative component having no or little distinguishing ability but the combined whole has achieved the distinguishing ability through the use process as prescribed in Clause 5 of this Article.
Article 77. Criteria for identifying geographical names and other signs indicating the geographical origin of goods and services

The identification of geographical names and other signs indicating the geographical origin of goods and services as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 and Points dd and dd1 Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be carried out according to the following criteria:
1. A sign indicating the geographical origin of goods and services is a sign used for goods and services of a locality and has the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of goods and services (indicating that goods and services originate from that locality).
Signs indicating the geographical origin of goods and services are local names or local symbols (images of typical local objects, such as symbols, maps, flags, emblems, scenic spots, special works of localities, etc.), or any other signs. Local names may be current names or historical names or official names or folk names of a geographical area currently used to replace the current name or widely known (defined according to administrative divisions or geographical methods).
2. A local name or local symbol used for ordinary goods and services (not specialty goods and services) may or may not have the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of goods and services, depending on the goods and services and the actual use of the local name or local symbol.
3. Local names or local symbols have the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of goods and services in the following cases:
a) Used for local specialties (products with a good reputation due to certain characteristics, produced in the locality);
b) Used for characteristic crops and livestock of the locality and products processed from such crops and livestock;
c) Used for products of natural raw material extraction (coal, iron, steel, aluminum, cement, stone, salt, wood, etc.) in the locality;
d) Used for products of industries developed in the locality;
dd) Used for specific services (services with a good reputation associated with certain characteristics of the locality);
e) Other cases shall be determined according to the products and the actual use of local names and signs for products.
4. Local names and signs shall not indicate the geographical origin of goods or services in the following cases:
a) They have been used as ordinary marks and widely recognized, i.e. they have acquired the meaning of indicating the source of trade (discrimination ability) and lost the meaning of indicating the geographical origin, e.g. Hanoi beer, Saigon beer;
b) The corresponding localities cannot be the places where goods are produced, e.g. Arctic cigarettes, etc. Local names and signs that do not indicate the geographical origin of goods or services may be protected as ordinary marks without the permission of local authorities.
5. Local names and signs that are common geographical knowledge known to many people (e.g. names of provinces, cities, scenic spots) used for ordinary goods and services of localities (including products that localities have business advantages but have not yet achieved reputation or quality characteristics), which are used by many business entities in localities for their goods and services with the meaning of indicating the place of production (but there is no sufficient basis to classify them into the cases specified in Clauses 3 and 4 of this Article) are signs that are not protected under the name of marks but may be used as a component part of ordinary marks of corresponding local organizations and individuals, provided that such local names are excluded from the scope of protection (not separately protected) and do not require permission from local authorities.
6. Signs indicating the geographical origin of Vietnam specified in Point dd1 Clause 1 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be understood as signs with the meaning of indicating the name of Vietnam, including “Viet” or “Viet Nam”, the corresponding English letters “Viet”, “Vietnam”, or “Viet Nam”.
Article 78. Assessment of the likelihood of confusion of signs specified in applications
1. In case the signs specified in applications are letter signs or shape signs, the assessment of the similarity to the extent of confusion of the signs to be registered with other marks shall be carried out as follows:
a) To assess whether the mark in the application is identical or confusingly similar to another mark (hereinafter referred to as the “comparing mark”), it is necessary to compare the pronunciation, meaning (for the mark in the form of a word, a combination of words and a mark in the form of a word and a mark in the form of a figure as prescribed in Points b and c of this Clause) and the goods or services bearing the mark with the goods or services bearing the comparing mark as prescribed in Clause 3 of this Article;
b) Identical marks with the mark in question: A mark is considered identical to the mark in question if it is identical to the mark in question in terms of its structure and mode of expression;
c) Marks which are considered to be confusingly similar to the mark in question if: c1) The mark is similar to the mark in question in its structure or pronunciation or meaning or form of expression to the extent that consumers may mistake the two objects for one another or that one object is a variant of the other or that the two objects have the same commercial origin; c2) The mark is merely a phonetic transcription or translation of the mark in question if the mark in question is a well-known mark.
2. In case the sign mentioned in the application is a sound sign, the assessment of the likelihood of confusion between the sign to be registered and another mark shall be carried out as follows:
a) To assess whether the signs of the application are identical or similar to the mark in a way that causes confusion, it is necessary to compare the sound elements (length, structure, components of the sound; melody, rhythm, tempo, etc.) and the linguistic elements (if any) as prescribed in Points b and c of this Clause, and at the same time compare the goods or services bearing the sign with the goods or services bearing the mark as prescribed in Clause 3 of this Article;
b) Identical marks with the mark in question: A mark is considered identical to the mark in question if it is identical to the mark in question in terms of sound elements and linguistic elements;
c) Marks that are considered similar to the mark in question to the extent of causing confusion with the mark in question if: c1) The mark is similar to the mark in question in terms of sound or/or linguistic elements (pronunciation) or/and meaning to the extent that consumers may mistake the two objects for one another or that one of the objects is a variant of the other or that the two objects have the same origin; c2) The mark is merely a pronunciation of the mark in question if the mark in question is a well-known mark.
3. The assessment of the similarity of goods and services shall be carried out as follows:
a) Two goods or two services are considered identical (of the same kind) when these goods or services have the following characteristics: a1) They have the same nature (composition, structure, etc.) and same functions and purposes; or a2) They have similar natures and same functions and purposes;
b) Two goods or two services are considered similar if they fall into one of the following cases:

b1) They are similar in nature and are put on the market through the same commercial channel (distributed in the same manner, sold together or in competition with each other, in the same type of store, to the same relevant public/related consumers, etc.);

b2) They are similar in function and purpose and are put on the market through the same commercial channel (distributed in the same manner, sold together or in competition with each other, in the same type of store, to the same relevant public/related consumers, etc.);
c) A good and a service are considered similar if they fall into one of the following cases: c1) They are related to each other in nature (the good, service or raw material, part of the good or service is made of the other good or service); or c2) They are related to each other in function (the good or service must use the other good or service to fulfill its function or they are often used together); or c3) They are closely related to each other in the mode of performance (the good or service is the result of the use or exploitation of the other good or service, etc.).
4. Signs that are considered identical or similar to the mark used for goods identical or similar in the following cases:
a) The sign is identical to the mark in question and the goods or services bearing the identical or similar sign to the goods or services bearing the mark in question;
b) Marks which are so similar to the mark in question that they are likely to cause confusion with the goods or services bearing the mark in question;
c) The sign is identical or similar to the mark in question if it is a well-known mark and the goods or services bearing the sign are not identical or similar to the goods or services bearing that mark, but the use of the sign as a mark may cause consumers to believe that there is a relationship between the goods or services bearing that sign and the owner of the well-known mark, which may actually reduce the distinctiveness of the well-known mark or damage the reputation of the well-known mark.
5. The assessment of other potential sources of confusion of the sign shall comply with Article 73 and Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following specific provisions:
a) Marks that are considered to cause confusion about the origin or source of goods or services in the following cases:

a1) Marks that are identical or similar to the names or symbols of a country or territory (national flag, national emblem, national title, country name, local name, local symbol, etc.) causing the misconception that goods or services bearing such marks originate from that country or territory, but actually originate from another country or territory;

a2) Marks that are identical or similar to protected geographical indications if the use of such marks may cause consumers to misunderstand the geographical origin of goods; marks that are identical to or contain geographical indications or are transliterated from protected geographical indications for wine or spirits, if the mark for registration as a label for wine or spirits does not originate from the geographical area bearing such geographical indication; a3) Marks identical or similar to the trade names of other persons which have been lawfully used for the same goods and services and are likely to cause consumers to believe that the goods and services bearing such marks are produced or performed by the persons whose trade names are mentioned above;

a4) Marks identical or similar to the real names, nicknames, pseudonyms or images of leaders, national heroes or famous persons of Vietnam and foreign countries; marks identical or similar to the names or images of characters or distinctive figures of well-known works, if the use of such marks is likely to cause consumers to believe that the goods and services bearing such marks are produced or performed by the owners of such works;

a5) Marks identical or not significantly different from industrial designs of other persons which have been and are being protected on the basis of industrial design registration applications filed on or before the filing date or priority date of the trademark registration application.
b) In the following cases, signs are considered likely to cause confusion or misinterpretation of the nature and value of goods or services:

b1) Signs which are words, drawings, images, symbols, sounds, etc. which cause a false impression of the functions and uses of goods or services, such as signs which are identical or similar to trademarks or other signs which are widely used to the extent that they are considered to be associated with a function or use of a certain type of goods or service, causing consumers to mistakenly believe that goods or services bearing such signs also have such functions and uses;

b2) Signs which are words, images, sounds which cause a false impression of the components and structure of goods or services, such as describing goods or services other than those bearing the sign, causing a false impression that goods or services bearing the sign are made up of or have the nature of goods or services described.
Article 79. Assessment of the first application submission
For mark registration applications that have been concluded to satisfy the protection conditions, before issuing the notice of the result of the content examination with the intention of granting the mark registration certificate according to the provisions of Points a, d2, dd, g1, g2 Clause 3 Article 80 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall conduct examination to ensure the first application submission principle specified in Clause 2 and Clause 3 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law according to the following regulations:
1. To examine the first application submission principle, it is necessary to search all mark registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property (as of the examination date) with the earliest application date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) or the same application date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) as the application being examined.
2. The search is to find cases where there are multiple applications (including the application being examined) of different persons registering marks that are identical or similar to the extent of causing confusion with each other for identical or similar goods or services, or multiple applications of the same person registering identical marks for identical goods or services; and to identify the application with the earliest application date or priority date.
3. If there are multiple applications under the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, the mark registration certificate shall only be granted to the mark in the valid application with the earliest application date or priority date among the applications that satisfy the conditions for granting the mark registration certificate.
4. Among the applications under the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, if there are multiple applications with the earliest application date or priority date, the mark registration certificate shall only be granted to the mark of one application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement is reached, all corresponding objects of those applications shall be rejected for grant of the mark registration certificate.
Article 80. Granting and rejection of mark registration certificates
1. A mark registration application shall be rejected for grant of the mark registration certificate if it falls under the cases specified in Points a, b, c, d, dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law.
2. A mark registration application shall be granted the mark registration certificate when it satisfies the provisions of Article 118 of the Intellectual Property Law.
3. For a trademark application falling under the cases specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks:
a) If the subject matter specified in the application meets the protection conditions and the application does not fall into any of the cases specified in Points b, c, d and dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of the assessment result, which includes the intention to grant the trademark registration certificate and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for the trademark registration certificate, the fee for the publication of the grant decision, and the fee for the registration of the grant decision;
b) If the subject matter of protection specified in the application does not satisfy the protection conditions or falls under the cases specified in Points b, c, d, dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property or satisfies the protection conditions but the application has deficiencies, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration, the reasons for refusal or the deficiencies of the application and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments;
c) If the applicant fails to rectify the deficiencies or provide any objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 15 working days from the expiration of the time limit specified above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the grant of the Certificate of Trademark Registration;
d) In case the applicant makes corrections or has objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 50 days from the date of receipt of the written correction or objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall assess the application as follows:

d1) If the correction does not meet the requirements or there are reasonable objections, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration;

d2) If the applicant has made corrections that meet the requirements or has reasonable objections to the whole or part of the assessment results, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment results, stating the intention to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration for the whole or part of the eligible protection and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for the Certificate of Trademark Registration, the fee for the publication of the grant decision, and the fee for the registration of the grant decision;
(d) If the subject matter of protection specified in the application contains a list of goods or services that do not satisfy the protection conditions or contains elements in the trademark form that are not separately protected (which the applicant either does not exclude or excludes but in an unreasonable manner), the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment result, stating the intention to grant the trademark registration certificate for the satisfied part, the fee for the grant of the trademark registration certificate and the fees and charges as prescribed that the applicant must pay in case of agreement with the entire result of the assessment of the application, the reasons for refusal to grant the certificate for the remaining part, the separately protected elements (if any), and at the same time set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide written comments;
e) If the applicant has no opinion or written consent and pays the fees and charges within the time limit specified in Point dd of this Clause, within 15 working days from the end of the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration;
g) In case the applicant has written comments within the time limit specified in Point g of this Clause, within 50 days from the date of receipt of the written comments, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall:

g1) If the applicant has reasonable objections to the entire assessment result, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment result, which includes the intention to grant the trademark registration certificate and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for the trademark registration certificate, the publication fee and the registration fee for the decision on granting the trademark registration certificate;

g2) If the applicant has reasonable objections but not reasonable or provides reasonable explanations for a part of the assessment result, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment result, which includes the intention to grant the trademark registration certificate for the part that meets the protection conditions, the reasons for refusal to grant the trademark registration certificate for the remaining part or the separate non-protected element (if any), and set a time limit of 02 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for the issuance of the Certificate of Trademark Registration, the fee for the publication and the fee for the registration of the decision on the issuance of the Certificate of Trademark Registration;

h) If the applicant fails to pay the fees within the time limit specified in Points a, d2, g1, g2 of this Clause, within 15 working days from the expiration of the corresponding time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the grant of the Certificate of Trademark Registration;
i) If the applicant fully and on time pays the fees and charges as prescribed in Points a, d2, dd, g1, g2 of this Clause and provides a written consent for the entire assessment result in the case specified in Point dd of this Clause, within 01 month from the date of full payment of fees and charges, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration with the corresponding protection conditions met, separate non-protected elements (if any), and refuse to grant the certificate for the non-protected elements (if any), clearly stating the reasons for refusal (if any).
4. The decision to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision. The published content includes information in the decision to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration (including the decision number and date) and the content specified in Clause 3 Article 72 of this Circular.
The collective trademark use regulations and the certificate of trademark use regulations shall be published on the website of the National Office of Industrial Property.
5. The Certificate of Trademark Registration made according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be issued in electronic and paper forms (if the applicant requests a paper form). In case the industrial property rights to the trademark are jointly owned, the Certificate of Trademark Registration shall be granted only to the first person on the list of joint applicants.
A duplicate of the Certificate of Trademark Registration made according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be issued by the National Office of Industrial Property in case the applicant requests a duplicate (stated in the trademark registration application) for other co-owners.
Section 4. HANDLING OTHER REQUESTS RELATED TO MARK REGISTRATION APPLICATIONS AND ACCEPTANCE OF FAMOUS MARKS
Article 81. Handling of requests for expedited examination of trademark registration applications
1. A request for expedited examination of a trademark registration application shall be received when the following documents are fully provided and the applicant pays the fees and charges for expedited examination:
a) Application for expedited content assessment using Form No. 14 specified in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) Documents proving that the registered mark falls under one of the cases specified in Point a Clause 2 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;
2. The Intellectual Property Office shall receive and process applications for expedited content assessment that meet the requirements specified in Clause 1 of this Article as follows:
a) If the application for expedited content assessment does not meet the requirements specified in Clause 2 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, within 15 working days from the date of receipt of the application, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the rejection of the application for expedited content assessment and the related mark application shall continue to be processed according to regulations;
b) If the application for expedited content assessment meets the requirements specified in Clause 2 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, the application for expedited content assessment shall be published at the same time as the publication of the mark application and content assessment shall comply with regulations in this Circular within the time limit specified in Clause 2a Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law, except for cases where the mark application is deemed invalid according to Article 71 of this Circular or the application falls under the cases specified in Points b and c Clause 4 Article 14a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
Article 82. Handling of objections to mark applications
1. Within 3 months from the date of publication of the mark application, any third party may object to the issuance of the mark registration certificate and must pay fees and charges. The objection must be made in Vietnamese and accompanied by documents or information sources. Documents or information sources may be in another language but must be translated into Vietnamese upon request.
2. If the objection to the mark application meets the requirements specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and handle the objection according to regulations, except for the case specified in Clause 3 of this Article.
a) Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the opposition, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of the opposition and fix a 1-month time limit from the date of the notice for the applicant to reply in writing, except for the following cases where the opposition is handled together with the examination of the application:

a1) The mark and goods/services in the application being opposed are identical to the mark and goods/services proposed by the opposing party or there is a clear basis for concluding that the opposing mark and goods/services are confusingly similar or confusingly dissimilar to the mark and goods/services proposed by the opposing party;

a2) Other cases where there is a clear basis for concluding that the opposition is unfounded;
b) After receiving the applicant's feedback within the time limit specified in Point a, Clause 2 of this Article, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant's feedback to the objector and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for the objector to reply in writing to such feedback;
c) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall organize dialogue between the objector and the applicant to clarify the issue of objection if deemed necessary or at the request of both parties;
d) Based on information, evidence, arguments provided by the parties, the results of dialogue (if any) as prescribed in Points a, b and c of this Clause and documents in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the objection and notify the result of processing the objection together with the result of assessment of the application’s contents to the objector, except for the case prescribed in Point dd of this Clause;
(d) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the opposition of the decision to withdraw the trademark application in writing within 1 month from the date of issuance of the decision to accept the withdrawal of the application.
3. In case of objections related to registration rights, the objections shall be handled as follows:
a) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objecting person to file a lawsuit with the competent court in accordance with the law on civil procedure and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of notification of the court's acceptance of the case, except for the following cases where the objection is handled during the assessment of the content:

a1) There is a clear basis to determine that the applicant does not have the right to register as prescribed in Clauses 2 and 7 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

a2) The objection to the right to file a trademark registration application for a sign which is or contains a sign which is a local geographical name or another sign indicating the origin of a local specialty of Vietnam as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

a3) The objection to the right to file a registration application without documents or source citations to prove or with documents or source citations but the documents, The source of information is insufficient to determine that the applicant has no right to register;
b) If the deadline specified in Point a of this Clause is expired and the objecting person fails to send a copy of the notice of acceptance of the case or the objection, the objection shall be deemed withdrawn and the application for registration of the mark shall be processed as if there is no objection;
c) In case the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a copy of the court's notice of acceptance of the case within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the processing of the trademark registration application to wait for the court's dispute resolution result, except for the cases specified in Point dd Clause 2 of this Article. The processing of the application shall be continued immediately after the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the court's legally effective judgment or decision.
4. The time limit for the applicant to respond to the opposition and the time limit for the opponent to respond to the applicant’s comments under Point a and b, Clause 2 of this Article shall not be counted in the time limit for the assessment of the application’s contents.
Article 83. Amendments to the application for registration of a mark
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant a certificate of registration of a mark, the applicant may take initiative or on the basis of the notification of the assessment result to amend or supplement the documents in the mark registration application.
2. The request for amendment or supplementation of the trademark registration application shall include the following documents:
a) In case of amendment or supplementation of the application made by the applicant, the amendment or supplementation request must be made using Declaration Form No. 10 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of amendment or supplementation of the application based on the notification of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam, the amendment or supplementation shall be made in the form of a written feedback clearly stating the contents of the amendment or supplementation;
c) The applicant may request amendment or supplementation of the same contents related to multiple trademark registration applications in one application or a written request for amendment or supplementation;
d) In case of amendment or supplementation related to the trademark form, the applicant must submit the amended trademark form (submit 05 trademark forms in case of submitting the application in paper form), amend the list of goods and services, and submit the amended list of goods and services together with a detailed explanation of the amendment or supplementation compared to the initial submitted document. In case the applicant actively amends or supplements without separately protecting the exclusion elements in the trademark form, the applicant must clearly state this in the amendment application;

dd) In case of amendment of the applicant's name, address, or country code, the applicant must submit a certified document (original or certified copy) or a legal document (certified copy) proving the change (decision on name or address change; business registration certificate with the recorded name or address change, etc.). The Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall take initiative to access and use information available in databases published by the database management authorities to replace the components of the application for administrative procedure settlement. In case it is impossible to access information or the accessible information is incomplete or inaccurate, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall send a written request to the individual or organization to supplement the application components and clearly state the reason. The time for requesting and supplementing the application shall not be counted towards the time limit for administrative procedure settlement;
e) In case of change of the industrial property representative, the applicant must submit a written declaration terminating the authorization for the old industrial property representative and appointing a new industrial property representative as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 8 of this Circular.
3. The applicant must pay the assessment fee for each amendment or supplementation and the information disclosure fee for the amendment or supplementation if the amendment or supplementation must be disclosed as prescribed in Point a Clause 5 of this Article, except for cases where the amendment or supplementation must be made to correct errors caused by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam's fault.
4. The amendment or supplementation of the trademark registration application shall not expand the scope (volume) of protection already indicated in the trademark form and the list of goods and services stated in the application, and shall not change the nature of the subject matter of the registration application stated in the application.
5. In case of amendment or supplementation of the application made at the applicant's initiative, the amendment or supplementation request shall be handled as follows:
a) If the request for amendment or supplementation of the application complies with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the request for amendment or supplementation of the application (except for cases where the amendment or supplementation of the application is made before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice of acceptance of the application or the refusal to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration due to the invalidity of the application in terms of form).
The amendment or supplementation shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette in cases where the request for amendment or supplementation of information related to the application is valid in terms of form, including the trademark form and the list of goods and services attached thereto;
b) If the request for amendment or supplementation of the application does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the request for amendment or supplementation of the application, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If, after the expiration of the set time limit, the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the remedy of the deficiencies does not meet the requirements, or there are no objections or objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal of the request for amendment or supplementation of the application;
c) In cases where the request for amendment or supplementation of the application relates to the trademark form, the list of goods and services bearing the trademark, or the change of information of the applicant (in case of registration of collective trademarks or certification trademarks), the collective trademark use regulations or the certification trademark use regulations submitted after the notice of intention to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration is received, the trademark registration application must be re-assessed and the applicant must pay fees as prescribed.
6. In cases where the applicant makes amendments or supplements to the application based on the assessment result notice of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the acceptance or refusal of the applicant's request for amendment or supplementation of the application shall be made by the National Office of Intellectual Property in the assessment result notice or the corresponding decision during the processing of the trademark registration application.
7. In cases where the request for amendment or supplementation of the application includes both the amendment or supplementation made by the applicant and the amendment or supplementation based on the assessment result notice, the request for amendment or supplementation must be made into a Declaration as prescribed in Point a Clause 2 of this Article. The handling of the request for amendment or supplementation in this case shall comply with the provisions of Clause 5 of this Article.
Article 84. Recording changes to the trademark registration applicant
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant the Certificate of Trademark Registration, the applicant may request the recording of the change of the applicant based on the transfer, inheritance or succession or the decision of a competent authority.
2. The application for recognition of change of applicant must comply with the following regulations:
a) The application for recognition of change of applicant shall be made using Form No. 11 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to assignment of application, the applicant must submit documents evidencing the assignment of the application (original or certified copy), which include the names and addresses of the assignor and assignee; the number of the assigned application or sufficient information to identify the application. If the document has multiple pages, each page must bear the signature of the parties or be sealed with a joint seal.

The assignee must satisfy the conditions for the person with registration rights as prescribed in Clause 6 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) In case of recognition of change of applicant due to inheritance, succession or according to the decision of a competent authority, the applicant must submit documents proving the inheritance or succession of property upon consolidation, merger, division, separation of legal entities, joint venture, association, establishment of a new legal entity of the same owner, conversion of business form or according to the decision of the Court or another competent authority, or inheritance due to the waiver of registration rights by another applicant;
d) The applicant must pay fees and charges as prescribed;
dd) The application for recognition of change due to assignment of multiple applications by the same applicant may be made in the same application form, provided that the assessment fee is paid according to the number of applications for recognition of assignment.
3. The application for recognition of change of applicant shall be handled as follows:
a) If the request for recognition of the change of applicant complies with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the change of applicant and publish it on the Industrial Property Gazette (except for cases where the request for recognition of the change of applicant is submitted before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice of rejection of the application due to formal defects);
b) If the request for recognition of the change of applicant does not comply with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to reject the recognition of the change of applicant and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the deficiencies are not satisfactory within the set time limit, or if there are no objections or the objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of rejection of the recognition of the change of applicant;
c) In case of recognition of the change due to the assignment of the trademark registration application submitted after the notice of intention to grant the trademark registration certificate, the trademark registration application shall be re-assessed and the assignment details shall be published. The applicant shall pay the application assessment fee and the publication fee as prescribed.
Article 85. Separation of trademark registration applications
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or reject the trademark registration certificate, the applicant may proactively or based on the assessment result notice separate a part of the list of goods and services in the submitted trademark registration application (hereinafter referred to as the initial application) into one or more new trademark registration applications (hereinafter referred to as the separation application).
2. For the separation application, the applicant shall submit a new trademark registration declaration for the separated list of goods and services. The separation application shall bear the number of the new application and the date of submission of the initial trademark registration application or the priority date of the initial trademark registration application (if any). The applicant shall pay the application fee and all fees and charges for procedures performed for the separation application in addition to those performed for the initial application without repetition and without paying the assessment fee for the priority right claim.
3. For the initial application, the applicant shall carry out the procedures for amendment of the application as prescribed in Article 83 of this Circular.
4. The separation application and the initial application shall be handled as follows:
a) The application for separation shall be examined for its form and further processed according to the procedures that have not been completed for the initial application. The application for separation must be published as prescribed;
b) The initial application shall be further processed according to the provisions of Article 83 of this Circular.
Article 86. Withdrawal of the trademark registration application
1. Before the Intellectual Property Office decides to grant or refuse to grant the trademark registration certificate, the applicant may declare the withdrawal of the trademark registration application.
2. The withdrawal of the trademark registration application must be carried out by the applicant himself or by his authorized representative through a written declaration. For applications submitted through representatives, the authorization document must clearly state the authorization for withdrawal or be accompanied by a letter specifying the number of the application to be withdrawn.
3. Within 2 months from the date of receipt of the declaration of withdrawal of the trademark registration application, the Intellectual Property Office shall process the withdrawal request as follows:
a) In case the withdrawal request complies with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice accepting the withdrawal request and simultaneously terminate the processing of the application. The withdrawn trademark registration application cannot be restored and can only be used as a basis for requesting priority rights;

b) In case the withdrawal request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements within the prescribed time limit, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the refusal of the withdrawal of the application within 15 working days from the expiration of the above-mentioned time limit.
Article 87. Re-examination of trademark registration applications
1. The Intellectual Property Office shall re-examine a trademark registration application in the following cases:
a) The re-examination of the trademark registration application is required due to the written opinion of a third party opposing the intention to grant the trademark registration certificate in the notice of the examination result together with credible evidence proving that due to force majeure events or objective obstacles, the applicant could not oppose the application within the time limit prescribed in Article 112a of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The re-examination of the application is required due to the request for amendment or supplementation of the application, or the recognition of changes to the applicant after the notice of the examination result, which includes the intention to grant the protection title as prescribed in Point c Clause 5 Article 83 and Point c Clause 3 Article 84 of this Circular.
2. The re-examination of a trademark registration application shall be carried out as follows:
a) The time limit for re-examination of a trademark registration application is prescribed in Clause 3 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
For complex cases with many details that need verification or expert opinion, the re-examination time limit may be extended but not exceed the time limit for the first examination as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The re-assessment shall be carried out in accordance with the corresponding provisions in Section 3 Chapter V of this Circular;
c) For each case specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the re-assessment of the trademark registration application shall be carried out only once for each third party.
3. After the re-assessment results are available, the trademark registration application shall be handled as follows:
a) For the case specified in Point a Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall continue to handle the application in accordance with the provisions of this Circular;
b) For the case specified in Point b Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall handle the application in accordance with Article 80 of this Circular.
Article 88. Recording of famous trademarks
1. The rights to a famous trademark shall be protected and belong to the owner of such trademark without registration procedures. The trademark owner may use the documents specified in Clause 2 of this Article to prove his/her ownership of the trademark and prove that the trademark meets the conditions for being considered famous.
2. The documents proving the ownership of the trademark and the fame of the trademark may include information on the scope, scale, degree, and continuity of the use of the trademark, including an explanation of the origin, history, and duration of continuous use of the trademark; the number of countries where the trademark has been registered or recognized as a famous trademark; the list of goods and services bearing the trademark; the territorial scope in which the trademark is circulated, the sales of products or provision of services; the quantity of goods and services bearing the trademark that have been produced and sold; the value of the trademark's assets, the value of the transfer or assignment of use rights, the value of the trademark's investment contribution; the investment and expenses for the trademark's advertising and marketing, including participation in national and international exhibitions; infringement cases, disputes, and court decisions or competent authority judgments; consumer survey data on the trademark's awareness through purchase, use, and advertising and marketing; the ranking and evaluation of the trademark's reputation by national and international organizations and mass media; the awards and medals that the trademark has won; the results of the assessment by an intellectual property assessment organization.
3. In case a famous mark is recognized leading to a decision on handling of the infringement of rights to that famous mark according to the provisions of Point d Clause 1 Article 129 of the Intellectual Property Law or leading to a decision on non-protection of another mark according to the provisions of Point i Clause 2 Article 74 of the Intellectual Property Law, that famous mark shall be recorded in the List of famous marks kept at the National Office of Intellectual Property for reference in establishing and protecting intellectual property rights.
Section 5. MADRID APPLICATIONS AND HANDLING OF MADRID APPLICATIONS
Article 89. Madrid applications

1. A Madrid application includes a Vietnamese-origin Madrid application and a designated Vietnam Madrid application.
2. For a Vietnamese-origin Madrid application, the applicant must submit it through the National Office of Intellectual Property and pay the fee for the international registration of a Vietnamese-origin mark.
3. A Vietnamese-origin Madrid application includes the following documents:
a) An application for international registration of a Vietnamese-origin mark made using Form No. 07 in Vietnamese in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) An MM2 application made using the form in English or French (2 copies in case of paper application);
c) The trademark form exactly as the trademark in the application filed in Vietnam (basic application) or the trademark registration certificate (basic registration) (2 trademark forms in case the application is filed in paper form);
d) Declaration MM18 in English if the application is designated to the United States (2 declarations in case the application is filed in paper form);
dd) Power of attorney in Vietnamese (if the application is filed through a representative);
e) Other relevant documents (if any).
4. The Vietnamese-origin Madrid application must satisfy the formal and content requirements as prescribed. The applicant must fully and accurately fill in the information in the declaration in accordance with the information in the basic application or basic registration.
5. For the Vietnamese-origin Madrid application, the date of international registration or the later designation date (in case of later designation) shall be considered the date of filing in Vietnam and the application shall enjoy the corresponding priority date (if any).
Article 90. Processing of Vietnamese-origin Madrid applications and related requests
1. After receiving the Vietnamese-origin Madrid application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall assess whether the application meets the requirements specified in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 89 of this Circular and carry out the following procedures:
a) In case the application is defective, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant to remedy the defect. If the applicant fails to remedy the defect within 03 months from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notice, the application shall be withdrawn;
b) In case the application is without any defects or has defects but the applicant has fixed the defects to meet the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of fees and charges that the applicant must pay directly to the International Office, sign the application and transfer the application to the International Office within 15 working days from the date of issuance of the notice on fees and charges;
c) The date on which the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam receives the Madrid application originating from Vietnam shall be considered the date of international registration of such application in case the International Office receives the application within 02 months from the date inscribed on the receipt stamp of the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam. In case the application is not completed for sending to the International Office within the aforementioned time limit, the date on which the application is received at the International Office shall be considered the date of international registration.
2. After the Vietnamese-origin Madrid application is submitted to the International Office, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant thereof and continue to process (in coordination with the applicant if necessary) the notifications, requests from the International Office or other matters related to the application (if any).
3. The following requests for implementation after a Vietnamese-originate Madrid application is granted an international registration number as specified below (expanding the protected territory), modifying the name and address of the owner of the international registration, limiting the list of goods and services, extending the validity of the international registration, designating a representative, changing a representative, recording the transfer of the international registration, etc., may be made directly with the International Office or through the National Office of Intellectual Property. In case of submission through the National Office of Intellectual Property, the applicant shall submit the following documents and pay the corresponding assessment fee for the relevant request:
a) An application form in Vietnamese Form No. 08 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) Corresponding declaration according to the form of the International Office (2 declarations in case of paper application);
c) A written authorization in Vietnamese (if the request is submitted through a representative);
d) Other relevant documents (if any).
4. Upon receipt of the requests specified in Clause 3 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) If the application is incomplete, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant to correct the deficiencies. If the applicant fails to correct the deficiencies within 3 months from the date of the notice, the application shall be deemed withdrawn;

b) If the application is complete or the deficiencies have been corrected by the applicant, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the fees payable by the applicant to the International Office, the applicant shall sign the request for confirmation, and the request shall be transferred to the International Office within 10 working days from the date of the notice on fees;
c) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the request for designation shall be considered the later designation date of the international registration in case the International Office receives such request within 2 months from the date inscribed on the receipt of the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property. If the request is not completed for submission to the International Office within the aforementioned time limit, the date on which the request is received at the International Office shall be considered the later designation date of such international registration.
5. In case the request for extension of international registration is submitted through the National Office of Intellectual Property, the applicant must submit the application no earlier than 6 months and no later than 1 month before the expiration date of the international registration. If the request for extension of the validity of the international registration is submitted during the grace period, the application must be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property no later than 1 month before the expiration of the grace period.
Article 91. Processing of Madrid applications designated for Vietnam
1. Upon receipt of the notice from the International Office of a Madrid application designated for Vietnam, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the contents of the application as for a trademark registration application submitted under the national procedure, except for the cases specified in Clauses 3, 4 and 12 of this Article. Within 12 months from the date of the notice from the International Office, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall conclude on the protectability of the trademark.
2. In case the mark satisfies the conditions for protection under the provisions of Vietnamese law, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) Before the expiration of the 12-month period specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to accept the protection of the mark registered internationally in Vietnam, record it in the national registration number of industrial property (the section on International Register of Marks) and send it to the International Office for declaration of protection;
b) Publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
The scope of protection shall be determined according to the contents of the application for international registration of the mark, which has been recorded by the International Office and accepted by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
3. In case the mark does not satisfy the conditions for protection for a part or all of the goods or services or the mark satisfies the conditions for protection but the international registration has deficiencies (such as lack of regulations on use of collective marks, certification marks, lack of photographs or drawings showing the perspective of the three-dimensional mark, unclear rights to file applications, lists of goods and services, etc.), before the expiration of the 12-month period specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a temporary refusal notice, clearly stating the contents (including a part or all of the list of goods or services refused) and the reasons for temporary refusal, and send such notice to the International Office.
4. In case the mark contains exclusion elements which the applicant has not specified in the application or has specified but are not reasonable, before the expiration of the 12-month period specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a temporary refusal notice and send such notice to the International Office.
5. Within 3 months from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property sends the temporary refusal notice specified in Clauses 3 and 4 of this Article, the applicant may correct deficiencies or object to the proposed refusal of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
The correction of deficiencies or objection to the proposed refusal shall be carried out according to the procedures for applications for mark registration filed under the national form, including the provisions on the method of filing applications.
6. For a mark temporarily refused as prescribed in Clause 3 of this Article, if the applicant for amendment to the deficiencies meets the requirements and/or has a valid objection to the temporary refusal notice after the 3-month time limit specified in Clause 5 of this Article expires, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) Issue a decision to accept the protection of the mark registered internationally in Vietnam with the scope (volume) of protection corresponding to the goods and services that meet the protection conditions; record it in the National Register of Industrial Property (International Register of Marks); and send it to the International Office for declaration of protection after the temporary refusal notice;
b) Publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
7. For a mark temporarily refused as prescribed in Clause 3 of this Article, if the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies do not meet the requirements, or has no objection or has an objection but it is not valid to the temporary refusal notice after the 3-month time limit specified in Clause 5 of this Article expires, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) If the mark does not meet the protection conditions for a part of the list of goods and services, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall apply similar procedures prescribed in Clause 6 of this Article to the goods and services that meet the protection conditions (goods and services not mentioned in the temporary refusal notice);
b) If the mark does not meet the protection conditions for the entire list of goods and services for which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to refuse to accept the protection of the mark registered internationally in Vietnam and sends a notice of total refusal to the International Office;
c) If there are grounds to determine that the mark meets the protection conditions, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall apply similar procedures prescribed in Clause 6 of this Article to the goods and services that meet the protection conditions.
8. For a mark temporarily refused as prescribed in Clause 4 of this Article, after the 3-month time limit specified in Clause 5 of this Article expires, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) In case the applicant has no objection or agrees with the contents stated in the temporary notice of rejection, the Intellectual Property Office shall apply similar procedures specified in Clause 6 of this Article with the exclusion factors stated in the temporary notice of rejection;
b) In case the applicant has a valid objection to the whole or part of the contents stated in the temporary notice of rejection, the Intellectual Property Office shall apply similar procedures specified in Clause 6 of this Article with corresponding exclusion factors;
c) In case the applicant has an invalid objection, the Intellectual Property Office shall apply similar procedures specified in Points a and b Clause 7 of this Article.
9. In case before the date of issuance of the decision to accept or reject the protection of the international mark registered in Vietnam, the international registration is not renewed or its validity is lost for the whole list of goods and services, the international registration in Vietnam shall be considered withdrawn.
10. The procedures for filing complaints and settling complaints against the decisions specified in Point a Clause 2, Clauses 6, 7 and 8 of this Article shall be carried out as for the mark registration application filed under the national form if there is a basis to believe that this decision is issued in contravention of the provisions of law on contents and issuance order. The result of complaint settlement shall be notified by the Intellectual Property Office to the applicant. In case after the result of complaint settlement, a part or the whole list of goods and services is rejected in the decision on protection acceptance or there is a change in the exclusion factors (not separately protected), the Intellectual Property Office shall send the next decision affecting the protection of the mark to the International Office using the form of the International Office on corresponding contents.
11. From the date of international mark registration accepted for protection in Vietnam, at the request of the mark owner, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a certificate of the international mark registered for protection in Vietnam, provided that the applicant pays the fees and charges as prescribed.
12. From the date the Madrid application is published by the International Office on the Official Gazette before the date of issuance of the decision on protection acceptance, or 12 months after the date the International Office notifies the application designating Vietnam, whichever is earlier, if a third party has an opinion on the Madrid application designating Vietnam, this opinion shall be considered as a reference information in the process of handling the application.
13. The contents of the publication specified in Point b Clause 2, Point b Clause 6 of this Article include the basic information stated in the corresponding decision (including the decision number and date) and other information specified in Clause 3 Article 72 of this Circular.
Article 92. Conversion of international trademark registration into national application
1. In case the owner of a trademark registered internationally in Vietnam is an individual or organization that is a member of the Madrid Protocol and the registration is invalidated under Article 6 of the Madrid Protocol, he/she may file a conversion application with the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam for protection of the same trademark for a part or all of goods and services listed in the international trademark registration that is invalidated under Article 9quinquies of the Madrid Protocol. The conversion trademark registration application shall be considered valid if it satisfies the following conditions:
a) The application is filed within 03 months from the date on which the corresponding international registration is recorded in the International Register;
b) The list of goods and services in the conversion trademark registration application has never been the subject of a decision on refusal, termination or cancellation in Vietnam;
c) The application is made according to Form No. 09 in Appendix I of this Circular (in which the list of goods and services in Vietnamese in the conversion application must be smaller than or equal to the list of goods and services invalidated in the corresponding international registration);
d) The application satisfies all other requirements regarding form of trademark registration application as prescribed by Vietnamese law;
dd) The applicant pays all fees and charges as prescribed for a national trademark registration application. In case specified in Point b Clause 2 of this Article, the applicant is not required to pay the application publication fee and the search fee for examination of the application.
The date of filing of the conversion trademark registration application shall be the date of registration of the international registration or the later designated date (in case of later designation in Vietnam). In case the international registration is entitled to priority under an international treaty, the conversion trademark registration application shall be granted the corresponding priority, unless there are grounds for rejection.
2. The National Office of Industrial Property shall assess the application for registration of a transformed trademark in accordance with the conditions for transformation specified in Clause 1 of this Article and the following principles:
a) For elements of form that have been accepted by the International Office in the corresponding international registration, the National Office of Industrial Property shall not re-assess them, except in cases where the application is defective (lacks the collective trademark use regulations, the certification trademark use regulations, lacks photographs or drawings showing the perspective of the three-dimensional trademark, etc.). The National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the trademark registration certificate in case the application fails to meet the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article;
b) For an application for registration of a transformed trademark from an international registration that has been accepted for protection in Vietnam, the National Office of Industrial Property shall not re-assess the contents. If the application meets the conditions for transformation specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall carry out the procedures for deciding to grant the protection title, record it in the national register of industrial property, and publish the decision in the Industrial Property Gazette as for an application filed under the national form;
c) For a valid application for registration of a transformed trademark that does not fall under the cases specified in Point b of this Clause, the National Office of Industrial Property shall carry out the procedures for determining that it is a valid application, publish the application, assess the contents, and carry out the subsequent procedures as for an application for registration of a trademark filed under the national form.
Chapter VI
                          PROCESSING OF APPLICATIONS FOR GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATION REGISTRATION
Section 1. APPLICATIONS FOR GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATION REGISTRATION
Article 93. Application for registration of geographical indication
An application for registration of a geographical indication shall include the following documents:
1. An application for registration of a geographical indication made using Form No. 05 in Appendix I of this Circular.
2. A description of the specific characteristics, quality and reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication and the distinctive elements of the natural conditions that give rise to the specific characteristics, quality and reputation of such product (hereinafter referred to as the description of specific characteristics).
3. A map of the geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication.
4. A form of geographical indication in case the geographical indication is not a word (5 forms in case the application is submitted in paper form).
5. Documents proving that the geographical indication is protected in the country where such geographical indication is located, if it is a foreign geographical indication.
6. Documents explaining the conditions for use and presentation of the geographical indication to ensure the possibility of distinguishing between geographical indications in case the registered geographical indication is a homonymic geographical indication.
7. A written authorization (if the application is submitted through a representative).
8. Other documents supplementing the application (if any).
Article 94. Requirements for applications for registration of geographical indications
1. Documents of an application for registration of a geographical indication must satisfy the specific requirements corresponding to the provisions in Clauses 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 of this Article and satisfy the general requirements on form as prescribed below:
a) Documents must be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented horizontally) on an A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), except for documents which are maps of geographical areas which may be presented on an A3-sized paper (420mm x 297mm), with margins of 20mm on all four sides, using Times New Roman font, with a font size of not less than 13, except for supplementary documents whose origins are not intended to be included in the application;
b) If a document consists of multiple pages, each page must bear the page number in Arabic numerals;
c) Documents must be typed or printed with a fade-resistant ink, clearly and cleanly presented, without erasure or correction. For documents that must bear signatures and seals, if the document consists of 02 pages or more, each page must be signed or sealed by the applicant/issuing authority (if any), except for electronic documents that are signed with digital signatures. Before the time of receipt of the application, if a minor spelling error is detected, the applicant may correct it and must sign (and seal, if any) at the corrected position; the signature and seal must not obscure the contents of the document;

d) Documents of an application submitted in electronic form via the electronic application submission system must be formatted as “.pdf”; the name of the electronic document must correspond to the name of the paper document as prescribed; individuals signing documents of an application for registration of a geographical indication may use digital signatures to sign directly on electronic documents or sign directly on paper documents and scan (scan) paper documents in color mode for cases of application submission via the VNeID application;
dd) Terms used in the application must be consistent and common (not using local languages, rare words, or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts, and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese national standards;
e) Documents and papers on transactions between applicants and the National Office of Intellectual Property must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents in Clauses 7 and 8 Article 93 of this Circular which may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese when requested;
g) Declarations and other documents of applications must ensure the completeness and consistency of mandatory information.
2. An application for registration of a geographical indication must ensure the consistency prescribed in Article 100 of the Law on Intellectual Property. Each application may only register one geographical indication for a product identified according to its nature, characteristics and connection to a geographical area, regardless of its form of existence or degree of processing.
3. The declaration for registration of a geographical indication must be filled in with all information as required in the appropriate places of the declaration.
4. The form of a geographical indication (for cases where the geographical indication is not a word) must be clearly presented with each form having a size of no more than 80mm x 80mm and no less than 20mm x 20mm.
5. The description of the distinctive nature must include the following contents:
a) Description of contents, including the following main information: a1) Information on geographical indications and products bearing geographical indications; a2) Specific characteristics and quality of products bearing geographical indications determined by geographical conditions - defined by sensory, qualitative and quantitative criteria on physical, chemical and biological aspects. These criteria must be determined and verifiable by technical means or experts according to sampling standards and a test method; and/or a3) The reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication determined by geographical conditions is determined through the widespread awareness of the product and the selection of the product by the relevant consumer community, which is verifiable; and a4) The geographical area; a5) The geographical conditions that create the specific characteristics, quality, and reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, including climatic, hydrological, geological, topographical, ecological, and other natural conditions; and the skill and craftsmanship of the producer, including the local traditional production process (which may include one, some, or all stages, from raw material production and processing to product creation, and may also include the product packaging stage if it affects the characteristics, quality, and reputation of the product). if such process is a factor in creating and maintaining the specific characteristics and reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, including information that is clear and detailed enough to be checked (if the above information contains confidential information or technical secrets that have not been disclosed or widely known outside the locality, the applicant may refuse to provide detailed information on such secrets if he/she is not able to commit to keeping such information confidential at his/her request); and a6) The organic relationship between the specific characteristics and reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication mentioned in points a2 and a3 of this Clause and the geographical conditions mentioned in point a5 of this Clause; a7) Information on the mechanism for self-inspection of the specific characteristics and quality of the product;
b) The summary section, which includes the summary of the characteristics, distinctive quality, reputation of the product, geographical conditions creating the characteristics, distinctive quality, reputation of the product and the organic relationship between the characteristics, distinctive quality, reputation of the product bearing geographical indications and geographical conditions.
6. The description of distinctive characteristics specified in Clause 5 of this Article must be accompanied by documents proving that the information on characteristics, quality/reputation is well-founded and authentic (test results, research results, investigation results, etc.). The characteristics and quality stated in the description must be the distinctive characteristics and quality of products of all producers in the corresponding geographical area (determined on the basis of collection, statistics, analysis, and synthesis of relevant information and data from all producers in the geographical area or by using a suitable sampling method to ensure representativeness of all producers in the geographical area). The applicant shall be held legally responsible for the authenticity of the information stated in the description of distinctive characteristics and the documents attached to the description of distinctive characteristics.

A description of distinctive characteristics is considered well-founded and authentic when the physical, chemical, and biological criteria of products bearing geographical indications are analyzed and tested at a recognized testing organization in accordance with the law on standards and technical regulations and other relevant laws, including corresponding tests.
In cases where the physical, chemical, and biological criteria of products cannot be tested at a recognized testing organization because technical conditions are not yet met (no standardized test methods are available, no recognized testing organization is available suitable for the type of criteria or specific characteristics of the product, etc.), the applicant may use analysis and test results from unrecognized organizations or other analysis methods, but must provide documents proving the sampling, analysis, and testing methods, implementation conditions, scientific and practical foundations of the methods, and the reliability of the results and other relevant documents to ensure scientific, objective, and honest nature.
7. Maps of corresponding geographical areas must fully display information sufficient to accurately identify the geographical area that meets all natural conditions creating the distinctive characteristics, quality, and reputation of the product. For Vietnamese geographical indications, the map must be certified by the People’s Committee of the province or centrally-run city where the corresponding geographical area is located and may be submitted together with documents describing the geographical area.
8. The explanation of conditions for use and presentation of geographical indications to ensure the ability to distinguish between geographical indications (in case of homonyms) must specify the presentation of information on geographical indications on products (product packaging, business means, transaction papers) in actual production and business activities of products bearing geographical indications to clearly identify the real geographical origin of the products and ensure that consumers do not confuse that the products bearing such geographical indications are of origin from other geographical areas, for example, fully stating the geographical origin as the name of the country/province/city next to the geographical indication or using other identification marks together with the geographical indication such as images, logos/symbols or other rules for labeling goods, etc.
Article 95. Receipt of applications for registration of geographical indications
1. An application for registration of a geographical indication shall be accepted when all of the following conditions are satisfied:
a) Having all the minimum documents, including: a1) The registration declaration; a2) The description of the specific nature;
b) The applicant has paid the fees and charges as prescribed.
2. In case the application fully meets the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall receive and confirm the date of filing and the application number for such application, and return the receipt of application to the applicant, which includes the date of filing, the application number, and the result of the examination of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application.
For electronic applications, upon receipt, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue the applicant a receipt of application containing information on the date of application, the application number and the result of the examination of the application dossier, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application through the electronic application system.
3. If an application does not satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 1 of this Article, it will be rejected (if the application is submitted via postal services, electronic applications submitted via the electronic application submission system, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a notice of rejection to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for rejection). For applications rejected via postal services, the Intellectual Property Office shall not return the application documents to the applicant, except for the original documents submitted for comparison.
Section 2. ASSESSMENT OF THE FORM OF APPLICATION FOR GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATION REGISTRATION
Article 96. Assessment of the form of application for geographical indication registration
1. The assessment of the form of application for geographical indication registration under Article 109 of the Law on Intellectual Property is the examination of compliance with the form requirements of the application, and the conclusion whether the application is valid or not.
2. An application for geographical indication registration shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) There is a basis to assert that the applicant does not have the right to register as prescribed in Article 88 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) The application is filed in contravention of Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
c) There is a basis to assert that the subject matter stated in the application is a subject matter not protected by the State as prescribed in Articles 8 and 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
d) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed (including cases where the application fee, the fee for publication of the application, the fee for assessment of the application and the fee for information search for assessment purposes have not been paid in full);
(d) Applications that fail to meet the formal requirements specified in Article 94 of this Circular (incomplete applications) include: failing to meet the quantity requirements of one of the mandatory documents; failing to meet the presentation requirements; failing to clearly state the products bearing geographical indications; failing to provide consistent information on applicants and organizations managing geographical indications in documents or failing to provide such information or failing to provide certified information as required; failing to provide sufficient information on applicants, organizations managing geographical indications, representatives, and/or failing to provide signatures and/or seals (if any) of applicants or representatives; etc.;
(e) Failing to provide a valid authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative).
3. Where there are grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of information in the application or where information in the application is unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to submit documents to verify or clarify such information within 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice. If the applicant fails to submit additional documents within the specified time limit, the application shall continue to be assessed based on the documents in the application.
4. The notification of assessment results shall be carried out as follows:
(a) If the application falls into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notification of assessment results, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication, the reasons for refusal or the deficiencies of the application, and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or make corrections;
(b) If the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause expires without the applicant making corrections or making unsatisfactory corrections, or without providing comments or providing comments that are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the time limit and send it to the applicant;
(c) If the application does not fall into one of the cases specified in Clause 2 of this Article (or the applicant makes satisfactory corrections or provides reasonable explanations), the application shall be deemed valid and transferred for publication and content assessment as prescribed.
5. Weekly, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall post on its website a list of applications for registration of geographical indications that have been transferred for publication, clearly stating the application number, the date of submission, and the date of transfer for publication of each application.
6. The date of filing of the application for registration of a geographical indication shall be determined as follows:
a) The date of filing of the application is the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Intellectual Property in accordance with Clause 2 Article 95 of this Circular. In case the application is filed via the electronic application system after working hours or on a public holiday or a public holiday in accordance with regulations, the date of filing of the application in accordance with this Point is the next working day;
b) For a geographical indication filed under an international request, the date of filing of the application is the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the official written request from the negotiating party of the international treaty, which includes a document describing the specific characteristics.
Article 97. Publication of applications for registration of geographical indications
1. A valid application for registration of a geographical indication shall be published in accordance with Article 110 of the Law on Intellectual Property and this Article.
2. The publication of an application for registration of a geographical indication on the Industrial Property Gazette shall include information on the applicant, the organization managing the geographical indication, the representative (if any); the application number and date of filing; information on the geographical indication, the product bearing the geographical indication, the geographical area, the summary specified in Point b Clause 5 Article 94 of this Circular and other information (if any).
3. The document describing the specific characteristics shall be published on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
Section 3. DETERMINATION OF CONTENT OF APPLICATIONS FOR REGISTRATION OF GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATIONS
Article 98. Examination of contents of applications for geographical indications
1. The assessment of the contents of a registration application for geographical indications in accordance with Article 114 of the Law on Intellectual Property is the evaluation of the protection eligibility of the subject matter specified in the application according to the protection conditions and the determination of the corresponding scope of protection in accordance with the provisions of this Article.
2. Assessment of the ability to grant protection titles of a geographical indication application includes:
a) Assess the suitability of the subject matter specified in the application for registration of geographical indications to the requirements for issuance of the certificate of registration of geographical indications as prescribed in Article 100 of this Circular;
b) To assess the subject matter specified in the application for registration of geographical indications according to the protection conditions prescribed in Article 101 of this Circular.
3. The assessment of the eligibility for grant of protection titles shall be completed when the suitability of the subject matter specified in the application is assessed and the subject matter specified in the application is assessed against all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that the subject matter is suitable or unsuitable, does not meet or meets the protection conditions, specifically:

a) Finding reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy one or some or all of the protection conditions; or b) Finding no reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy at least one of the protection conditions.
4. Based on the results of assessment of the ability to grant protection titles specified in Clauses 2 and 3 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall determine the corresponding scope of expected protection.
5. During the process of assessing the contents of the application, if the application does not fully reveal the nature of the subject matter, the National Office of Industrial Property may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application and provide information within the scope of the nature of the subject matter stated in the application to fully reveal the nature of the subject matter and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
Article 99. Minimum sources of information
The minimum sources of information in the assessment of the contents of an application for registration of a geographical indication include:
1. Trademarks currently protected in Vietnam for products identical or similar to products bearing the geographical indication, with the earliest protection date being earlier than the date of filing of the application for registration of the geographical indication, including trademarks protected under international treaties to which Vietnam is a signatory.
2. Trademarks in the trademark application with the earliest filing date or priority date in Vietnam for products identical or similar to products bearing the geographical indication.
3. Trademarks recognized by the National Office of Industrial Property as well-known signs.
4. Geographical indications currently protected in Vietnam for products identical to products bearing the geographical indication.
Article 100. Assessment of the suitability of the subject matter stated in the application for registration of a geographical indication The subject matter stated in the application for registration of a geographical indication is considered unsuitable for the type of geographical indication protection title if the subject matter is not a visible sign used to indicate the geographical origin of a product from a specific region, locality, territory or country as prescribed in Clause 22 Article 4 of the Law on Industrial Property.
Article 101. Assessment of conditions for protection of geographical indications
1. The assessment of geographical indications under the protection conditions shall comply with the following regulations:
a) The subject matter specified in the application shall satisfy the conditions prescribed in Article 79 of the Law on Intellectual Property, specifically proving the following conditions: a1) Existence of a geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication specified in the application; a2) The product originates from the geographical area specified above; a3) The product has characteristics, quality, and/or reputation determined by the geographical conditions of the geographical area specified above as prescribed in Article 82 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
b) The subject matter specified in the application does not fall under the cases specified in Article 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property. In which, the geographical indication is considered misleading to consumers about the actual geographical origin of the product bearing that geographical indication according to Clause 4 Article 80 of the Law on Intellectual Property if such indication is identical or similar to the extent that it is likely to cause confusion with a sign known as a geographical indication of goods or services, causing relevant consumers in Vietnam to have a misunderstanding about the actual origin of goods or services;
c) The assessment of conditions for protection of geographical indications under Points a and b of this Clause shall be based on information provided by the applicant and information available from the minimum information sources specified in Article 99 of this Circular;
d) Depending on the complexity of the assessment of the protection conditions specified in Point a of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property may consult the opinions of independent consultants, the advisory council on geographical indication or conduct additional surveys.
The geographical advisory council shall be established and operate on the basis of the principles specified in the decision of the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
An independent consultant shall be selected from the list of industrial property consultants compiled by the National Office of Intellectual Property and from other sources (if there is no suitable consultant on the list).
2. During the process of assessing conditions for protection of geographical indications, if a mark identical or similar to a geographical indication is found, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the mark owner for his/her comments in accordance with Article 112 or Article 112a of the Intellectual Property Law.
3. During the process of assessing conditions for protection of geographical indications, if there are grounds to determine that a geographical indication in a registration application is homophonous with a geographical indication being protected in accordance with Clause 22a Article 4 of the Intellectual Property Law or homophonous with a geographical indication in another geographical indication registration application that has been received, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the applicant and request the applicant to submit explanatory documents on conditions for use and presentation of the geographical indication to ensure the possibility of distinguishing between the geographical indications. At the same time, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the owner of the protected geographical indication and request him/her to provide information on the presentation of the geographical indication to serve as a basis for considering and assessing the possibility of distinguishing between homophonic geographical indications. If the geographical indications in two or more registration applications are determined to be homophonous with each other, the Intellectual Property Office shall request all applicants to submit explanatory documents on conditions for use and presentation of the geographical indication for consideration and decision on protection.
Where necessary, the Intellectual Property Office shall organize a meeting with all applicants and owners of homophonic geographical indications to clarify conditions for use and presentation of the geographical indications before deciding to grant protection titles.
Article 102. Granting and refusal to grant certificates of registration of geographical indications
1. An application for registration of a geographical indication shall be refused to grant a certificate of registration of the geographical indication if it falls under the cases specified in Points a, b, dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law.
2. An application for registration of a geographical indication shall be granted a certificate of registration of the geographical indication when it meets the requirements specified in Article 118 of the Intellectual Property Law.
3. For an application for registration of a geographical indication falling under the cases specified in Clause 1 or Clause 2 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall perform the following tasks:
a) If the subject matter specified in the application meets the protection conditions and the application does not fall under the cases specified in Point b, dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Intellectual Property Law, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of the assessment result, which includes the intention to grant a certificate of registration of the geographical indication and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for granting a certificate of registration of the geographical indication, the fee for publication and the fee for registration in the register to decide on the granting of the certificate of registration of the geographical indication;
b) If the subject matter specified in the application does not satisfy the protection conditions or falls under the cases specified in Points b and dd Clause 1 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property or satisfies the protection conditions but the application has deficiencies, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment result, clearly stating the intention to refuse to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indications, the reasons for refusal or deficiencies of the application and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments;
c) If the applicant fails to rectify the deficiencies or provide any objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 15 working days from the expiration of the time limit specified above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the issuance of the certificate of registration of geographical indications;
d) In case the applicant makes corrections or provides objections within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, within 50 days from the date of receipt of the written correction or objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall assess the application as follows:

d1) If the applicant has made corrections that meet the requirements or provides a plausible explanation for the content of the mediation, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of assessment result, which includes the intention to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the fee for grant of the certificate of registration of geographical indication, the fee for publication and the fee for registration of the decision on grant of the certificate of registration of geographical indication;

d2) If the applicant's corrections do not meet the requirements or provide objections but are not plausible, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication;
(d) If the deadline specified in Points a and d1 of this Clause is expired and the applicant has paid all fees and charges in full and on time as prescribed, within 01 month from the date of full payment of fees and charges as prescribed, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indications;
e) If the applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed within the time limit specified in Points a and d1 of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the issuance of the certificate of registration of geographical indications within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the time limit.
4. The decision to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication shall be published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision. The published information shall include the information in the decision to grant the certificate of registration of geographical indication (including the decision number and date) and the information specified in Clause 2 Article 97 of this Circular.
The description of the specific characteristics shall be published on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
5. Certificate of registration of geographical indications made according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall be issued in electronic and paper forms (if the applicant requests a paper form).
A copy of the certificate of registration of geographical indication made according to the form specified in Appendix I of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property in case the applicant requests a copy, shall be provided.

Section 4. HANDLING OTHER REQUESTS RELATED TO APPLICATIONS FOR REGISTRATION OF GEOGRAPHICAL INDICATIONS
Article 103. Handling objections to applications for registration of geographical indications
1. Within 3 months from the date on which the application for registration of geographical indication is published, any third party may object to the grant of the protection title to such application for registration of geographical indication and shall pay fees and charges.
The objection must be made in Vietnamese and accompanied by documents or information sources. Documents or information sources may be made in other languages but must be translated into Vietnamese upon request.
2. In case the objection to the application for registration of geographical indication complies with the provisions of Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall receive and handle the objection according to the following regulations, except for the case specified in Clause 3 of this Article:
a) Within 1 month from the date on which the objection is received, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of the objection and set a 1-month time limit from the date of notification for the applicant to respond in writing;
b) After receiving the applicant’s response within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector of the response and set a 1-month time limit from the date of notification for the objector to respond in writing to such response;
c) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall organize dialogue between the objector and the applicant to clarify the objecting issue if deemed necessary or at the request of both parties;
d) Based on information, evidence, arguments provided by the parties, the results of dialogue (if any) as prescribed in Points a, b and c of this Clause and documents in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the objection and notify the result of processing the objection together with the result of examination of the application’s contents to the objector, except for the case prescribed in Point dd of this Clause;
dd) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector of the suspension of processing of the objection regarding the application for registration of a geographical indication that has been withdrawn as prescribed in Article 116 of the Law on Intellectual Property within 01 month from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice of acceptance of the withdrawal of the application.
3. In case the objection of the objector relates to the right to register a geographical indication, the objection shall be processed as follows:
a) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the objector to file a lawsuit with the competent court as prescribed by the law on civil procedure and set a time limit of 02 months from the date on which the objector is notified of the court’s notice of acceptance of the lawsuit;
b) If the objector fails to file a copy of the court’s notice of acceptance of the lawsuit within the time limit prescribed in Point a of this Clause, the objection shall be deemed withdrawn and the application for registration of the geographical indication shall be processed as if there is no objection;
c) If the National Office of Intellectual Property receives a copy of the court’s notice of acceptance of the lawsuit within the time limit prescribed in Point a of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the processing of the application for registration of the geographical indication to wait for the court’s dispute resolution result, except for the case prescribed in Point dd of Clause 2 of this Article. The processing of the application shall be continued immediately after the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the court’s legally effective judgment or decision.
4. The time limit for the applicant to respond to the objection and the time limit for the objector to respond to the applicant’s response as prescribed in Points a and b of Clause 2 of this Article shall not be included in the time limit for examination of the application’s contents.
Article 104. Amendments to applications for registration of geographical indications
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to grant or refuse to grant a certificate of registration of a geographical indication, the applicant may take initiative or on the basis of the notification of the assessment result to amend the documents in the application for registration of the geographical indication.
2. The request for amendment of the application for registration of a geographical indication shall include the following documents:
a) In case of amendment of the application by the applicant, the request for amendment shall be made in a Declaration using Form No. 10 in Appendix I of this Circular;
b) In case of amendment of the application on the basis of the notification of the assessment result of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the amendment shall be made in a written reply clearly stating the contents of the amendment;
c) The applicant may request amendment of the same contents related to multiple applications for registration of geographical indications in a Declaration or a written request for amendment;
d) In case of amendment related to the description of distinctive characteristics, the map of the geographical area, the applicant shall submit the description of distinctive characteristics, the map of the geographical area that has been amended;

dd) In case of amendment of the applicant's address, country code, the applicant, the organization managing the geographical indication shall submit a certified document (original or certified copy) or a legal document (certified copy) proving the change (decision on change of name, address; certificate of business registration with record of change of name, address, etc.) except for cases where the National Office of Intellectual Property may take initiative to access and use information already available in databases published by the managing agencies of the databases. In case where information cannot be accessed or the accessed information is incomplete or inaccurate, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a written request to the individual or organization to supplement the dossier and clearly state the reason. The time for requesting and supplementing the dossier shall not be counted in the time limit for settlement of administrative procedures;
e) In case of change of industrial property representative, the applicant shall submit a written declaration on termination of authorization to the old industrial property representative and change of the new industrial property representative as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 8 of this Circular.
3. The applicant shall pay the assessment fee for each amendment and the fee for publication of information on amendments if the amendment must be published as prescribed in Point a Clause 5 of this Article, except for the case where the amendment must be made to remedy errors due to fault of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
4. The amendment to the application for registration of geographical indications shall not expand the scope (volume) of protection stated in the application and shall not change the nature of the geographical indication subject matter stated in the application.
5. In case of amendment to the application at the applicant’s initiative, the amendment request shall be handled as follows:
a) If the amendment request complies with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice accepting the amendment request (except for the case where the amendment is made before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the application or refuses to grant the protection title due to the application being invalid in form).
The amendment information shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette in case the amendment request complies with the form requirements of the application, including the description of the specific nature and products bearing the geographical indication;
b) If the amendment request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 2, 3 and 4 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the amendment request, clearly stating the reason and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If the time limit expires and the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the remedy fails to meet requirements, or provides no objection or provides an unreasonable objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice refusing the amendment request;
c) In case of amendment request related to the description of specific nature, the geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication is submitted after the notice of intention to grant the Certificate of registration of geographical indication, the application for registration of geographical indication shall be re-assessed and the applicant shall pay the fee as prescribed.
6. In case of amendment or supplementation of application made by applicant based on notification of assessment result of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the acceptance or rejection of the amendment or supplementation of application made by applicant shall be carried out by the National Office of Intellectual Property in the notification of assessment result or corresponding decision during the processing of the application for registration of geographical indication.
7. In case the application for amendment or supplementation includes contents of amendment or supplementation made by applicant and contents of amendment or supplementation of application based on notification of assessment result, the application for amendment or supplementation must be made into Declaration as prescribed in Point a Clause 2 of this Article. The processing of the application for amendment or supplementation in this case shall comply with Clause 5 of this Article.
Article 105. Recording of change of applicant of registration of geographical indication
1. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues decision on granting or rejecting the Certificate of registration of geographical indication, the applicant may request the recording of change of applicant based on inheritance or succession or according to decision of competent authority.
2. The request for recording of change of applicant of registration of geographical indication must satisfy the following regulations:
a) In case of recording of change of applicant based on inheritance or succession or according to decision of competent authority, the applicant must submit Declaration using Form No. 11 in Appendix I of this Circular and documents proving the inheritance or succession of property, rights and obligations arising from merger, consolidation, division, separation or other change in organization of the applicant, or according to decision of Court or other competent authority.
b) The applicant must pay fees and charges as prescribed.
3. The request for recording of change of applicant shall be processed as follows:

a) If the application for recognition of change of applicant complies with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of recognition of change of applicant and publish it on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property (except for cases where the amendment or supplementation of the application is made before the National Office of Industrial Property issues the notice of application or refuses to grant the protection title because the application is invalid in form).
b) If the application for recognition of change of applicant does not comply with the provisions of Clause 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse recognition of change of applicant and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or the deficiencies are not remedied to the satisfaction of the National Office of Industrial Property within the set time limit or fails to provide objections or the objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of refusal to recognize the change of applicant.
Article 106. Withdrawal of application for registration of geographical indications
1. Before the National Office of Industrial Property decides to grant or refuse to grant the Certificate of registration of geographical indication, the applicant may declare the withdrawal of the application for registration of geographical indication.
2. The withdrawal of the application for registration of geographical indication must be made by the applicant or his/her authorized representative through a written declaration. For applications submitted through representatives, the authorization document must clearly state the authorization for withdrawal of the application or be accompanied by a letter specifying the number of the application to be withdrawn.
3. Within 2 months from the date of receipt of the declaration of withdrawal of the application for registration of geographical indication, the National Office of Industrial Property shall process the withdrawal request as follows:
a) If the withdrawal request complies with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of withdrawal of the application and terminate the processing of the application, record the withdrawal of the application in the application dossier. The withdrawn application for registration of geographical indication cannot be restored.
b) If the withdrawal request does not comply with the provisions of Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies;
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements within the prescribed time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the rejection of the withdrawal request.
Article 107. Re-examination of applications for geographical indications
1. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall re-examine applications for geographical indications in the following cases:
a) The re-examination of an application for a geographical indication is carried out upon the written opinion of a third party opposing the intention to grant a certificate of registration of the geographical indication in the notice of the examination result together with credible evidence proving that due to force majeure events or objective obstacles, the applicant could not oppose the application within the time limit prescribed in Article 112a of the Intellectual Property Law;
b) The re-examination of an application is carried out upon the request for amendment or supplementation of the application, after the notice of the examination result in which the intention to grant a certificate of registration of the geographical indication is stated in accordance with Point c Clause 5 Article 104 of this Circular.
2. The re-examination of an application for a geographical indication shall be carried out as follows:
a) The time limit for re-examination of an application for a geographical indication is prescribed in Clause 3 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
For complex cases with many circumstances that need verification or expert opinion, the re-examination time limit may be extended but not exceed the time limit for the first examination as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 119 of the Intellectual Property Law.
b) The re-assessment shall be carried out in accordance with the corresponding provisions in Articles 98 through 101 of this Circular;
c) For each case specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the re-assessment of the application for geographical indication shall be carried out only once for each third party.
3. After the re-assessment results are available, the application for geographical indication shall be handled as follows:
a) For the case specified in Point a Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall continue to handle the application in accordance with the provisions of this Circular;
b) For the case specified in Point b Clause 1 of this Article, the Intellectual Property Office shall handle the application in accordance with Article 102 of this Circular.
Chapter VII
APPLICATIONS AND HANDLING OF APPLICATIONS CONCERNING PROTECTION AND INTERNATIONAL REGISTRATION

Article 108. Maintenance of validity of the patent/utility solution monopoly
1. An application for maintenance of validity of an invention/ utility solution patent shall include the following documents:
a) An application form No. 01 in Appendix II of this Circular;
b) A power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative);
2. An application for maintenance of validity of an invention/ utility solution patent and the fee for examination of the maintenance application, maintenance fee, fee for use of the invention/ utility solution patent, registration fee and publication fee must be paid to the National Office of Intellectual Property before the expiration date. This application may be filed after the above-mentioned time limit, but not later than 06 months after the expiration date, and the holder of the protection title must pay a fee for each month of delay as prescribed by law on fees and charges.
3. An application for maintenance of validity of an invention/ utility solution patent shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The application does not contain all the documents prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article or the documents are invalid;
b) The applicant fails to pay all fees and charges as prescribed;
c) The maintenance application is filed outside the time limit prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article;
4. The dossier of request for maintaining validity shall be handled as follows:
a) In case the application is valid, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice on the maintenance of the validity of the protection title, record it in the National Register of Industrial Property and publish it in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the notice;
b) In case the application is invalid, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse maintenance, clearly stating the reasons and setting a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections;
c) If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to satisfy the requirements within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, or if there is an objection but it is not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to maintain the validity within 1 month from the expiration date of the time limit specified above.
d) Apart from the cases specified in Point c of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse to maintain the validity in the following cases:

d1) The protection title has been invalidated or the protection period has expired according to regulations at the time of filing the request for validity maintenance;

d2) The protection title has been annulled at the time of filing the application or during the processing of the application.
5. In case the applicant submits a written request for cancellation of the validity, within 01 month from the date of receipt of the written request, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the cancellation of the validity as follows:
a) In case the written request for maintaining validity is withdrawn, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice accepting the withdrawal and terminating the handling of the request for maintaining validity. The withdrawn request for maintaining validity cannot be restored.
b) In case the written request for maintenance of validity is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the request and set a 15-day working period from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements within the prescribed time limit, the National Office of Industrial Property shall notify the refusal to withdraw the request for maintenance of validity.
Article 109. Extension of validity of industrial design exclusive rights certificates, trademark registration certificates
1. An industrial design exclusive rights certificate may be extended for a maximum of 2 consecutive times, each time for 5 years. If the protected industrial design has multiple solutions, the exclusive rights certificate may be extended for all or some of the solutions, including the basic solution.
2. A trademark registration certificate may be extended for multiple times, each time for 10 years, for the whole or part of the list of goods and services.
3. In one application, it is possible to request extension of validity for one or more industrial design exclusive rights certificates; one or more trademark registration certificates.
An application for extension of validity shall include the following documents:
a) An application form according to Form No. 02 in Appendix II of this Circular;

b) The original industrial design exclusive rights certificate, trademark registration certificate (if the protection title is issued in paper form and the extension is recorded in the protection title);
c) The authorization document (if the application is submitted through a representative). In case of extension of only some plans or part of the list of goods and services, the information on authorization of extension must be provided with corresponding scope.
4. The application for extension of validity of the Industrial Design Exclusive Right, the Certificate of Trademark Registration and the fee for examination of the application for extension of validity, the fee for extension of validity of the protection title, the fee for use of the protection title, the fee for registration and the fee for announcement of the decision on extension of validity of the protection title must be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property within 06 months as of the date of expiration of the Industrial Design Exclusive Right, the Certificate of Trademark Registration. The application for extension may be submitted after the above-mentioned time limit but not later than 06 months as of the date of expiration of the Industrial Design Exclusive Right, the Certificate of Trademark Registration, provided that the holder of the protection title must pay a fee for each month of delay as prescribed by law on fees and charges.
5. The application for extension of validity shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The application is incomplete with documents as prescribed in Clause 3 of this Article or the documents are invalid;
b) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed;
c) The application for extension of validity is submitted outside the time limit as prescribed in Clause 4 of this Article;
d) The application is missing information confirming that the holder of the protection title authorizes the extension of validity of only some plans or part of the list of goods and services (if the application is submitted through a representative).
6. The application for extension of validity shall be processed as follows:
a) In case the application is valid, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision on extension of validity, record it in the Industrial Design Exclusive Right Certificate, the Certificate of Trademark Registration (if any request is made), register it in the register and publish the decision on extension of validity in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision;
b) In case the application is invalid, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the extension of validity, clearly stating the reasons and setting a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections;
c) If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to satisfy the requirements within the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, or if there is an objection but it is not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the extension of validity within 1 month from the expiration date of the time limit specified above.
d) Apart from the cases specified in Point c of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse the extension of validity in the following cases:

d1) The protection title has been invalidated or its protection period has expired at the time of filing the application for extension of validity;

d2) The protection title has been annulled at the time of filing the application or during the processing of the application.
7. In case the applicant submits a written request to withdraw the request for extension of validity, the withdrawal of the request for extension of validity shall be handled in the same manner as the withdrawal of the request for maintenance of validity as prescribed in Clause 5 Article 108 of this Circular.
8. After the extension procedures are completed, if the owner of the industrial design exclusive right or the certificate of trademark registration requests the Intellectual Property Office to record the decision on extension of validity in the industrial design exclusive right or the certificate of trademark registration, he/she shall have to carry out the procedures for amendment of the industrial design exclusive right or the certificate of trademark registration and pay the fees and charges as prescribed.
Article 110. Amendment to protection titles
1. The holder of the protection title, the organization or individual that exercises the right to register geographical indications, and the organization managing geographical indications may request the modification of the following information on the protection title, provided that the fee for examination of the request for modification of the protection title, the fee for registration, and the fee for publication of the decision on recognition of the modification of the protection title are paid:
a) Changes to the name and address of the holder of the protection title; the name, address and nationality of the inventor, industrial design or layout designer;
b) Changes to the holder of the protection title (transfer of ownership rights due to inheritance, succession, merger, division, separation, consolidation, joint venture, alliance, establishment of a new entity of the same owner, change of business form or according to the decision of the court or another competent authority);
c) Amendments to the description, geographical indication, geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication, collective mark use regulations, certification mark use regulations. In this case, the holder of the protection title must pay additional fees for re-examination of the content.
2. The holder of the protection title may request a narrowing of the scope of protection as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 97 of the Intellectual Property Law, provided that fees for examination of the request for amendment of the protection title, fees for examination of the request for narrowing of the scope of protection, registration fees and fees for publication of the decision on amendment of the protection title shall be paid in the following cases:
a) Request for reduction of one or several independent or dependent points of the protection requirements stated in the Invention Patent or Utility Solution Patent;
b) Request for removal of one or several industrial design solutions, one or several products in the product set in the Industrial Design Patent;
c) Request for reduction of one or several goods, services or groups of goods, services in the list of goods and services stated in the Certificate of Registration of the Mark or removal of minor details that are excluded elements (not separately protected) but do not change the distinctiveness of the mark stated in the Certificate of Registration of the Mark.
3. The application for amendment specified in Clauses 1 and 2 of this Article shall include the following documents:
a) An application for amendment made according to Form No. 03 in Appendix II of this Circular, clearly stating the amendment request. One application for amendment may request amendment of multiple protection titles if they have the same amendment content, provided that the applicant must pay fees according to regulations for each protection title;
b) Original protection title in case the protection title is granted in paper form;
c) Documents certifying the change of name and address (original or certified copy); decision on name and address change; certificate of business registration stating the change of name and address; other legal documents proving the change of name and address (original or certified copy) if the amendment request is about name and address;
d) Documents proving the transfer of ownership rights according to regulations at Point b Clause 1 of this Article (original or certified copy), if the amendment request is about the change of protection title owner;
dd) Sets of photographs or drawings of the modified industrial design if the amendment request is about the industrial design protection title (submit 05 sets if the application is submitted in paper form); description of geographical indication, map of the geographical area corresponding to the modified geographical indication if the amendment request is about the geographical indication protection title (submit 02 copies if the application is submitted in paper form); regulation on use of collective mark, regulation on use of certified mark that has been amended, if the amendment request is about the collective mark or certified mark protection title (submit 02 copies if the application is submitted in paper form); mark sample, if the amendment request is about the mark sample according to regulations at Point c Clause 2 of this Article (submit 05 samples if the application is submitted in paper form);
e) Document explaining the amendment content in detail;
g) Power of attorney (if the request is submitted through a representative). For the case of amendment of protection title according to regulations at Clause 2 of this Article, information on authorization to request the narrowing of the scope of protection is required.
4. An application for amendment of protection title shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The application is incomplete with documents specified in Clause 3 of this Article or the documents are invalid;
b) The application is for a narrower scope of protection not covered by Clause 2 of this Article;
c) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed;
d) The application lacks information confirming the owner of the protected title authorizes the application for a narrower scope of protection (if the application is submitted through a representative).
5. An application for amendment of a protected title shall be handled as follows:
a) If the application is valid, within 2 months from the date of receipt of the application or from the date of expiration of the re-assessment period specified in Point d of this Clause, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to amend the protected title, record it in the protected title register, and publish the decision on amendment in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
b) If the application is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to reject the amendment request, clearly stating the reasons and setting a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections.
c) If the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause expires without the applicant remedying the deficiencies or failing to meet the requirements for remedying the deficiencies, or without providing objections or providing unfounded objections, within 1 month from the date of expiration of the time limit specified above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to reject the application for amendment of the protected title.

d) For requests to amend the provisions in Point c Clause 1 and Clause 2 of this Article, the corresponding procedures for re-assessment of applications shall be carried out in accordance with Article 114 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
dd) In case the protection title is invalidated or its protection validity expires at the time of application submission, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to reject the request for amendment of the protection title, except for cases where the request for amendment aims to record accurate information before the protection title's validity expires. In case the protection title is invalidated at the time of application submission or during the application processing, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to reject the request for amendment of the protection title;
e) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the processing of requests for amendment of protection titles in case of receiving a written request from a competent authority to suspend the processing for implementation of relevant decisions and procedures in accordance with law or documents on industrial property objects using the protection title as collateral. The request for amendment shall be continued to be processed when the National Office of Intellectual Property receives documents proving that the relevant decisions and procedures have expired.
6. In case the applicant submits a written request to withdraw the application for amendment of the protection title, within 01 month from the date of receipt of the written request, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the request for withdrawal of the application as follows:
a) In case the request for withdrawal of the application for amendment is valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice accepting the withdrawal request and terminating the processing of the request for amendment of the protection title. The withdrawn request for amendment of the protection title cannot be restored.
b) In case the request for withdrawal of the application for amendment is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to reject the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 15 working days from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies within the set time limit or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice rejecting the withdrawal request.
7. In case of detecting deficiencies in the protection title, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall, on its own or at the request of the person detecting the deficiencies, withdraw the protection title and re-issue the protection title with amended information. Within 02 months from the date of withdrawal of the original protection title by the National Office of Intellectual Property, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall re-issue the protection title with correct information and send it to the protection title holder.
The title of protection must pay the examination fee for the request for amendment of the title of protection according to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the registration fee and the information disclosure fee for amendment in case the title of protection has deficiencies that have been published if the deficiencies are due to the fault of the title holder, except for the case where the deficiencies are due to the fault of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
Article 111. Termination of the validity of the title of protection
1. Organizations and individuals requesting the termination of the validity of the title of protection according to the provisions of Clause 4 Article 95 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall pay the request fee, the examination fee for the request for termination of the validity of the title of protection, the registration fee and the information disclosure fee for the decision on termination of the validity of the title of protection.
2. The certificate of registration of a mark shall be terminated if the mark is not used by the owner or the person authorized by the owner for five consecutive years before the date of the request for termination of the validity of the mark without a valid reason according to the provisions of Point d Clause 1 Article 95 of the Law on Intellectual Property. A mark shall not be considered as used in case the act of use is only formal and does not reflect the actual commercial exploitation of goods or services bearing the mark such as the following cases:
a) The use of the mark only serves the business preparation, research or testing of goods or services bearing the mark without generating any commercial transaction or clear evidence of the actual implementation of the goods or services on the market;
b) The use of the mark only takes place within the internal operations of the applicant organization without the purpose of providing goods or services bearing the mark on the market;
c) The use of the mark is only shown on documents, reports, designs, websites (websites), advertising media or other information media without being associated with goods or services expected to bear the mark and the plan to bring goods or services to the market or have been brought to the market.
3. In one request for termination of the validity of the title of protection, only one title of protection may be requested to be terminated.
An application for termination of validity of a protection title shall include the following documents:
a) An application for termination of validity of a protection title made using Form No. 04 in Appendix II of this Circular;
b) Evidence (if any);
c) A written authorization (if the application is submitted through a representative);
d) A document explaining the reason for the application (clearly stating the number of protection titles, reasons, legal grounds, and the request for partial or total termination of validity of the protection title) and related documents.
4. Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application for termination of validity, if the application is defective in form (invalid written authorization; failure to pay the prescribed fees; lack of necessary information to determine the scope of the request, etc.), the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice and set a 1-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the defects.
If the applicant fails to submit a written remedy or submits a written remedy but fails to meet the requirements within the above-mentioned time limit, within 1 month from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice rejecting the request for termination of validity of the protection title.
5. If a third party requests termination of validity of a protection title, the application for termination of validity of the protection title shall be processed within 5 months from the date of submission of the application for termination of validity of the protection title; for complex cases or cases where the holder of the protection title has a different opinion from the requester, the processing time limit may be extended but shall not exceed 7 months from the date of receipt of the application for termination of validity of the protection title.
An application for termination of the validity of a protection title shall be handled as follows:
a) Within 01 month from the date of receipt of the application or the date on which the application for termination of the validity of the protection title is submitted, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the protection title holder in writing of the third party’s opinion, specifying a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for the protection title holder to provide his/her opinion;
b) After receiving the protection title holder’s feedback within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the feedback to the applicant for termination of the validity of the protection title and specify a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for the applicant for termination of the validity of the protection title to provide his/her written feedback;
c) If the information, documents, and evidence provided by the third party or the protection title holder are unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the third party or the protection title holder to submit additional documents and information for verification or clarification within 01 month from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues the notification;
d) Based on the information, evidence, arguments, and documents provided by the parties, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to terminate in part or in whole the validity of the protection title or notify the refusal to terminate in part or in whole the validity of the protection title;
dd) The decision to terminate the validity of the protection title shall be recorded in the national register of industrial property and published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
6. In case the protection title holder declares the waiver of industrial property rights as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 95 of the Law on Intellectual Property, within 01 month from the date of receipt of the declaration, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall handle the case as follows:
a) Issue a decision to terminate in part or in whole the validity of the protection title or notify the intention to refuse to terminate in part or in whole the validity of the protection title, specifying a time limit of 01 month for the protection title holder to provide his/her feedback;
b) If the protection title holder does not have any opinion or has an opinion but it is not reasonable after the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to terminate the protection title in part or in whole.
7. In case the applicant for termination of the protection title’s validity submits a written request for termination of the protection title’s validity, within 01 month from the date of receipt of the written request, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the request for termination of the protection title’s validity as follows:
a) If the request for termination of the protection title’s validity is valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the request for termination of the protection title’s validity and terminate the processing of the request for termination of the protection title’s validity. The request for termination of the protection title’s validity that has been withdrawn cannot be restored;
b) If the request for termination of the protection title’s validity is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse to withdraw the request and set a time limit of 15 working days from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies after the set time limit or remedies the deficiencies but fails to meet the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to withdraw the request and continue to consider the request for termination of the protection title’s validity.
8. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall terminate the processing of the entire or partial settlement of the request for termination of the protection title’s validity in case the protection title is subject to a request for termination of the protection title’s validity that has been entirely or partially invalidated according to the decision of the National Office of Intellectual Property (or according to the legally effective judgment or decision of the Court).
Article 112. Invalidation of protection titles

1. Organizations and individuals that request the invalidation of protection titles according to the provisions of Clause 4 Article 96 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall pay the request fee, the fee for assessment of the request for invalidation of the protection title, the registration fee and the fee for publication of the decision on invalidation of the protection title.
2. An invention/inventive solution patent shall be invalidated if the granted patent covers more than the scope disclosed in the initial description of the invention application as prescribed in Point dd Clause 2 Article 96 of the Intellectual Property Law in the following cases compared with the initial description and for a person of average knowledge in the corresponding technical field, the invention description contains changes in content and these changes result in the emergence of information that is not directly and clearly derived from the initial description of the application, specifically:
a) During the amendment of the application, the applicant includes in the description technical signs or technical signs that cannot be directly and clearly identified from the initial description;
b) The addition of information (including information on purpose, effectiveness, etc.) that cannot be directly and clearly identified from the initial description (including drawings) and/or the initial protection request to clearly disclose the invention or fully disclose the protection request;
c) The content added to the description is a technical sign related to the size parameter obtained by measuring the size parameter on drawings;
d) The addition of details or components not mentioned in the initial description of the application, which results in special effects and/or effects not included in the initial application;
dd) The addition of effects and/or effects (benefits) that a person of average knowledge in the corresponding technical field cannot identify from the initial application;
e) The change of technical signs of the protection request, which these changed technical signs are not disclosed or cannot be directly and clearly identified from the initial description;
g) The introduction of new contents by changing undefined contents into definite and specific contents;
h) Combining distinct technical signs of the initial application into a new technical sign while the relationship between these technical signs is not disclosed in the initial application;
i) To change one or more technical signs in the description to make the technical signs different from the technical signs stated in the initial description;
k) Removal of a technical sign from the protection request point which is necessary for the protection object to achieve the set purpose and/or the removal of this technical sign changes the technical sign or other technical signs.
3. The certificate of registration of a mark shall be invalidated if the applicant for registration of the mark has done so with bad intentions as prescribed in Point a Clause 1 Article 96 of the Intellectual Property Law if the mark falls into one of the following cases:
a) The applicant registers a large number of identical or similar marks which are difficult to distinguish from marks already used by others in Vietnam for identical or similar goods or services, where such registration exceeds the normal business capacity and there is no evidence of the actual intention to use such marks in production and business activities; or b) At the time of filing the application, the registered mark is identical or similar to the mark already recognized by relevant consumers in Vietnam as a source indicator of the goods or services of another person, or the registered mark is identical or similar to a well-known mark in another country; and such registration is intended to take advantage of the reputation of such mark for profit or mainly for resale, licensing or transfer of registration rights to persons holding the marks specified in this Clause or to prevent the entry of persons holding the marks specified in this Clause into the market to limit competition or other acts contrary to sound commercial practices.
4. In a request for invalidation, only one protection title may be requested to be invalidated. A request for invalidation of a protection title includes the following documents:
a) An application for cancellation of the validity of a protected title using Form No. 05 in Appendix II of this Circular;
b) Evidence (if any);
c) A written authorization (if the request is submitted through a representative);
d) A document explaining the reason for the request (clearly stating the number of titles, reasons, legal grounds, and the request to invalidate part or all of the validity of the protected title) and related documents;
5. Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the request for invalidation of the validity, if the request is defective in form (invalid written authorization; failure to pay the prescribed fees; lack of necessary information to determine the scope of the request, etc.), the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice and set a 1-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the defect.
If the applicant fails to submit a written remedy or submits a written remedy but fails to meet the requirements within the above-mentioned time limit, within 1 month from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice rejecting the request for invalidation of the validity of the protected title.
6. The request for invalidation of the validity of a protected title shall be processed within 12 months for a request for invalidation of the validity of an invention patent/utility solution patent and 5 months for a request for invalidation of the validity of an industrial design patent, certificate of registration of layout design, certificate of registration of trademark, certificate of registration of geographical indication from the date of submission of the request for invalidation of the validity; for complex cases, the processing time may be extended by a maximum of 3 months. The request for invalidation of the validity of a protected title shall be processed as follows:
a) Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the request or from the date on which the request for invalidation of the validity is submitted and meets the requirements, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify in writing the opinion of the third party to the protected title holder, setting a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the protected title holder to provide his/her opinion;
b) After receiving the feedback from the protected title holder within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the feedback to the requester for invalidation of the validity and set a 2-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the requester for invalidation of the validity to provide a written response to the feedback;
c) If the information, documents, and evidence provided by the third party or the protected title holder are unclear, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the third party or the protected title holder to submit additional documents and information for verification or clarification within 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice by the National Office of Intellectual Property. If necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property may organize a direct exchange of opinions between the third party and the protected title holder to clarify relevant issues;
d) Based on the information, evidence, arguments, documents provided by the parties, the results of direct exchange and appraisal, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to cancel part or all of the validity of the protection title or notify the refusal to cancel part or all of the validity of the protection title.
dd) The decision to cancel the validity of the protection title shall be recorded in the National Register of Industrial Property and published in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
7. In case the applicant for cancellation of validity submits a written request for cancellation of validity of the protection title, the handling of the request for cancellation of validity shall be similar to the handling of the request for termination of validity as prescribed in Clause 7 Article 111 of this Circular.
8. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall terminate the handling of the request for cancellation of validity of the protection title in whole or in part in case the protection title is subject to the request for cancellation of validity which has been canceled in whole or in part according to the decision of the National Office of Intellectual Property (or according to the legally effective judgment or decision of the competent court).
9. The provisions of Clauses 2 and 3 of this Article shall also apply to the processing of the application for invention registration and the application for trademark registration.
Article 113. Termination and cancellation of international registration
1. The application for termination and cancellation of international registration of a trademark shall be handled as follows:
a) For the application for termination and cancellation of international registration of a trademark under the Madrid Agreement or the Madrid Protocol submitted by a third party, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the contents of the application for termination and cancellation of international registration of the trademark to the trademark owner through the International Office, within 3 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the trademark owner to provide comments;
b) The international registration of a mark may be terminated or invalidated for a part or the whole of the list of goods or services;
c) In case the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam issues a decision to terminate or invalidate the international registration of a mark for a part or the whole of the list of goods or services and this decision is no longer subject to administrative complaints or lawsuits, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of termination or invalidation of the international registration of the mark according to the form of the International Office, clearly stating the list of goods or services subject to termination or invalidation and sending this notice to the International Office;
d) Other relevant provisions in Articles 111 and 112 of this Circular on handling of requests for termination or invalidation of mark protection titles based on the application for mark registration submitted under the national form in Articles 111 and 112 of this Circular shall apply to the handling of requests for termination or invalidation of international registration of marks.
2. An application for termination or invalidation of international registration of an industrial design shall be handled as follows:

a) For an application for termination or invalidation of international registration of an industrial design under the La Haye Convention submitted by a third party, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall notify the applicant of the request for termination or invalidation of the international registration of the industrial design to the owner of the industrial design through the International Office, setting a time limit of 03 months from the date of notification for the owner of the industrial design to provide comments;
b) The international registration of an industrial design may be invalidated for some or all of the industrial designs in that registration;
c) In case the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam issues a decision to invalidate the international registration of some or all of the industrial designs and this decision is no longer subject to administrative complaints or lawsuits, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall issue a notice of invalidation of the international registration of the industrial design according to the form of the International Office, clearly stating the industrial designs subject to invalidation and sending this notice to the International Office;
d) Other relevant provisions in Articles 111 and 112 of this Circular on handling of requests for termination or invalidation of industrial design protection titles granted based on the application for industrial design registration submitted under the national form shall apply to the request for termination or invalidation of international registration of industrial designs.
Chapter VIII
   APPLICATION FOR REGISTRATION OF CONTRACTS ON TRANSFER OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
Article 114. Application for registration of contract on transfer of industrial property rights
1. The holder of the protection title (the transferring party) or the party to which the industrial property rights are transferred has the right to submit an application for registration of contract on transfer of industrial property rights to request the National Office of Intellectual Property to record the transfer of industrial property rights, subject to payment of the application assessment fee, the search fee (in case of transfer of a mark), the fee for the grant of the mark registration certificate (in case of transfer of a part of the list of goods and services bearing the mark), the registration fee and the fee for the publication of the decision on recording the transfer of industrial property rights.
2. An application for registration of contract on transfer of industrial property rights shall include the following documents:
a) An application for registration of contract on transfer of industrial property rights, made according to Form No. 06 in Appendix II of this Circular;
b) The contract (the original or a certified copy); if the contract is made in a language other than Vietnamese, it must be accompanied by a translation of the contract into Vietnamese; if the contract has multiple pages, each page must bear the signature of the parties or be affixed with a seal;
c) The original protection title in case the protection title is granted in paper form;
d) Written consent of co-owners on the transfer of industrial property rights, if the corresponding industrial property rights are jointly owned; or the mortgagee in case the industrial property rights are used as collateral;
d) Power of attorney (if the request is submitted through a representative);
e) For applications for registration of contracts for transfer of collective marks or certification marks, in addition to the above-mentioned documents, the following documents must be enclosed: e1) Regulations on use of collective marks or regulations on use of certification marks of the transferee as prescribed in Article 105 of the Intellectual Property Law; e2) Documents proving the right of the transferee to file an application for certification marks or collective marks as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Intellectual Property Law. In this case, in addition to the fees and charges for applications for registration of contracts for transfer of industrial property rights as prescribed, the applicant must pay a fee for examination of the application.
3. Each application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights shall be granted only for one transfer. In case the industrial property subject matter is transferred in multiple steps, each transfer shall be subject to a separate application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights.
Article 115. Procedures for processing dossiers for registration of contracts on transfer of industrial property rights
1. An application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The dossier is incomplete with documents specified in Clause 2 Article 114 of this Circular or invalid documents, including: Contracts with insufficient signatures (and seals, if any) of the transferor and transferee; names and addresses of the transferor in the contract are inconsistent with corresponding information in the protection title, authorization document, declaration; names and addresses of the transferee in the contract are inconsistent with names and addresses stated in the authorization document, declaration;

a copy of the contract is not validly certified; the transfer contract lacks necessary contents or contains contents inconsistent with regulations on conditions for restriction of transfer of industrial property rights as prescribed in Articles 139 and 140 of the Law on Intellectual Property, etc.;
b) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed;
c) The transferee is not the owner of the protection title;
d) There are grounds to assert that the transfer of industrial property rights infringes upon the industrial property rights of a third party.
2. In case the application for registration of a valid contract on transfer of industrial property rights is valid, within 2 months from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks:
a) Issue a decision on recognition of the transfer of industrial property rights;
b) Record the new owner in the protection title; in case of transfer of a part of the list of goods and services bearing the protected trademark, issue a certificate of trademark registration to the transferee and determine the limit of the list of goods and services in the original protection title for the transferred part;
c) Record the transfer of industrial property rights in the National Register of Industrial Property;
d) Publish the decision on recognition of the transfer of industrial property rights in the Industrial Property Gazette within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision.
3. In case the application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights is invalid, within 2 months from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse registration of the contract, clearly stating the deficiencies of the application, and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or to submit objections to the intention to refuse registration of the contract.
4. In case the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies but fails to satisfy the requirements, or submits objections but the objections are not reasonable, within 2 months from the date of expiration of the time limit specified in Clause 3 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse registration of the contract.
5. In case, after the application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights is received, the parties have disputes related to the contract or a competent authority or organization issues a written request for the National Office of Intellectual Property to suspend the processing for the implementation of relevant decisions and procedures in accordance with law or documents on the industrial property subject matter under the protection title being used as security, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the consideration of the application until the documents proving the settlement of the dispute or the documents proving the termination of the relevant decisions and procedures are received, and then continue to consider the application in accordance with regulations.
6. In case the protection title is the subject matter of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights and the contract has been terminated, its validity has been annulled or the protection period has expired in accordance with regulations, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to refuse registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights.
7. Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a decision to record the transfer or refuse registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights, if one of the parties to the contract wishes to withdraw the application for registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights, the consent of the other party to the withdrawal of the application for registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights that has been submitted is required, except for the case of withdrawal of the application due to the inability to remedy the deficiencies as requested by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the request for withdrawal of the application for registration of the contract on transfer, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall handle the request for withdrawal of the application as follows:
a) In case the request for withdrawal of the application is valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of acceptance of the withdrawal request and terminate the processing of the request for registration of the contract on transfer. The withdrawn request for registration of the contract on transfer cannot be restored;
b) In case the request for withdrawal of the application is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the withdrawal request and set a time limit of 15 working days from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies.
If the applicant fails to remedy the deficiency or fails to meet the requirements after the prescribed time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the refusal to withdraw the application.
Chapter IX
 COMPLAINTS AND RESOLUTION OF COMPLAINTS CONCERNING INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY PROCEDURES
Article 116. Persons entitled to lodge complaints, subjects of complaints, time limits for lodging complaints and persons settling complaints
1. Persons entitled to lodge complaints are organizations and individuals as prescribed in Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property.
2. Decisions and notifications that may be complained about as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property are official decisions and notifications of the National Office of Intellectual Property or of organizations authorized by the People’s Committees of provinces and centrally affiliated cities to perform industrial property procedures in accordance with the corresponding provisions of this Circular and Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, including the following decisions and notifications:
a) Notifications of refusal to receive applications;
b) Notifications of acceptance or refusal to amend or supplement applications, convert applications, change applicants, withdraw applications;
c) Decision to refuse to grant protection titles; Decision to grant protection titles;
d) Decision to refuse to accept protection of internationally registered industrial designs; Decision to refuse to accept protection of internationally registered trademarks; Decision to accept protection of internationally registered industrial designs; Decision to accept protection of internationally registered trademarks;
dd) Notice of maintenance of validity of protection titles; Decision to refuse maintenance of validity of protection titles;
e) Decision to extend validity of protection titles; Decision to refuse extension of validity of protection titles;
g) Decision to amend protection titles; Decision to refuse amendment of protection titles;
h) Decision to terminate or cancel in whole or in part the validity of protection titles; Notice of refusal to terminate or cancel the validity of protection titles;
i) Decision and notice on handling of applications for termination or cancellation of validity of international registration of trademarks; Decision and notice on handling of applications for termination or cancellation of validity of international registration of industrial designs;
k) Decision to acknowledge transfer of industrial property rights; Decision to refuse registration of contracts on transfer of industrial property rights; Decision to acknowledge transfer of use rights of industrial property objects; Decision to refuse registration of contracts on transfer of use rights of industrial property objects;
l) Decisions, notices and other acts that meet the conditions for being subject to complaints as prescribed by the law on complaints.
Notices of information or requests for improvement of dossiers are not administrative decisions and thus are not subject to complaints. Examples include notices of assessment results, notices of deficiencies, requests for amendment or supplementation of documents, proposals for refusal, temporary notices of refusal to grant protection for registered international trademarks, and notices of refusal to grant protection for registered international industrial designs.
3. The following contents shall not be accepted during the complaint settlement process:
a) Requests for amendment or supplementation of registration applications for industrial property rights that are subject to complaints (such as requests for amendment of invention descriptions (including protection requests); requests for amendment of sets of photographs or sets of drawings and descriptions of industrial designs; requests for amendment of trademark samples and lists of goods and services bearing trademarks; requests for amendment of product names bearing geographical indications and descriptions of characteristics of products bearing geographical indications and maps of geographical areas corresponding to geographical indications for applications for registration of geographical indications), except for cases where international treaties to which Vietnam is a signatory provide otherwise;
b) Details that have existed during the assessment of industrial property registration applications but, for objective reasons, the National Office of Intellectual Property and relevant organizations and individuals can only learn of them after a decision on granting or refusing to grant protection titles has been issued (hereinafter referred to as “new details”), unless such details are presented by a third party as prescribed in Point b Clause 6 Article 119 of this Circular.
4. Complaints must be filed within the statute of limitations prescribed in Articles 9 and 33 of the Law on Complaints.
5. The first-instance competent persons to settle complaints are the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property; the second-instance competent persons to settle complaints are the Minister of Science and Technology (hereinafter referred to as “complaint-settlers”) for procedures related to industrial property rights prescribed in this Circular. For procedures related to industrial property rights prescribed in Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, the first-instance and second-instance competent persons to settle complaints shall be determined in accordance with the law on complaints.
Article 117. Complaint
1. A complaint shall be made in accordance with Clause 1 Article 3 of Decree No. 124/2020/ND-CP dated October 19, 2020 detailing a number of articles and measures for implementation of the Law on Complaints (hereinafter referred to as Decree No. 124/2020/ND-CP), which fully contains information specified in Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property and documents related to the complaint content as prescribed in Clause 2.

Article 8. Complaints:

1. A complaint is a written statement of a person’s grievance or request for protection of his/her rights and interests, which is submitted to a competent authority.

2. A complaint must contain the following information:

a) The complainant’s full name, address, contact information;

b) The subject matter of the complaint;

c) The facts of the complaint;

d) The request for protection of the complainant’s rights and interests.

3. A complaint must be submitted in writing and signed by the complainant.

4. A complaint must be submitted to the competent authority within 15 days from the date on which the complainant receives the information or documents that are relevant to the complaint.

5. A complaint must be submitted in Vietnamese.
a) Copies of the decision or notice on which the complaint is based and the copy of the application for industrial property registration that is the subject matter of the decision or notice (in case of a second complaint); or documents indicating information on the above-mentioned documents;
b) Copies of the first-instance decision on complaint settlement (for second-instance complaints);
c) Evidence (proof or material evidence) used to prove and clarify the complaint. Evidence may be submitted within 1 month from the date of filing the complaint.
2. In case the complaint is submitted through a legal representative in Vietnam as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property, the complaint must be accompanied by a written authorization or a copy of the written authorization if it falls under the case specified in Clause 5 Article 8 of this Circular.
3. A complaint shall be filed against only one decision or official notice related to industrial property procedures.
Article 118. Withdrawal of complaint and termination of complaint settlement
1. The complainant may withdraw the complaint as prescribed in Article 10 of the Law on Complaints. If the withdrawal of the complaint is authorized by the complainant, the authorization for withdrawal of the complaint must be clearly stated in the authorization document. The withdrawn complaint shall be treated as if it has not been filed. The complainant shall not be entitled to a refund of the complaint and the fees for service provision for complaint settlement that have been paid, except for the case where the complaint is withdrawn before the date of notification of the acceptance or non-acceptance of the complaint.
2. The complainant settlement officer shall issue a decision to terminate the complaint settlement in the following cases:
a) The complainant withdraws the complaint;
b) International protection titles, registration of marks, international registration of industrial designs in Vietnam have been annulled or international registration of marks has been invalidated according to relevant international treaties.
Article 119. Procedures for handling complaints
1. The acceptance of and settlement of complaints shall comply with Article 27 of the Law on Complaints and Clause 1 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property, as follows:
a) Within 10 working days from the date of receipt of the complaint, the complaint resolver shall: a1) Issue a notice of non-acceptance of the complaint if the complaint falls into one of the cases specified in Point b of this Clause, clearly stating the reason for refusal; or a2) Issue a notice of acceptance of the complaint if the complaint does not fall into the cases specified in Point b of this Clause, in which the date of acceptance of the complaint shall be recorded, and the fee for inquiry and/or assessment shall be determined for the case where re-assessment is required to serve the complaint resolution corresponding to the complaint content (if any) and set a time limit of 01 month for the complainant to pay the fee;
b) Complaints shall not be accepted for settlement if they fall into one of the following cases:

b1) The complained subjects are not official decisions or notifications specified in Clause 2 Article 116 of this Circular;

b2) The complained decisions, notifications, or acts are not directly related to the legitimate rights and interests of the complainants;

b3) The complaint is not submitted in accordance with Clause 2 Article 117 of this Circular;

b4) The complaint is submitted outside the statute of limitations, except for the cases specified in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 15 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;

b5) The complaint has already been settled by a decision;

b6) The complaint has been accepted by the court or has been settled by a court judgment or decision, except for the cases where the court has issued a decision to terminate the settlement of the administrative case; b7) The complainant continues to lodge a complaint after the 30-day time limit has expired since the date on which the decision to suspend the complaint settlement is issued in accordance with Article 10 and Clause 8 Article 11 of the Law on Complaints; b8) The complaint does not specify the illegal elements of the decision, notice, or act complained of, and only requests the amendment or supplementation of the complaint as the subject matter of the decision or notice; b9) The complaint is filed against a notice, administrative decision, or act related to an industrial property subject matter that is classified as a state secret.
c) If the complainant fails to pay the assessment fee in cases where re-assessment is required for complaint settlement as notified in the complaint acceptance and settlement notification specified in Point a2 of this Clause, the complaint shall be settled based on the documents in the dossier.

2. The time limit for handling complaints shall comply with Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property and Articles 28 and 37 of the Law on Complaints.
3. To verify the contents of the complaint, the complaint resolver shall seek opinions of persons with rights and obligations related to the accepted complaint according to Article 21 of Decree No. 124/2020/ND-CP (hereinafter referred to as “related parties”) as follows:
a) The complainant shall be notified in writing of the contents of the complaint and the complainant shall be given a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification to provide comments (if any);
b) The concerned parties have the right to provide information and evidence to prove their reasons within the time limit specified in Point a of this Clause, and the complainant resolution officer shall consider such information and evidence when resolving the complaint;
c) The complainant resolver shall notify in writing the contents of the opinion of the concerned party and set a time limit of 02 months from the date of notification for the complainant to give feedback on the opinion of the concerned party;
d) If the fixed time limit expires and one party has no opinion, the complaint will be settled on the basis of the documents in the complaint, including documents showing the opinion of the other party.
4. In the process of handling the first complaint about complex complaints, the first-time complaint resolver may take initiative or at the request of the complainant to re-assess according to the provisions of Clause 4 Article 119a of the Law on Intellectual Property. During the re-assessment, the first-time complaint resolver may consult with independent experts or advisory councils in accordance with the following regulations:
a) The first-time complaint resolver may consult independent consultants or advisory councils depending on the complexity of the content to be reassessed. The advisory council consists of a president and members. Independent consultants and advisory councils provide advice to the complaint resolver on technical and legal issues of the content to be reassessed and on the solution.
Independent consultants, chairpersons and members of the advisory council shall be persons with appropriate professional qualifications, selected from the list of industrial consultants and other sources (if there are no suitable consultants on the list).
The list of industrial property consultants shall be prepared and published on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property by the National Office of Intellectual Property under the Prime Minister's direction and in coordination with the administrative organization assigned to assist the Minister of Science and Technology in handling complaints.
b) The advisory council shall be organized and operate according to the following principles:

b1) The advisory council shall be established under the decision of the person who first resolves the complaint;

b2) The advisory council shall work in the form of meetings, collective discussions and voting according to the majority;

b3) The parties involved in the complaint, the parties with relevant rights and obligations may be invited to participate in the meeting of the advisory council to clarify the details of the case.
c) The following persons shall not participate in the advisory council and shall not act as independent consultants in the complaint cases: c1) The complainant (the person who issues the decision or notice that is complained); c2) The person who has appraised the application for registration of industrial property rights related to the decision or notice that is complained; c3) The person who has rights and interests directly related to the complaint case; c4) The person who may be unobjective in the complaint case, if there are grounds to determine that. The persons mentioned in this point shall be responsible for providing explanations, clarifications and information related to the work they have performed under the complaint.
d) The opinions of independent consultants, the opinions of the chairperson and members of the advisory council and the working results of the advisory council must be expressed in writing.
5. The first-time complaint resolver shall organize a dialogue meeting as prescribed in Article 30 of the Law on Complaints. Independent consultants or members of the advisory council on reassessment during the first-time complaint resolution process (if any) may be invited to attend the dialogue meeting.
6. The person competent to settle the complaint for the first time shall issue a decision on settlement of the complaint in accordance with the following regulations:
a) Based on the results of reviewing the decision or the notified decision, the person competent to settle the complaint for the first time shall issue a decision on settlement of the complaint, which must include the contents specified in Clause 2 Article 31 of the Law on Complaints;

b) In case the complainant is not the applicant or the person requesting the establishment of industrial property rights or the registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights is the subject of the decision or the notified decision, and new details are provided which may affect the conclusion on settlement of the complaint, the new details shall be re-assessed. Based on the results of such re-assessment, the person competent to settle the complaint for the first time shall issue a decision on settlement of the complaint in accordance with Point a of this Clause.
7. The regulations on first-time complaint settlement in this Circular shall also apply to second-time complaint settlement, except for the regulations on consulting experts in re-assessing the contents of the application specified in Clause 4 of this Article. For complex complaint cases, if necessary, the person competent to settle the complaint for the second time shall establish a consulting council in accordance with the guidance specified in Clause 4 of this Article to consult experts during the complaint settlement process.
8. In case the results of complaint settlement lead to the amendment, supplementation or cancellation of a part or the whole of an administrative decision, the National Office of Industrial Property shall restore the process of assessing the application in order to carry out the remaining procedures in accordance with the results of complaint settlement.
Article 120. Publication of decisions on complaint settlement
The decision on settlement of the complaint shall be published on the website of the complaint settlement agency within 15 working days and in the Industrial Property Gazette within 1 month from the date of issuance of the decision.
Article 121. Effect of decisions, notified decisions and decisions on settlement of complaints
1. Decisions and notifications that are complained about shall remain effective during the complaint settlement period, unless temporarily suspended under the written decision of the complaint-settling person as prescribed in Article 35 of the Law on Complaints or at the request of the Court as prescribed by law on procedures.
2. The National Office of Intellectual Property, the organization authorized by the People’s Committee of the province or centrally affiliated city to perform administrative procedures prescribed in Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall immediately implement the effective complaint settlement decisions as prescribed in Article 44 of the Law on Complaints, specifically as follows:
a) The first complaint settlement decision of the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property or the head of the organization authorized by the People’s Committee of the province or centrally affiliated city to perform administrative procedures prescribed in Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall take effect after 30 days from the date of signing if the complainant does not file a second complaint; for remote areas with difficult travel, the time limit may be extended but not more than 45 days;
b) The second complaint settlement decision of the Minister of Science and Technology or the President of the People’s Committee of the province or centrally affiliated city shall take effect after 30 days from the date of signing; for remote areas with difficult travel, the time limit may be extended but not more than 45 days.
3. The second complaint must be notified to the first complaint-settling person within 10 working days from the date of acceptance of the complaint. In this case, the first complaint settlement decision shall not take effect. The complained decision or notification shall remain effective until the second complaint settlement decision takes effect.
4. The second complainant may immediately notify the first complaint-settling person of the submission of the complaint to ensure their rights and interests.
Article 122. Measures to prevent complaints
1. The National Office of Intellectual Property, the organization authorized by the People’s Committee of the province or centrally affiliated city to perform administrative procedures prescribed in Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall inspect and re-examine issued decisions and notifications, and promptly rectify and remedy any signs of illegality to prevent complaints, unless such decisions or notifications are the subject of complaints.
2. In the course of handling complaints, the complaint-settlers shall encourage and create conditions for the disputing parties to mediate according to the law.
Chapter X
   INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY OFFICIAL GAZETTE AND NATIONAL INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REGISTRATION OFFICIAL GAZETTE
Article 123. Industrial property official gazette
1. The industrial property official gazette is an official publication published by the National Office of Intellectual Property for public information on the establishment, change, termination or cancellation of industrial property rights and other relevant information that must be published according to the Law on Intellectual Property, Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP and this Circular.

2. The industrial property official gazette is published in the form of a printed official gazette or an electronic official gazette.
3. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall:
a) Keep 01 copy of the official gazette issued each year for the printed official gazette. The retention period is permanent;
b) Database management of electronic Official Gazette includes electronic versions of published issues of the Official Gazette and information and attributes of published notices.
4. Contents of industrial property notices include information that must be published by the National Office of Intellectual Property when organizations and individuals carry out procedures related to industrial property rights specified in the Law on Intellectual Property, Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP and this Circular.
5. Geographical indications accepted for protection under international treaties shall be published by the National Office of Intellectual Property on the Official Gazette within 30 days from the date on which the relevant international treaties take effect. Necessary information related to the above-mentioned geographical indications shall be published in accordance with Clause 2 Article 11 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP, including: name of the geographical indication; name of the product bearing the geographical indication; summary of the specific characteristics of the product bearing the geographical indication; region of protection of the geographical indication and other information (if any).
Article 124. National register of industrial property, industrial property representatives
1. The National register of industrial property is an official and public database of the State, which fully reflects information on the legal status of established industrial property rights. The National register of industrial property includes the following types:
a) National register of inventions;
b) National register of utility solutions;
c) National register of industrial designs;
d) National register of semiconductor integrated circuit layout designs;
dd) National register of trademarks;
e) National register of geographical indications.
2. For industrial property objects registered under national procedures, the national register specified in Clause 1 of this Article shall include sections corresponding to each protection title, each section including:
a) Information on the protection title: number, date of grant of the protection title; name of the protected object, scope (volume) of protection, validity period; name and address of the title holder/registered person of the geographical indication, the organization managing the geographical indication, name and nationality of the author of the invention, industrial layout design, industrial design;
b) Information on the application for grant of the protection title (application number, date of filing, priority date of the application, name of the industrial property representative service organization (if any));
c) All amendments related to information on the amendment of the protection title, the validity status of the protection title (maintenance of validity, extension of validity, termination of validity, cancellation of validity); transfer of ownership, use rights of the industrial property object; number of re-grant, date of re-grant, grant of copies, number of copies (to the common owner), date of grant of copies, change of the industrial property representative service organization (if any), etc.
3. For trademarks, industrial designs accepted for protection under international registration procedures, the sections “International Trademark Registration” and “International Industrial Design Registration” in the corresponding national registers specified in Points c and dd Clause 1 of this Article shall include the following sections:
a) Information on protection status: decision number, date of issuance or date of acceptance of protection; name of protected subject, scope of protection, validity period; name and address of owner of protection title, name and nationality of industrial design author;
b) Information on application for protection title (application number, date of filing, priority date, name of industrial property representative service (if any));
4. For geographical indications protected under international treaties, the section on international geographical indications in the National Register specified at Point e, Clause 1 of this Article includes the following items:
a) Information on protection status: name of international treaty, date of entry into force of international treaty or date of acceptance of protection of geographical indication under international treaty; protected geographical indication, scope of protection; name and address of organization managing geographical indication;
b) Information on application for protection of related documents and materials (international treaty, international agreement on protection, database related to protected geographical indication (if any));
c) Any amendment related to legal status of protected geographical indication: validity status, scope of protection, transfer of management rights, etc.
5. The National Register of industrial property representatives includes sections corresponding to each industrial property representative service, specifically:
a) Information on industrial property representative service (full name, trade name, address, registration, deletion of name, amendment of information on industrial property representative service);
b) Information on the list of industrial property representatives of an organization (full names, permanent addresses, and numbers of practice certificates of each member in the list);
c) Information on changes to the list of industrial property representatives (new issuance, re-issuance, revocation of practice certificates, removal of names, etc.).
6. The National Register specified in Clauses 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 of this Article shall be made and kept in paper or electronic form by the National Office of Intellectual Property. Any person may search the electronic register (if any) or request the National Office of Intellectual Property to issue a copy or extract of the register, provided that a fee for the issuance of a copy or extract of the register is paid.
Article 125. Changes to information in the National Register of industrial property
1. The holder of a protection title, an organization or individual permitted by the State to exercise the right to register geographical indications, and the organization managing geographical indications may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to record changes to the organization providing industrial property representation services of the holder of the protection title, provided that a fee for the assessment of the request for recording, the registration fee, and the fee for the publication of the decision on recording changes to the organization providing industrial property representation services is paid as prescribed.
2. An application for changes to information on the organization providing industrial property representation services in the National Register of industrial property shall include the following documents:
a) An application made according to Form No. 03 in Appendix II of this Circular, clearly stating the request for recording changes. One application may request the recording of changes to the organization providing industrial property representation services of multiple protection titles if the same information is changed, provided that the applicant must pay fees according to regulations for each protection title;
b) A written authorization.
3. An application for change of information on industrial property representative service organization in the National Register shall be considered invalid if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The dossier is incomplete with documents specified in Clause 2 of this Article or invalid documents;
b) The applicant fails to pay the fees and charges as prescribed.
4. The application for registration of changes to information on industrial property representative service organizations in the National Register shall be processed as follows:
a) In case the application for recognition of change is valid, within 2 months from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision on recognition of the change in the organization of industrial property representative services in the National Register of Industrial Property, the Register and the publication on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property within 30 days from the date of issuance of the decision;
b) In case the application for recognition of change is invalid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse the recognition request, clearly stating the reason and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to rectify the deficiency or provide an objection;
c) If the applicant fails to rectify the deficiencies or fails to meet the requirements for rectification of the deficiencies within the prescribed time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, or if there is an objection but it is not reasonable, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to refuse to record the change in the industrial property representative service organization within 2 months from the date of expiration of the time limit specified above.
d) Apart from the case specified in Point c of this Clause, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to refuse to record the change of industrial property representative service organization in the following cases:

d1) The protection title has been invalidated or its protection period has expired at the time of filing the application;

d2) The protection title has been annulled at the time of filing the application or during the processing of the application.
5. In case the applicant requests withdrawal of the application for registration of changes to information on industrial property representation service organization in the National Register, the withdrawal of the application shall be handled in the same manner as the withdrawal of the application for amendment to the protection title as prescribed in Clause 6 Article 110 of this Circular.
Chapter XI
  INSTRUCTION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY LAW AND INSTRUCTION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY VALUATION

Article 126. Program for instruction of industrial property law
1. Duration and time of instruction: at least 400 hours of instruction on knowledge of law and skills in industrial property (excluding hours of instruction on basic knowledge of law for students without bachelor’s or master’s degree in law) and not exceeding 12 months.
2. Contents of the instruction program and guidance on implementation are provided in Appendix III of this Circular.
Article 127. Recognition of institutions for instruction of industrial property law The recognition of institutions for instruction of industrial property law shall be carried out as follows:
1. Institutions for instruction of industrial property law that wish to be recognized as eligible to organize courses on industrial property law as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 62 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall submit an application for recognition to the National Office of Intellectual Property.
2. An application for recognition as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article shall include the following documents:
a) A written request for recognition of a training institution for industrial property law made according to Form No. 01 in Appendix III of this Circular;
b) Documents proving that the organization meets the criteria and conditions for recognition (conditions on facilities and equipment; information on employees on the payroll who have been engaged in teaching at the university level in intellectual property for 5 years or more; list of information on instructors participating in the training program; a set of approved materials for use, consistent with the contents specified in the structure of the training program for industrial property law).
3. The Intellectual Property Office shall process an application for recognition of a training institution for industrial property law within 15 working days from the date of receipt in the following order:
a) In case the application is valid, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a decision on recognition of the training institution for industrial property law, record it in the List of institutions qualified to provide training in industrial property law, and publish it on the website of the Intellectual Property Office within 7 working days from the date of issuance of the decision;
b) In case the application is invalid, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the deficiencies of the application and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies;
c) In case the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies do not meet the requirements, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a decision on refusal to recognize the training institution for industrial property law, clearly stating the reasons for refusal.
4. The list of recognized training institutions as prescribed in this Article shall be posted and updated on the website of the Intellectual Property Office.
Article 128. Removal of names of institutions providing legal training on industrial property The removal of names of institutions providing legal training on industrial property from the List of institutions qualified to provide legal training on industrial property shall be carried out as follows:
1. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall remove names of institutions providing legal training on industrial property from the List of institutions qualified to provide legal training on industrial property in the following cases:
a) Institutions providing legal training on industrial property are suspended, terminated, or cease to exist as legal entities;
b) Institutions providing legal training on industrial property fail to meet the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 2 Article 62 of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;
c) Institutions providing legal training on industrial property fail to organize training in accordance with the legal training program on industrial property specified in Article 126 of this Circular.
2. In cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to remove the names of institutions from the List of institutions providing legal training on industrial property and publish information on the removal of the names of institutions on the National Office of Intellectual Property’s website within 7 working days from the date of issuance of the decision.
Article 129. Programs for professional training in industrial property assessment
1. Duration and time of training: At least 400 hours of lectures on legal knowledge, professional skills, and professional methods of industrial property assessment, and not exceeding 12 months.
2. Contents of the training program and guidance on implementation in Appendix III of this Circular.
Article 130. Recognition of industrial property assessment training establishments The recognition of industrial property assessment training establishments shall be carried out as follows:
1. Training establishments that wish to be recognized and fully meet the requirements for organizing industrial property assessment training courses as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 108a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP shall submit recognition applications to the National Office of Intellectual Property.
2. The recognition application as prescribed in Clause 1 of this Article shall include the following documents:
a) A written request for recognition of the industrial property assessment training establishment made according to Form No. 02 in Appendix III of this Circular;
b) Documents proving that the establishment meets the recognition criteria and requirements (requirements for physical facilities and equipment; information on employees on the payroll who have participated in industrial property assessment; list of information on instructors participating in the training program; approved set of standard documents for use, consistent with the contents specified in the structure of the industrial property assessment training program).
3. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the recognition application of the industrial property assessment training establishment within 15 working days from the date of receipt in the following order:
a) In case the application is valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision recognizing the industrial property assessment training establishment, record it in the List of establishments eligible for industrial property assessment training, and publish it on the website of the National Office of Intellectual Property within 07 working days from the date of issuance of the decision;
b) In case the application is invalid, the National Office of Industrial Property shall notify the deficiencies of the application and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of notification for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies;
c) In case the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies to the unsatisfactory level, within 15 working days from the date of expiration of the time limit specified in Point b of this Clause, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to refuse recognition of the industrial property assessment training establishment, clearly stating the reason for refusal.
4. The list of recognized training establishments under this Article shall be posted and updated on the website of the National Office of Industrial Property.
Article 131. Deletion of the name of industrial property assessment training establishments The deletion of the name of industrial property assessment training establishments from the list of qualified training establishments for industrial property assessment shall be carried out as follows:
1. The National Office of Industrial Property shall delete the name of the training establishment that no longer meets the conditions for industrial property assessment training in the following cases:
a) The training establishment is suspended, terminated, or its legal entity status is terminated;
b) The training establishment no longer meets the criteria and conditions specified in Clause 2 Article 108a of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP;
c) The training establishment fails to organize the training in accordance with the industrial property assessment training program under Article 129 of this Circular.
2. In cases specified in Clause 1 of this Article, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to remove the name from the list of industrial property assessment training institutions and publish information on the removal of the training institution’s name on the National Office of Industrial Property’s website within 7 working days from the date of issuance of the decision.
CHAPTER XII
                    INFORMATION ON INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY
Article 132. Measures to ensure information on industrial property rights The National Office of Industrial Property shall take the following measures to ensure information on industrial property rights for the purpose of industrial property information exploitation and industrial property rights exploitation:
1. Maintain an information technology infrastructure system to serve the activities of ensuring information on industrial property rights.
2. Develop and manage industrial property databases and the system for managing geographical indications in accordance with Article 9b and Article 9c of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
3. Carry out activities to disseminate information on industrial property rights, including providing information on industrial property rights according to granted titles, disseminating laws on the scope of rights of owners of protection titles, and providing training and refresher courses on knowledge and skills necessary for organizations and individuals to establish, exploit, and manage industrial property rights and respect industrial property rights.
Article 133. Development and management of databases on intellectual property transaction prices and databases on intellectual property rights protection
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall:

1. Develop, manage, maintain, and develop the database on intellectual property transaction prices in accordance with Article 9d of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
2. Develop and manage the database on intellectual property protection in accordance with Article 9e of Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP.
Article 134. Access to and use of information in the database on industrial property All organizations and individuals have the right to access and use information in the database on industrial property in two forms:
1. Self-searching and searching for information in databases made public by the National Office of Intellectual Property at information search facilities accessible to the public or published on the Internet.
2. Using information search and analysis services or information and material provision services provided by the National Office of Intellectual Property, provided that fees are paid as prescribed.
Article 135. Information search and analysis services and information and material provision services The National Office of Intellectual Property shall provide information search and analysis services and information and material provision services to organizations and individuals as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 134 of this Circular.
Article 136. Ensuring industrial property information in localities
1. Depending on their conditions and capabilities, the local industrial property authorities may set up and manage industrial property databases to ensure industrial property information for research, application, production and business development and protection of industrial property rights in the locality.
2. The industrial property authorities of the provinces and centrally-run cities shall have to and are entitled to carry out the activities of ensuring industrial property information in accordance with this Circular.
3. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall have to and is entitled to make statistics of information in the industrial property database to serve the industrial property management of the provinces and centrally-run cities on a periodic basis (at least every 6 months).
Article 137. Grant of copies of documents and certified applications for the first time to enjoy priority rights
1. All relevant organizations and individuals have the right to request the National Office of Intellectual Property to grant copies of documents issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property or copies from the master books made by the National Office of Intellectual Property or copies of documents made by themselves and submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property in the course of establishing industrial property rights. The applicant for the copy must pay the cost of copying the document.
2. The applicant has the right to request the National Office of Intellectual Property to provide a copy (certified) of the industrial property rights registration application submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property to request the priority rights (including applications rejected for acceptance of valid applications or applications withdrawn by the applicant) provided that the applicant must pay the cost of copying the document.
3. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall provide services for the copy and certification of applications to organizations and individuals as prescribed in Clause 2 of this Article.
Chapter XIII
                                    IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS
Article 138. Implementation provisions
1. This Circular takes effect on April 1, 2026.
2. Circular No. 23/2023/TT-BKHCN dated November 30, 2023 of the Minister of Science and Technology detailing a number of articles of the Law on Intellectual Property and measures to implement the Government’s Decree No. 65/2023/ND-CP dated August 23, 2023 detailing a number of articles and measures to implement the Law on Intellectual Property on industrial property, protection of industrial property rights, rights to plant varieties and state management of intellectual property related to procedures for establishing industrial property rights and ensuring industrial property information shall cease to be effective from the effective date of this Circular.
Article 139. Transitional provisions
Applications in procedures related to industrial property rights and complaint applications submitted before the effective date of this Circular shall be handled according to the law in force at the time of application submission, except for the following cases:
1. The provisions in Clause 4 Article 14, Clause 4 Article 41, Clause 4 Article 48, Clause 3 Article 71 and Clause 4 Article 96 of this Circular on notification of the outcome of formal examination shall apply to applications for registration of inventions, industrial designs, industrial forms, trademarks and geographical indications for which the application is not accepted.

2. The provisions in Article 76 and Article 78 of this Circular shall apply to applications for registration of sound marks for which the outcome of content examination has not been notified./.
