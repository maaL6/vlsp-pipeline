                                                  CIRCULAR

  AMENDING AND SUPPLEMENTING A NUMBER OF ARTICLES OF CIRCULAR NO. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN DATED FEBRUARY 14, 2007 ON GUIDANCE FOR IMPLEMENTATION OF DECREE NO. 103/2006/ND-CP DETAILING A NUMBER OF ARTICLES OF THE INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY LAW ON INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY, AMENDED AND SUPPLEMENTED ACCORDING TO CIRCULAR NO. 13/2010/TT-BKHCN DATED JULY 30, 2010 AND CIRCULAR NO. 18/2011/TT-BKHCN DATED JULY 22, 2011

  Pursuant to the Government’s Decree No. 28/2008/ND-CP of March 14, 2008, defining the functions, tasks, powers and organizational structure of the Ministry of Science and Technology;

  Pursuant to the Law on Intellectual Property of November 29, 2005 and the Law No. 36/2009/QH12 of June 19, 2009, amending and supplementing a number of articles of the Law on Intellectual Property of November 29, 2005 (hereinafter referred to as the “Law on Intellectual Property”);

  Pursuant to the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP of September 22, 2006, detailing and guiding the implementation of a number of articles of the Law on Intellectual Property on industrial property and the Government’s Decree No. 122/2010/ND-CP of December 31, 2010, amending and supplementing a number of articles of the Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP;

  The Minister of Science and Technology amends and supplements a number of provisions of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN of February 14, 2007, guiding the implementation of the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP, detailing a number of articles of the Law on Intellectual Property on industrial property, as amended by the Circular No. 13/2010/TT-BKHCN of July 30, 2010 and the Circular No. 18/2011/TT-BKHCN of July 22, 2011 (hereinafter referred to as the “Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN”) as follows:

  Article 1. Amendments to the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN

  1. Point 1.1 of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN is amended as follows:

  “1.1 Industrial property rights arising or established on the basis of grounds specified in Clause 3 Article 6 of the Intellectual Property Law, Clauses 1, 2, 3 and 4 Article 6 of the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP of September 22, 2006, detailing and guiding the implementation of a number of articles of the Intellectual Property Law on industrial property, amended and supplemented under Decree No. 122/2010/ND-CP and under specific provisions of this Point.”

  2. To amend Point 7.1.b (iii) and add Point 7.1.b (iv) of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “(iii) Maps of geographical areas (if the registered mark is a geographical origin certification mark, collective mark or certification mark containing a geographical name or other sign indicating the geographical origin of local specialties);

  (iv) Written permission of the People’s Committee of the province or centrally-run city for the registration of the mark as prescribed in Point 37.7.a of this Circular (if the registered mark is a collective mark or certification mark containing a geographical name or other sign indicating the geographical origin of local specialties).”

  3. To amend and supplement Point 13.8 of Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  13.8. Time limit for assessment of the form of the application

  a) The time limit for examination of the form of the application is 01 month from the date of filing the application as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 119 of the Law on Intellectual Property.

  b) In case the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice under Point 13.6.a of this Circular, the time for the applicant to respond to the notice shall not be counted into the time limit for form assessment. This time limit is understood as:

  (i) The time from the date of issuance of the notice to the date on which the applicant responds to the notice; or (ii) The fixed time limit in the notice (including the extended time limit as prescribed), in case the applicant fails to respond to the notice.

  c) In case the applicant actively requests amendment or supplementation of the application, or responds to the notice of the National Office of Intellectual Property specified in point 13.6.a of this Circular, the time limit for formal assessment shall be extended by 10 days according to clause 4 Article 119 of the Law on Intellectual Property.

  d) Before the expiration of the time limit specified in Points 13.8.a, 13.8.b or 13.8.c above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall have to finish the assessment of the application form and notify the applicant of the result in accordance with Point 13.6 or Point 13.7 of this Circular.”

  4. To amend Point 15.6.d, and add Points 15.6.d and 15.6.e to Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “d) Before issuing the notice of intention to grant protection titles as prescribed in Point 15.7.a (iii) of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall check the first application submission as prescribed in Points 25.7, 35.9 and 39.10 of this Circular.

  dd) The notice prescribed in Point 15.7.a (iii) of this Circular shall be issued for the following applications:

  (i) Applications not falling under the cases prescribed in Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

  (ii) Applications with earliest filing dates or priority dates among the patent registration applications under the cases prescribed in Clause 1 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

  (iii) Applications with earliest filing dates or priority dates among the industrial design registration applications under the cases prescribed in Clause 1 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

  (iv) Applications with earliest filing dates or priority dates among the trademark registration applications under the cases prescribed in Clause 2 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property;

  (v) Applications under the agreement prescribed in Clause 3 Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property.

  e) Applications not falling under the cases prescribed in Point 15.6.d of this Circular shall be handled as follows:

  (i) The application is rejected for failure to satisfy the first-file principle, if the application has the earliest filing date or priority date for grant of the protection title; or (ii) The application is considered as having the earliest filing date or priority date and is handled according to the provisions in Point 15.6.d above, if all applications with earlier filing dates or priority dates are rejected for grant of the protection title or withdrawn, or are considered withdrawn.”

  5. To amend and supplement Point 15.7.a (iii) of Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “(iii) If the subject matter specified in the application meets the protection conditions or the applicant has corrected deficiencies to meet requirements or has reasonable explanations within the time limit specified in Points 15.7.a (i) and (ii) above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of the assessment result of the application, which includes:

  - For applications falling under the cases specified in Point 15.6.d of this Circular: A notice of intention to grant the protection title and a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection title fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, the registration fee and the first-year maintenance fee for the invention. The applicant may request extension of the time limit as prescribed in Point 9.2 of this Circular.

  - For applications not falling under the cases specified in Point 15.6.d of this Circular: A notice of continued handling of the application under Point 15.6.e of this Circular.”

  6. To amend and supplement Point 15.8 of Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  15.8 Time limit for examination of application contents

  a) The time limit for examination of application contents shall comply with the provisions of Clause 2 Article 119 of the Law on Intellectual Property.

  b) In case the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice under Point 15.7.a (i) and (ii) of this Circular, the time limit for the applicant to respond to the notice shall not be included in the time limit for examination of the application form. This time limit shall be understood as:

  (i) The time from the date of issuance of the notice to the date on which the applicant responds to the notice; or (ii) The fixed time limit specified in the notice (including the extended time limit as prescribed), in case the applicant does not respond to the notice.

  c) In case the applicant proactively requests amendment or supplementation of the application, or responds to the notice of the National Office of Intellectual Property specified in Point 15.7.a (i) and (ii) of this Circular, the time limit for examination of the application contents shall be extended by the time limit for handling the amendment or supplementation request or explanation of the applicant as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 119 of the Law on Intellectual Property as follows:

  (i) For inventions, not more than 06 months;

  (ii) For trademarks, not more than 03 months;

  (iii) For industrial designs, not more than 02 months and 10 days;

  (iv) For geographical indications, not more than 02 months.”

  7. To amend and supplement Point 25.7 of Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “25.7 Examination of the first-prime filing principle for inventions

  For invention registration applications that have been concluded to satisfy the protection conditions, before issuing the notice of intention to grant the invention patent or utility solution patent according to the provisions of point 15.7.a (iii) of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine to ensure the first-prime filing principle specified in clause 1 and clause 3 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law according to the following provisions:

  a) To examine the first-prime filing principle, at least the information in the following mandatory sources must be searched (but not limited to the minimum sources): All invention registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property (as of the examination date) having the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application being examined - up to the classification index (third class index) and having a filing date or priority date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority) and has not been published or has a publication date later than the filing date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) of the application being examined.

  b) The search is to find cases where there are multiple applications (including the application being examined) registering identical or equivalent inventions and determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date.

  c) If there are multiple applications under the case specified in point 25.1.b above, the invention patent or utility solution patent can only be granted to the invention in the valid application with the earliest filing date or priority date among the applications that satisfy the conditions for grant of the protection title.

  d) Among the applications under the case specified in point 25.7.b above, if there are multiple applications with the earliest filing date or priority date, the invention patent or utility solution patent can only be granted to the invention of a single application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement is reached, all corresponding subjects of those applications shall be rejected for grant of the protection title.”

  8. To amend and supplement Point 35.9 of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “35.9 Examination of the first-prime filing principle for industrial designs

  For industrial design registration applications that have been concluded to satisfy the protection conditions, before issuing the notice of intention to grant the industrial design exclusive right certificate according to the provisions of Point 15.7.a (iii) of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine to ensure the first-prime filing principle specified in Clause 1 and Clause 3 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law according to the following provisions:

  a) To examine the first-prime filing principle, information in the mandatory sources specified in Point 35.4.b (iv) of this Circular must be searched.

  b) The search is to find cases where there are multiple applications (including applications under assessment) registering industrial designs of product parts and/or products that are identical or not significantly different from each other and to determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date.

  c) If there are multiple applications under the cases specified in Point 35.9.b above, the industrial design exclusive right certificate can only be granted to the industrial design in the valid application with the earliest filing date or priority date among the applications that satisfy the conditions for granting the protection certificate.

  d) Among the applications under the cases specified in Point 35.9.b, if there are multiple applications with the same earliest filing date or priority date, the industrial design exclusive right certificate can only be granted to the industrial design of a single application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement is reached, all corresponding objects of those applications shall be rejected for granting the protection certificate.”

  9. To amend and supplement Point 37.7 of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “37.7 Requirements for written permission for registration of collective marks, certification marks containing localities or other signs indicating the geographical origin of local specialties of Vietnam

  a) Written permission for registration of collective marks, certification marks containing localities or other signs indicating the geographical origin of local specialties shall be granted by the following competent authorities:

  (i) People’s Committees of provinces and centrally-run cities where the geographical area corresponding to the locality or other sign indicating the geographical origin of local specialties is located (in case the geographical area belongs to one locality);

  (ii) All People’s Committees of provinces and centrally-run cities where the geographical area corresponding to the locality or other sign indicating the geographical origin of local specialties is located (in case the geographical area belongs to multiple localities).

  b) Maps of geographical areas corresponding to localities or other signs indicating the geographical origin of local specialties must fully contain information sufficient to accurately identify the geographical area and must be certified by the competent authority specified in Point 37.7.a above.”

  10. Point 37.8 of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN is added as follows:

  “37.8 Criteria for determination of geographical names and other signs indicating the geographical origin of products

  a) A sign indicating the geographical origin of a product is a sign used for products of a locality and has the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of the product (indicating that the product originates from that locality).

  A sign indicating the geographical origin of a product is often a geographical name, but it may also be a local emblem (images of typical local objects, such as symbols, maps, flags, emblems, scenic spots, special works of the locality, etc.), or any other sign.

  A geographical name may be the current name or historical name, official name or folk name of a geographical area (determined according to administrative boundaries or geographical methods).

  b) A geographical name or local emblem used for ordinary products (not specialty products) may or may not have the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of the product, depending on the product and the actual use of the geographical name or local emblem.

  c) A geographical name or local emblem has the meaning of indicating the geographical origin of a product in the following cases:

  (i) Used for specialty products of the locality (special products, which are famous due to certain characteristics and are produced in the locality);

  (ii) Used for crops, livestock and products processed from crops and livestock of the locality;

  (iii) Used for products exploiting natural raw materials (coal, iron, steel, aluminum, cement, stone, salt, wood...) in the locality;

  (iv) Used for products of the developed industries in the locality;

  (v) Other cases shall be determined according to the products and the actual use of local designations and distinctive signs for the products.

  d) Local designations and distinctive signs are not of significance for indicating the geographical origin of the products in the following cases:

  (i) They have been used as ordinary marks and widely recognized, i.e. they have acquired the significance of indicating the commercial origin (discrimination ability) and lost the significance of describing the geographical origin, for example: Hanoi beer, Saigon beer;

  (ii) The corresponding locality cannot be the place where the products are produced, for example: Arctic cigarettes...

  Local designations and distinctive signs which are not of significance for indicating the geographical origin of the products may be protected as ordinary marks without the permission of the local authorities.

  dd) Local designations and distinctive signs which are of common geographical knowledge known to many people (for example, names of provinces, cities, scenic spots) used for ordinary products of the locality (including products of which the locality has business advantages but has not yet a reputation or quality characteristics), which are used by many business entities in the locality for their goods and services which have the significance of describing the place of production (but there is no sufficient basis to classify them into (c) and (d) above), will be not protected.

  However, local names and signs may still be used as an element constituting the ordinary mark of corresponding local organizations and individuals, provided that such local names are excluded from the scope of protection (not separately protected) and do not require permission from the local government.”

  11. To amend and supplement Point 39.10 of the Circular No. 01/2007/TT-BKHCN as follows:

  “39.10 Examination of the first-prime filing principle for marks

  For mark registration applications that have been concluded to satisfy the protection conditions, before issuing the notice of intention to grant the mark registration certificate according to the provisions of point 15.7.a (iii) of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall conduct examination to ensure the first-prime filing principle specified in clause 2 and clause 3 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law according to the following provisions:

  a) To examine the first-prime filing principle, all mark registration applications received by the National Office of Intellectual Property (as of the examination date) with filing dates or priority dates (if the application is entitled to priority) earlier than the filing date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) of the application being examined shall be searched.

  b) The search is to identify cases where there are multiple applications (including the application being examined) filed by different persons registering marks that are identical or similar to the extent of causing confusion with each other for identical or similar products or services, or multiple applications filed by the same person registering identical marks for identical products or services; and to identify the application with the earliest filing date or priority date.

  c) If there are multiple applications falling under the cases specified in point 39.10.b above, the mark registration certificate shall only be granted to the mark in the valid application with the earliest filing date or priority date among the applications that satisfy the conditions for grant of the protection title.

  d) Among the applications falling under the cases specified in point 39.10.b above, if there are multiple applications with the earliest filing date or priority date, the mark registration certificate shall only be granted to the mark of one application among those applications according to the agreement of all applicants; if no agreement can be reached, all corresponding objects of those applications shall be rejected for grant of the protection title.”

  Article 2. Effect

  This Circular takes effect 45 days after the date of its signing./.
