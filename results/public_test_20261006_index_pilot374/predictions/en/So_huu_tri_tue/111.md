Chapter I
                 PROCEDURES FOR ESTABLISHING INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
Section 1. GENERAL PROVISIONS ON PROCEDURES FOR ESTABLISHING INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY RIGHTS
1. Basis for establishing industrial property rights
1.1 Industrial property rights arise or are established on the basis of grounds specified in Clause 3 Article 6 of the Law on Intellectual Property dated November 29, 2005 (hereinafter referred to as the “Law on Intellectual Property”), Clauses 1, 2, 3 and 4 Article 6 of the Government’s Decree No. 103/2006/ND-CP dated September 22, 2006, detailing and guiding the implementation of a number of articles of the Law on Intellectual Property on industrial property (hereinafter referred to as the “Decree on industrial property”) and according to the specific provisions of this Point.
1.2 Industrial property rights to inventions, semiconductor integrated circuit layout designs (hereinafter referred to as “layout designs”), industrial designs and trademarks established on the basis of decisions of the National Office of Intellectual Property on granting protection titles to registrants of such objects. The persons granted protection titles by the National Office of Intellectual Property are owners and enjoy rights to industrial property objects within the scope of protection stated in the protection titles and for the duration of validity of the protection titles. In case of disputes, owners of industrial property objects may use protection titles as evidence of their rights without any other evidence.
1.3 Industrial property rights to geographical indications established on the basis of decisions of the National Office of Intellectual Property on granting certificates of registration of geographical indications to organizations managing geographical indications.

1.4 Industrial property rights to international registered marks under the Madrid Agreement and the Madrid Protocol (hereinafter referred to as “international registered marks”) established on the basis of decisions on granting protection or certificates of registration of international marks protected in Vietnam granted by the National Office of Intellectual Property at the request of mark owners. Such decisions and certificates shall have the same effect as protection titles granted to mark registrants in Vietnam.
1.5 Industrial property rights to well-known marks established on the basis of widespread use, making such marks well-known without having to go through registration procedures at the National Office of Intellectual Property. When exercising rights and settling disputes over well-known marks, the mark owner must prove his/her rights with appropriate evidence as prescribed in Article 75 of the Intellectual Property Law.
1.6 Industrial property rights to a trade name established on the basis of lawful use of such trade name without having to carry out registration procedures at the National Office of Intellectual Property. When exercising rights and settling disputes over trade names, the owner of a trade name must prove his/her rights by evidence showing the time, territory and field in which such trade name has been used.
1.7 The industrial property right to business secrets established on the basis of financial or intellectual investment activities or the results of other lawful activities to find, create or obtain information constituting business secrets and keep such information confidential without having to carry out registration procedures at the National Office of Intellectual Property. When exercising the right and settling disputes over the right to business secrets, the subject of business secrets must prove its right with evidence showing the activities in which the information constituting business secrets is created, found or obtained and measures to keep such information confidential.
1.8 The right to prevent unfair competition is established on the basis of the actual competition activities without having to go through the registration procedures at the National Office of Intellectual Property. When using the right to prevent unfair competition, the subject must prove its right with evidence showing the objects, fields, territories and time of business related to the competition activities.
2. The applicant for industrial property registration
2.1 The applicant for industrial property registration (hereinafter referred to as “applicant”) is an organization or individual that files an application for registration of an invention, industrial design, industrial style, mark or geographical indication. When the protection title for the invention, industrial design, industrial style or mark is granted, the applicant shall be recognized as the owner of the protection title. When the protection title for the geographical indication is granted, the applicant shall be recognized as the registrant of the geographical indication.
2.2 The applicant must satisfy the conditions for industrial property registration specified in Articles 86, 87, 88 of the Law on Intellectual Property and Articles 7, 8, 9 of the Decree on Industrial Property. If the applicant fails to satisfy these conditions, the industrial property registration shall be deemed invalid.
3. Representative of the applicant
3.1 The applicant may carry out the industrial property registration procedures at the Office either himself/herself or through a lawful representative in Vietnam.

3.2 The following organizations and individuals may act as representatives of the applicant:
a) For organizations and individuals specified in Clause 1 Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property:
(i) If the applicant is an individual: the legal representative or the representative of the applicant, the industrial property service organization authorized by the applicant;
(ii) If the applicant is an organization: the legal representative of the applicant or a person authorized by the legal representative of the applicant; an industrial property representation service organization (authorized by the applicant); the head of the representative office or the head of the branch in Vietnam (if the applicant is a foreign organization).
b) For organizations and individuals specified in Clause 2 Article 89 of the Law on Intellectual Property: industrial property representation service providers (authorized by the applicants).
3.3 When carrying out industrial property registration procedures, the National Office of Intellectual Property is only permitted to transact with the applicant or the applicant's lawful representative. Organizations and individuals not falling under the cases specified in Point 3.2 of this Circular that act as representatives for applicants shall be considered as unlawful representatives.
4. Authorization for representation of industrial property registration procedures 4.1 The authorization for representation and the exercise of authorization for representation of industrial property registration procedures (hereinafter referred to as “authorization”) must comply with the law on authorization in Part III of the Civil Code and the provisions of this Circular.
4.2 The authorization must be expressed in writing (authorization paper) and must contain the following main contents:
a) Full name and address of the authorized party and the authorized party;
b) Full name and address of the authorized replacement or re-authorization recipient (if any);
c) Scope of authorization and volume of work authorized;
d) Duration of authorization (the authorization document without a duration only ceases to be effective when the authorized party declares the termination of authorization);
e) Date of signing the authorization document;
g) Signature (clearly stating full name, position and seal, if any) of the legal representative of the authorized party (and of the authorized replacement, re-authorization recipient, if any).
4.3 The time when the authorization document is legally valid in the transaction with the National Office of Intellectual Property is determined as follows:
a) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the valid authorization document;
b) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property accepts the replacement of a valid authorization or reauthorization;

c) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the notice of change in the scope of authorization, termination of authorization before the expiration date, or change in the address of the authorized party.
4.4 In case of substitution or re-delegation, the power of attorney shall be considered valid only if the substituted or re-delegated party commits to take responsibility for all issues arising from the delegating party’s actions in the previous transaction with the National Office of Intellectual Property.

4.5 If the power of attorney covers multiple procedures that are independent of each other and the original copy of the power of attorney has been submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property, the authorized party must submit a copy of the power of attorney and provide the exact reference to the application number containing the original copy of the power of attorney when proceeding with subsequent procedures.
5. Responsibilities of the applicant and the applicant's representative
5.1 The applicant and the representative of the applicant shall ensure the truthfulness of the information and documents provided to the National Office of Intellectual Property in the course of industrial property registration in accordance with the following regulations:
a) All transaction documents must be self-certified by the applicant or his/her representative, and stamped with the seal of the organization (if any). In case of notarization or certification by another competent authority, it must be certified in accordance with regulations;
b) Any Vietnamese translation of foreign documents must be guaranteed by the applicant or his/her representative that it is a literal translation from the original.
5.2 The applicant shall be responsible for all consequences and obligations arising from the representative of the applicant in the transaction with the National Office of Intellectual Property.
5.3 The representative of the applicant shall be responsible to the applicant for all consequences caused by the dishonest declaration and provision of information in the transaction with the National Office of Intellectual Property, and shall pay compensation if any damage is caused.
5.4. Hereinafter, unless otherwise specified, the applicant and the representative of the applicant shall be referred to as “the applicant”.
6. Handling of third parties’ comments before issuing the decision on granting the protection title 6.1 From the date on which the industrial property registration application is published in the Industrial Property Gazette until the date on which the decision on granting the protection title is issued, any organization or individual may submit a written comment to the National Office of Intellectual Property regarding the registration right, priority right, protection conditions and other issues related to the industrial property registration application as prescribed in Article 112 of the Intellectual Property Law. The written comments of third parties shall be considered as a source of information for the processing of the industrial property registration application.
6.2 Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the written opinion of the third party, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of such opinion and set a maximum time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to respond in writing. Upon receiving the applicant's feedback, if deemed necessary, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the third party of such feedback and set a maximum time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the third party to respond in writing to such feedback. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the opinions of the applicant and the third party based on the evidence and arguments provided by the parties and the documents in the application.
6.3. If the opinion of a third party is considered unfounded, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall not notify the applicant of such opinion, but must notify the third party of the refusal to consider the opinion, clearly stating the reasons.
6.4 In case of opinions of third parties related to registration rights, if it is considered that the opinions of third parties cannot be determined whether they are well-founded or not, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the third parties to file their applications with the court for settlement. Within 01 month from the date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice, if the third parties do not notify the National Office of Intellectual Property that they have filed their applications with the court for settlement, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider that the third parties withdraw their opinions. If the National Office of Intellectual Property is notified by the third parties within the above-mentioned time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall suspend the handling of the applications to wait for the results of the dispute settlement by the court. After receiving the results of the court's settlement, the handling of the applications shall be carried out in accordance with such results.
6.5 The National Office of Intellectual Property shall organize a direct dialogue between the third party and the applicant to clarify the issue of opposition if deemed necessary and at the request of both parties.
6.6 The time limit for the applicant to respond to the objection of a third party shall not be counted in the time limit for the National Office of Intellectual Property to carry out the relevant procedures as prescribed.
7. General requirements for industrial property registration applications
7.1 Minimum documents

The Intellectual Property Office shall only accept industrial property registration applications (hereinafter referred to as “applications”) if, upon submission, they contain at least the documents specified in Points a, b and e Clause 1 Article 100 and Clause 1 Article 108 of the Intellectual Property Law and the following specific requirements:
a) For applications for registration of inventions, industrial designs, industrial forms, trademarks and geographical indications, the mandatory documents required for acceptance of the application include:
(i) Registration declaration;
(ii) Documents, specimens and information on the industrial property subject matter to be registered; Specifically: for an application for registration of an invention, it shall be an invention description; for an application for registration of an industrial design, it shall be a set of photographs, drawings and descriptions of the industrial design; for an application for registration of a trademark, it shall be a trademark sample and a list of goods and services bearing the trademark; for an application for registration of a geographical indication, it shall be a description of the specific characteristics and quality of the product bearing the geographical indication and a map of the geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication;
(iii) Evidence of payment of fees and charges.
If any of the above documents is missing, the Intellectual Property Office may refuse to accept the application.
b) For applications for collective mark registration or certification mark registration, in addition to the documents specified in Point 7.1.a above, the application must also include the following documents:
(i) Regulations on use of collective marks/certification marks;
(ii) A description of the characteristics and quality of the product bearing the mark (if the mark is registered as a collective mark used for a product with distinctive characteristics or a mark certifying the quality of the product or a mark certifying the geographical origin); (iii) A map identifying the territory (if the mark is registered as a mark certifying the geographical origin of the product).
7.2 Requirements for the application
a) An application must satisfy the general requirements specified in Articles 100 and 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the specific requirements for each type of industrial property subject matter specified in Articles 102, 103, 104, 105, and 106 of the Law on Intellectual Property, which are elaborated in Points 23, 28, 33, 37, and 43 of this Circular.
b) To ensure technical requirements during the processing, the application must also meet the following formal requirements:
(i) Each application shall be granted only one protection title and the type of protection title granted must be appropriate to the industrial property subject matter stated in the application;
(ii) All documents of the application must be made in Vietnamese, except for documents that may be made in other languages as prescribed in Points 7.3 and 7.4 of this Circular;
(iii) All documents of the application must be presented vertically (except drawings, diagrams and tables which may be presented horizontally) on one A4-sized paper (210mm x 297mm), with margins of 20mm on all four sides, except supplementary documents whose origins are not intended to be included in the application;
(iv) For documents that must be prepared using forms, the use of such forms is mandatory and all information required must be fully filled in at appropriate places;
(v) Each type of document, if it comprises multiple pages, must have the page number written in Arabic numerals;
(vi) Documents must be typed or printed with a fade-resistant ink, clearly, cleanly, without erasing or altering; if any insignificant errors, such as spelling mistakes, are detected in the documents submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property, the applicant may correct them, but the corrected places must bear the applicant's signature (and seal, if any);
(vii) Terms used in the application must be common terms (not local dialects, rare words or self-made words). Symbols, units of measurement, electronic fonts and spelling rules used in the application must comply with Vietnamese standards;
(viii) The application may be accompanied by supplementary documents which are electronic data carriers of a part or all of the contents of the application document.
c) The application must fully meet the requirements regarding the number of documents, forms, drawings, photographs and specific requirements for each corresponding type of application as prescribed in this Circular.

d) The declaration and other documents of the application must ensure that all mandatory information is complete and consistent; the Vietnamese translation of the application document must be consistent with the original; the authorization document must include the work within the scope of authorization.
e) The subjects mentioned in the application must be accurately grouped and classified according to regulations.
g) For documents that require certification by a competent authority (the authority that first received the application, the notary, the People's Committee, etc.), the seal of that authority must be affixed.

7.3 The following documents may be made in a language other than Vietnamese but must be translated into Vietnamese:
a) Power of attorney;
b) Documents confirming the right to registration if the applicant is entitled to the registration right of another person (inheritance certificate; certificate or agreement on transfer of the right to file an application, including the transferred application; contract for assignment of work or labor contract...);
c) Documents proving the grounds for entitlement to priority (confirmation by the order-receiving agency of copies of the first application(s); list of goods and services under the first trademark application; certificate of transfer of priority if the right is acquired from another person).
7.4 The following documents may be made in other languages than Vietnamese, but if the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam requests, they must be translated into Vietnamese.
a) A copy of the first application to prove the basis for entitlement to priority;
b) Other documents to supplement the application.
8. Fees and charges for industrial property registration
8.1 Applicants shall pay fees and charges as prescribed by the Ministry of Finance.
8.2 Collection of fees and charges
a) Upon receipt of an application or request for any other procedure, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to pay fees and charges as prescribed and examine the fee and charge payment documents attached to the application.
b) If fees and charges are not fully paid as prescribed, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a receipt and notify the applicant thereof.
In case the applicant fully pays fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue 02 receipts for fee and charge payment, clearly stating the paid amounts and rates, of which 01 receipt shall be enclosed with the application as a fee and charge payment receipt.
8.3 Refund of fees and charges
a) Fees and charges already paid shall be partially or fully refunded at the applicant’s request in the following cases:
(i) Fees and charges paid in excess of the prescribed amounts;
(ii) Fees and charges paid but the corresponding work is not carried out because the situation requiring the performance of the work does not occur.
b) In case of acceptance of the request for refund of fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall prepare a form for refund of fees and charges, clearly stating the amount and method of refund, and send it to the applicant.
c) In case of rejection of the request for refund of fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant, clearly stating the reason.
9. Time limits
9.1 The time limits prescribed in the Law on Intellectual Property, the Decree on Industrial Property and this Circular shall be calculated according to the provisions of Chapter VIII, Part I of the Civil Code.
9.2 The time limit for the applicant and the relevant party to submit, amend or supplement documents or provide comments may be extended once for the same time limit as the fixed time limit, provided that the applicant for extension must submit a written request for extension before the end of the fixed time limit and pay the fee as prescribed.
9.3 The applicant may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to carry out the procedures before the prescribed time limit when there is a written request and pay the fee as prescribed. If the National Office of Intellectual Property does not accept such request, it must notify the applicant, clearly stating the reason.
10. Forms of documents and forms of protection titles

10.1 The application forms specified in the appendices of this Circular. Applicants must use the above-mentioned forms to prepare application documents when carrying out industrial property registration procedures.
10.2 The forms of protection titles specified in the appendices of this Circular. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall keep the forms of protection titles issued for the purpose of checking the legality of the protection titles used. The forms of protection titles may be changed only on the basis of the decision of the Minister of Science and Technology.
11. General procedures
All industrial property registration applications shall be processed by the National Office of Intellectual Property in the following order: receipt of the application; examination of the application form; publication of the application; examination of the application content (except for design registration applications which do not undergo the examination of the application content); issuance or refusal of the protection title; publication of the decision on issuance of the protection title.
12. Submission and receipt of applications
12.1 Applications may be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property or other reception locations set up by the National Office of Intellectual Property. Applications may also be sent by post to the aforementioned reception locations.

12.2 Upon receipt of an application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the application documents and compare them with the list of documents stated in the application form to determine whether the application has been received or not:
a) If the application contains all the minimum documents specified in Point 7.1 of this Circular, the officer receiving the application shall affix the seal confirming the date of application submission and the application number on the application form;
b) In case an application is missing one of the minimum documents specified in Point 7.1 of this Circular, the application-receiving officer shall refuse to receive the application or send a notice of refusal of receipt of the application from the National Office of Intellectual Property to the applicant (if the application is submitted by post). For applications that are refused to be received, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall not return the application documents to the applicant, but shall refund the fees and charges paid according to the procedures for refund of fees and charges specified in Point 8 of this Circular;
c) In case of receipt of an application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue (send) a declaration to the applicant, which bears the stamp of the date of application, the application number and the result of the check of the list of application documents, clearly stating the full name and signature of the officer receiving the application. The above-mentioned declaration shall be valid as a receipt of the application.

13.3 Handling deficiencies of the application during the formal assessment stage

If the application contains the following deficiencies, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the applicant and within 1 month from the date of notification, the applicant shall remedy such deficiencies:
a) The application does not satisfy the formal requirements specified in Point 7.2 of this Circular (the number of copies of one of the mandatory documents is insufficient; the application does not meet the requirements for uniformity; the application does not satisfy the requirements for presentation; the application for registration of a mark does not clearly state the type of mark to be registered, lacks the description of the mark, the results of the classification of goods and services are inaccurate, lacks the translated documents of the priority right, if necessary; the information on the applicant in the documents is inconsistent or erased or not certified as required...);
b) Failure to pay the application fee and the fee for publication of the application;
c) No authorization or invalid authorization (if the application is submitted through a representative).

13.4 Determination of the date of application submission
The date of filing is determined as follows:
a) The date of filing is the date on which the application is received by the National Office of Intellectual Property and recorded in the receipt stamp on the application form as prescribed in Point 12.2.a of this Circular;
b) For an international application designated or selected Vietnam, the date of filing is the date of filing of the international application.

13.5 Determination of priority date
a) If the application does not request the right to priority or, although it requests the right to priority, the Intellectual Property Office does not approve it, the application shall be considered as having no priority date.
b) If the application requests the right to priority, the priority date (or priority dates) is the date stated in the application and approved by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
c) The determination of the priority date according to the request for priority based on the first (first) application (applications) filed in Vietnam shall comply with the principles specified in Article 91 of the Intellectual Property Law and the corresponding provisions in Points b, c and dd Clause 1 Article 10 of the Decree on Industrial Property.
13.6 Notification of assessment of form and notification of acceptance of valid application
a) If the application falls into one of the cases specified in Point 13.2 of this Circular or has deficiencies specified in Point 13.3 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a notice to the applicant indicating its intention to reject the valid application. The notice must clearly state the applicant’s name and address; the name of the industrial property representative service organization (if the application is submitted through such organization); the subject matter specified in the application; the date of application submission and the application number; the reasons and deficiencies that may lead to the rejection of the application, and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or remedy the deficiencies.
b) In case of a valid application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a notice to the applicant indicating its intention to accept the application, which must clearly state the applicant’s name and address, the name of the authorized representative (if any), and information about the subject matter specified in the application, the date of application submission, and the priority date (if the request for priority is not approved, the reasons must be clearly stated).
13.7 Rejection of application
In case the applicant has been notified by the National Office of Intellectual Property of its intention to reject the application as prescribed in Point 13.6.a of this Circular but fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies are inadequate, or fails to provide comments or provides inappropriate comments within the set time limit, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send a notice to the applicant rejecting the application and refunding the fees and charges paid for the post-formal assessment work at the applicant’s request.
13.8 Time limit for formal assessment of application
a) The time limit for formal assessment of application is 1 month from the date of application submission.
b) If, during the formal assessment of the application, the applicant proactively or at the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property carries out the remedy or supplementation of documents, the time limit for formal assessment shall be extended by the period allocated for the remedy or supplementation of documents.
c) Before the expiration of the time limit specified in Point 13.8.a above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall have to finish the assessment of the application form and send the result notification to the applicant as prescribed in Point 13.6 of this Circular.
14. Declaration of validity
14.1 All valid applications shall be published by the National Office of Industrial Property on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property. Applicants shall pay the application publication fee.
14.2 Time limit for announcement of applications

a) Publishing the invention registration application:
(i) The invention application shall be published in the 19th month from the priority date or the date of filing, if the application has no priority date or within 2 months from the date of acceptance of the valid application, whichever is later;
(ii) The application for patent registration under the Agreement on Cooperation in Inventions (hereinafter referred to as “the PCT application”) shall be published within 02 months from the date on which the application is accepted as valid after it enters the national phase; (iii) The application for patent registration that requests early publication shall be published within 02 months from the date on which the Intellectual Property Office receives the request for early publication or from the date on which the application is accepted as valid, whichever is later.
b) Publishing other applications: application for registration of design arrangement, application for registration of industrial design, application for registration of mark, application for registration of geographical indication shall be published within 02 months from the date of acceptance of valid application.
14.3 Contents of the application for publication
Information related to a valid application, including a separated application published in the Industrial Property Gazette, includes information related to the valid application in terms of formalities stated in the notice of acceptance of the valid application, information related to the valid application (application transfer, application separation, original number of the separated application, etc.); a summary of the invention enclosed with drawings (if any); a set of photographs or drawings of the industrial design; a trademark sample and a list of goods and services enclosed with it; a summary of the specific characteristics of the product bearing a geographical indication and the name of the product bearing a geographical indication.
14.4 Accessing information on valid applications published
Anyone may access detailed information on the nature of the subject matter stated in the application published in the Industrial Property Gazette or request the Intellectual Property Office to provide such information and must pay the information provision fee as prescribed.
15. Examination of contents of application
15.1 Purpose and scope of application
a) The purpose of the examination of the contents of the application is to assess the protection possibility of the subject matter stated in the application according to the protection conditions and determine the corresponding scope (volume) of protection.
b) The procedures for assessment of contents not applicable to design registration applications for arrangement.
15.2 Use of information search results
a) During the examination of contents of the application for invention registration or industrial design registration with priority, the Intellectual Property Office may use the information search results and the examination results of the corresponding application filed abroad.
b) The applicant may (on his/her own initiative or at the request of the Intellectual Property Office) provide the following documents for examination of contents of the application:
(i) For the application for invention registration or industrial design registration: information search results or examination results of the application filed abroad for the subject matter specified in the application; copies of the protection titles granted at the same level as the application filed abroad; documents related to the technical state of the subject matter specified in the application for invention registration provided by the applicant to the competent foreign authority and other documents;
(ii) For the application for trademark registration or geographical indication registration: documents proving the trademark or geographical indication has been protected abroad, including documents explaining the process of using the trademark or geographical indication and other documents.
15.3 Correction of deficiencies, explanation of contents of the application, provision of information
a) The Intellectual Property Office may request the applicant to explain the contents of the application, provide information within the scope of the nature of the subject matter specified in the application, if the request fully reveals the nature of the subject matter is necessary for examination of contents of the application.
b) All modification and supplementation of the defective application documents must be carried out by the applicant himself/herself. The Intellectual Property Office may only carry out the above-mentioned modification and supplementation if the applicant makes a written request. The applicant must pay the fee for modification and supplementation of the application as prescribed. The documents requesting modification and supplementation of the application are attached to the relevant application documents and are considered official documents of the application.
15.4 Termination of content assessment before the deadline
a) The content assessment of an application shall be terminated before the deadline in the following cases:

(i) The application fails to clearly show the nature of the subject matter: documents related to the nature of the subject matter, such as descriptions, lists of goods or services, etc., are incomplete to the extent that the nature of the subject matter cannot be determined or the information on the nature of the subject matter of the invention registration application is unclear or too brief or too general to determine the subject matter of protection;
(ii) The subject matter is not suitable for the type of protection title requested or the subject matter is not protected by the State as prescribed;
(iii) There are reasons to firmly assert that the subject matter does not meet one or several protection conditions, so it is not necessary to evaluate other conditions and it is still possible to conclude that the subject matter does not meet the protection conditions;
(iv) The applicant fails to fulfill the request for correction of deficiencies, explanation of the application content or failure to provide necessary information as requested by the Intellectual Property Office as prescribed in Point 15.3 of this Circular;
(v) The applicant requests termination of the content assessment of the application or makes a declaration of withdrawal or renunciation of the application.
b) Except for the cases prescribed in Point 15.4.a (v) above, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a notice of termination of the content assessment before the deadline to the applicant, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments.
15.5 Restoration of content assessment of applications
a) In case the applicant has a written objection to the notice of termination of the examination of the application contents within the time limit specified in Point 15.4.b of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider the applicant's objection.
b) If the objection is justified, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall resume the examination of the application and the time allocated to the applicant shall not be counted into the examination period. If the objection is not justified, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall terminate the examination of the application and issue a notice of refusal to grant the protection title. The applicant may lodge a complaint against this notice according to the procedures specified in Point 22 of this Circular.
15.6 Contents of assessment
a) Examination of the contents of the application, including the following contents:
(i) Assess the suitability of the subject matter specified in the application to the type of protection title granted; (ii) Assess the subject matter according to each protection condition;
(iii) Check the first application submission.
b) The assessment according to the protection conditions shall be carried out in order according to each subject (if the application includes multiple subjects but still ensures uniformity). For each subject, the assessment shall be carried out in order according to each protection condition:
(i) For an invention application, the assessment shall be carried out in order according to each point specified in the scope (claims) of protection;
(ii) For an application for registration of an industrial design, the assessment shall be carried out in order with the design of each product (if the application refers to a set of products); in case of referring to multiple solutions, the assessment shall be carried out in order with each solution, starting from the basic solution (the first solution stated in the application);
(iii) For a trademark application, the assessment of each component of the trademark shall be carried out in respect of each goods or service specified in the list of goods and services.
c) The assessment of the contents of each subject matter specified in Points 15.6.b (i), (ii), (iii) above shall be completed when the subject matter is evaluated under all protection conditions and there are sufficient grounds to conclude that the subject matter either does not satisfy or satisfies the protection conditions, specifically:
(i) Finding reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy one or some/all of the protection conditions; or (ii) Finding no reasons to conclude that the subject matter does not satisfy at least one of the protection conditions.
d) Before issuing the notice specified in Point 15.7.a of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall review the results of the assessment of the contents of the application based on the examination of related applications with earlier priority dates received by the National Office of Intellectual Property after the start date of the assessment of the contents of the application.

15.7. Completion of the assessment of the contents of the application
a) Notification of the result of the assessment of the contents of the application

At the latest by the end of the time limit for examination of the application specified in Point 15.8 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send one of the following notifications to the applicant:
(i) If the subject matter specified in the application does not satisfy the protection conditions, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of intention to refuse to grant the protection title, clearly stating the reasons for refusal, and may guide the modification of the scope (volume) of protection and set a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments and satisfy the requirements. The applicant may request extension of the time limit as prescribed in Point 9.2 of this Circular;
(ii) If the subject matter specified in the application satisfies the protection conditions but the application has deficiencies, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of intention to refuse to grant the protection title, clearly stating the deficiencies of the application and setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to provide comments or make corrections to the deficiencies. The applicant may request extension of the time limit as prescribed in Point 9.2 of this Circular;
(iii) If the subject matter specified in the application satisfies the protection conditions or the applicant has corrected the deficiencies to meet the requirements or provided convincing comments within the time limit specified in Points 15.7.a (i) and (ii) above, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of intention to grant the protection title and set a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to pay the protection title fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, the registration fee and the first-year maintenance fee for the invention. The applicant may request extension of the time limit as prescribed in Point 9.2 of this Circular.
b) If the time limit specified in Points 15.7.a (i) and (ii) above expires without the applicant making corrections or making unsatisfactory corrections, or without any objection or objectionable comments, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of refusal to grant the protection title within 15 days from the date of expiration of the time limit. The applicant may lodge a complaint against this notice as prescribed in Point 22 of this Circular.
c) If the applicant fails to pay the protection title fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, and the registration fee within the time limit specified in Point 15.7.a (iii) above, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue a notice of refusal to grant the protection title within 15 days from the date of expiration of the corresponding time limit. Specifically, for invention registration applications, if the applicant has paid the protection title fee, the fee for publication of the decision on grant of the protection title, and the registration fee within the time limit specified in Point 15.7.a (iii) above but has not paid the first-year maintenance fee for the patent or utility solution patent, such patent shall still be granted but its validity shall be terminated immediately after the date of grant.
15.8 Time limits
a) The time limit for examination of application contents shall be determined as follows:
(i) 12 months for invention registration applications from the date of receipt of the request for examination of application contents (if such request is filed after the date of publication of the application) or from the date of publication of the application (if such request is filed before the date of publication of the application);
(ii) 06 months from the date of announcement of the application for industrial design, mark and geographical indication registration.
b) If, during the process of assessing the contents of the application, the applicant takes the initiative or at the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property to make amendments to the application, supplement documents or provide explanations, the time limit for assessing the contents of the application shall be extended by the time limit for the applicant to perform such tasks.
16. Re-examination of applications
16.1 Re-examination of applications after receiving objections after notification of the intention to grant or propose to refuse to grant protection titles
a) Re-examination of applications as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be carried out in the following cases:
(i) A written opinion of the applicant submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property during the period from the date of issuance of the notice of intention to grant/issue a protection title to the date of issuance of the decision on grant/issue of the protection title; or a written opinion of a third party with reasonable grounds for the lack of conditions or opportunities to express their opinion; (ii) The opinion stated in point 16.1.a (i) above is well-founded, accompanied by evidence or references to reliable sources of information;
(iii) The reasoning and evidence proving the opinion stated in point 16.1.a (i) above are different from the reasoning and evidence (if any) that have been presented in the previous period, or although such reasoning and evidence are not different, the Intellectual Property Office has not responded according to the provisions of point 6.2 of this Circular.

b) The time limit for re-assessing the application shall be two-thirds of the time limit for the first assessment specified in Point 15.8 of this Circular; for complex cases with many circumstances that need verification or expert opinion solicitation, the time limit may be extended but not exceeding the time limit for the first assessment.
c) The contents and procedures of re-assessment shall comply with the corresponding provisions in Points 15.6 and 15.7 of this Circular.
d) The re-assessment of the application shall only be carried out once.
16.2 Re-examination of applications upon request by holders of protection titles to narrow the scope of protection In case the holders of protection titles request to narrow the scope of protection of industrial property rights as prescribed in Clause 3 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall re-examine the corresponding applications according to the contents and procedures prescribed in Points 15.6 and 15.7 of this Circular, provided that the applicants shall pay fees and charges as prescribed.
17. Modification, supplementation, separation, conversion, transfer of forms
17.1. Amendments to the application
a) Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues a notice of rejection of a valid application, a notice of refusal to grant a protection title or a decision on granting a protection title, the applicant may, on his/her own initiative or at the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property, amend the application documents.
b) For requests for amendment to the following documents, the applicant shall submit the corresponding amended documents, enclosed with a detailed explanation of the amendment compared to the initial submitted documents:
(i) Description and summary of the invention for the application for patent registration;
(ii) Drawings, photographs, descriptions for industrial design registration applications;
(iii) The trademark, list of goods and services bearing the trademark for the trademark application; (iv) Description of distinctive characteristics, map of geographical area corresponding to the geographical indication for the geographical indication application.
c) Amendments to applications shall not extend the scope (volume) of protection beyond the contents disclosed in the description section of the application for an industrial design or utility model, or the list of goods and services of the application for a trademark, and shall not alter the nature of the subject matter specified in the application. If the amendment extends the scope (volume) of protection or alters the nature of the subject matter, the applicant shall file a new application and all procedures shall be re-processed from the beginning.
d) The applicant may request correction of errors in the name or address of the applicant or author.
e) Any request for amendment or supplementation must be made in writing using Form 01-SDD as prescribed in Appendix B of this Circular. It is possible to request amendment of the same content related to multiple applications, provided that the applicant must pay fees according to the number of applications.
g) In case the applicant actively modifies or supplements the application documents after the Intellectual Property Office has issued a notice of acceptance of the valid application, the modification or supplementation shall comply with the provisions of Points 13.2, 13.3 and 13.6 of this Circular. The modified or supplemented application shall be published in the Industrial Property Gazette as prescribed in Point 14 of this Circular, and the applicant shall pay the application publication fee as prescribed.
h) Amendments to applications, including changes to authorized persons, shall be made by applicants or at the request of the National Office of Intellectual Property and must be made in writing, clearly stating the amendments and accompanied by documents proving the payment of fees for amendments. The amended application must comply with the provisions in Points 7, 10 and 13 of this Circular.
17.2 Separation of the application
a) The applicant may, on his/her own initiative or at the request of the Intellectual Property Office, split the application (split one or more technical solutions in the invention registration application, one or more industrial designs in the industrial design registration application, one or more components of the trademark or part of the list of goods and services in the trademark registration application into one or more new applications, referred to as split applications).
b) The split application shall bear the number of the new application and shall be filed on the date of filing of the original application or (the) priority date(s) of the original application (if any); it shall be published in accordance with Point 14 of this Circular after the notice of acceptance of the valid application.
c) For each split application, the applicant shall pay the application fee and all fees and charges for procedures performed independently of the original application, but shall not pay the priority claim fee. The split application shall be examined for form and further processed according to the procedures not yet completed for the original application. The split application shall be re-published and the applicant shall pay the publication fee if the split application is filed after the Intellectual Property Office has issued a notice of acceptance of the valid application for the original application.
d) The original application (after being split) shall be further processed according to the normal procedures and the applicant shall pay the fee for amendment and supplementation of the application.
17.3 Conversion of applications
a) Before the Intellectual Property Office issues a notice of refusal to grant the protection title or a decision to grant the protection title, the applicant for invention registration may convert the request for grant of the invention patent into a request for grant of the utility solution patent or vice versa in accordance with Point dd Clause 1 Article 115 of the Intellectual Property Law, provided that the applicant shall pay the application conversion fee in accordance with regulations.
b) Upon receipt of a valid application conversion request, the Intellectual Property Office shall further process the converted application in accordance with regulations, but shall not re-perform the procedures already performed for the application before the conversion request.
c) The conversion request filed after the time limit specified in Point 17.3.a above shall not be considered. The applicant may file a new application, but the date of filing (priority date, if any) of the original application shall be used.
17.4 Transfer of application
Before the National Office of Intellectual Property issues one of the notices specified in Point 17.1.a of this Circular, the applicant may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to record the transfer of the application to another person. The request for recording the transfer shall be made using Form 02-CGD specified in Appendix B of this Circular and the corresponding provisions in Point 17.1 of this Circular. The request for recording the transfer of the application must include documents proving that the transferee meets the requirements for registration rights.
18. Refusal to grant, grant, re-grant copies of protection titles and re-grant protection titles 18.1 Refusal to grant protection titles
An application for a protection title shall be rejected if it falls into one of the cases specified in Clauses 1 and 2 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property. In these cases, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the procedures for rejection of the protection title as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 117 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
18.2 Grant of protection titles
a) Within 10 days from the date on which the applicant fully and timely pays the fees and charges, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the procedures for granting the protection title in accordance with Article 118 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
b) After the protection title is granted, if the protection title holder finds any errors, he/she may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to correct the protection title in accordance with Point 20.2 of this Circular.
c) The transfer of the application will not be considered from the date the IPB office issues the protection title.
18.3 Issue of copies of protection titles and re-issue of protection titles
a) In case of common ownership of industrial property rights, protection titles shall be issued only to the first person on the list of applicants for common protection titles. Other co-owners may request the Intellectual Property Office to issue copies of protection titles, provided that they pay the fee for copies.
b) In the following cases, the industrial property owners who have been issued protection titles/copies of protection titles may request the Intellectual Property Office to re-issue protection titles/copies of protection titles, provided that they pay the corresponding fees:
(i) The protection titles/copies of protection titles are lost;
(ii) The protection titles/copies of protection titles are damaged, torn, dirty or faded to the extent that they are no longer usable.
c) Requests for issue of copies or re-issue of protection titles
Except for the case where the request for issue of copies is already stated in the declaration of registration of industrial property objects, the request for issue of copies or re-issue of protection titles must be made in writing and include the following documents:
(i) The declaration of request for issue of copies or re-issue of protection titles made according to Form 03-PBVB specified in Appendix C of this Circular);
(ii) 01 label; 01 set of photographs of industrial design drawings identical to the label, set of photographs of industrial design drawings in the original protection title;

(iii) Power of attorney (if the request is submitted through a representative);
(iv) Receipts of fees for grant of protection titles or fees for grant of copies of protection titles.
d) Processing of requests for issuance of copies and re-issuance of protection titles
(i) Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall consider the request for issuance of a copy or re-issuance of the protection title. If the request for issuance of a copy or re-issuance of the protection title complies with the above regulations, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision on issuance of a copy of the protection title or re-issuance of the protection title and record it in the register section of the corresponding protection title in the National Register of Industrial Property;
(ii) The contents of the duplicate of the protection title shall fully reflect the information of the corresponding protection title. The contents of the re-issued copy of the protection title/duplicate of the protection title shall fully reflect the information of the protection title/duplicate of the protection title issued for the first time and must be accompanied by the indication "Re-issued copy", "Duplicate" or "Re-issued copy of the duplicate";
(iii) In case the request for re-grant of the copy of the protection title does not satisfy the provisions in Point 18.3.c above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to grant the copy of the protection title or a notice of refusal to grant the re-grant of the protection title, clearly stating the reason.
19. National register of industrial property, announcement of decisions on granting protection titles 19.1 National register of industrial property
a) The National Register of Industrial Property is the official and public database of the State, which fully reflects the legal status of established industrial property rights. The National Register of Industrial Property comprises the following types:
(i) The National Register of Inventions;
(ii) The National Register of Utility Solutions;
(iii) The National Register of Industrial Designs;
(iv) The National Register of Semiconductor Integrated Circuit Layout Designs;
(v) The National Register of Trademarks;
(vi) The National Register of Geographical Indications;
(vii) The National Register of Industrial Property Rights Transfer;
(viii) National register of industrial property representatives.
b) The national register specified in Points 19.1.a (i), (ii), (iii), (iv), (v) and (vi) above includes sections corresponding to each protection title, each section including:
(i) Information on protection titles: number and date of grant of protection title; name of protected subject matter, scope/quantity of protection, validity period; name and address of owner of protection title/industrial geographical indication registrant, name and address of inventor, industrial design designer, industrial design type; (ii) Information on application for grant of protection title (application number, date of filing, priority date of application, name of industrial property representative service organization, if any);
(iii) All information on the modification of protection titles, the validity of protection titles (maintenance of validity, extension of validity, termination of validity, cancellation of validity); transfer of ownership or use rights to industrial property objects; the number, date of issuance and the person who issues copies or reissues protection titles.
c) The national register specified in Point 19.1.a (vii) above includes sections corresponding to each registered industrial property right transfer contract, specifically:
(i) Information on the certificate of registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights (number, date of issuance);
(ii) Information on the registered contract (contract name, date of signing, place of signing, names and addresses of the transferring and receiving parties, subject matter of transfer, scope of transfer);
(iii) Information on changes related to the contract (amendments, supplements, extensions, termination, or cancellation of the contract);
(iv) Information on transfer and termination of transfer of right to use invention under compulsory decisions.
d) The national register specified in point 19.1.a (viii) above includes sections corresponding to each industrial property representative service provider, specifically:

(i) Information on industrial property representative service organizations (full names, transaction names, addresses, registration, deletion of names, modification of information on industrial property representative service organizations); (ii) Information on list of industrial property representatives of organizations (full names, permanent addresses, number of practice certificates of each member in the list);
(iii) Information on the change of the list of industrial property representatives (new, re-issuance, revocation of practice certificates...).
e) National registers shall be made and kept by the National Office of Intellectual Property in paper, electronic or other forms. Any person may search electronic registers or request the National Office of Intellectual Property to issue copies or extracts of registers, provided that a fee for the copy is paid.

19.2 Publication of decisions on granting protection titles
a) All decisions on granting protection titles and decisions on acceptance of international registration shall be published by the National Office of Intellectual Property on the Industrial Property Gazette within 2 months from the date of issuance of such decisions, after the applicants have paid the publication fees as prescribed.
b) Information disclosed under Point 19.2.a above includes information stated in the corresponding decisions: invention summaries; sets of photographs or industrial design drawings; trademark samples and lists of goods and services bearing trademarks; geographical indications and products bearing geographical indications.
20. To amend or change the title of a title, narrow the scope of protection, maintain or extend the validity of a title of protection.
20.1 Recording changes to the name and address of the holder of the protection title, narrowing the scope of protection and amending the description of the characteristics, quality or reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, map of the corresponding geographical indication, collective mark use regulations, certification mark use regulations
a) Request for amendment or change to the holder of the protection title
The holder of the protection title may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to record changes to the protection title in the following cases:
(i) Change to the name and address of the holder of the protection title;
(ii) Change to the holder of the protection title (transfer of ownership rights by inheritance, succession, merger, division, joint venture, alliance, establishment of a new entity of the same owner, change in business form or according to the decision of a competent authority).
The applicant for recording changes to the name and address, change to the holder of the protection title shall pay the fee for amendment of the protection title, the fee for announcement of the decision on recording of amendment of the protection title.
b) Request for narrowing the scope of protection and amending the description of the characteristics, quality or reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, map of the corresponding geographical indication, collective mark use regulations, certification mark use regulations
The request for narrowing the scope of protection according to the provisions of Clause 3 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property may include one of the following contents:
(i) Request for modification of certain details but not significantly changing the trademark sample stated in the Certificate of Trademark Registration;
(ii) Request for reduction of one or some goods or services or groups of goods or services in the list of goods or services in the Certificate of Trademark Registration without changing the trademark form;
(iii) To request the reduction of one or more independent or dependent points within the scope (request) of protection specified in the patent or utility solution patent;
(iv) To request the removal of one or some industrial design schemes, one or some products in the set of products specified in the industrial design monopoly certificate; to request the removal of one or some basic design features of the industrial design.
c) Written request for modification of the protection title
Depending on the contents to be amended or supplemented in Point 20.1.a and Point 20.1.b above and Point b Clause 1 Article 97 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the written request shall include the following documents: (i) A declaration of request for amendment of protection title, in which clearly state the request for recognition of changes to the name and address of the protection title holder, change of protection title holder, request for narrowing the scope of protection or amendment of the description of characteristics/quality/reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, map of the corresponding geographical area, regulations on use of collective marks, regulations on use of certification marks, made according to Form 01-SĐVB specified in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Original of the protection title;

(iii) Documents certifying the change of name and address (original or certified copies of the decision on name and address change; business registration license bearing the change of name and address; other legal documents proving the change of name and address) - if the request for amendment is for name and address; (iv) Documents proving the transfer of ownership rights as prescribed in Point 20.1.a (ii) above, if the request is for change of the title holder (documents proving inheritance, merger, division, joint venture, association, establishment of a new entity of the same owner, change of business form or according to the decision of a competent regulatory authority);
(v) Detailed explanatory documents on the contents of the amendments;
(vi) 05 revised trademark samples (if the trademark is requested to be amended in detail); 05 sets of photographs or industrial design drawings (if the industrial design is requested to be amended); 02 descriptions of the properties/qualities/reputation of the product bearing the geographical indication, maps of the geographical area corresponding to the amended geographical indication (if the geographical indication is requested to be amended); 02 regulations on the use of collective marks, regulations on the use of certified marks that have been amended (if the collective mark or certified mark is requested to be amended);
(vii) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
(viii) Evidence of payment of fees for amendment of protection titles, fees for examination of the contents of the application for reduction of the scope of protection, fees for publication of the decision on amendment and registration according to regulations.
An application for amendment of a protection title may relate to multiple protection titles if they have the same amendment, provided that the applicant must pay fees for each protection title.
d) Processing of applications for amendment of protection titles
Within 01 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider the application for amendment of the protection title. If the application is deemed valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on amendment of the protection title, register it and publish the decision on amendment of the protection title in the Industrial Property Gazette. In the opposite case, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall send the applicant a notice of the intention to refuse the amendment, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 01 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide objections. If within the set time limit the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies or remedies the deficiencies do not meet the requirements, or there are no objections or the objections are not reasonable, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice refusing the application for amendment of the protection title.
20.2 Remedying deficiencies in protection titles
a) In case of detecting any deficiencies in the protection title, the Intellectual Property Office shall, on its own or at the request of the person detecting the deficiency, withdraw the protection title with deficiencies and issue a new protection title.
b) The holder of a protection title shall pay the fee for amendment of the protection title as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 97 of the Intellectual Property Law if the deficiency is due to fault of the holder of the protection title. If the deficiency is due to fault of the Intellectual Property Office, the holder of the protection title shall not pay the fee for the amendment.
20.3 Maintenance of validity of invention protection titles
20.4 Extension of validity of protection titles The holder of a protection title must pay a maintenance fee within 06 months before the expiration date. The maintenance fee may be paid later than the above-mentioned date, but not later than 06 months after the expiration date, and the holder of the protection title must pay an additional 10% of the maintenance fee for each month of delay.
a) The invention patent, utility solution patent, and design registration certificate shall not be renewed. The industrial design patent shall be renewed for a maximum of 2 consecutive times, each time for 5 years. The trademark registration certificate may be renewed for a maximum of 10 consecutive times, each time for 10 years.
b) Within 06 months before the expiration date of the Industrial Design Exclusive Right Certificate or the Trademark Registration Certificate, the owner of the Industrial Design Exclusive Right Certificate or the owner of the Trademark Registration Certificate shall submit an application for extension to the National Office of Intellectual Property.
An extension request may be submitted later than the above-mentioned time limit but must not exceed 06 months from the date on which the protection title expires, and the holder of the protection title must pay the extension fee plus 10% of the extension fee for each month of delay.
c) Application for extension
An application for extension of validity of a protected title shall include the following documents:

(i) An application for extension of validity of a protected title, made according to Form 02-GHVB specified in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Original copy of the protection title (if the extension is requested to be recorded in the protection title); (iii) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
(iv) Documents on payment of extension fees, announcement of extension decisions and registration of extension decisions on protection titles according to regulations.
d) Processing of extension requests
The Intellectual Property Office shall consider the extension application within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application. If the application is without any defects, the Intellectual Property Office shall issue an extension decision, record it in the protection title, register and publish it in the Industrial Property Gazette.
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse extension, clearly stating the reasons and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice for the applicant to remedy deficiencies or provide objections, if the application for extension falls into one of the following cases:
(i) The application for extension is invalid or submitted in violation of regulations;
(ii) The applicant for extension is not the owner of the corresponding protection title.
If, within the prescribed time limit, the applicant fails to rectify the deficiencies or fails to rectify the deficiencies to the satisfaction of the authority, or if there is no objection or an unjustified objection, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a notice of refusal to grant the extension.
21. Termination and cancellation of protection titles
21.1 Request for termination or cancellation of protection titles
To terminate or invalidate the protection title in accordance with Article 95 and Article 96 of the Intellectual Property Law and this Point.
21.2 Written request for termination or cancellation of protection titles
a) In a document, it is possible to request the termination or cancellation of the effect of one or more protection titles if the same reason applies, provided that the applicant must pay the fees according to regulations for each protection title.
b) Written request for termination or cancellation of protection titles, including the following documents: (i) Declaration of request for termination or cancellation of protection titles, made according to Form 04-CDHB specified in Appendix C of this Circular;
(ii) Evidence (if any);
(iii) Power of attorney (if submitting a written request through a representative);
(iv) Written explanation of the request (clearly stating the number of titles, reasons, legal grounds, and the request for termination or cancellation of part or all of the protection title’s validity) and relevant documents as prescribed in Points 7.2, 22.2, and 22.3 of this Circular;
(v) Receipts for payment of fees and charges as prescribed.
21.3 Handling of requests for termination or cancellation of protection title validity
a) In case a third party requests the termination or cancellation of a protection title’s validity, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the protection title holder in writing of the third party’s opinion, setting a time limit of 2 months from the date of notification for the protection title holder to provide comments. The National Office of Intellectual Property may organize a direct discussion between the third party and the protection title holder.
b) Based on the opinions of the parties, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to terminate or cancel part or all of the protection title’s validity or notify the refusal to terminate or cancel the protection title’s validity as prescribed in Clause 4 Article 95 and Clause 4 Article 96 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
c) If the requester or relevant party disagrees with the National Office of Intellectual Property’s decision to handle the request for termination or cancellation of the protection title’s validity, they may lodge a complaint against the decision or relevant notification according to the procedures prescribed in Point 22 of this Circular.
d) Decision on termination or cancellation of validity of protection titles published in the Official Gazette of industrial property and recorded in the National Register of industrial property within 02 months from the date of signing the decision.
21.4 Termination and cancellation of international registration of a mark

a) For the application for termination or cancellation of the effect of a third party against an international trademark registration under the Madrid Agreement or the Madrid Protocol, the Intellectual Property Office shall notify the application to the trademark owner through the International Office, within 03 months from the date of notification, for the trademark owner to provide comments.
b) The decision to terminate or cancel the international registration of the mark shall be sent to the International Office for the performance of relevant procedures according to the corresponding provisions of the Madrid Agreement or the Madrid Protocol and published in the Industrial Property Gazette.
22. Complaints and settlement of complaints related to procedures for establishment of industrial property rights 22.1 Persons entitled to lodge complaints, complainants and time limits for complaints Persons entitled to lodge complaints specified in Clause 1 Article 14, within the time limit specified in Clause 4 Article 14 of the Decree on industrial property, may lodge complaints against official notices and decisions of the National Office of Intellectual Property related to procedures for establishment of industrial property rights.

22.2 Complaint forms
a) Each complaint shall refer to one decision or notice that is complained about. A complaint may also refer to multiple decisions or notices if they have the same content and reasons for complaint, provided that the complainant shall pay the complaint fee according to regulations for each decision or notice that is complained about.
b) The complaint must include the following documents:
(i) A written complaint made according to Form 05-KN specified in Appendix C of this Circular; (ii) A written explanation of the complaint and evidence proving the grounds for the complaint (clearly stating the reasons, legal grounds, contents of the complaint, list of attached evidence, if any);
(iii) Copies of the decision or notice of complaint of the National Office of Intellectual Property;
(iv) A copy of the first-instance decision on complaint settlement (for the second complaint); (v) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
(vi) Documents on payment of fees and charges related to complaints as prescribed.
c) Evidence is documents (evidence) or material evidences (evidence) used to prove and clarify the grounds of the complaint. Evidence must satisfy the following requirements:
(i) Evidence may be documents in foreign languages, provided that they are accompanied by Vietnamese translations in case the competent person handling the complaint requests it;
(ii) In case the evidence is a document made by an individual or organization without a valid seal or by a foreign individual or organization, the signature must be certified by a notary or a competent authority;
(iii) In case the evidence is information carriers (products, tapes, etc.), depending on each case, the origin and time of release and publication of the above-mentioned documents must be specified, or the origin and time of publication of the information displayed on such information carriers must be specified;
(iv) The evidence must be accompanied by documents clearly describing the characteristics directly related to the complaint.

22.3 Responsibilities of the complainant
The complainant must provide evidence honestly and be responsible for the consequences of providing dishonest evidence.
22.4 Withdrawal of complaint
a) At any time, the complainant may send a written notice of withdrawal of the complaint. If the withdrawal of the complaint is made by the industrial property representative service organization, the authorization for withdrawal of the complaint must be clearly stated in the authorization document.
b) The withdrawn application shall be deemed as not submitted. The complainant shall not be entitled to a refund of the complaint application and the complaint fees and charges paid.
22.5 Handling of complaints
a) Within 10 days from the date of receipt of the complaint, the person competent to settle the complaint shall examine the complaint according to the requirements on form and issue a written notice to the complainant on whether the complaint is accepted or not, in which the date of acceptance of the complaint or the reason for not accepting the complaint shall be recorded.
b) Complaints shall not be accepted if they fall into one of the following cases:
(i) The complainant has no right to complain;

(ii) Complaints filed outside the statute of limitations;
(iii) The complaint does not satisfy the requirements specified in Points 22.1 and 22.2 of this Circular.

22.6 Relevant parties
a) For complaints that have been accepted, the competent person shall notify in writing the contents of the complaint to the person directly concerned with the complaint (“related party”) and set a time limit of 01 month from the date of notification for the latter to give his/her opinion.
b) The concerned parties shall have the right to provide information and evidence to prove their reasons within the time limit specified in Point 22.6.a above, and the persons competent to settle complaints shall have to consider such information and evidence when settling the complaints.
c) If the concerned parties do not have any comments after the above-mentioned time limit, the complaint shall be settled based on the comments of the complainant.
22.7 Decision on handling complaints
a) Based on the arguments and evidence of the complainant and the concerned parties, the competent person shall issue a decision on handling the complaint within the time limit for handling complaints as prescribed by law on complaints.
b) Before issuing a decision on handling a complaint, the person competent to handle the complaint shall notify the complainant and the concerned party of the arguments and evidence used by the other party to handle the complaint, as well as the conclusion on handling the complaint.
c) The decision on handling the complaint must contain the contents prescribed by the law on complaints.

22.8. The decision on handling the complaint shall be published in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision.
22.9 Effect of decision on handling of complaint
Any procedure for establishing industrial property rights depending on the outcome of complaint settlement shall be carried out on the basis of:
a) The first-instance decision on handling the complaint, if the complainant fails to file a second complaint after the expiration of the second time limit for filing a complaint and fails to file an administrative lawsuit after the expiration of the time limit for filing an administrative lawsuit; or b) The decision on handling the second complaint, if the complainant fails to file an administrative lawsuit within the prescribed time limit or according to the effective court decision if the complainant files an administrative lawsuit.
Section 2. PROCEDURES FOR INVENTION REGISTRATION
23. Requirements for an invention registration application
23.1 An invention application (application) must satisfy the general requirements specified in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements specified in this Point.
23.2 The application must clearly specify the protected subject matter as a product or process in accordance with Clause 12 Article 4 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
23.3 The application must comply with the provisions of Clause 1 and Clause 2 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following guidelines.
An application shall be considered consistent if:
a) To request protection of a single object; or b) To request protection of a group of objects having a common technical relationship and showing a common inventive intent, falling into the following cases:
(i) An object used to create (produce, manufacture, prepare) another object;
(ii) An object used to perform another object;
(iii) An object used for the use of another object;
(iv) Objects of the same kind and function to ensure the same results.

23.4 In case of grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of information in the application, the Intellectual Property Office may request the applicant to submit documents to verify such information within 1 month from the date of issuance of the request notice, especially documents confirming the lawful registration rights if the applicant is entitled to the application rights of another person (inheritance rights certificate, certificate or agreement on transfer of application rights; job assignment contract or labor contract, etc.); documents showing the results of drug testing on human, animal or plant bodies mentioned in the description (when the object of protection is a drug for human, animal or plant use).
23.5 Requirements for the declaration
The applicant shall submit 02 copies of the application form according to Form 01-SC specified in Appendix A of this Circular. In the section “International classification” of the application form, the applicant shall specify the classification number of the technical solution to be protected according to the latest International Classification of Patents (according to the Strasbourg Convention) published by the National Office of Industrial Property on the Official Gazette of Industrial Property. If the applicant fails to classify or classifies incorrectly, the National Office of Industrial Property shall classify the application and the applicant shall pay the classification service fee as prescribed.
23.6 Requirements for the invention description
The applicant shall submit 02 copies of the invention description. The invention description must include the description of the invention and the scope of protection of the invention.
a) The description section of the invention description must fully disclose the nature of the registered technical solution. The description must contain sufficient information so that any person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field can implement the solution; the novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability of the technical solution must be clarified (if the protection title granted is an invention patent); the novelty and industrial applicability of the technical solution must be clarified (if the protection title granted is an utility solution patent).
An individual with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field is understood as an individual with ordinary technical practical skills and clear knowledge of common knowledge in the corresponding technical field.
b) The description must include the following contents:
(i) The title of the invention: a brief description of the registered objects (hereinafter referred to as “objects”); the title of the invention must be concise and not exaggerated or promotional; (ii) The field of use of the invention: the field in which the objects are used or related; (iii) The technical state of the field of use of the invention: the technical state of the said field at the time of filing (similar known objects, if any);
(iv) Technical nature of the invention: the nature of the object, which must clearly state the signs (features) that make up the object and must indicate the signs (features) that are new compared to the known technical solutions;
(v) A brief description of the enclosed drawings (if any);
(vi) A detailed description of the inventive solutions;
(vii) Examples of the inventive solutions;
(viii) The benefits (efficacy) that can be achieved.
c) Scope of protection of the invention (hereinafter referred to as “scope of protection” or “protection request”) The scope (request) of protection is used to determine the scope of industrial property rights to the invention. The scope (request) of protection must be presented concisely and clearly, in accordance with the description and drawings, in which the new features of the protected subject matter (hereinafter referred to as “subject matter”) must be clarified and must comply with the following regulations:
d) The scope (request) of protection must be fully illustrated by the description, including the basic technical features necessary and sufficient to identify the subject matter, to achieve the set objectives and to distinguish the subject matter from the prior art.
e) The technical features in the scope (request) of protection must be clear, precise and accepted in the corresponding technical field.
g) The scope (request) of protection must not refer to the description and drawings, except for cases where it refers to parts that cannot be accurately described in words, such as nucleotide sequences and amino acid sequences, diffraction patterns, state diagrams, etc.
h) If the application contains drawings illustrating the protection request, the signs specified within the protection scope (request) may be accompanied by reference numbers, but they must be enclosed in brackets. These reference numbers shall not be considered as limiting the protection scope (request).
i) The scope (claim) of protection shall (but not necessarily) be expressed in two parts: the "limited part" and the "different part", in which: the "limited part" includes the name of the subject matter and the signs of that subject matter which coincide with the signs of the nearest known subject matter and are connected to the "different part" by the phrase "different in" or "characterized by" or equivalent words; the "different part" includes the signs different from the nearest known subject matter and these signs combined with the signs of the "limited part" constitute the subject matter of the protection claim.
k) The scope (claim) of protection may include one or more points. The scope (claim) of protection of multiple points may be used to represent an object to be protected, with the first point (called the independent point) and the subsequent points (points) used to specify the independent point (called the dependent point); or to represent a group of objects to be protected, with a number of independent points, each independent point representing an object to be protected in that group, each of which may have dependent points.
l) Points of the scope (claim) of protection must be numbered in Arabic numerals, followed by a dot.
m) The scope (claims) of protection of multiple points used to represent a group of objects must satisfy the following requirements: the independent points must represent separate objects and must not refer to other points of the scope (claims) of protection, except for cases where such reference allows the complete avoidance of repetition of the contents of other points; dependent points must be immediately after the independent points to which they depend.
23.7 Requirements for invention abstracts
The applicant shall submit 2 invention summaries. An invention summary is a brief description (not more than 150 words) of the nature of the invention. The summary must disclose the main contents of the technical solution for information purposes. The summary may include drawings and distinctive formulas.
23.8 Additional regulations on applications for biotechnology-related inventions
a) Apart from the general requirements for the description of the invention specified in point 23.6 of this Circular, for an application for registration of an invention relating to a gene sequence or a part of a gene sequence, the description must include a list of the gene sequence expressed according to the WIPO ST.25, section 2(ii) (the standard for expression of the list of nucleotide sequences and amino acid sequences in the application for registration of the invention).
b) The National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant to submit an electronic data carrier (e.g. floppy disk, optical disk) readable by common electronic devices, which contains the nucleotide sequence and amino acid sequence identical to the list of the sequence specified in the description.
c) For inventions relating to biological materials which cannot be described or cannot be fully described to the extent that a person with average knowledge in the field of biotechnology can perform them, the invention shall be considered fully disclosed only if the following conditions are satisfied: (i) The biological material sample has been submitted for deposit at a competent depositing agency in accordance with point 23.9 of this Circular no later than the date of filing of the application;
(ii) The description clearly states the necessary information about the properties of the biological material which the applicant can obtain;
(iii) The declaration clearly states the depositing agency of the biological material, the depositing number of the biological material sample submitted for deposit by the depositing agency and documents certifying this information have been submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property within 16 months from the priority date, or no later than the date of filing of the request for early publication of the application (if any) depending on which date is earlier, except for the case specified in point 23.9.d of this Circular.
d) In case the applicant is not the biological material depositor, the declaration must clearly state the name and address of the depositor and documents certifying the lawful use of the biological material must be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property within 16 months from the priority date, or no later than the date of filing of the request for early publication of the application (if any) depending on which date is earlier, except for the case specified in point 23.9.d of this Circular.
23.9 Submission of biological material samples for deposit
a) The purpose of submitting biological material samples for deposit is to serve the process of examination of the contents of the application for registration of the invention relating to biological materials.
b) Biopsies must be submitted to the competent authority for biopreservation no later than the date of filing the application for registration of the invention relating to such biopreservation.
c) The competent authority for biopreservation is an agency in Vietnam or a foreign country designated or recognized by the Ministry of Science and Technology for the function of biopreservation.
d) The submission of biopreservation samples and the documents certifying the international patent application shall comply with the Convention on the Protection of Industrial Property (PCT).

e) For biopreservation samples submitted for biopreservation at a foreign agency, the National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant to submit additional biopreservation samples at a competent authority in Vietnam if it deems it necessary to clarify the nature of the subject matter of protection or to satisfy the requirements of a third party for access to such subject matter.
23.10 Additional provisions for applications for registration of inventions relating to pharmaceuticals In addition to the general requirements for the invention description specified in Point 23.6 of this Circular, for applications for registration of inventions relating to pharmaceuticals, the description section must state the results of clinical trials and the pharmacological effects of the pharmaceutical, including at least the following information:
a) The substance or mixture used;
b) The method (system) of testing used;
c) The results of the testing.
d) Correlation between the pharmacological effects obtained in the experiment and the actual application of the pharmaceutical in the diagnosis and treatment of diseases.
23.11 Additional requirements for patent applications related to genetic resources or traditional knowledge
In addition to the general requirements for patent applications specified in Points 23.1 through 23.7 of this Circular, a patent application related to genetic resources or traditional knowledge must also include a document explaining the origin of the genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge that the inventor or applicant has accessed, if the patent directly relies on such genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge. If the inventor or applicant cannot identify the origin of the genetic resources and/or traditional knowledge, such must be clearly stated and the applicant shall be responsible for the truthfulness of such statement.
24. Formal examination and publication of patent applications
The procedures for formal examination and publication of patent applications shall comply with the general procedures specified in Points 13 and 14 of this Circular.
25. Examination of patent application contents
25.1 Requirements for examination of patent application contents
a) The applicant or any third party may request the Intellectual Property Office to examine the contents of the patent application in accordance with Article 113 of the Intellectual Property Law and shall be specified as follows:
(i) The request for assessment of the contents of the invention registration application must be made in writing according to Form 03-YCTD specified in Appendix B of this Circular or in the invention registration application (if the applicant is the applicant and the request is made immediately upon filing the application); (ii) The request for assessment of the contents of the invention registration application must be filed within 42 months from the priority date, if the application includes a request for issuance of an invention patent, or within 36 months from the priority date if the request includes a request for issuance of a utility solution patent; The time limit for filing a request for assessment of the contents may be extended, but not more than 06 months if there are valid reasons;
(iii) The applicant for examination of the contents of the invention application shall pay the search fee and the examination fee as prescribed; if the examination request is submitted after the fixed time limit, the applicant shall pay an additional extension fee as prescribed in Point 20.4.b of this Circular; if the examination fee is not paid, the examination request submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property shall be considered invalid and the National Office of Intellectual Property shall not conduct the examination of the contents of the application.
b) To request the assessment of the contents of the invention application filed after the date of publication of the application on the Industrial Property Gazette in the second month from the date of receipt of the request; if such request is made by a third party, the applicant shall be notified thereof.
The request for assessment of the contents of the invention application filed before the date of publication of the application shall be published together with the corresponding application.
c) If there is no request for content assessment within the time limit specified in Point 25.1.a above, the application shall be considered withdrawn at the end of that time limit.
25.2 Order of carrying out procedures for content assessment
The examination of the contents of the invention registration application shall be conducted in accordance with the general order prescribed in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific provisions of this Point.
25.3. Assessing the compatibility between the subject matter specified in the application and the type of invention protection title

a) The subject matter specified in the invention application shall be considered not suitable for the type of invention protection title granted to the applicant (invention patent/efficacy solution patent) if the subject matter is not a technical solution, specifically not a product or process. The identification of a technical solution is specified in point 25.3.b below.
b) The technical solution or protected object under the name of an invention is a complete set of information on the technical method and/or technical means for solving a specific task (problem).
Technical solutions may take one of the following forms:
(i) Products in the form of objects (tools, machines, equipment, components, circuits, etc.) which are represented by a set of information identifying an artificial product characterized by signs (features) of structure, the product having a function (use) as a means to satisfy a certain human need; or products in the form of substances (materials, substances, food, pharmaceuticals, etc.) which are represented by a set of information identifying an artificial product characterized by signs (features) of the presence, proportion and state of elements, having a function (use) as a means to satisfy a certain human need; or products in the form of biological materials (genes, genetically modified plants/animals, etc.) which are represented by a set of information about a product containing genetically modified genetic information under human influence, capable of self-reproduction;
(ii) The process (technology process; diagnostic, forecasting, inspection, processing methods...) is expressed by a set of information that defines the way a specific process or job is carried out, characterized by signs (features) of order, conditions, participating components, measures, and means for performing operations in order to achieve a specific purpose.
c) The subject matter stated in the application shall not be considered a technical solution in the following cases: (i) The subject matter stated in the application is only an idea or intention, only stating (posing) the problem without being a solution to the problem, and cannot answer the question “how” or “by what means”; (ii) The problem (task) posed for solution is not a technical problem and cannot be solved by a technical method;
(iii) Natural products, which are not human-made products.
25.4 Assessment of applicability of industrial property in accordance with Article 62 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) The technical solution specified in the application is considered “feasible” if:
(i) Information on the nature of the solution together with instructions on necessary technical conditions shall be presented in a clear and complete manner, allowing a person with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field to create, produce, use, exploit or implement the solution;
The term “person with average knowledge of the corresponding technical field” shall be construed in accordance with Point 23.6.a of this Circular;
(ii) The creation, production, use, exploitation or implementation of the above-mentioned solutions may be repeated with the same results and similar to the results stated in the invention description.
b) Technical solutions considered incapable of industrial application in the following cases: (i) The nature of the object or the indications for the realization of the object go against the basic principles of science (e.g. do not comply with the principle of conservation of energy...);
(ii) The objects include elements or components that are not technically related to each other or cannot be related (coupled, bound, dependent, etc.) to each other;
(iii) The subject contains internal contradictions;
(iv) Only the instructions on the subject matter can be carried out within a certain number of times (cannot be repeated);
(v) The person who implements the solution must have special skills which cannot be taught or transferred to others;
(vi) Results obtained from different times are inconsistent with each other;
(vii) The results obtained are different from those stated in the application;
(viii) There are no or insufficient important instructions for implementing the solution; (ix) Other valid reasons.
25.5 Assessment of novelty according to Article 60 of the Law on Intellectual Property

a) Minimum mandatory information sources
To assess the novelty of the technical solution stated in the application, it is necessary to search for information in the following mandatory sources (but not limited to these sources): (i) All patent applications received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application being examined - up to the third level classification index and having a publication date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined;
(ii) Invention registration applications or invention protection titles published by other organizations or countries within 25 years before the date of filing or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority) shall be kept in the invention database at the National Office of Intellectual Property and other information sources specified by the National Office of Intellectual Property, within the scope of search specified in point 25.5.a (i) above.
Where necessary and possible, the search shall be extended to scientific reports, reports on results of programs and research projects and other documents of the same technical field that are published and kept at the National Science and Technology Information Center.
b) Purpose of search
The purpose of the search is to find technical solutions that are similar or identical to the technical solutions stated in the application.
In this point:
(i) Two technical solutions are considered identical when all the basic signs (features) are identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
(ii) Two technical solutions are considered similar when most of the basic signs (features) are identical or equivalent (interchangeable);
(iii) “Contrast technical solution” means the technical solution that is identical or most similar to the technical solution stated in the application;
(iv) “Contrast document” means a document that describes the contrast technical solution or evidence that proves that the contrast technical solution has been publicly disclosed.
c) Report on search
The search results must be presented in the search report, which must clearly state the search field, scope, and search results within that scope (statistics of found technical countermeasures, clearly stating the identical signs, names of the countermeasures, page numbers, line numbers, source of the documents and the date of publication of the corresponding documents) and must state the full name of the person who makes the report (the searcher).
d) How to assess the novelty of technical solutions
To assess the novelty of the technical solution stated in the application, it is necessary to compare the basic signs (features) of that technical solution with the signs of the control technical solution found during the information search; where:
(i) The basic signs of a technical solution may be its functional characteristics, utility, structure, connections, components, etc., together with other basic signs that form a set of necessary and sufficient signs to determine the nature (content) of the object;
(ii) The basic signs of the technical solution specified in the application, in the protection titles, which are expressed within the scope of the invention protection (claim);
(iii) The basic signs of the technical solution specified in other documents are expressed and detected according to the description or actual form of that technical solution.
e) Conclusion on the novelty of the technical solution
Corresponding to a point of the scope (claim) of protection, the technical solution specified in the application shall be considered new with respect to the world technical level if:
(i) There is no prior technical solution found in the information search; or (ii) There is a prior technical solution found but the technical solution specified in the application has at least one basic feature not present in the prior technical solution (and this feature is called a different basic feature).
25.6 Assessment of inventive level according to Article 61 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) Minimum information sources

When assessing the inventive level of a technical solution, information must be searched in at least the minimum information sources (but not limited to these sources) specified in Point 25.5.a of this Circular.
b) Assessment of inventive level
The assessment of the inventive level of the technical solution specified in the application shall be carried out by assessing the different basic features specified in the scope (claim) of protection to draw the conclusion that: (i) The different basic features are not considered disclosed in the minimum information sources or;
(ii) The set of different basic features is not considered obvious to a person of ordinary skill in the corresponding technical field.
An inventive solution is considered to be of inventive level if the introduction of a basic sign different from the rest of the set of basic signs of the technical solution is the result of an inventive activity and not an obvious result of ordinary knowledge in the corresponding technical field.
c) In the following cases (but not limited to these cases), a technical solution is considered to be of non-inventive level:
(i) The set of basic signs is obvious (any person with average knowledge in the corresponding technical field knows that the set of signs must be used to perform the defined function or achieve the defined purpose, and vice versa, when using the set of signs, the corresponding purpose or function must be achieved); (ii) The set of basic signs is disclosed in the form of identity or equivalence in one or more known technical solutions in the mandatory minimum information source; (iii) The technical solution is a simple combination of known technical solutions with the same function, purpose and effectiveness, or a simple combination of the function, purpose and effectiveness of each known technical solution.
d) In this point:
(i) Two signs are considered identical if they have the same nature;
(ii) Two signs are considered equivalent if they have similar nature and the same purpose and the same method of achieving the basic purpose.
25.7 Examination of the first application submission according to the provisions of Article 90 of the Law on Intellectual Property Before issuing a decision to grant a protection title, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the first application submission for an invention application that has been reviewed and concluded to meet the protection conditions, according to the following regulations:
a) Mandatory minimum information source
To check the first application submission principle, at least the following mandatory sources must be searched (but not limited to the minimum sources):
All invention registration applications received by the Intellectual Property Office (as of the examination date) having the same classification index as the classification index of the subject matter stated in the application being examined - up to the classification index (third level index) and having a filing date or priority date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority) and has not been published or has a publication date later than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined (if the application is entitled to priority);
b) The purpose of the search is to find (the) application(s) registering the same invention and determine the application with the earliest filing date or priority date;
c) In case of multiple applications registering the same invention, the protection title can only be granted to the valid application with the earliest priority date or filing date among the applications that meet the conditions for granting the protection title;
d) In case of multiple applications registering the same invention that meet the conditions for granting the protection title and have the same priority date or the same earliest filing date, the protection title can only be granted to one application among them according to the agreement of all applicants; otherwise, all applications will be rejected for protection title granting;
e) In case the applicant registering the invention requests to benefit from the priority based on the first application registering the same invention filed in Vietnam, the protection title can only be granted to the application registering the invention that requests to benefit from the priority and the first application filed in Vietnam is considered withdrawn.

25.8 Notification of the result of the examination of the application content
The notification of the result of the examination of the application content shall be carried out according to the general procedures specified in point 15.7.a of this Circular.

Grant, registration and publication of invention patents and utility solution patents The procedures for issuing decisions on grant, registration and publication of invention patents and utility solution patents shall comply with the general procedures specified in Points 18 and 19 of this Circular.
27. Handling of international patent applications
27.1. Receiving agencies
The competent authority for receipt of international patent applications in Vietnam is the Vietnam Intellectual Property Office. The Vietnam Intellectual Property Office shall:
a) Receive international applications originating from Vietnam;
b) Collect fees for submission of international applications and notify fees according to regulations to applicants for transfer to the International Office and the International Search Agency as prescribed by the Patent Cooperation Treaty (hereinafter referred to as “the Treaty”);
c) Check whether the fees are paid on time;
d) Examination and processing of international applications of Vietnamese origin in accordance with the Treaty;
e) Determination of the protected subject matter: If the protected subject matter of the application is a national secret, the next steps and fees shall not be carried out and the applicant shall be refunded the fees except for the deposit fee and the international application fee;
g) Send a copy (application) of the international document of Vietnamese origin to the International Office and a copy (search) to the international search agency;
h) Sending and receiving letters from applicants and international agencies.
27.2 Language
An international application originating in Vietnam submitted to the Intellectual Property Office must be made in English. Each application shall be made in 03 copies.
In case the number of copies is insufficient, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall make additional copies to meet the required number, and the applicant shall pay the fee for making additional copies of the international application.
27.3 International search agencies and international preliminary examination agencies For Vietnamese-originate international applications, the competent international search agencies and preliminary examination agencies are the invention, industrial property or intellectual property agencies of Austria, the Russian Federation, Sweden, South Korea and the European Patent Office.

27.4 International applications designated for Vietnam
a) If Vietnam is designated in the international application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall be the designated body. In this case, in order to enter the national stage, within 31 months from the date of priority, the applicant must submit to the National Office of Intellectual Property:
(i) An application for registration of an invention made according to Form 01-SC specified in Appendix A of this Circular; (ii) A copy of the international application (if the applicant requests the national phase before the international publication date);
(iii) Vietnamese translation of the international application: description, comprising description, protection request, illustrations and summary (original or initial publication, if the application has not been published, and amendments and explanations of amendments, if the international application is amended under Article 19 of the Treaty); (iv) National fees and charges.
b) An international application filed with the Intellectual Property Office within 06 months from the date of expiration of the time limit specified in Point 27.4.a above may be accepted, provided that the applicant pays the fees and charges as prescribed.
27.5 International applications with Vietnam as a choice
a) If Vietnam is selected in the international application, the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam shall be the selected agency. In this case, if the selection of Vietnam is made within 19 months from the priority date for entry into the national stage, within 31 months from the priority date, the applicant shall have to submit the following documents to the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam:
(i) An application for registration of an invention made according to Form 01-SC specified in Appendix A of this Circular;

(ii) Vietnamese translation of the international application: the description, comprising the description, the protection request, the description of the drawings and the summary (the initial announcement or the original, if the application has not been announced and the amendment and the explanation of the amendment, if the international application is amended under Article 19 and/or Article 34(2)(b) of the Treaty);
(iii) Vietnamese translation of the annexes of the preliminary international test report (when the test of the contents of the application is requested);
(iv) National fees and charges.
b) International applications filed with the Intellectual Property Office within 6 months from the date of expiry of the time limit specified in point 27.5.a above may be accepted, provided that the applicant pays the fees as prescribed.
27.6 Right of priority claims
To claim the right of priority, the applicant must reassert this in the application, pay the priority claim fee and, at the request of the Intellectual Property Office, submit the Vietnamese translation of the documents already submitted to the international office and the necessary documents under Rule 17.1(a) of the Treaty Implementation Regulation.
For PCT applications, the handling of the right of priority claim shall comply with the PCT Treaty and the Treaty Implementation Regulation.
27.7 Handling of international applications during the national phase
a) Modification and supplementation of documents during the national phase
In accordance with Rule 51bis of the Treaty Implementation Regulation, the applicant must submit a power of attorney and a transfer of the right to file the application during the national phase (if any) within 34 months from the date of priority.
In accordance with Article 28 and Article 41 of the Treaty and Rules 52.1(b) and 78.1(b) of the Treaty Implementation Regulations, the applicant may amend or supplement the application documents during the national stage. At the time of entering the national stage, the applicant may also amend or supplement the description. Such amendments must comply with the provisions of Point 17 of this Circular.
The additional or amended documents submitted by the applicant to the Intellectual Property Office must be made in Vietnamese.
b) Time of commencement of the national stage
The time of commencement of the national stage shall be the first day of the thirty-second month from the priority date, if the applicant does not submit a written request for early entry into the national stage before the above-mentioned time limits.
c) Examination of the international application
After entering the national stage, the international application shall be examined for form and content according to the procedures prescribed for a regular patent application. If the applicant submits a written request for examination before the time limit and pays the fee as prescribed, the international application shall be examined before the time limit specified in Point 27.7(b) above in accordance with Article 23(2) of the Treaty.
d) International application considered withdrawn
In addition to the cases of withdrawal as prescribed by the Treaty and the Treaty Implementation Regulations, the international application designated or selected for Vietnam shall be considered withdrawn if the national fee is not paid to the Intellectual Property Office or if the Vietnamese translation is not available after the prescribed time limit.
27.8 International registration fees and charges
a) An international applicant in the national stage shall pay fees and charges as prescribed for an application for an invention filed directly in Vietnam.
b) An international applicant of Vietnamese origin shall pay fees and charges as prescribed in the Treaty Implementation Regulations and the Circular guiding the collection, payment, management and use of industrial property fees and charges of the Ministry of Finance.

28. Requirements for a design patent application

28.2 The documents, specimens and information on the design patent specified in Point 7.1.a (ii) of this Circular include:

b) Manufacturing process samples according to the layout design, including 04 samples, if the layout design has been commercially exploited;
c) A description of the integrated production circuit according to the layout design, including information to clarify the nature of the layout design.
28.3 The application must ensure uniformity as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property, specifically requesting protection of only one layout design of a semiconductor integrated circuit.

The applicant shall submit 02 copies of the application form according to Form 02-TKBT specified in Appendix A of this Circular.

28.6 Requirements for the set of photographs and set of design drawings
a) General requirements: The set of photographs or drawings of layout design must fully show the spatial structure of the elements of the circuit and the connections between these elements in the integrated semiconductor circuit so that, based on the set of photographs or drawings, a unique layout design can be determined. For the purposes above, the set of photographs or drawings of layout design must satisfy the conditions specified in points 28.6.b, c and d below.
b) Kind of documents:
A set of photographs or drawings of layout design must include at least one of the following documents: (i) A drawing of layout design made by computer for each layer of integrated circuits;
(ii) Drawings or photographs of photolithography masks for manufacturing each layer of integrated circuits;
(iii) Photos of each layer of layout design shown in the integrated circuit;
c) Form of documents: A set of photographs or a set of design drawings must be submitted in paper form and may be accompanied by supplementary documents, which are electronic data carriers of a part or the whole set of photographs or design drawings.
d) Form of the document
(i) A set of photographs or drawings of layout design shall include separate sets of photographs or drawings for each layout design layer, accompanied by the symbol of each layer, the size of the integrated circuit and magnification; (ii) All photographs or drawings of layout design shall be at the same scale. For paper documents: each photograph or drawing of layout design must be at least 20 times magnified the size in the integrated circuit so that the basic circuit design can be seen by the naked eye;
(iii) Each photograph/drawing of layout design may be presented on A4 paper or larger paper size, provided that it is folded into A4 size;
(iv) Photographs or drawings of layout must be clear and sharp.
28.7 Requirements for manufacturing integrated circuits according to layout design
a) The submitted sample must be the whole or a part of the corresponding integrated circuit which is exactly the same as the layout design specified in the application. If the integrated circuit is produced according to the layout design and is an inseparable part of another product, the submitted product must be accompanied by a document accurately indicating the part of the integrated circuit produced according to that layout design.
b) If the design is commercially exploited anywhere in the world before the date of filing, the applicant shall have to submit the first such commercially exploited form.
28.8 Requirements for the description of semiconductor integrated circuits manufactured according to the design The description must include the following details about the semiconductor integrated circuits manufactured according to the design which are subject to the protection claim:
a) Name/Designation: a set of letters and/or numbers used to distinguish this integrated circuit from other integrated circuits when it is put into circulation on the market;

b) Description of the basic functions of the integrated circuit (e.g. memory or logic or other functions);
c) Description of the basic structure of the integrated circuit (e.g. bipolar or MOS, or Bi-MOS, or optoelectronic or other structure);
d) Description of the technology used to manufacture the integrated circuit (e.g. TTL or DTL or ECL or ITL or CMOS or NMOS or PMOS or other technology);
e) Description of the main characteristics which distinguish it from other semiconductor integrated circuits on the market at the time of filing or at the time of first commercial exploitation in the world, whichever is earlier.

c) A drawing or photograph with a part that is not visible, provided that the characteristics of the layout design are basically visible.

30. Examination of the form of application for registration of layout design

Section 4. PROCEDURES FOR INDUSTRIAL DESIGN REGISTRATION
33. Requirements for an application for registration of industrial design
33.1 An industrial design registration application must satisfy the general requirements specified in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements specified in this Point.
33.2 An application for registration of industrial design must comply with the provisions of Clause 1 and Clause 3 Article 101 of the Intellectual Property Law and the following regulations.
An industrial design application shall be considered uniform if:
a) To request protection of industrial design of a product; or b) To request protection of industrial design of a set of products, each of which has a corresponding industrial design; or c) To request protection of industrial design of a product together with one or more variants of that industrial design.
33.3 Information provision request
a) Where there are grounds (information, evidence) to doubt the authenticity of information in the application for industrial design registration, the Intellectual Property Office may request the applicant to submit documents verifying such information within 1 month, especially documents certifying the lawful registration rights if the applicant is entitled to the right to file the application of another person (inherited rights certificate, certificate or written agreement on transfer of the right to file the application; contract for assignment of work or labor contract...);
b) The National Office of Industrial Property may also request the applicant to submit within 01 month a document certifying the ownership or lawful use rights of a trade mark (trademark, geographical indication, trade name), industrial design protected by another person, if there is a reason to suspect that the industrial design specified in the application contains such objects.
33.4 Requirements for the application form
The applicant must submit 02 application forms according to Form 03-KDCN specified in Appendix A of this Circular. Apart from other required fields, the application form must specify the international classification index of the industrial design requested for protection according to the International Classification of Industrial Designs (according to the Locarno Convention). If the applicant fails to classify or classifies incorrectly, the National Office of Industrial Property will classify and the applicant must pay the classification fee as prescribed.
33.5 Requirements for the industrial design description
The applicant must submit 01 industrial design description, including the following contents:

a) Name of the industrial design: is the name of the product bearing the industrial design, expressed in common words, not for advertising purposes, not containing symbols, annotations, trade marks;
b) Field of use of the industrial design: is the specific field of use of the product bearing the industrial design, in which the purpose of use of such product is clearly stated;
c) Most similar industrial design: clearly states the industrial design least different from the industrial design of the same type of product specified in the application, which is widely known before the date of filing of the application or the priority date (if the application requests the right to priority), in which the source of information revealing such most similar industrial design must be indicated;
d) List of photographs or drawings: list of photographs, perspective drawings (three-dimensional), projections, sections, etc. of the industrial design, in accordance with the order of the photographs or drawings;
e) The description of the industrial design must comply with the following regulations:
(i) Fully disclose the nature of the industrial design to be protected, which must fully describe the design features that show the nature of the industrial design, and at the same time, indicate the new design features that are different from the closest similar industrial design specified in point 33.5.c above, in accordance with the design features shown in the set of photographs or drawings;
(ii) The distinctive features of the industrial design to be protected shall be presented in the following order: the three-dimensional features, the line features, the relationship between the three-dimensional features and/or line features, the color features (if any);
(iii) For products having different states of use (e.g. products with lids or foldable...), the industrial design of the product in different states must be described; (iv) If the industrial design comprises multiple solutions, the distinctive features of the remaining solutions compared with the basic solution (the first solution stated in the application) must be clearly specified; (v) If the industrial design is a design of a set of products, the design of each product in that set must be described.
g) Scope of industrial design protection (or “application for protection”): must fully list the design features necessary and sufficient to determine the nature of the industrial design application for protection and the scope of industrial property rights to the industrial design, which is shown in the photograph or drawing in the application, including the new design features, different from the known similar industrial design.

33.6 Requirements for the set of photographs or drawings of industrial design The applicant must submit 05 sets of photographs or 05 sets of drawings of industrial design. The set of photographs or drawings must fully show the design features of the industrial design application for protection to the extent that any person with average knowledge in the corresponding field can identify the industrial design and according to the following guidelines:
a) Photographs and drawings must be clear and sharp; drawings must be drawn with continuous lines; the background of photographs and drawings must have a uniform color and contrast with the industrial design; photographs and drawings must only show the product bearing the industrial design to be protected (not accompanied by other products).
b) Photographs and drawings must show the industrial design in the same scale. The size of the industrial design in photographs and drawings must not be smaller than 90mm x 120mm and not larger than 190mm x 277mm.
c) Photographs and drawings must show the industrial design in the same perspective and in the following order: a three-dimensional perspective of the industrial design, a front, rear, right, left, top and bottom view of the industrial design; the views must be frontal.
d) For industrial designs with symmetrical views, photographs and drawings may not need to show symmetrical views, provided that this is clearly stated in the list of photographs and drawings in the description.
e) For industrial designs of products that can be unfolded (e.g. boxes, packaging...), the views of the industrial design may be replaced by photographs and drawings of the industrial design in the unfolded state.
g) Depending on the complexity of the industrial design, additional photographs and perspective drawings from other angles, cross-sections, magnified parts, separate parts of the product... may be necessary to clearly show the new and distinctive features of the industrial design to be protected.
h) For products with different states of use (e.g. products with lids or foldable), photographs and drawings of the industrial design of the product in different states must be provided.

i) For industrial designs of parts of a complete product, additional photographs and drawings illustrating the position of installation and use of the part on the complete product must be provided.
k) Each solution of the industrial design must have a set of photographs and drawings fully showing each solution as prescribed in this point.
l) For a set of products, a perspective of the entire set of products and a set of photographs and drawings of each product in the set must be provided as prescribed in this point.
33.7 Characteristics of the industrial design's appearance
a) The characteristics of the shaping of an industrial design are elements expressed in the form of lines, shapes, colors, positional or size correlations, which, when combined with other characteristics (signs), form a complete set necessary to create that industrial design.
b) The following elements shall not be considered as characteristics of the industrial design: (i) The shape and lines are determined by the technical functions of the product (e.g. the flat shape of a data disk is determined by the relative movement between the disk and the read head...);
(ii) Elements whose presence in the set of signs is insufficient to create aesthetic impression (the impression of the product's shape does not change when such elements are present and when they are absent; for example, a change in a familiar shape or line is not sufficient to be recognized, so the changed shape or line is still recognized as the old one); (iii) Materials used to manufacture the product;
(iv) Signs attached, pasted... on products for the purpose of providing information on the origin, characteristics, structure, uses, and methods of use of such products; for example, words on the labels of goods;
(v) Size of the product, except for cases of changing the size of decorative elements of fabric patterns and similar materials.
c) Basic and non-basic design features Basic design features are those which are easily recognizable/remembered, necessary and sufficient for identifying industrial designs and distinguishing industrial designs from other industrial designs used for the same product.
The characteristics of posing that fail to satisfy the above conditions are called “basic posing characteristics.”
34. Examination of the form and publication of applications for registration of industrial designs The procedures for examination of the form and publication of applications for registration of industrial designs shall comply with the general procedures prescribed in Points 13 and 14 of this Circular.
35. Examination of the contents of applications for registration of industrial designs
35.1 Assessment of similarity of industrial designs:
a) Two industrial designs shall be considered identical when they are used for products of the same type and have the same set of basic and non-basic design features;
b) Two industrial designs shall be considered similar when they are used for products of the same type and have a number of basic design features in common;
c) Two industrial designs shall be considered most similar when the number of basic design features in common between the two similar industrial designs is greater than that of all other similar industrial designs.
35.2 Order of carrying out procedures for examination of contents of applications for registration of industrial designs The examination of contents of applications for registration of industrial designs (applications) shall be carried out in accordance with the general procedures prescribed in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific procedures prescribed in this Point.
35.3 Assessment of compatibility between the subject matter specified in the application and the type of industrial design protection title
An object specified in an application shall be considered not suitable for industrial design protection if:
a) It is not an external shape of a product;
b) The object specified in the application is:
(i) An external shape of a product that is required by the technical characteristics of the product; (ii) An external shape of a civil or industrial construction work;

(iii) An internal shape (unvisible part) during the use of the product (exploitation of the product's utility in a normal manner, performed by any user, excluding maintenance, repair or modification of the product).
35.4 Information search
a) Information search purposes
The purpose of information search is to search for industrial designs identical or similar to the industrial design specified in the application in the minimum information sources.
b) The minimum information sources required for the assessment of the application’s contents include the following documents:
(i) The industrial design registration applications received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and having a filing date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being assessed (if the application is entitled to priority);
(ii) The industrial design registration applications and industrial design protection titles published by other organizations or countries within 25 years before the filing date or priority date of the application being assessed (if the application is entitled to priority), which are stored in the existing industrial design database at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam;
(iii) Other information related to the industrial design collected and stored by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam;
(iv) The industrial design registration applications received by the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam and having a filing date or priority date (if the application is entitled to priority) earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being assessed (used to check the first-filing principle specified in Point 35.9 of this Circular).
c) In case of necessity and if possible, the search shall be expanded beyond the minimum information sources.
35.5 Search report
The search result shall be presented in a search report, which must clearly state the search field, scope, and search result within that scope (statistics and specify the found industrial designs, source of information, and date of publication of corresponding information), and must include the full name of the report preparer (the searcher).
35.6. Assessment of industrial applicability of industrial designs according to Article 67 of the Law on Intellectual Property
a) An industrial design stated in an application shall be considered industrially applicable if, based on information about the industrial design presented in the application, a person of average knowledge in the relevant field can use such industrial design as a model to manufacture a product with an external shape identical to such industrial design by industrial or artisanal methods.
The concept of “person of average knowledge” in the relevant field shall be understood in accordance with Article 23.6.a of this Circular.
b) In the following cases, the subject matter stated in the application shall be considered non-industrially applicable:
(i) The subject matter stated in the application is the shape of a product in an indefinite state (products in gaseous, liquid, etc. form);
(ii) Products with the shape of the subject matter stated in the application can only be created with special skills or the manufacturing of products with the shape of the subject matter stated in the application cannot be repeated;
(iii) Other cases with valid reasons.
35.7. Assessment of novelty of industrial designs according to Article 65 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
a) The assessment of novelty of industrial designs
To assess the novelty of the industrial design specified in the application, the set of basic design features of the industrial design must be compared with the set of basic design features of the closest identical or similar industrial designs used as control industrial designs, which are found during the information search process.

b) Conclusion on novelty of industrial design
An industrial design specified in the application shall be considered new if:
(i) The industrial design in question is not found in the mandatory minimum information source; or (ii) Although the industrial design in question is found in the mandatory minimum information source, the industrial design specified in the application has at least one basic design feature that is not present in (does not belong to) the set of basic design features of the industrial design in question; or (iii) The industrial design in question is the industrial design specified in the application that is published/ disclosed under the provisions of Clauses 3 and 4 Article 65 of the Intellectual Property Law.

35.8 Assessment of the inventive step of the industrial design under Article 66 of the Intellectual Property Law
a) The assessment of the creativity of industrial designs
To assess the creativity of the industrial design specified in the application, it is necessary to compare the set of basic design features of such industrial design with the set of basic design features of each identical or similar industrial design found during the information search.
b) Conclusion on the creativity of industrial designs
The industrial design specified in the application shall be considered non-creative in the following cases:
(i) Industrial design is a simple combination of known design features (design features that have been publicly disclosed are arranged or assembled in a simple way, such as replacement, repositioning, addition or removal of quantity...);
(ii) Industrial design is a shape that copies or simulates a part or whole of the natural shape of trees, fruits, animals, etc., or a shape of geometric figures (circle, ellipse, triangle, square, rectangle, regular polygon, prism with cross-section being the above figures) that is widely known;
(iii) Industrial design is the simple copying of the shape of products or works that are already famous or widely known in Vietnam or the world;
(iv) Industrial designs imitating industrial designs of other fields, if such imitation is widely known in practice (e.g., toy cars, motorcycles, etc.). If not falling into the above cases, the industrial design is considered to be creative.

35.9 Examination of the first application according to the provisions of Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law
a) To check the first application submission principle, information in the mandatory sources specified in Point 35.4.b (iv) of this Circular must be searched.
b) The industrial design specified in the application shall be considered as the first application if no identical or substantially different industrial design specified in applications that satisfy the conditions for granting of the industrial design exclusive certificate is found during the information search.
c) The industrial design of the product part specified in the application shall also be considered to satisfy the first application principle if no duplicate or significantly different industrial design of the product part and/or product specified in the applications satisfying the conditions for granting the industrial design exclusive certificate is found during the information search.
d) In case there are multiple applications for industrial designs that are identical or not significantly different from each other, all of which meet the conditions for granting industrial design exclusive rights and have the earliest priority date or application date, the industrial design specified in the application shall still be considered to satisfy the first application submission principle specified in Clause 2 Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law if all applicants reach an agreement on the applicant's name in a single application among these applications to be granted an industrial design exclusive right.
35.10 Notification of results of content examination
The notification of results of content examination of an industrial design registration application shall comply with the general provisions specified in Point 15.7.a of this Circular.
36. Granting, registration and publication of industrial design exclusive rights

The procedures for issuing decisions on granting, registering and publishing decisions on granting industrial design exclusive rights shall comply with the general procedures specified in Points 18 and 19 of this Circular.
Section 5. PROCEDURES FOR MARK REGISTRATION
37. Requirements for mark registration applications
37.1 A mark registration application must satisfy the general requirements for application documents specified in Points 7 and 10.1 of this Circular and the specific requirements specified in this Point.
37.2 An application must comply with the provisions of Clause 1 and Clause 4 Article 101 of the Law on Intellectual Property. Each application may only request registration of a trademark for one or more goods or services.

37.3 In case of doubt about the authenticity of the information stated in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property may request the applicant to submit the following documents within 1 month for verification of such information:
a) Documents proving the applicant’s eligibility:
(i) Business registration papers, contracts or other documents certifying the applicant’s production of products or provision of services as prescribed in Clause 1 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; (ii) Agreement or written confirmation that the producer does not use the trademark and does not object to the registration of the trademark of the person conducting the commercial activities of the producer’s products as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(iii) Decision or license for establishment, articles of association of an organization certifying the functions and competence to manage collective marks, quality certification marks, geographical origin certification marks of goods and services as prescribed in Clauses 3 and 4 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property; (iv) Agreement, business registration papers, documents related to the registration of marks of co-owners as prescribed in Clause 5 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(v) Documents certifying that the applicant is entitled to register a mark on behalf of another person as prescribed in Clause 6 Article 87 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(vi) Agreement or consent letter of the mark owner certifying the mark registration rights of the representative or agent as prescribed in Clause 7 Article 87 of the Intellectual Property Law and Article 69 of the Paris Convention on industrial property.
b) Documents proving the representative capacity of the applicant: the original power of attorney of the applicant; documents certifying that the applicant is the legal representative of the trademark registration organization or the person authorized by such organization; documents certifying that the authorized person of the applicant meets the requirements to act as the representative of the applicant as prescribed in Point 3 of this Circular.
c) Documents proving the right to use/registration of a mark containing special signs for: (i) Names, symbols, flags, emblems of domestic and international agencies or organizations or certification marks, inspection marks, warranty marks of international organizations as prescribed in Clause 2 and Clause 4 Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
(ii) The name of the character or image of the work under the protection of copyright is widely known or the trade name, trade mark, origin indication, award, medal or distinctive sign of a certain product is likely to cause confusion as prescribed in Clause 5, Article 73 of the Intellectual Property Law;
(iii) The sign under the protection of industrial design of another person as prescribed in Point n Clause 2 Article 74 of the Intellectual Property Law;
d) Documents proving the priority right;
e) Necessary information to clarify or affirm the contents stated in the collective mark use regulation, the certification mark use regulation or other application documents.
37.4 Requirements for the application
The applicant must submit 02 application forms according to Form 04-NH specified in Appendix A of this Circular with the following notes:
a) The description of the mark in the application must clearly specify the type of mark registered (common mark, collective mark, affiliation mark, certification mark);
b) For a mark registered as an affiliation mark, the applicant must clearly specify the affiliation elements of the mark or the goods or services as prescribed below:
(i) In case the linked element is a mark (similar to another mark of the applicant used for the same goods or services or for similar goods or services), it must be specified whether any of the linked marks is considered the basic mark, if any, and which mark is it; if one or some of these marks have been registered or have been stated in the previous application, the protection certificate number and the number of the previous application must be specified; (ii) In case the linked element is a good or service (a mark used for similar or related goods or services), it must be specified whether any of the goods or services is considered the basic one, if any, and which one is it; if one of these goods or services has been registered earlier or has been stated in the previous application, the protection certificate number and the number of the previous application must be specified;
(iii) If the applicant fails to specify the basic mark or basic goods and services, all marks and all goods and services related to the mark specified in the applicant’s application shall be considered independent of each other. The assessment of the distinctiveness of the mark specified in the application shall not be made on an exception basis for the associated mark specified in point e clause 2 Article 74 of the Intellectual Property Law, but shall comply with the general provisions on assessment of distinctiveness specified in point 39 of this Circular.
c) For certification marks, the applicant must specify in the application the purpose, contents and methods of the certification (what is certified: quality, origin, geographical origin or a combination of these purposes; contents of certification: specific conditions regarding the subject, goods or services; how the certification is made: the order and procedures for granting the certification, the methods of inspection and maintenance of the certification purposes and contents).
d) The declaration must include the trademark sample and a written description of the trademark in accordance with the following regulations:
(i) If a mark is composed of multiple elements, the mark must clearly specify the elements and the combination of these elements; if the mark contains a figurative element, the mark must clearly specify the content and meaning of the figurative element;
(ii) If a request for protection of a color mark is made, the request must be clearly stated and the color represented on the mark must be named;
(iii) If the mark contains letters or words which are not Vietnamese, the pronunciation (transliteration into Vietnamese) must be clearly stated, and if such letters or words have meaning, they must be translated into Vietnamese; (iv) If the mark contains digits which are not Arabic or Roman numerals, they must be translated into Arabic numerals.
e) The “List of goods and services bearing the mark” in the declaration must be grouped in accordance with the International Classification of Goods and Services under the Nice Agreement published by the National Office of Industrial Property in the Official Gazette of Industrial Property.
37.5 Requirements for the trademark sample
Apart from the trademark sample affixed on the application, the application must be enclosed with 09 identical trademark samples and must satisfy the following requirements:
a) The trademark sample must be clearly presented with the size of each component in the trademark not larger than 80mm and not smaller than 8mm, the whole trademark must be presented in the trademark sample with the size of 80mm x 80mm on the application;
b) For the 3D trademark, the trademark sample must be enclosed with a photograph or drawing showing the perspective and may be enclosed with a description in the form of a projection;
c) For the trademark with color protection requirements, the trademark sample must be presented in the color required for protection. If color protection is not required, the trademark sample must be presented in black and white.
37.6 Requirements for the regulations on use of collective trademarks and certification trademarks The regulations on use of collective trademarks and regulations on use of certification trademarks must contain the contents corresponding to those prescribed in Clauses 4 and 5 Article 105 of the Intellectual Property Law and must clarify the following issues:
a) Brief information on the trademark, the trademark owner, the goods and services bearing the trademark;
b) Conditions for the trademark applicant to be licensed to use the trademark and conditions for termination of the right to use the trademark;
c) Obligations of the trademark user (to ensure quality and distinctive characteristics of goods and services bearing the trademark, to be subject to the control of the trademark registrant, to pay trademark management fees, etc.);

d) Rights of the trademark registrant (to control the compliance with the trademark use regulations, to collect trademark management fees, to suspend the right to use the trademark of the user who fails to satisfy the conditions prescribed in the trademark use regulations, etc.);
e) Mechanisms for licensing, control and inspection of the use of trademarks and ensuring the quality and reputation of goods and services bearing the trademark;
g) Mechanisms for dispute resolution.
37.7 Requirements for documents certifying the permission for registration and use of geographical origin certification marks
a) For geographical origin certification marks, in addition to the trademark use regulations and necessary documents proving the right to register the trademark, the application must also be accompanied by a permit from the relevant local government allowing the applicant to register the mark certifying the bearing of a geographical origin indication (geographical name, symbol, map of a region or locality) for goods and services bearing such mark.
b) In case it is impossible to identify the region or locality bearing the geographical origin indication based on administrative boundaries and the permit from the local government specified in Point 37.7.a above, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall request the applicant to provide a geographical map clearly indicating the scope of the region or locality bearing the geographical origin indication of the goods and services, certified by the relevant local government.
38. Examination of the form and publication of trademark registration applications
The procedures for examination of the form and publication of the trademark registration application shall comply with the general procedures specified in Points 13 and 14 of this Circular.
39. Examination of the contents of the trademark registration application
39.1 The National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the validity of the contents of the application in accordance with the general procedures specified in Point 15 of this Circular and the specific provisions of this Point.
39.2 Assessment of the compatibility between the subject matter specified in the trademark registration application and the request for issuance of the trademark registration certificate
a) According to the provisions of Clause 1 Article 72 of the Intellectual Property Law, a sign registered as a trademark must be a visible sign in the form of letters, numbers, words, drawings, images, including three-dimensional shapes or combinations of these elements expressed in one or a number of specific colors.
b) The following types of signs shall not be protected as trademarks:
(i) Signs which are only colors and are not combined with letter signs or shape signs or are not expressed in the form of letter signs or shape signs;
(ii) Signs which are not protected as trademarks according to the provisions of Article 73 of the Intellectual Property Law;
(iii) Signs contrary to social order and harmful to national security.
39.3 Assessment of the distinguishability of signs in the form of letters and numbers (hereinafter referred to as “letter signs”) in accordance with Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property.
Except for the exceptional cases specified in Point 39.5 of this Circular, the following letter signs are considered not distinguishable:
a) Characters of languages that Vietnamese consumers with ordinary knowledge cannot recognize and remember (cannot read, understand, or remember) such as non-Latin characters: Arabic, Slavic, Sanskrit, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, etc.; unless such characters are accompanied by other components that form a whole capable of being distinguished or presented in a graphic or other special form.
b) Although being Latin characters, the sign consists of only one letter or only numbers, or although having two letters, they cannot be read as a word - even when accompanied by numbers; unless such signs are presented in a graphic or other special form.
c) A set of too many letters or words that cannot be recognized and remembered as a sequence of too many characters not arranged in a certain order or according to a certain rule or a text, a paragraph;
d) Although being Latin characters, it is a word with a meaning that has been widely used and common in Vietnam in the relevant field to the extent of losing distinguishability;
e) A word or a set of words used in Vietnam as the common name of the relevant goods or services;

g) A word or a set of words that describes the main goods or services bearing the mark as a sign indicating time, place, or geographical origin (except for marks registered as geographical origin certification marks or collective marks), production methods, types, quantities, quality, properties (except for marks registered as quality certification marks), components, uses, or value of goods or services;
h) A word or a set of words that describes the legal form or business sector of the mark owner;
i) A sign identical or similar to one of the objects under the industrial property rights protection scope of another person as prescribed in Points e, g, h, i, k, l, m, Clause 2, Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
k) A sign that is misleading, confusing, or deceptive to consumers regarding the origin, properties, uses, quality, value, or other characteristics such as composition, production process, raw materials, or superiority of goods or services as prescribed in Clause 5, Article 73 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
l) A sign that is identical or similar to the extent of causing confusion with the real name, nickname, or pen name of a leader, national hero, or famous person of Vietnam or a foreign country; or identical or similar to the extent of causing confusion with the name of a character or figure in a work under the copyright protection scope of another person that is widely known, except for cases where permission is granted by the copyright owner.
39.4 Assessment of the distinctiveness of signs in the form of drawings or images (hereinafter referred to as “signs in the form of images”) as prescribed in Clause 2, Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property
Except for the exceptional cases prescribed in Point 39.5 of this Circular, signs in the form of images are considered not distinctive if:
a) The sign in the form of an image is a common shape or geometric shape such as a circle, ellipse, triangle, quadrilateral, or simple drawing; drawings or images are used only as backgrounds or decorative lines of products or product packaging;
b) The drawing or image is too complicated and confusing, making it difficult for consumers to perceive and remember the characteristics of such a drawing or image, including too many images, lines combined or overlapped;
c) The drawing, image, symbol or sign is widely used;
d) The drawing or image describes the main goods or services bearing the mark; the location, production method, geographical origin, type, quantity, quality, nature, composition, use, value or other characteristics of the goods or services bearing the mark;
e) The sign is identical or not significantly different from the industrial design of another person that is protected;
g) The sign is identical or similar to the image of a national leader, national hero or famous person of Vietnam or foreign countries; identical or similar to the image of a character or figure in a work under the protection of another person's copyright that is widely known, unless otherwise permitted by the owner of such work.
39.5 The following exceptions shall apply when assessing the distinctiveness of a sign:
a) The sign falls under the cases specified in Points 39.3.a, b, c, g, h and Points 39.4.a, b, c, d, e of this Circular and is already used as a mark and widely known by consumers, thereby achieving distinctiveness for related goods or services.
b) To apply this exception, the applicant must provide evidence of the widespread use of the mark (the time of use, scope, current level of use, etc., where the mark is considered “used” only when such use is conducted in lawful production, business, commercial, advertising and marketing activities) and evidence of the mark's distinctiveness for related goods or services of the mark owner. In this case, the mark is recognized as having distinctiveness only when it is presented in the form in which it is continuously and widely used in practice.
39.6 Assessment of distinctiveness of signs combining a character sign and a figurative sign (hereinafter referred to as “combined signs”)

A combined mark is considered capable of being distinguished when the combined mark of the character mark and the figurative mark is capable of being distinguished, specifically:
a) The signs and marks are capable of being distinguished and forming a distinguishable whole;
b) The strong components of a mark (the elements that strongly affect the consumer's sense, attract attention and create impression of the mark when observed) are the sign or mark that is capable of distinguishing, although the rest of the components are not or are not capable of distinguishing;
c) In case the combined mark comprises signs of words and signs of figures which are not or are unlikely to be distinguishable from each other but the unique way of combining these signs creates a distinct impression, the whole combined mark shall still be considered as distinguishable;
d) Combined signs consisting of written and graphic elements which are not or are unlikely to be distinguishable from each other but which, as a whole, are distinguishable through use as prescribed in Point 39.5 of this Circular.
39.7 Minimum information sources
a) To assess the potential for confusion of the sign mentioned in the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall, at least, search the following minimum sources of information:
(i) The trademark registration applications filed at the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam with filing dates or priority dates earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined and the international trademark registration applications designated for Vietnam which the Intellectual Property Office of Vietnam has been notified by WIPO with filing dates or priority dates earlier than the filing date or priority date of the application being examined for goods or services identical or similar;
(ii) Trademarks which have been registered for protection or recognized for protection and are still valid in Vietnam (including well-known marks) used for goods or services which are identical, similar or related; (iii) Registered marks which have expired for a period of not more than 5 years, except for marks which have expired due to non-use as prescribed in Point d Clause 1 Article 95 of the Intellectual Property Law, used for identical or similar goods or services;
(iv) Geographical indications currently protected in Vietnam;
(v) Indication of the geographical origin of goods and services; geographical names, quality marks, inspection marks; national flags and coats of arms; flags, names and symbols of Vietnamese and foreign agencies and organizations; names and images of national leaders and heroes, famous Vietnamese and foreign figures... collected and preserved by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
b) Where necessary, reference information sources other than the minimum information sources specified in Point 39.7.a above may be consulted, such as applications for industrial design registration, trade names, etc.;

39.8 Assessment of the likelihood of confusion of the mark to be registered with other marks
a) To assess whether the mark in the application is identical or similar to another mark (hereinafter referred to as the “comparing mark”) to the extent of causing confusion, it is necessary to compare the structure, content, pronunciation (for the word mark), meaning and form of the mark (for both the word mark and the figurative mark), and compare the goods or services bearing the mark with the goods or services bearing the comparing mark as prescribed in this point.
b) Identical marks: A mark is considered identical to a mark if it is identical to the mark in terms of structure, content, meaning and form of expression.
c) Signs of being considered similar to the mark in question to the extent of causing confusion with the mark in question if:
(i) Such sign is similar to the mark in question in terms of structure or/or content or/or pronunciation or/or meaning or/or form of expression to the extent that consumers may mistake the two objects for one another or this object for a variant of the other object or that the two objects have the same origin;
(ii) Such sign is merely a phonetic transcription or translation of the mark in question if the mark in question is a well-known mark.
39.9 Assessment of similarity of goods and services
a) Two goods or two services are considered identical (of the same type) when such goods or services have the following characteristics:
(i) They have the same nature (composition, structure...) and the same function and purpose of use; or (ii) They have similar nature and the same function and purpose of use;

b) Two goods or two services are considered similar when such goods or services have the following characteristics:
(i) They are similar in nature; or (ii) They are similar in function and purpose of use; and (iii) They are put on the market through the same commercial channel (distributed in the same manner, sold together or in competition with each other, in the same type of store...);
c) A good and a service are considered similar if they fall into one or the following cases: (i) They are related to each other in terms of nature (goods, services or raw materials, parts of goods or services are made from goods or services of the other); or (ii) They are related to each other in terms of function (the use of goods or services of the other is necessary to complete the function of goods or services of this one or they are often used together); or (iii) They are closely related to each other in terms of mode of performance (goods or services of this one are the result of the use or exploitation of goods or services of the other...).
39.10. Check the first submission principle
Before issuing the certificate of registration of a mark, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall conduct examination to ensure the first application submission as prescribed in Article 90 of the Intellectual Property Law.

39.11 Conclusion on the likelihood of confusion between the sign and the counter-mark The sign is considered identical or similar to the counter-mark used for identical or similar goods or related goods in the following cases:
(i) The sign is identical to the mark in question and the goods or services bearing the identical or similar sign to the goods or services bearing the mark in question;
(ii) The sign is identical to the mark in question and the goods or services are identical to the goods or services bearing the mark in question of the same mark owner;
(iii) The signs are so similar that they are likely to cause confusion with the mark in question and the goods or services bearing the mark in question, except for exceptional cases where the similarity of goods or services and the similarity of signs are insufficient to cause confusion when using the similar signs;
(iv) The sign is identical or similar to the mark in question, which is a well-known mark, and the goods or services bearing such sign are not identical or similar to the goods or services bearing such mark, but the use of such sign as a mark may cause consumers to believe that there is a connection between the goods or services bearing such sign and the owner of the well-known mark, which may actually reduce the distinctiveness of the well-known mark or damage the reputation of the well-known mark.
39.12 Assessment of other potential confusion-inducing marks
The assessment of other potential sources of confusion of the sign shall comply with Article 73 and Clause 2 Article 74 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the following specific provisions.
a) Signs of being considered as confusing the origin or source of goods or services in the following cases:
(i) Marks identical or similar to the name or symbol of a State or territory (flag, coat of arms, national name, country name, local name) or so similar as to cause confusion with the name or symbol of a State or territory, giving the impression that goods or services bearing such mark originate from that State or territory, but in fact originate from another State or territory; (ii) Marks identical or similar to geographical indications which are protected, if the use of such marks may cause consumers to misunderstand the geographical origin of goods; marks identical to geographical indications or containing geographical indications or being transliterated from or translated from protected geographical indications for wine or spirits, if the mark for registration as a mark for wine or spirits does not originate from the geographical area bearing such geographical indication;
(iii) Signs which are identical or similar to the trade names of other persons which have been lawfully used for the same goods or services and are likely to induce consumers to believe that the goods or services bearing such signs are produced or performed by the persons whose trade names are mentioned; signs which are identical or similar to the trade marks of other persons which have been lawfully used for the same goods or services and are likely to induce consumers to believe that the goods or services bearing such signs are produced or performed by the persons whose trade marks are mentioned;

(iv) Marks identical or similar to the real names, nicknames, pen names, images of leaders, national heroes, famous persons of Vietnam and foreign countries; marks identical or similar to names or images of characters, distinctive figures of widely known works, if the use of such marks is likely to cause consumers to mistake goods or services bearing such marks as being produced or performed by the owners of such works;
(v) The sign is identical or substantially identical to the industrial design of another person protected on the basis of an industrial design registration application with a filing date or priority date earlier than the filing date or priority date of the trademark registration application.
b) In the following cases, the signs are considered likely to cause confusion or misinterpretation of the nature and value of goods and services:
(i) Signs which are words, drawings, images, symbols, etc. which create a false impression of the functions and uses of goods or services, such as signs which are identical or similar to trademarks or other signs which are widely used to the extent that they are considered to be associated with a certain function or use of a certain type of goods or services, causing consumers to mistakenly believe that goods or services bearing such signs also have such functions and uses;
(ii) Signs are words or images that create a false impression of the composition or structure of goods or services, such as describing goods or services other than those bearing the sign, creating a false impression that goods or services bearing the sign are made up of or have the nature of the described goods or services.

41. Processing of applications for international registration of Vietnamese-origin marks and applications for international registration of marks designated with Vietnam
41.1 Procedures for international trademark registration applications of Vietnamese origin The procedures for handling trademark registration applications specified in this Circular shall also apply to the handling of international trademark registration applications at the National Office of Intellectual Property.
41.2 Right to register international trademarks based on the registration of the mark at the Vietnamese office
a) The person who has been granted the Certificate of registration of a mark in Vietnam is entitled to register the corresponding mark internationally under the Madrid Agreement;
b) The applicant for the mark registration in Vietnam and the applicant for the mark registration certificate in Vietnam may apply for the international registration of the corresponding mark under the Madrid Protocol.

41.3 The application for international registration of a mark originating from Vietnam
a) The application for international registration of a mark must specify the country wishing to be protected as a member of the Madrid Convention and must not specify any country as a member of the Madrid Protocol in French.
b) An application for international registration of a mark designating at least one country as a member of the Madrid Protocol, including simultaneously designating the country as a member of the Madrid Convention, must be made in English or French.
c) The applicant shall submit an application for international registration of a mark using Form 06-ĐKQT specified in Appendix C of this Circular and an application for international registration of a mark using a form provided free of charge by the National Office of Intellectual Property. The application must clearly state the countries that are members of the Madrid Convention (which may simultaneously be members of the Madrid Protocol) and the country that is only a member of the Madrid Protocol and the applicant wishes to register the mark for protection. The application for international registration of a mark must be made by accurately and fully filling in the information in the sections for the applicant and must be enclosed with the mark forms exactly as the mark forms that have been registered in Vietnam.
d) The applicant shall preliminarily calculate the total amount of fees and charges according to the fee schedule printed on the application form or may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to inform the applicant of the exact amount of fees and charges to be paid to the International Office. The applicant shall pay these fees and charges directly to the International Office and pay additional fees and charges related thereto according to regulations for the National Office of Intellectual Property.
e) The applicant shall ensure that the information (especially the name and address of the applicant, goods and services and the goods and services group) declared in the application for international registration of a mark is accurate, including the language, translation and consistency with the information stated in the certificate of registration of the basic mark or the corresponding basic mark registration application. The applicant shall be responsible for paying any fees arising from the amendment or supplementation of the application for international registration of a mark due to the declaration of inaccurate or inconsistent information according to the notice of the International Office.

g) All correspondence and transactions related to the application for international registration of a mark shall be made through the National Office of Intellectual Property. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall be responsible for promptly notifying the applicant's requests to the International Office and vice versa, in accordance with the relevant international treaty.
41.4 The agency receiving the application for international registration of a mark originating from Vietnam
a) The application for international registration of a mark shall be submitted to the International Office through the National Office of Intellectual Property. The National Office of Intellectual Property shall be responsible for transferring the application for international registration of a mark to the International Office within 30 days from the date of receipt of a valid application document according to regulations.
b) The date on which the National Office of Intellectual Property receives the application for international registration of a mark shall be considered the date of submission of the application for international registration of a mark in case the International Office receives the application within 2 months from the date inscribed on the receipt stamp of the National Office of Intellectual Property. In case the application is not completed by the applicant and sent to the International Office within the aforementioned time limit, the date of receipt of the application at the International Office shall be considered the date of submission of the application for international registration of a mark.
41.5 Addition, amendment, and transfer of international trademark registration applications and international trademark registrations of Vietnamese origin
a) After the application is submitted to the International Office, all transactions between the applicant and the International Office related to the modification of the name, address, restriction of the list of goods and services specified in the application for international registration of the mark, international registration of the mark, or extension of the validity of the international registration of the mark shall be made according to Form 08-SĐQT specified in Appendix C of this Circular and all transactions shall be processed through the National Office of Intellectual Property. The applicant shall pay fees and charges for such transactions as prescribed.
b) The mark owner shall request the International Office to record the transfer of the mark ownership right under the international mark registration through the National Office of Intellectual Property (or directly request the International Office for international mark registration in a country that is only a member of the Madrid Protocol), in accordance with the relevant international treaties and pay the fees and charges as prescribed.

41.6 Processing of international mark registration applications designated for Vietnam
a) Upon receiving the notification of the International Office of the application for international registration of a mark designated for Vietnam, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall examine the contents of the application according to the procedures applicable to applications for mark registration filed directly with the National Office of Intellectual Property. Within 12 months from the date of the notification of the International Office, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a conclusion on the protectability of the mark.
b) For a mark eligible for protection under Vietnamese law, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision to accept the protection of the internationally registered mark and publish it in the Industrial Property Gazette, and record it in the National Register of Marks within 1 month from the date of issuance of the decision. The scope of protection shall be confirmed according to the contents of the mark registration application recorded by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) and confirmed by the National Office of Intellectual Property.
c) For a mark which is not eligible for protection or is partially rejected, before the expiration of the 12-month period, the Intellectual Property Office shall send a written notice of rejection to the International Office for notification to the applicant, clearly stating the reasons and contents of the rejection.
d) Within 3 months from the date of the written refusal, the applicant may lodge a complaint against the written refusal. The complaint and settlement procedures shall be the same as those for the application for registration of a mark submitted directly to the National Office of Industrial Property. The results of the complaint settlement shall be notified by the National Office of Industrial Property to the International Office and the applicant.
e) From the date on which the internationally registered mark is recognized and takes effect in Vietnam, the Intellectual Property Office shall, at the request of the mark owner, issue a certificate of the internationally registered mark protected in Vietnam, provided that the applicant pays the fee as prescribed.
41.7 Extension of validity of international registration of Vietnamese-origin trademarks Within 06 months before the expiration date of the international registration of a trademark (20 years from the date of filing of the application for international registration of a trademark for a member country of the Madrid Agreement, including a country that is also a member of the Madrid Protocol; 10 years from the date of filing of the application for international registration of a trademark for a country that is only a member of the Madrid Protocol), the trademark owner shall pay the fee for extension of validity of the international registration of the trademark as notified by the International Office.

41.8 Application for trademark conversion due to cancellation of the international registration of the trademark
a) In case the owner of the trademark registered internationally in Vietnam is a country which is a member of the Madrid Protocol and the Madrid Protocol has been declared invalid under Article 9quinquies of the Madrid Protocol, such owner may file a trademark registration application for conversion using Form 07-ĐKCĐ specified in Appendix C of this Circular to the National Office of Intellectual Property for registration of protection for the same trademark for a part or all of goods and services listed in the invalid international trademark registration.

b) A registration application for a transformed mark shall be accepted if it satisfies the following conditions: (i) The application is filed within 03 months after the expiration date of the registration of the corresponding international mark;
(ii) Goods and services described in the application for registration of the transformed mark fall within the scope of the list of goods and services stated in the international trademark registration;
(iii) The application for registration of a transformed mark shall satisfy all requirements on form and content as prescribed by Vietnamese law and the applicant shall pay all fees and charges as prescribed.
c) The date of filing of the international mark application or the priority date of the corresponding international mark application (if the international mark application is entitled to priority under an international treaty) shall be the date of filing of the application for conversion of the mark.
d) The National Office of Intellectual Property shall process the application for registration of a transformed mark as for a regular mark.
42. Recording of famous marks
42.1 A well-known mark shall be protected under Vietnamese law as prescribed in Article 75 of the Intellectual Property Law and in accordance with Article 6bis of the Paris Convention on industrial property.

42.2 The right to a well-known mark shall be protected and belong to the owner of such mark without registration procedures. The mark owner may use the documents specified in Point 42.3 of this Circular to prove his/her ownership of the mark and prove that the mark meets the conditions for being considered well-known.
42.3 Documents proving the ownership of a mark and proving the mark's reputation include information on the scope, extent, degree and continuity of the use of the mark, including an explanation of the origin, history and duration of the continuous use of the mark; the number of countries where the mark has been registered or recognized as a famous mark; the list of goods and services bearing the mark; the territorial scope in which the mark is used, the sales of products or provision of services; the number of goods and services bearing the mark that have been manufactured or sold; the value of the mark's assets, the value of the transfer or assignment of the right to use the mark, the value of the mark's investment contribution; the investment and expenditure on the mark's advertising and marketing, including participation in national and international exhibitions; infringement cases, disputes and court decisions or decisions of competent authorities; data on consumer surveys on the mark's awareness through purchase, use and advertising and marketing; rankings, reputation of national and international organizations, mass media; awards and medals that the mark has won; results of the assessment of the intellectual property assessment organization.
42.4 In case a famous mark is recognized according to civil procedure or a recognition decision of the National Office of Intellectual Property, such famous mark shall be recorded in the List of famous marks kept at the National Office of Intellectual Property.

Section 1. PROCEDURES FOR REGISTRATION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY TRANSFER AGREEMENTS
47. Application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights 47.1 An application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights, made according to Form 01-HCN specified in Appendix D of this Circular;
b) 02 copies of the contract (original or valid copy); if the contract is made in a language other than Vietnamese, it must be accompanied by a Vietnamese translation; if the contract has multiple pages, each page must bear the signature of the parties or be stamped with a seal;
c) Original of the protection title;
d) Written consent of co-owners on the transfer of industrial property rights, if the corresponding industrial property rights are jointly owned;
e) Power of attorney (if submitting the application through a representative);
g) Documents on payment of fees and charges.
47.2 An application for registration of a contract for transfer of use rights of an industrial property object (the industrial property license contract) must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for registration of a contract for transfer of use rights of an industrial property object, made according to the form 02-HDSD specified in Appendix D of this Circular;
b) 02 copies of the contract (the original or a valid copy); if the contract is made in a language other than Vietnamese, it must be accompanied by a Vietnamese translation; if the contract has multiple pages, each page must bear the signature of the parties or be affixed with a seal;
c) Written consent of co-owners on the transfer of use rights of the industrial property object, if the corresponding industrial property rights are jointly owned;
d) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
e) Documents on payment of fees.

48. Procedures for handling registration applications
48.1 In case the application for registration of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights does not have the deficiencies specified in Point 48.3 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall perform the following tasks:
a) To issue decisions on granting certificates of registration of contracts for transfer of industrial property rights/transfer of use rights of industrial property objects;
b) (For contracts on transfer of industrial property rights): Record in the protection title the new owner; in case of transfer of a part of the list of goods and services bearing the protected trademark, issue a new trademark registration certificate to the transferee and determine the limit of the list of goods/services in the original protection title for the transferred part; or (for contracts on transfer of the right to use industrial property objects): Issue a certificate of registration of the contract on transfer of the right to use industrial property objects to the applicant; affix the registration seal to 02 copies of the contract and give 01 copy to the applicant, keep 01 copy;
c) Record the transfer of industrial property rights in the National Register of Transfer of Industrial Property Rights;
d) Publish the decision on granting the Certificate of registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights in the Industrial Property Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision.

48.2 In case the application for registration of the contract on transfer of industrial property rights is incomplete as prescribed in Point 48.3 of this Circular, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the following procedures:
a) Issue a notice of intention to refuse registration of a contract, clearly stating the deficiencies of the application and setting a time limit of 01 month from the date of signing the notice for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or provide comments on the intention to refuse registration of the contract;
b) Issue a notice of refusal to register a contract if the applicant fails to rectify or fails to rectify the deficiencies to the extent required, or if there is no objection or objection is not reasonable to the proposal to refuse registration of the contract within the prescribed time limit.
48.3 A dossier on registration of a contract for transfer of industrial property rights shall be considered defective if it falls into one of the following cases:
a) The declaration is invalid;
b) One of the documents in the list of required documents is missing;
c) The authorization letter is invalid;
d) The copy of the contract is not validly certified;
e) The name and address of the transferring party in the contract are inconsistent with the corresponding information in the protection title or the contract that is the basis for the emergence of the right to transfer, authorization letter, or declaration; the name and address of the transferees in the contract are inconsistent with the name and address stated in the authorization letter or declaration;
g) The contract is not fully signed (and stamped, if any) by the transferring party and the transferees;
h) The transferring party is not the owner of the protection title;
i) The relevant industrial property subject matter is no longer valid for the protection period or is in dispute;
k) The contract lacks the necessary contents as prescribed in Article 140 or Clause 1 Article 144 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
l) The contract contains contents inconsistent with the provisions on conditions restricting the transfer of industrial property rights in Article 139 of the Law on Intellectual Property or contains unreasonable clauses restricting the rights of the transferee to use the industrial property object as prescribed in Clause 2 Article 144 of the Law on Intellectual Property;
m) There are grounds to assert that the transfer of industrial property rights infringes upon the industrial property rights of a third party.
48.4 The time limit for processing applications for registration of contracts on transfer of industrial property rights is 2 months (excluding the time for applicants to correct deficiencies).
49. Recording of modification, extension, or termination of contracts on transfer of industrial property rights
49.1 The modification, extension, or termination of a registered contract on transfer of industrial property rights must be recorded at the National Office of Intellectual Property as prescribed in this Point.

49.2 Application for recording of modification, extension, or termination of a contract
a) An application for recording of modification, extension, or termination of a contract on transfer of industrial property rights must be made in writing and include the following documents:
(i) 02 copies of the application for recording the modification of the contract, extension of the contract or termination of the contract, made according to Form 03-SĐHĐ specified in Appendix D of this Circular;
(ii) Original copy of the Certificate of registration of the contract for transfer of the right to use the industrial property object (in case of registration of the modification of the contract or extension of the contract);
(iii) Documents proving the modification of the names and addresses of the parties to the contract;
(iv) Agreement or document recording the specific clauses to be modified or supplemented in the contract, including the extension or termination of the contract before the expiration date;
(v) Power of attorney (if the application is submitted through a representative);
(vi) Receipt of the fee.
b) The application for extension of the contract must be submitted no later than 01 month before the expiration date of the contract specified in the Certificate of registration of the contract for transfer of the right to use the industrial property object.
49.3 Within 01 month from the date of receipt of the application for recording the modification of the contract, extension of the contract or termination of the contract, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider the application according to the following regulations:
a) In case the application is valid, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to record the modification of contents, extension of validity, or termination of validity of the contract on transfer of right to use industrial property objects; record the contents of modification of contents, extension of validity, or termination of validity of the contract on transfer of right to use industrial property objects in the Certificate of registration of the contract on transfer of right to use industrial property objects; record the contents of modification of contents, extension of validity, or termination of validity of the contract on transfer of right to use industrial property objects in the National Register of Transfer of Industrial Property Rights; publish the decisions on recording the modification of contents, extension of validity, or termination of validity of the contract on transfer of right to use industrial property objects in the Industrial Property Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision;
b) In case the application is incomplete, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a notice of intention to refuse to record the amendment, extension or termination of the contract on transfer of rights to use industrial property objects, clearly stating the deficiencies of the application and setting a time limit of 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice of intention to refuse for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies or express objections to the intention to refuse registration of the contract; issue a notice of refusal to record the amendment, extension or termination of the contract on transfer of rights to use industrial property objects in case the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies of the application or remedies the deficiencies of the application but fails to meet the requirements, or fails to express objections or express unreasonable objections within the prescribed time limit.
Section 2. Procedures for decision on compulsory transfer of rights to use inventions
50. Application for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of invention use rights 50.1 An organization, individual capable, having the task or having the need to use the invention specified in Points a, b and c or being obstructed from competition specified in Point d Clause 1 Article 145 of the Intellectual Property Law may request a competent person specified in Clause 1 Article 147 of the Intellectual Property Law to issue a decision on compulsory transfer of invention use rights to him/her according to specific provisions in this Section.

50.2 An application for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of invention use rights must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention, made according to Form 04-CGBB specified in Appendix D of this Circular;
b) Documents proving the request for issuance of a decision on compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention are well-founded according to law, specifically:
(i) In case of request for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention based on the provisions in Point a Clause 1 Article 145 of the Intellectual Property Law, the application must contain documents proving that at the time of filing, there is a public, non-commercial use of the invention for national defense, security, disease prevention and treatment, nutrition of the people or for other urgent needs of society, but the inventor has not used the invention and the non-use of the invention will affect the achievement of the above-mentioned purposes;

 (ii) In case of request for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention based on the provisions in Point b Clause 1 Article 145 of the Intellectual Property Law, the application must contain documents proving that the inventor has not fulfilled the obligation to use the invention specified in Clause 1 Article 136 and Clause 5 Article 142 of the Intellectual Property Law and that at the time of filing the application, the 4-year period from the date of filing the application for registration of the invention and the 3-year period from the date of grant of the exclusive right to the invention have expired;
(iii) In case of request for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention based on the provisions in Point c Clause 1 Article 145 of the Intellectual Property Law, the application must contain documents proving that the inventor has failed to reach an agreement with the inventor with the right to use the invention on signing a contract for use of the invention, despite reasonable attempts to negotiate at an acceptable price and commercial conditions, which must clearly state the use of the invention, the duration of the negotiation, the specific price and commercial conditions proposed by the inventor;
(iv) In case of request for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention based on the provisions in Point d Clause 1 Article 145 of the Intellectual Property Law, the application must contain documents proving that the inventor has committed acts considered to be prohibited anti-competitive acts as prescribed by the law on competition;
(v) In case of request for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention in the field of semiconductor technology, the application must contain documents proving that the invention is used only for public, non-commercial purposes or documents proving that the inventor has committed acts considered to be prohibited anti-competitive acts as prescribed by the law on competition;
c) Power of attorney (if the application is filed through a representative);
d) Receipt of payment of fees.
51. Procedures for handling applications for issuance of decision on compulsory transfer of right to use invention 51.1 Applications shall be filed in accordance with the following regulations:
a) Applications falling under the cases specified in Points b, c and d Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property;
b) Applications falling under the cases specified in Point a Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property shall be submitted to the ministries and ministerial-level agencies managing the field of inventions.
51.2 Dossier assessment
When a dossier falls under the cases specified in Point 51.1.b of this Circular, the ministries and ministerial-level agencies shall designate an agency to receive and assess the dossier.
Within 2 months from the date of receipt of the dossier, the National Office of Intellectual Property or the dossier-assessing agency of the ministries and ministerial-level agencies (hereinafter referred to as “dossier-assessing agencies”) shall consider the dossier according to the following regulations:
a) In case the dossier is valid: Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the dossier, the dossier-assessing agency shall issue a notice of the request for transfer of invention use rights under the mandatory decision to the holder of the invention use rights and request that person to provide a written opinion within 1 month from the date of issuance of the notice; request the concerned parties to renegotiate to resolve disagreements in order to sign the contract on transfer of invention use rights, if deemed necessary; in case no agreement is reached between the parties and if the holder of the invention use rights is deemed unreasonable for refusing to sign the contract on transfer of invention use rights, report the results of dossier consideration and request the Minister of Science and Technology or the Minister or Head of the ministerial-level agency to issue a decision on mandatory transfer of invention use rights.
If the request falls under the cases specified in Point a Clause 1 Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property and the invention is used for public and non-commercial purposes, the ministries and ministerial-level agencies may issue a decision on mandatory transfer of invention use rights without requesting the holder of the invention use rights to provide an opinion and without requesting the parties to renegotiate.
b) In case the request for issuance of a decision on transfer of invention use rights lacks valid grounds as prescribed in Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the dossier-assessing agency shall report the results of dossier consideration and request the Minister of Science and Technology or the Minister or Head of the ministerial-level agency to issue a notice of the proposed refusal, clearly stating the reasons for refusal and setting a 1-month time limit from the date of issuance of the notice for the dossier-submitter to provide an opinion on the proposed refusal.
The time limit for applicants to submit documents to remedy deficiencies in their applications or to provide objections shall not be counted in the time limit for consideration of applications.

c) For dossiers under the cases specified in Point a, Clause 1, Article 145 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the dossier-approving agencies of ministries and ministerial-level agencies shall send the dossiers to the Ministry of Science and Technology (via the National Office of Intellectual Property) for comments before submitting them to the ministers or heads of ministerial-level agencies for decision as prescribed in Points a and b above. Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the dossiers, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall consider the dossiers and report to the Ministry of Science and Technology for the minister to issue a written opinion requesting the minister or head of the ministerial-level agency to issue a decision on compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention or a notice of refusal.
51.3 Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the report on the examination of the application by the National Office of Intellectual Property, the Minister of Science and Technology shall consider and issue a decision on compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention, or send a notice of refusal of compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for refusal.
Within 15 days from the date of receipt of the written request of the Minister of Science and Technology, the Minister or the head of a ministerial agency shall consider and issue a decision on compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention, or send a notice of refusal of compulsory transfer of the right to use the invention to the applicant, clearly stating the reason for refusal.
In case of disagreement with the proposal of the Minister of Science and Technology, the Minister or the head of the ministerial agency shall notify in writing, clearly stating the reason.
51.4 The decision on compulsory transfer of the right to use an invention shall be sent by the Minister or the head of a ministerial agency to the person to whom the right to use the invention is transferred, the person holding the exclusive right to use the invention and the National Office of Intellectual Property.
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall record the decision in the National Register of Industrial Property Transfer within 01 month and publish it in the Industrial Property Gazette within 02 months from the date of signing the decision.
52. Request for termination of the right to use an invention under a compulsory decision 52.1 The termination of the right to use an invention under a compulsory decision must be decided by the Minister or the head of a ministerial agency - the person who has issued the compulsory decision on transfer. 52.2 A request for termination of the right to use an invention under a compulsory decision shall include the following documents:
a) Written request for termination of the right to use the invention under the compulsory decision;
b) Documents proving that the grounds for the transfer of the right to use the invention under the compulsory decision no longer exist and cannot be re-established, and that the termination of the use of the invention does not cause damage to the person to whom the right to use the invention under the compulsory decision is transferred;
c) Power of attorney (if the request is submitted through a representative);
d) Receipts of fees paid.
52.3 The procedures for receiving and handling the request for termination of the right to use the invention under the compulsory decision and issuing the termination decision shall be the same as those for receiving and handling the application for transfer of the right to use the invention under the compulsory decision specified in Point 51 of this Circular.
Chapter III
                            INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION
Section 1. GRANT AND REVOCATION OF PROFESSIONAL LICENSES FOR INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATION
53. Grant of practice certificates
53.1 Conditions for issuance of practice certificates
Only persons who satisfy the conditions specified in Clause 2 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property may be granted certificates of industrial property representation practice. To be granted the certificate, such person must submit an application to the National Office of Intellectual Property as prescribed in this Point.
53.2 Dossier of application for issuance of practice certificate
An application for an industrial property representation practice certificate must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for the industrial property representation practice certificate (practice certificate), made according to Form 01-CCHN specified in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) A copy of the notice of passing the industrial property representative professional examination organized by the National Office of Intellectual Property;
c) 02 photos 3x4;

d) Documents on payment of fees.
53.3 Processing of applications for issuance of practice certificates:
In case of a valid application, within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, based on the application, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on issuance of the practice certificate and issue the practice certificate to the applicant; record the issuance of the practice certificate in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives; publish the issuance of the practice certificate in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision; or notify the deficiencies of the application and set a 1-month time limit for the applicant to remedy the deficiencies; or issue a notice of refusal to issue the practice certificate, clearly stating the reason for refusal, if the applicant fails to remedy the deficiencies/remedy the deficiencies to meet the requirements or the application is invalid.
54. Revocation of practice certificates
If an industrial property representative commits a violation and is sanctioned with revocation of the practice certificate, based on the decision on revocation of the practice certificate of the industrial property representative service of the competent administrative violation-handling authority, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue a decision on revocation of the practice certificate; remove the name of the industrial property representative from the list of industrial property representatives of the industrial property representative service; publish the revocation of the practice certificate in the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision.
55. Reissuance of practice certificates
The National Office of Intellectual Property shall carry out the procedures for reissuance of the practice certificate of the industrial property representative service at the written request made according to Form 02-CLCC specified in Appendix E of this Circular by the industrial property representative in case the practice certificate is lost or damaged (torn, dirty, faded, etc.) to the extent that it cannot be used.
The procedures for reissuance of the practice certificate are similar to the procedures for issuance of the practice certificate specified in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
Section 2. RECORDING, MODIFICATION AND DELETION OF NAMES OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATIVE SERVICE PROVIDERS
56. Recording of industrial property representative service providers
56.1 To be officially entitled to represent applicants before the National Office of Industrial Property, industrial property representative service providers must carry out procedures for recording in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives as prescribed in this Point.
56.2 An application for recording of an industrial property representative service provider in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for recording of an industrial property representative service provider, made according to Form 03-YCGN specified in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) List of members of the organization with certificates of industrial property representative service practice, enclosed with copies of recruitment decisions or labor contracts for each of the above-mentioned members;
c) Copies of business registration certificates or operation registration certificates of the organization;
d) The organization's industrial property representative service fee schedule;
e) A written authorization of the head of the organization to one of the members of the list specified in Point 56.2.b above (if necessary);
g) Receipts of fees paid.
56.3 Within 1 month from the date of receipt of the application, the National Office of Industrial Property shall consider the application for registration of an industrial property representation service provider in the same manner as the procedures for granting certificates of industrial property representation service providers specified in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
57. Recording of changes to information on industrial property representation service organizations 57.1 Industrial property representation service organizations may request the Intellectual Property Office to record changes to their names and addresses or changes to their members on the list of industrial property representatives.
57.2 An application for registration of amendment to information on industrial property representative services must include the following documents:
a) 02 copies of the application for registration of amendments to information on industrial property representative services, made according to Form 04-YCSĐ specified in Appendix E of this Circular;
b) Copies of the business registration certificate/operation registration certificate that have been amended by the industrial property representative service (in case of change of the organization’s name or address);

c) Decision on recruitment or termination of labor contracts for industrial property representatives who are members of an organization (in case of change of members in the list of industrial property representatives of the organization);
d) Receipts of fees paid.
57.3 The procedures for handling dossiers on request for modification of information on industrial property representative services are similar to those specified in Point 53.3 of this Circular.
58. Deletion of names of industrial property representative service organizations
In the cases specified in Clause 2 Article 156 of the Law on Intellectual Property, the National Office of Industrial Property shall issue a decision to remove the name of the industrial property representative service organization; record the removal of the name of the industrial property representative service organization in the National Register of Industrial Property Representatives; notify the competent authority that grants the business registration certificate or the operation registration certificate of the organization of the removal of the name of the industrial property representative service organization; publish the removal of the name on the Industrial Property Official Gazette within 2 months from the date of signing the decision.
Section 3. PROFESSIONAL EXAMINATION OF INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY REPRESENTATIVE
59. Examination of industrial property representation professional competence
The examination of industrial property representative competence (hereinafter referred to as “examination”) as prescribed in Article 28 of the Decree on industrial property shall be conducted as follows.
59.1. Industrial property office representative examination council:
The Industrial Property Representative Examination Council (hereinafter referred to as “the Examination Council”) is established by the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property and is tasked with setting examination questions, conducting examination and grading examination papers in accordance with the Examination Regulations on Industrial Property Representative Examination issued by the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property (hereinafter referred to as “the Examination Regulations”).
The Examination Council comprises the Chairman of the Council - the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property, the Deputy Chairman of the Council, the Secretary and other members appointed by the Chairman of the Council from the list of experts eligible to join the Examination Council. The list of experts eligible to join the Examination Council comprises experts familiar with the law and the practice of law on establishment of industrial property rights of the National Office of Intellectual Property and the professional and social organizations on industrial property representation approved by the Minister of Science and Technology on the basis of proposals of these agencies and organizations. Decisions of the Council are passed by a vote with two-thirds of the members of the Council.
Remuneration for the work of the members of the Council is paid from the revenue from fees for industrial property representative examination in accordance with the Examination Regulations.
59.2 Registration for examination:
a) Only persons who fully satisfy the conditions specified in Points a to dd Clause 2 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property may register for examination in accordance with this Point.
b) The application for registration for examination shall be submitted to the National Office of Intellectual Property, including the following documents: (i) 02 copies of the application for registration for examination (made according to Form 05-KTNV specified in Appendix E of this Circular);
(ii) A copy of the university degree certificate;
(iii) Documents proving that the applicant has been trained in industrial property law or has experience in this field: A copy of the certificate of completion of the industrial property law training course recognized by the Ministry of Science and Technology or the certification of the training institution where the applicant has completed their undergraduate or postgraduate thesis on industrial property topics; or a document certified by a competent authority that the applicant has directly worked on the assessment of industrial property registration applications at national or international industrial property agencies for 5 years or more or has directly worked on industrial property law as specified in Point d Clause 1 Article 155 of the Law on Intellectual Property (including inspection, examination, supervision, adjudication, legal affairs, legal consultation on industrial property; scientific research with the title of researcher, teaching industrial property);
(iv) 02 photos 3x4;
(v) Documents on payment of examination fees.
59.3 In case the application is valid, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the applicant of his/her eligibility for the examination and specify the time, place and schedule of the examination.

59.4 Contents and examination questions:

a) Contents of the examination include:
(i) Vietnamese industrial property laws and international treaties to which Vietnam is a party; (ii) Procedures for making, filing and pursuing industrial property registration applications;
(iii) Industrial property information search and exploitation;
(iv) Other contents, if any.
b) The test paper shall be enclosed with the answers and the mark scale prepared by the examination board, approved by the chairman of the examination board and kept confidential until the start of the examination.
59.5 Testing organization
a) The examination shall be conducted in batches (not periodically) when there are at least 05 people registering for the examination.
b) The test shall be graded by the examination board according to the approved answer key and mark scale.
c) The results of the examination shall be notified to the examinee by the National Office of Intellectual Property. The examinee may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to re-examine the examination, and the examination council shall re-examine the examination as prescribed by the Director of the National Office of Intellectual Property.
The examination result is valid for 2 years for the application for the industrial property representation service certificate.
Chapter IV
                     INDUSTRIAL PROPERTY INFORMATION GUARANTEE
60. Development and management of the national database on industrial property 60.1 The National Office of Intellectual Property shall develop and manage the national database on industrial property in order to promptly, fully and accurately meet the needs for industrial property information for research, application, development and other socio-economic activities.
60.2 The national database on industrial property comprises the following information that has been published, which is collected in a systematic and selective manner, suitable for the purpose of search:
a) Applications for industrial property registration;
b) The granted protection titles and industrial property rights recognized and protected in Vietnam;
c) The patent granted by the country/region with the most advanced science and technology;
d) Other types of certificates of protection of industrial property depending on the purpose of use of information.
61. Access to and use of industrial property information in national databases 61.1 All organizations and individuals have the right to access and use information in national databases on industrial property after registering their user accounts with the National Office of Intellectual Property.
61.2 Users of industrial property information (“information users”) must search for information in national databases.
Users may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to search for information in the national database and must pay the search fee as prescribed.
62. Industrial property information search services
62.1 When requesting the National Office of Industrial Property to search for industrial property information, the user of the information must make a search request form (using Form 01-YCTC, Form 02-YCTC, or Form 03-YCTC specified in Appendix F of this Circular), clearly stating the purpose and scope of the search request (field, type of information, time, country or region to be searched for, etc.).
62.2 Within 01 month from the date of receipt of the request for search, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall reply to the user.
In case of a request for validity search (with a request form for validity search as prescribed in Point 62.1 of this Circular and paid the search fee), the National Office of Intellectual Property shall reply by sending a “search report” to the user, clearly stating the results of the search and information search as requested by the user.

In case of invalid search requests (invalid search request forms, unclear search purposes and scope, failure to pay search fees, etc.), the National Office of Intellectual Property shall notify the rejection of the search request, clearly stating the reasons for rejection.
62.3 A search report shall include only the information found and the guidance on the origin of such information. If no information is found in the requested sources, the search report must also clearly state this.
The search report must not contain comments or evaluations on the information found.

62.4 The search report must clearly state the full name of the person conducting the search and take responsibility for the search result.
63. Ensuring industrial property information in localities 63.1 Depending on their conditions and capabilities, the industrial property management agencies (Science and Technology Departments) of provinces and centrally-run cities may set up and manage industrial property databases to ensure industrial property information for research, application, production and business development and protection of industrial property rights in localities.

63.2 The industrial property management agencies of provinces and centrally-run cities shall have to conduct and have the right to conduct activities to ensure industrial property information in accordance with this Circular.
64. Issuance of copies of documents
64.1 All relevant organizations and individuals may request the National Office of Intellectual Property to issue copies of documents issued or kept by the National Office of Intellectual Property, including originals or copies. Only applicants may request copies of documents related to their applications. Applicants must pay fees as prescribed.
64.2 Requests for copies of documents include:
a) A written request for copies of documents, in 02 copies;
b) The original document issued by the National Office of Intellectual Property (if the document is not kept by the National Office of Intellectual Property);
c) Proof of payment of fees.
64.3 Within 03 working days from the date of receipt of the request for copies of documents, the National Office of Intellectual Property shall issue copies or issue a notice of refusal, clearly stating the reason.
Chapter V
                                   IMPLEMENTATION PROVISIONS
65. Regulations on industrial property procedures The National Office of Intellectual Property shall promulgate regulations on industrial property procedures in accordance with the Decree on industrial property and this Circular.
66. Transitional provisions
66.1 An application for an invention registration under this Circular shall be understood as including both an application for an invention patent and an application for a utility solution patent as prescribed before.
66.2 For an international trademark recognized and protected in Vietnam under the previous regulations, the WIPO Bulletin or the National Office of Intellectual Property's Bulletin of industrial property shall publish such international trademark as a basis for confirming the right to such trademark.
66.3 Before the new regulations on fees and charges are promulgated, the fees and charges specified in this Circular shall be applied according to the Ministry of Finance's Circular No. 132/2004/TT-BTC dated December 30, 2004, guiding the collection, payment, management and use of industrial property fees and charges.
66.4 The previous forms of documents shall continue to be used until the National Office of Intellectual Property has completed the technical conditions and issued an official notice on the application of the forms of documents specified in this Circular.
67. Effect
67.1 This Circular replaces the following Circulars:
a) Circular No. 3055/TT-SHCN dated December 31, 1996 of the Ministry of Science, Technology and Environment guiding the implementation of regulations on procedures for establishing industrial property rights and other procedures in Decree No. 63/CP dated October 24, 1996 of the Government detailing industrial property;

b) Circular No. 29/2003/TT-BKHCN dated November 5, 2003 of the Ministry of Science and Technology guiding the implementation of procedures for establishing industrial property rights for industrial designs;
c) Circular No. 30/2003/TT-BKHCN dated November 5, 2003 of the Ministry of Science and Technology guiding the implementation of procedures for establishing industrial property rights for inventions/utilitarian solutions.
67.2 This Circular takes effect 15 days after its publication in the Official Gazette.
